## Money for the Internet Age

In the first two lessons, you learned about Bitcoin and the blockchain beneath it. Now let's step back and look at the whole category: **cryptocurrency**.

Cryptocurrency is digital money secured by **cryptography** — the science of secret codes. It lives on a blockchain and can be sent peer-to-peer, anywhere in the world, without a bank. That definition covers Bitcoin, but it also covers thousands of other digital assets, each with its own design and purpose.

Here is a helpful way to think about it: "cryptocurrency" is like the word "vehicle." A bicycle, a truck, and a racing car are all vehicles, but you would never use them interchangeably. Similarly, Bitcoin, Ethereum, and stablecoins are all cryptocurrencies — but they were built for very different jobs.

## What Makes Something a Cryptocurrency?

Three ingredients are almost always present:

1. **It is digital.** There are no physical coins. A "Bitcoin" is really just an entry on the Bitcoin blockchain — ownership recorded in the shared notebook you learned about in the last lesson.
2. **It is secured by cryptography.** Ownership is proven with private keys and digital signatures, not with usernames, passwords, or ID cards. Mathematics replaces the bank manager.
3. **It runs on a decentralized network.** Transactions are verified by a distributed network of computers following open rules, not by a single company approving them.

If a digital asset is missing these — for example, if a company can freeze it, reverse transactions, or print unlimited amounts at will — then whatever it is, it is not really a cryptocurrency in the meaningful sense. This is a useful filter when you encounter new projects later.

## The Main Types of Cryptocurrency

### Bitcoin (BTC) — The Original

The first and most widely recognized cryptocurrency, created in 2009. Bitcoin's design goal is simple: be sound, scarce, neutral money for the internet. People often call it **"digital gold"** — not because it is backed by gold, but because it shares gold's key traits: it is scarce (21 million maximum), hard to produce, and nobody's liability.

### Ethereum (ETH) — Programmable Money

Launched in 2015, Ethereum took the blockchain idea and made it **programmable**. Instead of only recording payments, Ethereum's blockchain can run *smart contracts* — self-executing programs that live on the network. This turned Ethereum into a platform for decentralized apps, and most of the crypto innovations you hear about (DeFi, NFTs) started there.

### Stablecoins (USDT, USDC) — The Steady Ones

These are cryptocurrencies designed to hold a stable value, usually pegged 1:1 to the US dollar. One USDT aims to always be worth about one dollar. Traders use them as a calm parking spot between volatile trades, and people in countries with unstable local currencies use them to hold dollar-like value. You will get a full lesson on stablecoins later in this track.

### Altcoins — Everything Else

"Altcoin" simply means any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin — Solana, Cardano, and thousands of others. Some altcoins are serious engineering projects with distinct designs; many are experiments; a large number are low-quality or outright scams. As a beginner, the safest mental default is: **Bitcoin and Ethereum are the established networks; everything else deserves extra skepticism and research.**

> A beginner's rule of thumb: if you can't explain in one sentence what a coin *does* that Bitcoin or Ethereum don't already do, you don't understand it well enough to trust it.

## How Do You Get Cryptocurrency?

Most beginners in India acquire crypto through an **exchange** — a platform where you can buy crypto with rupees via UPI or bank transfer after completing KYC identity verification. You will learn exactly how exchanges work in the next-but-one lesson.

Once you own crypto, you store it in a **wallet** — which, as the next lesson will explain, really means you hold the *keys* that control it. There are two broad kinds:

- **Hot wallets** — apps on your phone or browser, connected to the internet. Convenient for small amounts and learning, but more exposed to hacks and scams.
- **Cold wallets** — offline hardware devices (like a USB stick with a screen). Slower to use, but far safer for meaningful amounts.

For this course, understanding the *distinction* matters more than owning either. Do not rush to buy anything — the whole point of this track is to learn before you act.

## Tokens vs. Coins — A Quick Distinction

You will hear both words constantly, so let's settle it now:

- A **coin** has its own independent blockchain. Bitcoin runs on the Bitcoin blockchain; Ether runs on Ethereum.
- A **token** lives *on top of* someone else's blockchain. Most tokens you hear about are built on Ethereum or similar platforms, using their security and infrastructure.

It's like the difference between owning land (a coin) and renting an apartment in someone else's building (a token). Both are real, but the apartment depends on the building standing.

## A Reality Check Before You Go Further

Crypto prices are **highly volatile** — drops of 50% or more in weeks are normal history, not rare disasters. Many projects fail completely. Scams are everywhere, and in India, victims have almost no legal recourse once funds leave to an anonymous wallet.

India's tax rules are also strict: crypto gains are taxed at a flat **30%** (plus surcharge and cess), and a **1% TDS** applies on transfers. There is no special lower rate for long-term holding like there is for stocks. These rules apply whether you profit in rupees or in crypto terms.

So treat this space the way you would any powerful, high-risk technology — with genuine curiosity and genuine caution. The goal of this course is to make you *informed*, not to make you *invest*.

**Try it yourself (mentally):** The next time a friend says "I bought some crypto," ask them: *Which one? What does it do? Where are your keys stored?* If they can't answer all three, they are gambling, not investing — and now you know the difference.

## Recap

- Cryptocurrency = digital money secured by cryptography, running on decentralized networks.
- Main types: Bitcoin (digital scarcity), Ethereum (programmable platform), stablecoins (steady value), altcoins (everything else — research carefully).
- Coins have their own blockchains; tokens live on someone else's.
- You acquire crypto on exchanges and hold the keys in wallets — both covered in upcoming lessons.
