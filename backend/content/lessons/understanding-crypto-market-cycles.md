Crypto prices do not move in straight lines. They surge, crash, stagnate, and surge again — in rough patterns called **market cycles**. Understanding cycles will not tell you what happens next (nothing can), but it will help you recognise the emotional traps that cause most beginners to lose money: euphoria at the top and despair at the bottom.

> **Educational note:** This lesson describes historical patterns for emotional education only. It is not financial advice, contains no predictions, and nothing here suggests buying, selling, or holding any asset.

## What Is a Market Cycle?

A **market cycle** is the repeating pattern of expansion and contraction in asset prices, driven largely by human psychology. The classic version has four phases:

1. **Accumulation:** After a crash, prices are flat and boring. Most people have lost interest. Patient participants quietly build positions.
2. **Markup (bull market):** Prices start rising. Early gains attract attention, media coverage grows, and optimism builds. This is when your relatives start asking about crypto.
3. **Distribution:** Prices are high and euphoric. Headlines celebrate new records. Experienced participants start selling to the flood of newcomers arriving late.
4. **Markdown (bear market):** Prices fall — often 70 to 90 percent from the peak. Headlines declare crypto dead. Interest evaporates, and the cycle resets.

The full loop has historically taken roughly **four years** in crypto, loosely connected to Bitcoin's **halving** — an event every four years that cuts new Bitcoin supply in half. But "roughly" is doing heavy lifting: each cycle has differed in length, size, and character.

## A Brief, Factual History

- **2017:** Bitcoin's first mainstream mania, peaking near $20,000 in December, followed by a brutal 2018 bear market.
- **2020–2021:** A new bull run drove Bitcoin past $60,000, fuelled by institutional interest and pandemic-era liquidity.
- **2022:** A cascade of failures — major lenders and the FTX exchange collapsed — crushed prices and trust.
- **2023–2024:** Gradual recovery, followed by another halving in 2024.

Notice the pattern: every "crypto is dead" moment so far has eventually been followed by recovery — but also notice that past patterns **do not guarantee** future ones. Each cycle had unique causes, and believing "it always comes back" is itself a dangerous assumption.

## The Psychology Is the Real Lesson

Cycles are powered by two emotions: **greed** and **fear**. They feed on each other:

- When prices rise, **FOMO** (fear of missing out) pulls newcomers in at exactly the moment risk is highest.
- When prices crash, **panic** pushes people to sell at exactly the moment losses become permanent.
- **Social proof** amplifies both: everyone buying makes buying feel safe; everyone selling makes selling feel necessary.

The investors who survive cycles tend to share boring traits: they decide their approach in advance, they do not check prices obsessively, and they never invest money they need soon. There is no secret — just emotional discipline, which is far harder than it sounds.

## What Cycles Cannot Do

Be sceptical of anyone who claims to **time** cycles precisely. Common red flags:

- **"The top is in" / "the bottom is here"** — stated with certainty. Nobody knows in real time; tops and bottoms are only obvious in hindsight.
- **Charts that "prove" the next move.** Technical patterns describe the past; they do not predict the future.
- **Cycle theories sold as courses or signals.** If someone could reliably time markets, they would not need your subscription fee.

A healthier use of cycle knowledge: when you feel euphoric, ask whether you are in a distribution phase; when you feel despair, ask whether you are in accumulation. Use the framework to **question your emotions**, not to make trades.

## India-Specific Context

Indian investors face an extra wrinkle: **taxation**. India taxes crypto gains at a flat 30 percent (plus surcharge/cess) with no offsetting of losses, and a 1 percent TDS on transfers. This means frequent trading in and out of cycles is especially punishing — every profitable trade is taxed in full while losses give no relief. This is factual context, not advice: understand the tax rules before acting, and consider consulting a tax professional.

## Common Mistakes

- **Buying the top out of FOMO.** If everyone at a wedding is talking about a coin, the easy gains are usually gone.
- **Panic-selling the bottom.** Selling after a 70 percent crash locks in the loss permanently. (This is not advice to hold either — it is advice to make decisions calmly, in advance.)
- **Using leverage.** Borrowed-money trading during volatile cycles wipes out beginners with near certainty. Avoid it entirely while learning.
- **Assuming the last cycle repeats exactly.** "Bitcoin always recovers" is a belief about the past, not a fact about the future.
- **Checking prices constantly.** It feeds anxiety and impulsive decisions. Set a schedule — or step away.

## Recap Checklist

- Markets move in rough **cycles**: accumulation → bull market → distribution → bear market.
- Cycles are driven by **human psychology** — greed at tops, fear at bottoms.
- **History rhymes but does not repeat**; no one can time tops and bottoms reliably.
- Use cycle awareness to **manage your emotions**, never to predict prices.
- In India, the **30 percent tax + 1 percent TDS** regime makes frequent trading especially costly — know the rules.
- The winning traits are boring: a plan made in advance, money you can afford to lose, and emotional discipline.
