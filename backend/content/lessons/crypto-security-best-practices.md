You have now learned the individual pieces of crypto security: wallet safety, phishing defence, seed phrase protection, 2FA, hardware wallets, and public Wi-Fi caution. This lesson assembles them into a coherent set of habits — the daily and monthly practices that keep real users safe over years, not just days.

## Think in Layers, Not Silver Bullets

No single measure makes you safe. Security works like an onion: each layer is imperfect, but together they make attacking you expensive and difficult. Your layers:

1. **Your keys** — seed phrase on paper, offline, backed up and tested.
2. **Your accounts** — strong unique passwords plus authenticator-app 2FA.
3. **Your devices** — locked, updated, and free of sketchy software.
4. **Your behaviour** — sceptical of urgency, careful with links and signatures.
5. **Your storage split** — pocket money hot, savings cold.

An attacker must defeat all five layers to win. Most give up long before that.

## The Daily Habits

These take seconds and prevent the majority of incidents:

- **Lock your devices.** PIN, fingerprint, or face lock — always. An unlocked phone is an open wallet.
- **Use unique passwords.** Never reuse a password between your exchange, your email, and other sites. A password manager makes this painless; one master password protects dozens of unique ones.
- **Verify before you click.** Links in emails, DMs, and search ads get the bookmark treatment: navigate manually to the real site.
- **Check URLs before logging in.** Thirty seconds of attention defeats most phishing.
- **Keep wallet apps and your OS updated.** Updates patch the holes attackers actually use.

## The Setup Habits (Do Once, Review Yearly)

- **Write down and test every backup.** Seed phrases, 2FA setup keys — on paper, verified by a test restore.
- **Split storage by purpose.** Decide what lives in hot wallets (small, convenient) and what lives in cold storage (larger, offline).
- **Set up a hardware wallet** once your holdings justify it, bought only from the manufacturer.
- **Create an inheritance plan.** Write down — sealed, secure, and known to one trusted person — how your heirs can access your crypto if something happens to you. Without this, your crypto dies with you.
- **Document your accounts.** A simple list (kept offline) of which exchanges and wallets you use, so you can spot an unfamiliar login or a forgotten account.

## The Mindset Habits

Technology helps, but most crypto theft exploits psychology:

- **Default to scepticism.** In crypto, the safe assumption is that an unsolicited message is hostile until proven otherwise.
- **Slow down.** Every scam in this industry uses urgency as fuel. Deliberate people are hard to rob.
- **Never invest to "get rich quick."** Desperation makes people skip every safety step. Crypto is volatile and risky; only ever use money you can afford to lose completely.
- **Admit what you do not understand.** If you cannot explain what a dApp, signature, or token approval does, do not touch it yet. There is no shame in waiting — there is great shame in losing money to something you never understood.
- **Keep learning.** Attackers invent new tricks constantly. Revisiting security material every few months keeps your instincts sharp.

## Organising Your Security: A Simple System

Here is a concrete system many careful users follow:

| What | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Seed phrases | Paper/metal, two locations | Never digital, never shared |
| 2FA setup keys | Paper, with seed backups | Saved at setup time |
| Daily spending crypto | Hot wallet | Small amounts only |
| Savings crypto | Hardware wallet / cold storage | Verified addresses only |
| Exchange accounts | Unique password + authenticator 2FA | Withdrawal whitelists on |
| Email | Unique password + authenticator 2FA | Secured first — it guards everything |

Write your own version of this table, keep it offline, and review it twice a year.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- **Security theatre.** Installing five antivirus apps while keeping the seed phrase in a phone note. Focus on the fundamentals first: keys, accounts, behaviour.
- **All eggs, one basket.** Entire net worth on a single exchange, in a single wallet, with a single backup. Distribute and duplicate.
- **"I'll set up 2FA later."** Later is when the breach happens. The ten-minute setup is the cheapest insurance in crypto.
- **Trusting "crypto experts" in DMs.** Nobody legitimate offers to grow your money through private messages. Ever.
- **Forgetting the human layer.** The fanciest hardware wallet cannot help if you read your seed phrase aloud to a fake support agent.

## India-Specific Notes

- With 30% tax on crypto gains and 1% TDS in India, good record-keeping is part of good security: maintain an offline log of your wallets, exchanges, and major transactions. It protects you at tax time and helps you notice unauthorized activity.
- Use the official apps of Indian exchanges from the Play Store or App Store, and enable every security feature they offer — 2FA, withdrawal address whitelisting, and email/SMS alerts for logins and withdrawals.
- Report crypto fraud to cybercrime.gov.in and to the impersonated platform. Reporting helps the next potential victim even when recovery is unlikely.

## Recap Checklist

- I protect keys (paper backups, tested), accounts (unique passwords + 2FA), devices (locked, updated), behaviour (sceptical, deliberate), and storage (hot/cold split).
- I have a written, offline inventory of my wallets, exchanges, and backups.
- I have an inheritance plan so my crypto is recoverable by my heirs.
- I review my security setup at least twice a year.
- I treat urgency and unsolicited offers as hostile by default.

Security is not a product you buy; it is a practice you maintain. The habits in this lesson, repeated quietly over time, are what separate the people who thrive in crypto from the cautionary tales.
