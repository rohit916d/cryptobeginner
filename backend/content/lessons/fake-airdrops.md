An **airdrop** is when a crypto project distributes free tokens to users — usually to reward early supporters or spread awareness. Legitimate airdrops have made some early users significant money, which is exactly why scammers counterfeit them. **Fake airdrops are now one of the most common ways beginners lose their entire wallets.** This lesson teaches you to tell the difference.

> **Educational note:** This lesson is about security. It contains no financial advice and does not recommend chasing any airdrop.

## What a Legitimate Airdrop Looks Like

Real airdrops share these traits:

- They come from **established projects** with a real product, public team, and months or years of history.
- Eligibility is based on **past activity** (you used the product before a announced or unannounced snapshot date) — not on paying a fee or completing sketchy tasks.
- They are announced through the project's **official channels**: its verified website, official X account, or official Discord.
- Claiming may require a transaction (paying gas), but **never requires sending tokens to someone** or sharing private information.

Famous legitimate examples include Uniswap's 2020 airdrop to early users and Arbitrum's 2023 distribution. Notice the pattern: real usage first, reward later — never the reverse.

## How Fake Airdrops Work

### The wallet drainer

The most dangerous variant. You see an announcement — "Claim 500 XYZ tokens, worth $2,000!" — and connect your wallet to the claim site. The site asks you to **sign a transaction or grant token approvals**. Hidden in that innocent-looking signature is permission for the scammer's contract to **drain your wallet**: transferring out your ETH, stablecoins, and NFTs in seconds.

This is the critical thing to understand: **connecting your wallet is safe; signing malicious transactions is not.** The theft happens at the signature step, when victims approve without reading.

### The advance-fee claim

"To receive your airdrop, first send 0.1 ETH as a gas/verification fee." Real airdrops never work this way. The fee goes straight to the scammer, and no tokens ever arrive.

### The dusting + phishing combo

Scammers send tiny amounts of a token ("dust") to thousands of random wallets. Curious recipients look up the token, find a professional-looking website, connect their wallet to "sell" the tokens — and hit the drainer described above. **Unexpected tokens appearing in your wallet are bait, not gifts.** Never interact with them.

### The impersonator announcement

Fake social media accounts copy a real project's branding and announce a "surprise airdrop," often as replies to the project's genuine posts. The link leads to a pixel-perfect clone of the real claim site — with a drainer underneath.

## How to Verify Before You Touch Anything

Run every airdrop through this gauntlet:

1. **Source check:** Is it announced on the project's official website or long-standing verified account? Announcements that exist *only* as DMs, replies, or ads are fake.
2. **URL check:** Type the official URL yourself or use a bookmark. Never click airdrop links from messages, emails, or ads — lookalike domains are the whole game.
3. **The fee test:** Does claiming require sending crypto to someone? Then it is a scam. (Paying network gas for your own transaction is different — but be suspicious of any "fee" going to an address.)
4. **The too-good test:** An unknown project offering thousands of dollars in free tokens to everyone is not marketing — it is bait.
5. **The approval test:** Before signing anything, read what the signature actually does. If your wallet warns about granting access to "all" your tokens, **reject it immediately**.

## Defensive Habits

- **Use a separate "airdrop wallet"** with minimal funds for any experimental claiming. Your main holdings should live in a wallet that never touches unfamiliar sites.
- **Regularly review and revoke token approvals** using tools like revoke.cash or your wallet's approval manager. Old approvals to forgotten sites are ticking time bombs.
- **Keep wallet software updated** — updates often include new phishing and drainer warnings.
- **When in doubt, skip it.** No legitimate opportunity requires you to act in the next ten minutes. Missing a real airdrop costs you nothing; falling for a fake one can cost you everything.

## Common Mistakes

- **Interacting with surprise tokens.** If tokens appear in your wallet uninvited, ignore them. Looking them up and connecting to their site is exactly what the scammer wants.
- **Signing without reading.** The signature request is where theft happens. Slow down and read every approval — especially ones requesting unlimited access.
- **Using your main wallet for experiments.** One bad signature can empty years of holdings. Compartmentalise.
- **Trusting follower counts and slick websites.** Scammers buy followers and clone designs in hours. Polish proves nothing.
- **FOMO claiming.** "Claim ends in 1 hour!" is pressure by design. Real distributions give days or weeks.

## Recap Checklist

- Real airdrops reward **past usage** of established projects; they never demand upfront payments.
- The deadliest fakes are **wallet drainers** — theft happens when you **sign a malicious transaction**, not when you merely connect.
- **Unexpected tokens in your wallet are bait** — do not interact with them.
- Verify via **official channels only**, type URLs yourself, never click airdrop links from messages or ads.
- Read every signature; **reject unlimited token approvals** from unfamiliar sites.
- Use a **separate low-value wallet** for anything experimental, and revoke old approvals regularly.
- When in doubt, **skip it** — missing out costs nothing, getting drained costs everything.
