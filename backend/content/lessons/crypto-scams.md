Welcome to the Security track — the most important lessons on this site. Here is the uncomfortable truth that motivates everything here: **crypto transactions are irreversible**. If a bank transfer goes to a fraudster, your bank might reverse it. If you send crypto to a scammer, it is gone — no customer support, no chargeback, no recovery. Scammers know this, which is why crypto attracts them like nothing else.

This lesson maps the most common scam patterns so you can recognise them instantly. Memorise the patterns, not just the examples: scammers constantly invent new stories, but the underlying tricks barely change.

> **Educational note:** This lesson is about protecting yourself. It contains no financial advice.

## Why Crypto Scams Work

Three features of crypto make it scammer-friendly:

1. **Irreversibility:** Once confirmed, a transaction cannot be undone by anyone.
2. **Pseudonymity:** Wallet addresses do not show real names, so scammers operate with little fear of identification.
3. **Hype and complexity:** Beginners who do not fully understand what they are doing are easier to rush, confuse, and pressure.

Add the promise of fast money, and you have a perfect hunting ground. In India, crypto-investment frauds reported through WhatsApp and Telegram groups have become depressingly common — often targeting people who are new to both investing and technology.

## The Major Scam Patterns

### 1. Phishing

Fake websites, emails, or messages that imitate real exchanges or wallets to steal your login credentials or seed phrase. The site looks identical to the real one; the URL is one letter off. **No legitimate service will ever ask for your seed phrase** — anyone who does is a scammer, full stop.

### 2. Impersonation and giveaway scams

"You have won 2 BTC! Send 0.1 BTC to verify your wallet and receive your prize." Fake celebrity accounts, fake exchange support staff, fake "customer care" numbers on Google — all variations of the same trick: **authority plus urgency plus a small upfront payment.** Real giveaways never ask you to send money first.

### 3. Pig butchering

A scammer befriends you over weeks or months — often through a wrong-number text or dating app — then introduces a "profitable investment opportunity," usually a fake trading platform showing fake gains. You deposit more and more ("fattening the pig") until you try to withdraw and discover the platform was fiction. This is now one of the costliest scam types worldwide.

### 4. Ponzi schemes and rug pulls

**Ponzis** pay early investors with later investors' money, collapsing when new deposits slow. **Rug pulls** involve a seemingly legitimate token or project whose creators suddenly drain all funds and vanish. Warning signs for both: guaranteed returns, referral-heavy marketing, anonymous teams, and pressure to recruit others.

### 5. Fake apps and fake exchanges

Malicious apps that mimic real wallets or exchanges, distributed through ads, third-party sites, or even official app stores briefly. They steal whatever you deposit or the keys you enter. Always download wallet and exchange apps from official links, and verify the developer name.

### 6. Pump-and-dump groups

Telegram or Discord groups promising coordinated buying to "pump" a coin. In reality, the organisers bought early and sell into the buying frenzy they created. Members buying late are the exit liquidity.

## Red Flags: The Universal Checklist

Nearly every scam waves several of these flags. If you spot two or more, walk away:

- **Guaranteed or unusually high returns** ("earn 5% daily," "double your crypto")
- **Urgency and pressure** ("offer ends in 2 hours," "only 3 spots left")
- **Unsolicited contact** — DMs about investments from strangers are scams until proven otherwise
- **Requests for your seed phrase, private keys, or screen-sharing** while you log in
- **Upfront payments** to unlock winnings, withdrawals, or "verification"
- **Too-good-to-be-true testimonials** and rented-luxury marketing
- **No verifiable identity** — anonymous teams with no track record asking for your money

## If You Have Been Scammed

Act fast, even though recovery is unlikely:

1. **Stop all contact** with the scammer immediately. Do not pay "recovery fees" — recovery scammers prey on victims twice.
2. **Move remaining funds** to a brand-new wallet (new seed phrase) in case your current one is compromised.
3. **Document everything:** transaction IDs, wallet addresses, screenshots, chat logs.
4. **Report it:** in India, file a complaint at the cybercrime portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the helpline 1930. Also report to the exchange involved, if any.
5. **Warn others** — share the pattern (not your private details) so fewer people fall for it.

## Common Mistakes

- **Trusting DMs.** Real exchanges and projects do not pitch investments through direct messages. Ever.
- **Searching for "customer care numbers" on Google.** Scammers buy ads for fake support numbers. Use only the official site's contact channels.
- **Entering your seed phrase anywhere except your own wallet device.** No website, form, or "support agent" legitimately needs it.
- **Believing screenshots of profits.** Fake platforms show whatever numbers keep you depositing. Only a real withdrawal proves anything.
- **Thinking "it won't happen to me."** Pig-butchering victims include doctors, engineers, and finance professionals. Intelligence is no shield; only scepticism is.

## Recap Checklist

- Crypto's **irreversibility** makes scams permanent — prevention is everything.
- Learn the patterns: **phishing, impersonation, pig butchering, Ponzi/rug pulls, fake apps, pump-and-dumps.**
- **Two or more red flags = walk away.** Guaranteed returns, urgency, unsolicited contact, seed-phrase requests.
- If scammed: **cut contact, secure remaining funds, document, report** (cybercrime.gov.in / 1930 in India).
- Never pay anyone who promises to **recover** stolen crypto — that is a second scam.
