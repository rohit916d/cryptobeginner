Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, has priced its initial public offering at $83 a share — above its marketed range of $70 to $78 — and enlarged the deal to 980 million shares, raising about $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record. The shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq today, Thursday 19 November, under the ticker ANT. For crypto readers in India, the Anthropic IPO matters for a specific reason: last week, as the offering's subscription books opened, Bitcoin fell to $68,000, and several market analysts said investors were selling liquid assets to free up cash for the deal. This guide explains what Anthropic actually priced, why the deal is so big, what the crypto connection really is, and what — if anything — Indian crypto users should take from a listing that has nothing to do with crypto itself.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a market event; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 18–19 November 2026 by Bloomberg, Reuters, CNBC TV18, The Hindu BusinessLine, FinanceFeeds, Gulf News and ZeroHedge, is:

- **The price is $83 a share.** Anthropic priced its IPO at $83.00 per share on Wednesday 18 November, $5 above the top of the $70–$78 range it had marketed the previous week. Bloomberg first reported the pricing, and Reuters confirmed it through a person familiar with the transaction. The price is about 6.4% above the top of the range and about 12.2% above the $74 midpoint.
- **The deal was upsized.** Instead of the 825 million shares in the original plan, Anthropic is selling 980 million Class A shares — 956,351,354 by the company itself and 23,648,646 by existing stockholders. Anthropic receives no proceeds from the shares sold by the selling stockholders.
- **The raise is a record $81.3 billion.** At $83, the 980 million shares are worth about $81.34 billion in gross public share sales, before any exercise of the underwriters' option. CNBC TV18 reported that the deal makes Anthropic the biggest IPO in the world, surpassing SpaceX, which debuted only a few months earlier.
- **Trading starts today.** The shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on Thursday 19 November under the symbol ANT. The offering is expected to close on 20 November, subject to customary closing conditions.
- **The underwriters have a greenshoe.** Anthropic has granted the underwriters a 30-day option to buy up to an additional 147 million shares. If that option is exercised in full, it would add about $12.2 billion, taking the total toward roughly $119.5 billion by one estimate.
- **A $26 billion private placement runs alongside it.** Seven technology companies — Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Microsoft, Micron and TSMC — have agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price in a separate private placement, according to Anthropic's filing and CNBC TV18.
- **Demand was about six times the shares on offer.** Bloomberg reported that demand during the offering reached as much as six times the available shares. About 20% of the shares were earmarked for individual investors, according to earlier reporting on the deal.
- **Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the deal.** The two banks are the lead underwriters for an offering with a 22-bank underwriting syndicate, according to The Hindu BusinessLine.

None of these figures is a prediction about what the shares will do when trading starts. The $83 price is the price at which the shares were sold in the offering. The price at which they first trade tonight is set by the market when trading opens, and it is not known yet.

## The Numbers Behind the Deal

The headline figure — $81.3 billion — is the largest IPO ever, but it is only part of the picture. Anthropic's valuation depends on how the company's many share classes, convertible securities and outstanding equity awards are counted, and different outlets have landed on different numbers from the same filing.

|---|---|---|
| $83 per share | IPO price, above the $70–$78 range | Bloomberg / Reuters |
| 980 million shares | Shares sold, upsized from 825 million | Anthropic announcement |
| $81.34 billion | Gross value of the public offering | FinanceFeeds / CNBC TV18 |
| ~$1.1 trillion | Market value on about 13.2 billion shares outstanding after the deal | ZeroHedge share bridge |
| ~$1.5 trillion | Fully diluted value including employee stock options and restricted shares | Gulf News |

| 147 million shares | Underwriters' 30-day option (greenshoe) | Anthropic announcement |

| $33.24 billion | Revenue, first nine months of 2026, vs $2.545 billion a year earlier | Anthropic SEC filing |
| $16.96 billion | Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 alone | Anthropic SEC filing |

The revenue figures are the reason the market is willing to pay this much. Anthropic reported revenue of $33.24 billion for the first nine months of 2026, compared with $2.545 billion in the same period of 2025 — roughly a thirteen-fold increase in a year — with $16.96 billion of it coming in the third quarter alone. That growth is extraordinary by any standard, and it is the core of the bull case for the listing.

But the filing also carries the other side of the story. The company is still loss-making under generally accepted accounting principles, and it carries substantial long-term computing obligations — the cost of the data centres and chips needed to train and run its models. Its preliminary prospectus devotes about 93 pages to risk factors, including a warning that advanced AI systems could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity," and CNBC TV18 noted two high-profile disputes with the Trump administration earlier this year. Lock-up agreements restrict sales by many existing stockholders for about 180 days, with longer restrictions for co-founders and certain major holders. A listing this large, at this valuation, is a bet on the growth continuing — and the filing is unusually open about what could go wrong.

## Why This Is the World's Biggest IPO

The Anthropic deal takes the record from SpaceX, which listed only a few months earlier — and the comparison is instructive, because SpaceX is the closest precedent for what happens when a much-hyped private company finally gets a public price.

SpaceX priced its IPO at $135 a share and opened at $150 on 12 June, hit a record $225.64 four days later, and was trading near $143 by mid-November, according to Seeking Alpha data cited by TheStreet. In other words, the first-day excitement, the early surge and the later drift back are all part of the same story — and none of them was knowable on the day the price was set. The $83 figure for Anthropic tells you what the company and its bankers agreed the shares were worth in the offering. It tells you nothing about what the market will pay at 7:00 PM IST tonight, when trading opens, or next week, or next month.

What makes Anthropic's deal bigger than SpaceX's is a combination of three things. First, the sheer size of the offering: 980 million shares at $83 is about $81.3 billion of stock changing hands in a single deal, before the greenshoe. Second, the upsizing itself: the deal grew by 19% from the original 825 million shares, which is a sign that the banks found more demand than they had planned for. Third, the private placement: the $26 billion from seven of the world's largest technology companies, buying at the IPO price, is an unusual show of support — and it also means that some of Anthropic's biggest customers and chip suppliers are now also its shareholders, a point ZeroHedge highlighted in its coverage.

Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives, one of Wall Street's most closely watched technology analysts, called the deal an "important step" for the AI buildout, noting that it marks "the first time a U.S. frontier lab carries a standalone public market price, uncluttered by an ad business or a rocket business." That is the point that matters for the wider market: until today, there was no public share price for a frontier AI lab. From tonight, there is one, and every other AI company, every chipmaker and every investor in the sector will be measured against it.

## Why Crypto Traders Are Watching an AI IPO

An AI company's listing has nothing to do with crypto on the surface. The reason crypto traders are watching it is the same reason they watched Nvidia's earnings last week and the Nasdaq sell-off the week before: money moves between markets, and a very large offering can pull cash out of the most liquid assets first.

The capital-rotation theory works like this. When a huge IPO opens its subscription books, investors who want a large allocation need cash. Some of that cash comes from selling assets they can sell quickly and in size — and Bitcoin, which trades around the clock and in deep markets, is one of the easiest assets in the world to turn into cash at short notice. Several market analysts said exactly that last week: Xapo Bank chief executive Seamus Rocca attributed Bitcoin's dip to $68,000 to subscription books pulling capital out of liquid markets, and a Bitfinex report dated 11 November noted that the overlap between crypto retail investors and Anthropic IPO buyers could intensify competition for limited capital. This site covered that theory in detail in its 14 November post on the Anthropic IPO and Bitcoin's $68,000 fall.

It is important to be honest about how strong that evidence is. The theory is plausible, and it fits the timing — Bitcoin fell as the IPO books opened — but timing alone does not prove causation. Bitcoin was already falling for other reasons in the same week: the Fed minutes released in the early hours of 19 November showed almost all officials expecting more rate hikes, December hike odds climbed toward 96%, the dollar hit a 19-month high, and Bitcoin slipped below $63,000 to about $62,800. The Nasdaq sell-off, the hot October inflation print and the Nvidia chief executive's resignation were all pulling in the same direction. When several forces push the same way, it is not possible to say how much of a move belongs to any one of them — and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

There is a second, more direct crypto link that appeared in the Indian press on the morning of the debut. ET Markets' live crypto page on 19 November noted that traders are engaging in speculative strategies ahead of Anthropic's expected stock-market debut, capitalising on price differences in derivatives related to the company's valuation. In plain language, some traders are already betting on what the listing will do to the prices of related assets — and those bets are being placed in crypto derivatives markets, where trading never stops and leverage is easy to obtain. That is a real, observable connection between the IPO and crypto markets, and it is different from the rotation theory: it is not about cash leaving crypto, but about traders using crypto markets to express a view on an AI listing.

A third connection is the private placement. Google, Nvidia and AMD are among the seven companies putting $26 billion into Anthropic at the IPO price — and Nvidia is the company whose earnings and whose former chief executive's resignation have driven crypto prices twice in the past week. The AI trade and the crypto trade have been moving together for months, through the same set of big technology names, and the Anthropic listing ties them together more tightly: the same companies that supply the chips, buy the models and now own the shares are the names crypto traders watch as a signal for risk appetite.

None of this means the IPO will move Bitcoin in any particular direction tonight. The debut is a test of the rotation theory in the other direction: if the theory is right, some of the cash that was pulled out of liquid markets should flow back once the offering closes and the shares start trading. But the Fed minutes, the dollar and the December rate decision are still in play, and Bitcoin was already near $62,800 before the debut. The honest statement is that the IPO is one of several forces acting on the price, and the size of its effect will only be visible in hindsight.

## What Happens When Trading Starts Tonight

The shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT later today, with the offering closing on 20 November. Here is what is known — and what is not — about the debut itself.

What is known: the IPO price is $83, the shares sold are 980 million, the greenshoe is 147 million more, and the $26 billion private placement is priced at $83 as well. The first trade will happen when the Nasdaq opens the stock, which in Indian time is in the evening. Some commentary has speculated that the price was set so that a customary first-day rise would push the stock above $100 — but that is speculation, not a fact, and the opening price is set by the market, not by the company.

What is not known: the opening price, the first-day close, or how the stock trades in its first week. The SpaceX precedent is a warning against assuming a smooth ride: a 10% first-day rise, a record high four days later, and a drift back to near the IPO price within five months. Anthropic's own filing, with its 93 pages of risk factors and its disclosure of continued losses, is a warning against assuming the growth rate of the past nine months will continue in a straight line.

For a beginner, the most useful way to think about a debut like this is to separate three questions that often get mixed up. The first is whether the company is a good business — Anthropic's revenue growth suggests it is growing fast, and its losses and computing obligations suggest it is expensive to run. The second is whether $83 is a fair price — that is a judgement for the market, and the market will give its answer tonight. The third is whether any of this should change what you do with your own money — and on that question, this guide is deliberately silent. It explains an event; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding anything, and it makes no prediction about Anthropic's shares or about Bitcoin.

## What This Means for Crypto Users in India

For Indian readers, the Anthropic IPO story has four practical layers: the price, the rules, the coverage, and the calendar.

The price is the most immediate. Bitcoin was trading near $62,800 after the Fed minutes, and ET Markets showed Bitcoin at ₹58,59,879 at 01:38 IST on 19 November, down 1.58% on the day, with Ethereum at ₹1,64,140, down 2.02%. At roughly $62,800, one Bitcoin is worth about ₹58.6 lakh at current exchange rates. The dollar's strength matters here in a way it does not for an American holder: when the dollar rises against the rupee, Bitcoin becomes more expensive in rupees even if its dollar price does not move. The Indian premium on top of the global price is a separate factor — the Crypto Times India Ethereum Premium Index stood at +10.88% at its 18 November fix — and it is a reminder that Indian prices carry their own markup on top of the global move.

The rules are unchanged, and it is worth saying so plainly because a story this big often creates the impression that something else has changed too. There is no Indian listing of Anthropic, the 30% tax on crypto gains and the 1% TDS on covered transfers are unchanged, and losses cannot be set off against other income. The IPO does not change the FIU-IND registration requirement for Indian exchanges, and it does not change the banking problems that have affected Indian users this month — Coinbase's INR withdrawals, Unocoin's suspension and ZebPay's delays are all separate stories, covered in earlier posts on this site. The coverage is worth noting because it shows how seriously Indian media is taking this listing. The Hindu BusinessLine carried the pricing on the morning of 19 November, CNBC TV18 reported the record $81.3 billion raise and the SpaceX comparison the same morning, and ET Markets' live crypto page linked the debut to derivatives trading in crypto markets. Indian readers are not getting this story second-hand — it is on the front pages of Indian business media, and it is being framed, rightly, as a market event with consequences for every risk asset, including crypto.

The calendar is the part a beginner can actually use. The debut is tonight, the offering closes on 20 November, and the next big scheduled events for crypto are the US jobs report on 4 December, the Fed's decision on 8–9 December and the next inflation print on 10 December. Each of those releases is a moment when prices can move sharply in minutes. That does not mean anyone should trade around them — the minutes after a data release are among the most volatile of the month, spreads widen, and stop-losses can be hit before the market settles. It means that if you see Bitcoin move several percent on a Thursday evening or a Friday afternoon in the next three weeks, the first place to look is the market calendar, not a rumour on social media.

One more India-specific point: the Anthropic IPO is not about Indian regulation at all, and it is not a crypto story in itself. Its link to crypto runs through the flow of money between markets, through the derivatives traders positioning ahead of the debut, and through the same big technology names — Nvidia above all — that have driven crypto prices all month. That link is real, but it is indirect, and it is the same indirect link that has driven most of the big crypto moves of the past month.
