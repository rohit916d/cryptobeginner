Bitcoin is heading for a sixth straight weekly loss, and the weekend took it to its lowest price in four months. Early on Sunday 15 November 2026, Bitcoin fell to $62,006 on Binance, its weakest level since 13 July, as nearly $2 billion in leveraged crypto positions was wiped out in 24 hours. The fall caps a week in which the world's largest cryptocurrency slid from a weekly open near $75,000 to about $63,000 — and it arrived three days after the US Federal Reserve published a report naming exactly the risks that drove the sell-off. This guide explains what happened, what a weekly loss streak actually measures, what the Fed's report said, why the fall was so fast, and what it means for readers in India — without predicting where any price goes next.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a market event; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 14–15 November 2026 by TokenPost, The Straits Times, CoinDesk, Crypto Briefing, EconoTimes, crypto.news and KuCoin, are:

- **A four-month low.** Bitcoin fell to $62,006 on Binance early on Sunday 15 November, its lowest since 13 July, according to TokenPost. The Straits Times put the low at $62,112, the weakest since 14 July. Bitcoin had already lost 5.7% in the US session on Saturday 14 November, then dropped further in Asian trading on Sunday. At 01:00 UTC on Sunday it traded around $62,900, and by 02:11 UTC it was at $63,696, down 6.59% in 24 hours, according to crypto.news. EconoTimes put the 24-hour fall at 8.19% to $62,713 as of 01:26 UTC.
- **A sixth straight weekly loss.** Bitcoin's weekly candle opened near $75,000 and is now around $63,000, according to Global Eye 24. It closed last Sunday at $74,740, which was itself the fifth consecutive lower weekly close since the $86,490 print on 5 October, according to crypto.news. This week's close will make it six in a row. Bitcoin is now about 49.5% below its record high of $126,080, set on 6 October 2025. Its low for 2026 remains $57,742, reached on 1 July.
- **The whole market fell with it.** Ether dropped 8% in the US session and fell to $1,711–$1,731 on Sunday, its lowest since 8–9 July, according to The Straits Times and TokenPost. Solana traded around $68.67–$69, down about 11% in 24 hours. XRP fell 9.38% to $0.9132, and Dogecoin lost 12.85% to $0.0562, according to EconoTimes. The total crypto market capitalisation fell to about $2.3 trillion, down about 6% in a day, with more than $500 billion wiped out in ten days, according to Global Eye 24.
- **Nearly $2 billion in liquidations.** About $1.96 billion in crypto positions was forcibly closed in 24 hours, according to TokenPost, and about $2 billion according to Coinglass data cited by The Straits Times. Roughly 319,000 traders had positions closed. Bitcoin long positions accounted for about $645 million in liquidations, against about $62 million for shorts. Bitcoin accounted for about $774 million of the total and Ether about $488 million. The largest single liquidation was a $16.17 million ETHUSDT long on Binance. On Friday 13 November alone, the slide had already triggered about $1.3 billion in liquidations of long positions.
- **Extreme fear.** The Crypto Fear and Greed Index printed 10 on Sunday, in its "extreme fear" band, according to crypto.news. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $185.45 million in outflows on Friday, a seventh straight session of outflows and about $1.91 billion since 5 November, according to SoSoValue data.
- **A warning published three days earlier.** The Federal Reserve's November Financial Stability Report, published on Friday 13 November, ranked a correction in risk assets as the second-most-cited risk among the market contacts it surveyed — and the weekend delivered one.
- **A Sunday rebound.** After the low, the market bounced: Bitcoin recovered to about $64,820, up 4.54% from its low of $62,006, while Ether returned above $1,800 at $1,814.56, up 6.05%, and Solana rebounded to $71.15, according to a KuCoin flash report at 03:14 UTC citing Huo Xing Finance. All three were still down over 24 hours — Bitcoin 4.25%, Ether 5.15% and Solana 6.69%.

## What a Weekly Loss Streak Actually Measures

For a beginner, the most confusing thing about a "sixth straight weekly loss" is what is being counted. Crypto trades around the clock, so there is no opening bell or closing bell. Traders still use a weekly close — the price at the end of Sunday trading — as the yardstick for a week, because it is the one fixed point in a market that never sleeps. A weekly loss means this Sunday's close is lower than last Sunday's. Six in a row means the close has fallen every week for a month and a half.

The arithmetic of this streak is straightforward. Bitcoin printed $86,490 on 5 October 2026. It then closed lower for five consecutive weeks, ending last Sunday at $74,740. This week it opened near $75,000 and fell to about $63,000 — a weekly drop of roughly $12,000, or about 16%. That is the biggest weekly fall of the streak, and it is what turned a grinding decline into a four-month low.

What a streak does not do is predict anything. Six lower closes in a row tells you the direction and the persistence of the move, not where it stops. Onchain analyst James Check described the move as Bitcoin's fourth capitulation-style sell-off since its record high, according to TokenPost — but he also cautioned that this is not a confirmed bottom. A streak can end with a bounce, a sideways drift, or a further fall, and the weekly close itself says nothing about which one comes next. The streak is a description of the past six weeks, not a forecast of the seventh.

It is also worth keeping the scale in view. A 49.5% fall from the October 2025 record sounds dramatic, and it is — but Bitcoin has fallen by half or more from a record before, and the 2026 low of $57,742 on 1 July is still below where the price stands now. The streak matters because it shows selling pressure that has not let up for six weeks, not because six is a magic number.

## The Fed Report That Named the Risks Three Days Early

The most striking document of the week was not a price chart. It was the Federal Reserve's November 2026 Financial Stability Report, published on Friday 13 November, three days before Bitcoin's weekend low.

The report is the Fed's twice-yearly assessment of vulnerabilities in the US financial system, and its data runs through 28 October. Its most-watched section is a survey of salient risks, run by New York Fed staff among 23 market contacts — people at banks, broker-dealers, investment funds and advisory firms — in September and October. The results, reported on 14 November by blockchainreporter, madrobot, ranzware, CoinStats and aistockwire, were:

- **Artificial intelligence, cited by 87% of contacts**, up from 50% in the spring survey — the top-cited risk, ahead of everything else. Respondents named three separate worries: elevated equity valuations and the chance that weaker-than-expected earnings lead to a market correction; cybersecurity risk, from attacks by sophisticated state actors or damage caused by frontier AI models themselves; and risks from complex forms of AI-related financing.
- **A correction in risk assets, cited by 52%**, up from 35% in the spring — the second-most-cited risk. The report says respondents "widely cited the risk of a sharp correction in AI-related equities, driven by uncertain returns on large capital expenditures."
- **Higher long-term rates, cited by 48%**, up from 20% in the spring.
- **Cyberattacks, cited by 43%**, up from 20%.
- **Fiscal debt sustainability, cited by 43%**, up from 30%.

Two other findings in the report help explain why the sell-off was so broad. First, leverage at hedge funds reached its highest level since the data began in 2013, and it was concentrated in a small number of large funds — the kind of leverage that turns a fall into a cascade. Second, debt issued to pay for advanced computing passed $680 billion by October 2026, about five times the $130 billion issued in all of 2023, with outstanding public debt tied to advanced computing above $1.1 trillion. The AI build-out that powered the stock market's rise is itself heavily financed with debt.

The report also had a short passage on stablecoins, in its funding-risks chapter, under a heading that reads "Stablecoin assets plateaued". It says the market capitalisation of stablecoins "remained relatively flat at around $310 billion over the past year amid lower crypto-asset prices." The GENIUS Act, the US law that creates a federal framework for payment stablecoins, becomes effective on 18 January 2027 — 66 days after the report's publication. The report's Table 4.1 puts total runnable money-like liabilities at $28,157 billion, up 11.8% year over year.

Three caveats matter. First, the report mentions Bitcoin nowhere, and the survey does not mention digital assets at all — crypto sits inside the "risk asset correction" category without being named in it. Second, the Fed stresses that the survey sums up what respondents told it, not the view of the Fed's board or the New York Fed. Third, the report is a stability assessment, not a market call: it describes vulnerabilities, it does not predict that they will break, or when. The weekend's fall is consistent with the risks the survey named — a risk-asset correction, higher long-term rates, stretched valuations — but the report did not cause it, and it did not predict its timing.

## Why the Fall Was So Fast: Liquidations and Thin Weekend Liquidity

The speed of the fall has a mechanical explanation, and it is the same one this site explained after the October liquidation cascade: leverage. Many traders borrow money to bet on prices rising — these are "long" positions. When the price falls far enough, the exchange forcibly closes those positions to stop the losses growing. That forced selling pushes the price down further, which triggers more forced selling. The result is a cascade.

The weekend's numbers show the cascade clearly. About $1.96–2.0 billion in positions was liquidated in 24 hours, and the overwhelming majority were longs: Bitcoin longs accounted for about $645 million against about $62 million for shorts, according to TokenPost. In other words, the market was positioned for prices to rise, and the fall caught those bets on the wrong side. The largest single liquidation — a $16.17 million Ether long on Binance — shows how large individual leveraged bets had become.

Weekends make cascades worse, for a simple reason: fewer people are trading. Banks are closed, many professional desks run with smaller staff, and the order books — the lists of buy and sell orders waiting to be filled — are thinner. A large sell order that would be absorbed easily on a Tuesday can push the price much further on a Saturday night. That is why the sharpest moves of this sell-off have repeatedly come on weekends, and why the low of $62,006 came in the early hours of Sunday in India.

Technical levels added to the pressure. Bitcoin had already broken below its 200-day moving average of about $73,000 earlier in the week, according to The Straits Times — a level many traders watch as a dividing line between an uptrend and a downtrend. It then dropped below $69,000 on Thursday, according to TokenPost, and below $65,000 on Saturday, each break triggering more forced selling as positions placed just below those levels were hit. None of these levels has any power on its own; what matters is that many traders had placed orders around them, so breaking them released a wave of selling at once.

## The Sunday Rebound — and Why It Does Not Erase the Week

By Sunday morning, the market had bounced. Bitcoin recovered to about $64,820, up 4.54% from its low of $62,006, while Ether returned above $1,800 and Solana rebounded to $71.15, according to the KuCoin flash report at 03:14 UTC. The bounce was real, and it was fast — the kind of move that follows a liquidation cascade, when forced selling stops and buyers step in at prices that look low compared with a few hours earlier.

But the bounce does not erase the week, and it does not change what the week measured. All three assets were still down over 24 hours at the time of the report — Bitcoin 4.25%, Ether 5.15% and Solana 6.69% — and the weekly loss streak stands regardless of where Sunday's close lands, because the close would have to recover to above $74,740 to avoid a sixth straight weekly loss. A bounce after a cascade is a normal feature of volatile markets, not evidence that the selling is over. It is also not evidence that it will resume. The rebound is one more data point in a fast-moving weekend, and it should be read as such.

## What This Means for India

For Indian readers, the weekend's move lands in rupees, in tax rules that do not change with the market, and in a banking system that is already strained.

The rupee arithmetic is straightforward. Bitcoin is priced in dollars globally, and Indians buy it with rupees. At $62,000 and roughly ₹94 to the dollar, one Bitcoin was worth about ₹58 lakh at the low — down from about ₹71–75 lakh when Bitcoin was near $75,000 in early October. The fall in the dollar price has been much bigger than any movement in the rupee, so Indian holders have seen the rupee value of their holdings fall sharply in a week.

The effective cost for an Indian buyer is higher than the headline price, because of the premium Indians pay for crypto. TokenTimes' live data on 15 November put one Bitcoin at about ₹65.5 lakh at peer-to-peer USDT rates — the rate Indian buyers typically trade at — against about ₹58.98 lakh at the market exchange rate, with the rupee trading about 11% above the official rate on the peer-to-peer market. The premium exists because of capital controls, the 1% TDS that drove professional traders offshore, and limited local liquidity, and it tends to widen when markets are stressed. A weekend like this one, with global liquidity tightening, is exactly when the premium grows.

Three India-specific points follow:

- **The tax does not change with the market.** Gains on virtual digital assets are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS on transfers, and those rates apply whether the market is rising or falling. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. A fall does not create a tax refund, and losses on crypto cannot generally be set off against other income. The market's direction changes the value of a holding; it does not change the Indian tax treatment.
- **The banking link is already under strain.** The same weekend brought a reminder of how fragile the rupee on-ramp is: Coinbase's INR withdrawals remain halted after an outage at its banking partner, Unocoin has suspended all deposits and withdrawals since 9 November, and ZebPay's INR withdrawals are delayed. ZebPay says crypto withdrawals are temporarily paused and it is targeting Tuesday 17 November for resumption, with BSC and TRX withdrawals possibly taking an extra day. There is currently no confirmed evidence that the three exchanges share the same banking institution or that a common cyberattack is responsible. For a reader trying to move rupees during a volatile weekend, the practical point is that the banking route is slow or unavailable at exactly the moment it is most wanted.
- **The RBI is watching the same data, from the other side.** In June, RBI deputy governors said banks and financial institutions should stay away from crypto-related businesses, and the same week brought fresh evidence of how fragile the banking link is. A Fed that is expected to raise rates in December, a dollar at a 19-month high, and a risk-asset correction do not make the RBI more likely to open the banking door to crypto. Indian readers should not expect the Fed's December decision to change anything about how easily rupees move in and out of exchanges.

## What This Does Not Mean

Four things are worth stating plainly, because a four-month low and a six-week streak are easy to over-read:

- **This is not a prediction that Bitcoin will keep falling.** Six lower weekly closes describe the past, not the future. The same reports that named the fall also recorded the Sunday rebound to about $64,820, and analysts are split on how much of the move was leverage, how much was the Fed's rate expectations, and how much was technology-stock weakness. A market can fall on rate fears and then recover on the same fears if the actual decision turns out milder than expected.
- **This is not a confirmed bottom either.** James Check called the move a capitulation-style sell-off, but cautioned that it is not a confirmed bottom. A bounce after forced selling is normal, and it is not evidence that the selling is finished.
- **The Fed's report is not a market call.** The Financial Stability Report describes vulnerabilities and surveys what market contacts worry about. It does not predict a crash, it does not name a date, and it mentions Bitcoin nowhere. Reading it as a forecast would be a mistake.
- **The streak is not a verdict on crypto as a whole.** A six-week fall in price is a market event. It does not change how Bitcoin works, how the network is secured, or how Indian tax law treats it. Price and technology are different things, and this post is about the price.
