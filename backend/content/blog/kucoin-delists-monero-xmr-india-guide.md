## KuCoin to Delist Monero (XMR) on 30 November: India Guide

KuCoin will delist Monero (XMR) on 30 November 2026, ending all four of its Monero spot trading pairs and winding down every related service — savings, conversions, trading bots and VIP loan collateral — within days. The announcement, made on 26 November, makes KuCoin the second major exchange this month to drop the privacy coin, after MEXC removed Monero spot trading on 13 November. Monero fell sharply on the news, dropping by roughly 10 to 17 percent depending on the measure, and Indian holders who keep XMR on KuCoin now face a hard deadline: trading ends on 30 November, and withdrawals close on 31 December.

This guide explains what KuCoin announced, the deadlines in order, why exchanges delist privacy coins, what the move means for Indian users, and what holders should do before the windows close. It is an explainer, not investment advice — and it makes no prediction about where Monero's price goes next.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported exchange delisting; it does not recommend buying, selling, holding or investing in any asset.

## What KuCoin Announced

KuCoin published its delisting notice on Thursday, 26 November 2026. In the exchange's own words, quoted by several outlets that covered the notice: "In accordance with the platform policies, KuCoin will delist Monero (XMR) at 8:00 on November 30, 2026 (UTC)."

Four spot trading pairs are affected, and all four end at the same minute:

| Pair | Ends |
|---|---|
| XMR/USDT | 30 Nov 2026, 08:00 UTC |
| XMR/USDC | 30 Nov 2026, 08:00 UTC |
| XMR/ETH | 30 Nov 2026, 08:00 UTC |
| XMR/BTC | 30 Nov 2026, 08:00 UTC |

KuCoin told users to cancel or update their spot orders before that time. Any open orders left standing will be automatically removed by the exchange when trading stops.

The delisting goes well beyond spot trading. KuCoin is removing Monero from its other products on a staggered schedule, and the details matter because each service has its own cut-off:

- **Deposits:** already suspended, and KuCoin says the deposit service "will remain closed and will not be resumed". There is no way to send new XMR into a KuCoin account.
- **XMR Flexible Savings (KuCoin Earn):** ends on 27 November at 12:00 UTC. Principal and accrued earnings move back to users' Funding Accounts.
- **KuCoin Convert:** stops supporting Monero on 30 November at 03:00 UTC, and outstanding convert limit orders are cancelled.
- **Trading Bots:** stop supporting XMR on 30 November at 06:00 UTC. That covers Spot Grid, AI Spot Trend, Spot Martingale, Infinity Grid, DCA, Spot Grid AI Plus and Smart Rebalance — any bot left running is shut down by the system rather than by its owner.
- **VIP Loans:** from 30 November, Monero no longer qualifies as collateral for KuCoin's Classic and UTA loan products. The exchange warned borrowers to "add other supported cryptocurrencies as collateral as soon as possible to avoid an excessively high LTV ratio, which may trigger liquidation".
- **Withdrawals:** scheduled to remain open until 31 December 2026 at 08:00 UTC. KuCoin added a caveat: it may close withdrawals earlier if problems affecting the Monero network arise.

KuCoin also warned that a delisted token left in an account may be converted into stablecoins on the user's behalf — but that such a conversion "is not guaranteed, and market conditions may cause the value of a delisted token to fall sharply or become worthless". In plain language: leaving XMR on KuCoin after the deadlines is not a safe storage plan.

## What Happened to the Price

Monero fell sharply within hours of the announcement. The size of the drop varies a little between sources, because each measured it at a different moment:

- NewsBTC, via TradingView, reported that Monero lost about 15% in less than five hours on Thursday, falling from $525.36 to $446.54.
- CryptoBriefing reported that Monero plunged as much as 17% after the announcement.
- CoinStats reported a 10.5% fall to $449.98 as of 19:00 UTC on Thursday, and noted that the drop extended a broader pullback from roughly $625 on 3 November.
- CryptoCompass reported Monero at $455.45 at 00:10 UTC on 27 November, down 10.9% in 24 hours.

Those numbers describe what happened, not what will happen. A delisting announcement can move a coin's price because it removes a venue where the coin can be bought and sold, and because holders rush to exit at the same time — but the price after 30 November will depend on many factors no one can predict, including trading on the platforms where Monero remains listed. This guide does not forecast it.

## Why Exchanges Delist Privacy Coins

KuCoin has not given a specific reason for removing Monero, citing only its platform policies and what it calls its Special Treatment Rules. That silence is itself informative: exchanges rarely explain delistings in detail. But the pattern around Monero is well established, and it is worth understanding.

Monero is a privacy coin. Unlike Bitcoin, where every transaction is visible on a public ledger and wallet addresses can be traced, Monero hides the sender, the receiver and the amount of each transaction by default, using a combination of ring signatures, stealth addresses and confidential transactions. For a user who values financial privacy, that is the point of the coin. For an exchange, it is a compliance problem.

Exchanges in most major markets must follow anti-money-laundering rules. The most important of these for crypto is the FATF travel rule, which requires exchanges to collect and share information about the sender and receiver of a transfer. A coin that hides both ends of a transaction by design makes that rule very hard to follow. The result is a slow, steady squeeze: exchanges either build expensive workarounds, restrict the coin, or drop it.

KuCoin is not the first to make that choice this month. MEXC stopped Monero spot trading and removed its perpetual futures contracts earlier in November, on 13 November 2026. Other large exchanges dropped Monero in earlier years, and regulators in the European Union have moved to bar regulated crypto firms from handling privacy coins at all. The direction of travel is consistent, even if no single regulator ordered KuCoin's decision.

There is an important contrast inside the privacy-coin family itself. Cointelegraph's coverage of the KuCoin delisting was headlined around a divergence: while Monero was being dropped, Grayscale was doubling down on Zcash, a different privacy coin. Zcash offers privacy as an option rather than a default — its transparent transactions work like Bitcoin's, and its shielded transactions are private. That design difference is one reason institutional products have been more comfortable with Zcash than with Monero. For beginners, the lesson is simple: "privacy coin" is not one thing. How a coin implements privacy decides how exchanges and regulators treat it.

## KuCoin and India: What Changes for Indian Users

Monero has never been a staple listing on India's large domestic exchanges, so most Indian users who hold XMR hold it on offshore platforms such as KuCoin. That is exactly why this delisting matters in India, even though KuCoin's announcement says nothing about India specifically.

KuCoin is one of the offshore exchanges that registered with India's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND) in 2024, bringing it inside the Indian compliance net rather than outside it. Registration does not protect a coin from delisting — the decision is KuCoin's, made under its own platform policies — but it does mean Indian users on KuCoin are dealing with a platform that reports to Indian authorities, and their activity there is not invisible to the Indian tax system.

Three practical points follow for Indian users:

**Your other coins are unaffected.** The delisting covers Monero only. Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT and every other asset on KuCoin continue to trade normally. If you hold no XMR, nothing in this announcement requires any action from you.

**Selling has tax consequences in India.** If you choose to sell your XMR rather than withdraw it, the proceeds are crypto income. India taxes crypto gains at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and 4% cess) under Section 115BBH, with 1% TDS under Section 194S on transfers above the specified thresholds, and losses cannot be set off against other income. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Withdrawing to a wallet you control is the cleanest option.** A self-custody wallet — one where you hold your own keys — does not depend on any exchange's listing decisions. Moving XMR off KuCoin before 31 December keeps the coins under your control regardless of what any exchange does next. If you are new to self-custody, read the guide linked in Keep Learning before moving funds, and test with a small amount first.

One more India-specific caution: Monero's privacy features make it a frequent subject of scam pitches in India, where fraudsters promise "untraceable" returns or ask for payment in XMR because it cannot be traced back. A delisting does not make those pitches legitimate — it makes them more suspicious, because the coin is becoming harder, not easier, to convert back into rupees. Anybody asking you to pay in Monero, or promising returns in Monero, should be treated as a red flag and reported through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the helpline 1930.

## What Indian XMR Holders Should Do Before the Deadlines

If you hold Monero on KuCoin, work through this checklist in order. The deadlines are close together, and the cost of missing one is real.

**1. Cancel open orders now.** Any XMR spot orders you leave open will be removed automatically on 30 November at 08:00 UTC. Cancelling them yourself avoids surprises and lets you decide the terms of any sale, rather than having the exchange decide for you.

**2. Stop trading bots that use XMR.** Spot Grid, DCA, Martingale and the other bot products stop supporting Monero at 06:00 UTC on 30 November. A bot left running will be shut down by the system. Check your active bots and remove XMR from them before that time.

**3. Fix VIP Loan collateral before 30 November.** If Monero is backing a KuCoin VIP Loan, it stops counting as collateral on 30 November. Add other supported crypto as collateral first, or the loan-to-value ratio can spike and trigger a forced liquidation. This is the deadline most likely to catch people out, because it is easy to forget a loan is collateralised with a coin you rarely think about.

**4. Withdraw your XMR before 31 December.** This is the hard deadline. Withdrawals are scheduled to close at 08:00 UTC on 31 December 2026, and KuCoin says they may close earlier if the network has problems. Withdraw to a wallet where you control the keys, double-check the address, and remember that Monero withdrawals need the correct wallet address and, where applicable, the right payment ID or integrated address format for the wallet you use. Do not leave XMR sitting on KuCoin hoping for an extension — the exchange has said deposits will never reopen, and it has warned that leftover tokens may be converted to stablecoins with no guarantee, at whatever value the market gives them.

**5. Keep records for tax.** Whether you sell or withdraw, keep a record of the transaction: dates, amounts, the price in rupees at the time, and any fees. Indian tax law requires you to report crypto income, and a clean record is the difference between a straightforward filing and a painful one. If you sell, the 30% rate and 1% TDS described above apply, with the September 2026 qualifier.

**6. Do not panic-buy or panic-sell.** A delisting is a deadline, not a verdict on the coin. Monero continues to trade on other platforms, and its price after 30 November is unknowable. The right move is the boring one: move your coins somewhere safe, or sell them deliberately if that is your decision, and do it before the deadline — not in a rush on the last day.

## What This Delisting Does Not Mean

Three misunderstandings are already circulating, and each is worth correcting.

**It is not a ban on Monero.** KuCoin is one exchange. Monero remains listed on other platforms, and the coin's network continues to run exactly as before. A delisting removes a venue; it does not switch off a blockchain.

**It is not proof of wrongdoing.** KuCoin cited its platform policies, not any allegation against Monero or its developers. Exchanges delist coins for commercial and compliance reasons all the time, and a delisting says nothing about whether a coin's technology works.

**It is not a signal about the price.** The drop after the announcement is a fact, reported above. What happens next is not predictable from it. Anyone telling you that Monero will certainly fall further, or that it will bounce back, is guessing — and this guide does not make that guess.

## The Bigger Picture: Privacy Coins Under Pressure in 2026

The KuCoin delisting is the second Monero delisting by a major exchange in a single month, and it sits inside a much larger pattern. The MEXC removal on 13 November, the EU's moves against anonymous coins, the US Treasury's FinCEN order cutting off MEXC over alleged illicit flows, and the EU's new Crypto Sanctions Task Force all point in the same direction: the space for fully anonymous crypto on regulated platforms is shrinking.

That pressure does not fall evenly. Coins that offer privacy as an option, with transparent transactions available, are finding institutional acceptance that fully private-by-default coins are not. Zcash is the clearest example — the same week KuCoin dropped Monero, coverage highlighted growing institutional interest in Zcash. The distinction is technical, but its consequences are practical: it decides which coins exchanges will list, which coins funds will hold, and which coins ordinary users can buy without friction.

For Indian users, the pattern has a second layer. India's own enforcement agencies have spent November 2026 treating crypto as a routine channel in money-laundering investigations — from the ED's FEMA searches at CoinDCX and Delta Exchange to the attachment of a hotel in Karaikal allegedly bought with crypto and hawala money. None of those cases involved Monero specifically. But they show the direction of Indian policy too: the authorities are watching how crypto moves across borders, and the coins that are hardest to trace attract the most attention. A privacy coin becoming harder to trade on large exchanges is consistent with that direction, not a break from it.

None of this means privacy coins disappear. Monero has survived delistings before, and it has a committed user base that values its privacy guarantees. But it does mean that holding a privacy coin now requires more care than holding a mainstream one: fewer venues, tighter deadlines, and a need to keep your own keys rather than relying on an exchange to hold the coins for you.

## What Happens Next

The next date to watch is 30 November itself: whether the delisting goes ahead on schedule, and whether any other exchange follows KuCoin and MEXC in the same month. A third delisting would confirm that this is an industry-wide shift rather than two isolated decisions.

The second date is 31 December, when KuCoin's withdrawal window closes. After that, any Monero left on the exchange is at the mercy of KuCoin's conversion policy — not guaranteed, and at whatever value the market gives it. If you hold XMR on KuCoin, that is the date that matters most.

The longer question is whether the gap between Monero and coins like Zcash keeps widening — whether optional privacy becomes the accepted standard while private-by-default coins are pushed further to the edges of the regulated market. That question will not be answered this week, but the KuCoin delisting is one more data point in that direction, and Indian users who hold privacy coins should plan as though the squeeze will continue: keep your own keys, keep your records clean, and never assume an exchange will keep listing a coin indefinitely.
