On 13 November 2026, Bitcoin fell to $68,000 — its lowest price since August — on a day when the market was already reeling from Nvidia's chief executive resigning, the dollar at a 19-month high, and a sixth straight week of declines. Amid all of that, one explanation kept coming up in the day's market coverage: Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, is about to launch what could become the largest public listing in history, and investors may be selling liquid assets like Bitcoin to free up cash for it. This guide explains what the Anthropic IPO is, how the capital-rotation theory works, what happened the last time a blockbuster listing pulled money out of crypto, and what Indian users should — and should not — take from the story.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. This guide explains a market event and a theory about why it happened; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset, and it does not predict where prices will go.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 13 November 2026 by CoinDesk, TradingView, CoinCourier, Finance Biggo and other outlets, are:

- **Bitcoin fell to $68,000 overnight on 13 November.** CoinDesk reported that Bitcoin sank to $68,000, its lowest since August, extending a slide of more than 20% since early October. TradingView's coverage put the intraday low at $68,527 on Binance's USDT market, with Bitcoin trading around $68,920, down about 6.7% on the day. CoinCourier, citing Binance data, recorded a low of $67,809.87 against USDT at 05:56 UTC, down 7.88% over 24 hours. The exact low differs by venue and by the time it was measured — that is normal for a market that trades around the clock on hundreds of exchanges.
- **It was the sixth straight weekly decline.** Bitcoin was on course for its sixth consecutive weekly loss, matching the losing streak in the first quarter of 2026, when prices bottomed near $60,000. The all-time record is nine consecutive weekly losses, set in 2022. For the year so far, Bitcoin was down 11% in November, 17% in the fourth quarter and 21% in 2026, according to CoinDesk's 13 November reporting.
- **Anthropic's IPO is expected around 19 November.** The AI company aims to raise up to $64.4 billion through a Nasdaq listing, offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, alongside a $26 billion private placement. At the top of the range, the offering would value Anthropic at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. The shares are expected to trade under the ticker ANT. Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on 10 November.
- **Demand is reported to be about six times the shares available.** Bloomberg reported that orders for the offering were roughly six times the shares on offer, according to CoinDesk's coverage. That figure is a media report, not a number confirmed in Anthropic's own announcement or prospectus — a distinction worth keeping in mind, and one CoinCourier made explicitly in its own reporting.
- **About 20% of the offering is earmarked for retail investors.** Roughly $12.9 billion at the top of the price range is reserved for individual investors — the same kind of small investor who, according to a Bitfinex report dated 11 November, overlaps heavily with crypto's retail base.
- **Indian search interest in Bitcoin spiked overnight.** Google Trends data for India shows "bitcoin" interest jumping to the maximum value of 100 at 12:58 AM IST on 14 November, and "crypto" peaking at 100 at 1:22 AM, after running near zero for most of 13 November. "Anthropic" itself showed no spike in India — it stayed flat at zero through the same window. In other words, Indian users were searching hard for what was happening to Bitcoin; the IPO is the freshest explanation circulating in market coverage, not a term Indians were searching for directly.

## What Is the Anthropic IPO?

Anthropic is the AI company behind Claude, one of the leading AI chatbots, and its listing is big by any measure. The public offering alone — up to $64.4 billion — would be one of the largest in stock-market history, and the $26 billion private placement running alongside it brings the total financing to roughly $90 billion. Nvidia has committed around $10 billion to the private placement, according to CoinDesk's reporting.

The company's own numbers explain why the market is paying attention. Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate reached $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion in a single month, according to the same reporting. The offering is priced at $70 to $78 per share for 825 million shares, with pricing expected in the middle of the week of 17 November and first trades expected around 19 November. About 20% of the shares on offer — roughly $12.9 billion at the top of the range — are reserved for individual investors rather than institutions.

That retail slice is the detail that connects this story to crypto. Big IPOs are usually an institutional affair, but this one reserves an unusually large share for ordinary investors. A Bitfinex report dated 11 November, cited in the day's coverage, noted that the overlap between crypto retail investors and Anthropic IPO buyers could intensify the competition for limited capital — the same pool of money, in other words, chasing two different things at once.

## How an IPO Can Pull Money Out of Crypto — the Rotation Theory

The theory linking the IPO to Bitcoin's fall is simple, and it has a name in market commentary: capital rotation. When a very large, very popular offering opens its subscription books, investors who want an allocation need cash. Some of that cash comes from money sitting idle. Some of it comes from selling things that are easy to sell quickly — liquid assets. Bitcoin is one of the most liquid assets in the world: it trades 24 hours a day, seven days a week, in any size, with no market close and no settlement delay. That makes it a convenient source of cash when an investor needs to free up funds in a hurry.

The argument, as put by Seamus Rocca, chief executive of Xapo Bank, and quoted by CoinDesk on 13 November, is that "the latest dip can be attributed to the opening of Anthropic's subscription books — which received six times more orders than shares available — pulling capital out of liquid markets to chase what is set to become the largest public listing in history, by a wide margin." Rocca compared the rush for Anthropic shares to the SpaceX IPO earlier this year, which he said "temporarily drained liquidity from bitcoin as investors moved money into the offering." Benjamin Cowen, founder of Into The Cryptoverse, offered a softer version of the same view, saying the offering "may be one factor" behind the recent drop.

It is important to be precise about what this theory is and is not. It is an interpretation offered by market participants, not a measurement. Nobody can look at a Bitcoin sale and know the seller's reason for it — the blockchain records the transaction, not the motive. The theory is plausible because the timing lines up: the subscription books opened, demand was reported at six times supply, and Bitcoin fell sharply in the same window. But timing is not proof, and the same day's coverage lists several other forces pulling in the same direction: rising short positions, outflows from BlackRock's IBIT, concerns over Strategy's place in MSCI indexes, the Nvidia turmoil, and expectations of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December. Any one of those could have moved the price on its own. The honest statement is that the IPO is one candidate explanation among several, and the market's own reporting says so.

## The SpaceX Precedent — What Happened Last Time

The reason the Anthropic theory gained traction so quickly is that market watchers have seen something like this before, earlier this year, with SpaceX. When SpaceX prepared to go public, Bitcoin fell sharply in the run-up: it dropped about 20% in the 30 days before SpaceX began trading on 12 June, sliding from around $79,300 on 13 May to about $63,580 on the day of the debut, according to the figures cited in the 13 November coverage. CoinDesk's own account puts the same move slightly differently — a fall of roughly 20%, from around $75,000 to $60,000, between 1 and 5 June — before Bitcoin rebounded toward $68,000 after the IPO launched. The two accounts differ in the exact numbers because they measure different windows, but they agree on the shape: a steep fall before the listing, then a recovery after it.

Bitfinex's 11 November report, cited in the coverage, added a useful detail about the timing. It observed that Bitcoin sold off sharply before the SpaceX roadshow even began, and then traded largely sideways from the start of the roadshow through the first week of trading. That pattern matters because it suggests the selling, if it was IPO-related at all, happened while investors were preparing for the offering — not on the day it listed.

None of this is a forecast. The fact that Bitcoin fell before one big listing and recovered after it does not mean it will do the same thing this time. Every listing is different, the market conditions are different — Bitcoin is already down 21% in 2026, the dollar is at a 19-month high, and the Federal Reserve is expected to raise rates in December — and the SpaceX comparison is a single example, not a pattern. It is included here as context for why analysts reached for this explanation, not as a prediction of what happens next. This site does not make price predictions, and this guide will not start now.

## What Analysts Actually Said — and What They Did Not

Because this story is built on interpretation rather than on a single verifiable event, it is worth separating what was actually said from what was merely implied.

**What was said.** Seamus Rocca of Xapo Bank attributed the latest dip to the opening of Anthropic's subscription books and the pull of capital into the offering. Benjamin Cowen said the offering may be one factor behind the recent drop. Bitfinex's 11 November report flagged the overlap between crypto retail investors and Anthropic IPO buyers as a reason to watch the competition for capital. Bloomberg reported demand at roughly six times the shares available.

**What was not said.** CoinDesk's own reporting is explicit that analysts see "no clear direct link" between Nvidia's troubles and Bitcoin's decline, and that the Anthropic offering is one possible explanation alongside a broader risk-off move. CoinCourier, in its detailed 13 November piece, stated plainly that the available evidence "does not prove that the Anthropic offering directly caused bitcoin's decline," and that reports of six-times oversubscription are "claims attributed to media or other sources rather than figures confirmed in the company's announcement or prospectus." Neither Anthropic nor any regulator nor any exchange has said the IPO moved Bitcoin's price. And the $64.4 billion figure, the $26 billion private placement, the $1.4 trillion valuation and the 19 November listing date all describe a proposed offering, not a completed one — the registration statement was not yet effective when the roadshow was announced, and the deal could still change.

That is the right way to hold this story: a large, real event, a plausible mechanism, several named analysts who find it convincing, and an explicit admission from the same coverage that the causal link is unproven. A guide that presented the IPO as the proven cause of the fall would be overstating the evidence. A guide that ignored it would be missing the freshest explanation the market itself was discussing on the day Bitcoin hit $68,000.

## What Indian Users Should Know

For Indian users, the Anthropic IPO story lands in a specific context, and four points are worth making.

**First, the price in rupees.** At roughly $68,000, Bitcoin was trading at about ₹63 lakh, at the prevailing exchange rate. That is down from roughly ₹65 lakh when it crossed $70,000 earlier in the week, and well below the roughly ₹1.18 crore implied by its all-time high near $126,000 in October 2025. The rupee figure moves with both the dollar price and the exchange rate, so it is a snapshot, not a fixed level.

**Second, the tax position is unchanged.** The 30% flat tax on crypto gains plus a 4% cess, and the 1% TDS on transfers, are Indian law. A US stock-market listing does not change them, and nothing in the IPO story changes how an Indian user's gains are taxed. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Third, the retail overlap is the real India angle.** The Bitfinex point about crypto retail investors and Anthropic IPO buyers being the same people is not an abstract observation. Indian retail investors are among the most active crypto users in the world, and the same apps and brokerages that give them access to US stocks also give them access to crypto. When a $64.4 billion offering reserves 20% of its shares for individuals, some of those individuals will be the same people who hold Bitcoin — and if they need cash for an allocation, Bitcoin is the asset they can sell fastest. That is a mechanism, not a judgement: it says nothing about whether selling is a good idea, only that the channel exists.

**Fourth, be careful with IPO-related offers.** Whenever a famous listing is in the news, messages appear offering "guaranteed" allocations, pre-IPO shares, or discounted Anthropic stock — usually through WhatsApp, Telegram or social media, and usually asking for payment in crypto. Anthropic's shares are not available to the public before the listing, and no legitimate broker sells guaranteed IPO allocations through a chat message. Anyone offering them is running a scam. The same rule that applies to every crypto message applies here: if someone you do not know offers you a deal that sounds too good to be true, it is. Do not send money, and do not share wallet details, seed phrases or exchange logins with anyone claiming to help you buy IPO shares.

## The Bottom Line

On 13 November 2026, Bitcoin fell to $68,000, its lowest since August, on its way to a sixth straight weekly loss. Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, is preparing a Nasdaq listing expected around 19 November that aims to raise up to $64.4 billion, with 825 million shares at $70 to $78, a $26 billion private placement alongside it, and demand reported at roughly six times the shares available. Several market analysts say investors may be selling liquid assets, including Bitcoin, to free up cash for the offering — a capital-rotation theory with a precedent in the SpaceX listing earlier this year, before which Bitcoin fell about 20% and then recovered after the debut. But the causal link is unproven: the same coverage that reports the theory also says analysts see no clear direct link between the day's other shocks and Bitcoin's fall, and that several forces — short positions, ETF outflows, Strategy's MSCI removal, Nvidia's turmoil and December rate-hike expectations — were pulling in the same direction at once. For Indian users, the price was roughly ₹63 lakh, the tax rules are unchanged, and the practical lesson is narrow: understand the mechanism, do not mistake an analyst's theory for a proven cause, and do not trust anyone offering you pre-IPO shares through a chat message. The listing is expected around 19 November — and whatever happens then, it will be a new event, not a repeat of this one.
