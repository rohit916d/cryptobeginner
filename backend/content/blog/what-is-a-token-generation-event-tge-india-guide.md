A crypto project you follow online announces its TGE date. Your timeline fills with countdown posts, "TGE soon" memes, and people asking where to claim their tokens. If you are new to crypto, the excitement can make it feel like a launch party you cannot afford to miss. In reality, a token generation event is a technical milestone, not a celebration — and understanding what actually happens on that day is the difference between making an informed decision and getting swept up in someone else's hype. This guide explains what a TGE is, how one works step by step, how it differs from ICOs, IDOs and airdrops, and the red flags Indian beginners should watch for before touching one.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## What Is a Token Generation Event?

A **token generation event (TGE)** is the moment a crypto project's tokens are created on a blockchain and made available for distribution for the first time. Before the TGE, the token exists only as an idea — in a whitepaper, a website, or a test version of the product. At the TGE, the smart contract that defines the token is deployed or activated, a supply is minted, and the tokens begin flowing to the people and wallets the project promised them to: early backers, the team, the community, and sometimes the public.

Think of it like a new currency being printed for the first time. Until that day, nobody can hold, trade, or use the token, because it does not exist yet. After the TGE, the token is real — it has a contract address, a total supply, and holders. Whether it has any lasting value is a completely separate question, and nothing about the event itself guarantees it.

A TGE is not the same as a token going on sale. Some projects sell tokens before the TGE; others give them away through airdrops; some distribute them to people who earned them by testing the product. The TGE is the event where the tokens themselves come into existence and start moving. The sale, the airdrop, and the exchange listing are separate steps that may happen on the same day, or weeks apart.

It is also worth knowing that the term is used loosely. Projects sometimes announce a "TGE" when they really mean a token launch, a listing, or a claim window. The technical meaning is narrower: the generation — the creation — of the tokens. Keeping that distinction in mind helps you read announcements more carefully, because a project that calls everything a TGE may be dressing up an ordinary listing as something bigger.

## How a TGE Works, Step by Step

Most TGEs follow the same basic sequence, even when the details differ:

**1. The token contract is deployed.** The project publishes a smart contract on a blockchain — often Ethereum, Solana, or another network — that defines the token's name, symbol, total supply, and rules. This is the token's birth certificate. From this point on, anyone can look the contract up on a block explorer and check that it matches what the project announced.

**2. The supply is minted.** The contract creates the tokens, usually the full total supply at once. That does not mean all of them are released: most are locked into vesting schedules or held in the project's treasury, and only a portion circulates on day one.

**3. Allocations are distributed.** Tokens flow to the groups named in the project's tokenomics: the team, early investors, advisers, the community treasury, and the public. Each group typically receives its tokens on a different schedule — the public allocation may be available immediately, while team and investor tokens unlock gradually over months or years.

**4. A claim window opens.** If you earned tokens — through an airdrop, a testnet programme, or a community campaign — the project usually opens a claim page where you connect your wallet and collect them. Legitimate claims never ask for your seed phrase, and most do not ask you to send any crypto first.

**5. Liquidity is added.** For the token to be tradeable, the project (or a market maker) adds some of it to a decentralised exchange pool, paired with a stablecoin or a major crypto. Until liquidity exists, you may hold tokens you cannot sell anywhere.

**6. Trading begins.** Once the token is listed on an exchange or a pool, anyone can buy or sell it. This is the moment prices start moving — often violently, because a brand-new token has thin liquidity and a crowd of people trying to sell at the same time.

## TGE vs ICO vs IDO vs Airdrop

Beginners often mix these terms up, because they all happen around token launches. They describe different things:

| Event | What it is | Do you pay? |
|---|---|---|
| **TGE** | The tokens are created and distributed for the first time | Not by itself — it is the creation event |
| **ICO** | An initial coin offering — a public sale of tokens, usually before or at the TGE | Yes, you buy tokens directly from the project |
| **IDO** | An initial DEX offering — a token sale held on a decentralised exchange | Yes, you buy through a DEX launchpad |
| **Airdrop** | Free tokens sent to wallets that meet certain conditions | No, but you may need to claim them |

A single project can have several of these in sequence: an ICO to raise funds, then a TGE to create the tokens, then an airdrop to reward early users, then an IDO on a launchpad. Knowing which event you are actually looking at matters, because the risks are different. A sale asks for your money; a claim asks for a wallet connection; a TGE by itself asks for nothing at all.

## What Happens After the TGE?

The days after a TGE are usually the most volatile in a token's life, and understanding why helps you stay calm. Three forces act at once.

First, **new supply meets the market**. Early recipients — airdrop hunters, testnet participants, early backers whose tokens unlocked — often sell quickly. When many people sell a token that few people are buying, the price can fall sharply, even if the project itself is sound. This is normal mechanics, not proof that the project has failed.

Second, **liquidity is thin**. A brand-new trading pool may hold only a small amount of funds, so even modest trades move the price a lot. That is why you see wild swings in the first hours: the market is simply too small to absorb normal-sized trades smoothly.

Third, **attention fades**. A TGE is a marketing peak. Once the countdown ends and the tokens are out, the hype that carried the launch often dies down, and the token's price starts reflecting what the project actually does — which takes months or years to judge, not minutes.

None of this is a prediction about any particular token. It is the pattern of how new-token markets behave, and it is why experienced observers treat TGE week as a spectator event rather than a buying opportunity. If you are still learning, watching one TGE from the sidelines — checking the contract, the unlock schedule, and the liquidity — teaches you more than participating in it.

## 7 Red Flags in a TGE Announcement

TGE season is also scam season. Fake claim pages and impersonator accounts appear within minutes of a real announcement. Watch for these warning signs:

1. **A claim link in a reply or direct message.** Real projects pin their claim link on their official account or website. A link that arrives in your DMs, or in a reply from a lookalike account, is almost always a wallet drainer.
2. **You are asked to send crypto to "claim" tokens.** No genuine claim requires you to pay first. Any page asking you to send funds, or to pay a "gas fee" to a random address, is a scam.
3. **The project asks for your seed phrase.** No legitimate website or support agent ever needs your recovery phrase. Anyone asking for it is trying to empty your wallet.
4. **A countdown with no contract address.** If a project announces a TGE but will not show the token contract, the tokenomics, or the unlock schedule, there is nothing to verify — and nothing to trust.
5. **Most of the supply unlocks on day one.** When insiders can sell everything immediately, early buyers absorb the selling. A healthy schedule staggers unlocks over months or years.
6. **"Guaranteed" returns or "100x" promises.** No one can guarantee a token's price. Promises of fixed returns are a classic sign of a pump scheme or an outright fraud.
7. **Paid spots or "VIP whitelist" fees.** Some scammers sell fake early-access spots to a TGE that does not exist, or to a real one they have no connection with. Genuine whitelists are earned through participation, not bought from strangers.

If you spot even one of these, step back. Missing a TGE costs you nothing. Falling for a fake one can cost you your whole wallet.

## A 5-Minute Checklist Before You Interact With a TGE

If a TGE interests you, run through this check before connecting your wallet or spending anything:

1. **Verify the contract address** on the project's official website, then check it on a block explorer. The name, symbol, and supply should match what the project published.
2. **Read the unlock schedule.** Find out how much of the supply unlocks at the TGE and over the following months, and who holds it. A small circulating supply with huge unlocks coming is a warning sign.
3. **Check the liquidity.** See whether the token trades on a real exchange or a known decentralised exchange, and how deep the pool is. A token with almost no liquidity is hard to exit.
4. **Use a separate wallet.** Keep any wallet you connect to a new claim page separate from your main holdings, and hold only small amounts in it.
5. **Search for the project's name plus "scam".** Quick searches often surface warnings from other users, fake-site reports, or the project's own alerts about impersonators.

Five minutes of checking will not make a bad project good, but it will keep you away from most of the traps.

## TGEs and Indian Users: Tax and Rules

For Indian users, a TGE raises two practical questions: tax and access.

**Tax.** Tokens you receive for free — for example through an airdrop tied to a TGE — are generally treated as income at their market value when received, and a later sale can trigger the flat 30% tax on crypto gains, with 1% TDS applying to many transfers. Losses cannot be set off against gains, and every receipt and sale should be recorded for your income tax return, including in Schedule VDA. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Access.** Indian users typically reach new tokens through FIU-IND registered exchanges or through decentralised exchanges, where buying requires a compatible wallet and a network fee. Some new tokens are only available on offshore platforms or decentralised exchanges that Indian exchanges do not list, and availability can change quickly. Buying a token on an unregistered platform, or through a channel that bypasses KYC, carries its own risks — including frozen funds and no way to complain. If a TGE token is not available on a platform you can verify, waiting is usually the safer choice.

Finally, remember that no Indian regulator approves individual tokens. A TGE happening, or a token being listed somewhere, is not an endorsement — it is simply an event. The responsibility for checking the project, the contract, and the tax treatment stays with you.

## The Bottom Line

A token generation event is the day a project's tokens come into existence — a technical milestone, not a promise of value. The useful questions to ask are boring ones: what is the contract address, who holds the supply, when does it unlock, and where is the liquidity? If a project cannot answer those clearly, or if the only thing around its TGE is hype and countdown posts, you have learned everything you need to know. In crypto, the safest move is often the least exciting one: watch, verify, and decide slowly.
