# How to Transfer Crypto Between Exchanges Safely in India

Learning how to transfer crypto between exchanges is a basic skill every Indian crypto user needs sooner or later. You might have bought Bitcoin on one platform and want to move it to another exchange, shift a coin to an account with better INR support, or consolidate holdings that are spread across two or three apps. The transfer itself is simple — but crypto transfers cannot be reversed, so one wrong address or one wrong network can mean a permanent loss.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

This guide walks you through the full process step by step: what to check before you start, how the transfer works, what it costs, and the safety habits that protect Indian users every time.

## Why People Transfer Crypto Between Exchanges

Beginners often assume crypto bought on one exchange must stay there forever. It does not. Crypto lives on a blockchain, and any exchange or wallet that supports the same coin and network can receive it. Common reasons to transfer crypto between exchanges include:

- **Consolidation:** You opened accounts on CoinDCX, WazirX, and ZebPay over time and want everything in one place for easier tracking.
- **A coin is listed elsewhere:** A token you hold may trade on one platform but not another, so you move it where it is supported.
- **Better experience or features:** You may prefer another exchange's app, INR deposit options, or reporting tools.
- **Moving towards self-custody:** A transfer between exchanges is often the first step before later moving funds to a personal wallet.

Whatever your reason, the mechanics are the same — and so are the risks. Treat every transfer like sending money to an account number you cannot correct later, because that is exactly what it is.

## Before You Start: The Pre-Transfer Checklist

Most failed transfers were lost before the send button was pressed, because a basic check was skipped. Run through this list first:

1. **Both exchanges support the coin.** Confirm the receiving exchange lists the exact token you are sending. Similar names are not enough — check the full token name.
2. **Both exchanges support the same network.** A coin can travel on several networks. The sending and receiving sides must agree on one shared network, covered in detail below.
3. **Your accounts are fully verified.** Complete KYC on both platforms. Some exchanges restrict withdrawals or deposits on unverified accounts.
4. **Check minimums and limits.** Each exchange sets a minimum deposit and a minimum withdrawal amount. A transfer below the minimum deposit may not be credited.
5. **Note the fees in advance.** The sending exchange charges a withdrawal fee, usually deducted from the amount you send. Know it before you confirm.
6. **Keep records from the start.** Save screenshots or notes of amounts, dates, and transaction IDs. You will want them for your own tracking and for India's tax filing later.

## Step by Step: How to Transfer Crypto Between Exchanges

### Step 1: Open the deposit page on the receiving exchange

Log in to the exchange that will receive the crypto. Find the Deposit option, select the coin, and the platform will show you a deposit address — a long string of characters — and possibly a QR code. This address is the destination for your transfer.

### Step 2: Choose the network carefully

If the coin works on more than one network, the deposit page will ask you to pick one. Networks have names like the coin's own native chain, ERC-20 (Ethereum), or BEP-20, among others. Remember your choice exactly: the sending side must use the identical network. Choosing different networks on the two sides is the single most common way beginners lose funds.

### Step 3: Copy the full deposit address — and the memo if there is one

Copy the complete address using the copy button, never by typing it. Some coins also show a second field called a memo, destination tag, or payment ID. If the receiving exchange shows one, it is mandatory — the address identifies the exchange, and the memo identifies your account within it. Missing the memo can mean your deposit is not credited.

### Step 4: Go to the sending exchange and start a withdrawal

On the exchange that currently holds your crypto, open Withdraw, select the same coin, and paste the deposit address. Select the same network you chose in Step 2. Enter the memo or tag if there is one. Double-check that the pasted address matches the original in full — first characters, middle, and last characters.

### Step 5: Send a small test transfer first

For any new destination, send a small amount first — just above the minimum. Wait until it appears as confirmed in the receiving exchange. Only then send the remaining balance. The extra fee for splitting the transfer is small insurance against losing everything to one wrong character or one wrong network.

### Step 6: Confirm with your security checks and wait

The sending exchange will ask for confirmation, typically an email or app confirmation plus a one-time code or authenticator approval. Complete them, then wait. The exchange processes the withdrawal, the blockchain confirms it, and the receiving exchange credits your account after its required number of confirmations.

### Step 7: Verify arrival and save the transaction ID

Check that the full expected amount (minus the withdrawal fee) has arrived. Save the transaction ID — often called the TXID or hash — along with the date and INR value at the time. That record connects the two sides of the transfer if you ever need to prove the movement was between your own accounts.

## The Mistakes That Cost Beginners Money

| Mistake | What happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Different networks on each side | Deposit is not credited and may be unrecoverable | Pick one network and match it exactly on both exchanges |
| Copying an address from old history | Lookalike-address scams can redirect your funds | Always copy fresh from the receiving exchange's deposit page |
| Forgetting the memo or tag | The exchange receives funds but cannot credit your account | Copy both fields whenever a memo is shown |
| Sending below the minimum deposit | The amount may not be credited | Check the minimum on the receiving side before sending |
| Sending the full amount first | One error loses everything | Test with a small amount, confirm arrival, then send the rest |
| Using an unsupported coin or token copy | Funds go to a contract the exchange does not recognise | Verify the exact token name on both platforms |

## Fees and Timing: What a Transfer Actually Costs

A transfer between exchanges has one main cost: the withdrawal fee charged by the sending exchange. This fee pays the network's transaction cost and is usually a fixed amount of the coin, not a percentage, so it takes a bigger bite out of small transfers. The receiving exchange normally does not charge a deposit fee. Before confirming, the sending exchange shows the fee and the exact amount that will arrive — read that screen carefully.

Timing varies by network. Some networks confirm in minutes; Bitcoin can take considerably longer when busy, and each exchange adds its own confirmation requirement before crediting. A transfer that is "still processing" after twenty minutes is often normal, not lost. Check the status with the TXID on a block explorer before worrying.

One India-specific point: moving crypto between your own exchange accounts is a transfer, not a sale, so it does not itself trigger the tax on gains. Tax applies when you sell or swap crypto for value. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Keeping clean transfer records is what lets you prove which movements were transfers and which were sales.

## Safety Rules for Every Exchange-to-Exchange Transfer

- **Use whitelists where offered.** Many Indian exchanges let you save and lock verified withdrawal addresses. A whitelisted address cannot be changed instantly, which frustrates both your mistakes and an attacker.
- **Never copy addresses from transaction history.** Scammers plant lookalike addresses there. Always start from the receiving exchange's deposit page.
- **Beware of clipboard tricks.** Some malware swaps a copied address for the attacker's. After pasting, compare the pasted address against the original in full before confirming.
- **Do transfers on your own secure connection.** Avoid public Wi-Fi when moving funds, and make sure you are on the genuine exchange app or website, not a lookalike.
- **Stay calm under time pressure.** Anyone urging you to transfer quickly — a group admin, a "support" account, a doubling scheme — is showing you a scam pattern, not an opportunity.

## What to Do If a Transfer Does Not Arrive

First, find the TXID on the sending exchange and paste it into a block explorer for that network. If the explorer shows the transfer as confirmed and sent to the correct address, the delay is on the receiving side — contact its support with the TXID, coin, network, amount, and time. If the explorer shows a different address or network than you intended, be honest with yourself quickly: a confirmed transfer to a wrong address cannot be reversed by anyone. If you used the wrong network but the right exchange address, ask the receiving exchange whether recovery is possible; some platforms help for a fee, many cannot. Either way, save all records — they matter for support and for your tax trail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to transfer crypto between exchanges?

It depends on the network and how many confirmations the receiving exchange requires. Fast networks can credit a deposit within a few minutes, while Bitcoin transfers often take longer, especially when the network is busy. Always wait for the receiving exchange to show the deposit as confirmed before assuming anything has gone wrong.

### Is transferring crypto between my own exchanges taxable in India?

Moving crypto between two accounts that both belong to you is a transfer, not a sale, so it does not by itself create a taxable gain. Tax applies when you sell or exchange crypto for value. Keep records of every transfer anyway, because you will need a clear trail of purchase prices and movements when you file. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

### What happens if I choose the wrong network for a transfer?

Your crypto may arrive at an address the receiving exchange cannot credit, or it may be effectively stranded on a network the platform does not support. Recovery is sometimes possible with the exchange's help, but it is never guaranteed and can take a long time. This is why matching the network on both sides and sending a small test transfer first are so important.

## Conclusion

To transfer crypto between exchanges safely, the whole skill is preparation: confirm the coin and network on both sides, copy the fresh deposit address in full along with any memo, send a small test first, and save the transaction ID when it lands. Do that every time — whether you are moving funds between CoinDCX, WazirX, ZebPay, or any other platform — and a routine transfer stays routine instead of becoming an expensive lesson.
