Every week, thousands of Indians learn about crypto the painful way — through a scam. The stories repeat like clockwork: a "customer care executive" calls about a stuck withdrawal, a Telegram guru promises to double your money in a week, or a WhatsApp message from a stranger turns into a romance that ends with an empty wallet. India's crypto boom has created millions of first-time investors, and scammers have built an entire industry around them.

This guide walks you through the seven scams that target Indian crypto users most often, why they work, and the concrete steps that keep you safe. No hype, no jargon — just a practical shield.

> **Companion read:** scams also have a *delivery* side — the fake Google ads, WhatsApp "wrong numbers," and impersonator accounts scammers use to reach you. Learn to recognise those channels in [How Crypto Scammers Contact You](/blog/top-5-crypto-scams-to-avoid).

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Scammers constantly invent new tricks — treat every unsolicited crypto offer, call, or message with suspicion, no matter how convincing it sounds.

## Scam #1: Fake Exchange Customer Support

This is the most reported crypto scam pattern in India. You have a real problem — a deposit that hasn't credited, a withdrawal stuck "under review" — so you Google the exchange's customer care number or ask for help in a public Telegram group. Someone responds claiming to be from the exchange's support team.

The "support agent" sounds professional. They may know your name, your transaction amount, even your ticket number (often picked up from the public message you posted). Then comes the ask: "share your screen on AnyDesk," "enter this OTP," or "deposit a small verification fee to release your funds." Within minutes, your account is drained.

**Why it works:** Real Indian exchanges generally offer only ticket-based or in-app support — not inbound phone calls. But panicked users don't verify this in the moment.

**How to avoid it:**
- Never share your screen with anyone claiming to be support. Real support never needs it.
- Never share OTPs, passwords, or your seed phrase. Ever. With anyone.
- Only contact exchanges through the official app or website's support section. Don't Google phone numbers — scammers SEO-poison fake numbers.
- Don't post your transaction IDs or ticket numbers in public Telegram/Discord groups.

## Scam #2: Telegram "Doubling" and Guaranteed-Return Schemes

"Invest ₹10,000, get ₹20,000 in 7 days." These schemes flood Telegram channels, Instagram reels, and WhatsApp forwards. They often use names of famous investors, Bollywood-style success stories, and screenshots of "payouts" from supposed users. Some even do small real payouts at first — funded by the next batch of victims — before disappearing with everyone's money.

This is the classic Ponzi scheme dressed up for the crypto age. There's no trading, no mining, no investment happening behind the scenes. Your "returns" are just someone else's deposit, and the whole thing collapses when new deposits slow down.

**Why it works:** It exploits trust — the scheme usually arrives via a friend or relative who genuinely believes they're earning. Social proof beats skepticism.

**How to avoid it:**
- Guaranteed high returns with no risk do not exist in crypto or anywhere else. That sentence alone filters out 90% of scams.
- Check whether the platform is registered anywhere and whether withdrawals actually work for amounts larger than the minimum. Most Ponzi apps block big withdrawals.
- If a friend is promoting it, warn them — they may be an early victim, not a fraudster.

## Scam #3: Fake UPI Payment Proofs in P2P Trading

Peer-to-peer (P2P) trading is popular in India, and most trades go smoothly. But scammers exploit the gap between "the buyer says they paid" and "the money actually arrived." The trick: a buyer sends a convincing fake UPI payment screenshot — or a real payment that they immediately reverse via a fraudulent chargeback-style trick — then pressures the seller to release crypto quickly, claiming they're in a hurry.

Another variant: the scammer sends payment from a third party's account (a stolen or mule account), the real account holder disputes it, and the seller's crypto is long gone.

**How to avoid it:**
- Never release crypto based on a screenshot. Log into your own bank/UPI app and confirm the money has actually arrived and is available.
- Only accept payments to the exact account details registered on the P2P platform.
- Never cancel a trade or release crypto under time pressure. Urgency is the scammer's favourite tool.
- Stick to the platform's escrow and dispute process — never take a P2P deal "off-platform" on WhatsApp to save a fee.

## Scam #4: Fake Crypto Apps and "Mining" Apps

Search the Play Store for a popular coin or exchange name and you'll sometimes find lookalike apps with similar logos and names — "CoinDCX Pro Max" style clones. These apps look functional: they show charts, fake balances, even fake profits. Then they ask for a deposit to "activate withdrawals" or "upgrade your plan" — and the money vanishes.

A close cousin: cloud-mining apps that promise daily Bitcoin earnings for a small subscription. Real Bitcoin mining requires enormous specialised hardware; a ₹499 app subscription cannot mine anything. The "earnings" shown on screen are fiction.

**How to avoid it:**
- Install apps only from links on the exchange's official website — not from Play Store search results, ads, or forwarded APK files.
- Be deeply suspicious of any app promising mining income or daily profits for a subscription fee.
- Check app reviews and developer names carefully. Fake apps often have a handful of generic 5-star reviews.

## Scam #5: KYC and Regulatory Impersonation

Scammers in India weaponise the fear of government action. You get a call or SMS claiming to be from the Income Tax department, RBI, or "cyber cell," warning that your crypto account will be frozen for tax evasion unless you "verify" immediately — usually by sharing personal details, paying a "penalty" in crypto, or installing an app.

Others pose as exchange compliance teams asking you to re-do KYC through a link that steals your Aadhaar/PAN details and selfies — perfect ingredients for identity theft and loan fraud.

**Why it works:** Crypto tax rules in India (30% tax, 1% TDS) genuinely confuse people, so threats about tax trouble feel plausible.

**How to avoid it:**
- Government agencies do not call demanding crypto payments or immediate KYC over the phone.
- Do KYC only inside the official exchange app or website. Never through links in SMS, email, or WhatsApp.
- Never share full PAN/Aadhaar details or selfies with anyone who contacts you first.

## Scam #6: Pig-Butchering (Romance + Investment Combo)

This one is devastating because it plays on emotion, not greed. It usually starts with a "wrong number" WhatsApp message or a friendly stranger on a dating app. Over weeks, they build a genuine-feeling friendship or romance. Eventually they casually mention their amazing crypto "trading strategy" and invite you to try a small amount on a professional-looking trading website.

Small early withdrawals work — that's the "fattening" phase (hence the name). Then you're encouraged to invest bigger. When you try to withdraw the big amount, you're told to pay "taxes" or "fees" first. The person, the website, and your money all disappear together.

**How to avoid it:**
- Be extremely cautious if a new online friend or romantic interest steers you toward crypto investing. This is the single biggest red flag of this scam.
- "Can't lose" trading platforms that only exist as a website link they've given you are fake. Real platforms are public and well-known.
- Never send more money to "unlock" a withdrawal. That's the trap closing.

## Scam #7: Fake Airdrops and Seed-Phrase Phishing

"Claim your free 500 USDT airdrop!" The link leads to a polished website that looks like a real DeFi project. To claim, you "connect your wallet" — and the malicious site drains it. Or a slightly different version asks you to "verify" by entering your 12- or 24-word seed phrase.

A related classic: someone in a help forum or DM offers to help you fix a wallet problem, and asks for your seed phrase or a screenshot of it. The moment you share it, your wallet is empty. There is no legitimate situation in which anyone needs your seed phrase — not support, not developers, not "verification."

**How to avoid it:**
- Your seed phrase is the master key to your wallet. It never leaves your possession. No exceptions.
- Treat every airdrop link with suspicion, especially ones arriving via DM. Check the project's official channels before interacting.
- Use a hardware wallet for significant holdings so that even a compromised website can't sign away your funds.

## The Universal Red Flags

If you remember nothing else, remember this list. Any crypto opportunity showing one of these deserves an immediate "no":

| Red Flag | What it really means |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed returns / "risk-free profit" | Ponzi or fake platform |
| Pressure to act now / limited slots | Designed to stop you thinking |
| Asked for seed phrase, OTP, or password | Theft in progress |
| Asked to install remote-access apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) | Account takeover attempt |
| Contacted you first (call, DM, WhatsApp) | Legitimate services don't do this |
| Off-platform deals to "save fees" | No escrow = no protection |
| Pay a fee to unlock your withdrawal | The classic exit trap |

## What To Do If You've Been Scammed

Act fast — speed matters:

1. **Report immediately** on India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the cyber helpline **1930**. The sooner you report, the better the chance of freezing fraudulent accounts.
2. **File an FIR** at your local cyber cell with every detail: wallet addresses, transaction hashes, screenshots of chats, phone numbers, bank/UPI transaction references.
3. **Notify your exchange** if funds passed through one — they may flag or freeze the scammer's account.
4. **Secure your remaining assets** — move any untouched crypto to a new wallet with a fresh seed phrase, and change passwords everywhere.
5. **Don't pay "recovery agents"** who promise to get your money back for a fee. That's a second scam targeting victims of the first.

## The Bottom Line

Scammers don't hack blockchains — they hack human psychology: panic, greed, trust, and fear. Every scam in this list fails against a calm person who verifies independently and never shares secrets under pressure.

Build three habits and you'll be safer than 95% of crypto users in India: keep your seed phrase and OTPs completely private, verify every support contact and app through official channels only, and treat guaranteed returns as proof of fraud. The crypto space rewards the patient and the careful — be both.
