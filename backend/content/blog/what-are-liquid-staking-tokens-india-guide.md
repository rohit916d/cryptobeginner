# What Are Liquid Staking Tokens? A Beginner's Guide for India

Staking is one of the first things many beginners hear about after buying crypto: lock your coins, help secure a network, and earn rewards. But staking has an obvious drawback — your coins are locked. While they are staked, you cannot sell them, spend them, or use them anywhere else.

Liquid staking tokens, often shortened to LSTs, were created to solve exactly that problem. They let you earn staking rewards while keeping your crypto usable. In this guide, we explain what liquid staking tokens are, how they work, what can go wrong, and what Indian beginners should check before touching them.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## Staking, in One Paragraph

On proof-of-stake blockchains such as Ethereum, validators secure the network by locking up (staking) the network's own coin. In return, validators earn new coins as rewards. Regular holders who do not run a validator can usually stake through an exchange or a staking service, and they receive a share of the rewards.

The catch is the lock-up. Staked coins are tied to the validator until they are unstaked, and unstaking can take hours or days depending on the network. During a sharp market move, that delay matters: you cannot sell what is locked. Liquid staking was designed to remove that trade-off.

## What Is a Liquid Staking Token?

A liquid staking token is a receipt token. When you deposit crypto into a liquid staking protocol, the protocol stakes your coins with validators on your behalf and gives you a token back. That token represents your claim on the staked coins plus the rewards they earn.

Think of it like a coat-check ticket. You hand over your coat (your staked crypto), and you receive a ticket. The ticket itself is useful: you can trade it, lend it, or use it in other decentralised finance (DeFi) apps — and when you want your coat back, you hand in the ticket and redeem the underlying crypto plus accumulated rewards.

The most well-known examples are stETH (from Lido) for Ethereum and similar tokens on other networks. Each protocol has its own token, its own validators, and its own rules, so the details vary — but the core idea is the same: staked, but still usable.

## How Liquid Staking Works, Step by Step

Here is the basic flow, using Ethereum as the example:

1. **You deposit ETH** into a liquid staking protocol through its app or website.
2. **The protocol stakes your ETH** with a set of validators it chooses or lets token holders vote on.
3. **You receive an LST** (for example, stETH) in your wallet, roughly equal in value to the ETH you deposited.
4. **Rewards accumulate.** As validators earn rewards, the value of your LST slowly grows — either the token's price rises, or the number of tokens in your wallet increases, depending on the design.
5. **You use the LST** however you like: hold it, trade it on a decentralised exchange, lend it, or provide it as collateral in a lending protocol.
6. **You redeem it** when you want out, either by swapping it on the open market or by burning the LST through the protocol to get your ETH back.

The important point is that at no stage do you run a validator yourself. The protocol handles the technical work, and you pay for that convenience through fees taken from the rewards.

## Two Kinds of LSTs: Rebasing and Reward-Bearing

LSTs come in two main designs, and the difference matters if you plan to use them in DeFi:

**Rebasing tokens** keep a fixed price close to the underlying coin (usually 1 token = 1 ETH), but your balance grows over time as rewards arrive. stETH works this way: if you hold 1 stETH today, you might hold 1.03 stETH a year later.

**Reward-bearing tokens** keep your balance fixed, but the token's price rises over time as rewards accumulate. If you hold 1 token today, you still hold 1 token a year later — but it can now be redeemed for more ETH than before.

Why does this matter? Some DeFi apps handle growing balances poorly, so reward-bearing tokens are often easier to use in lending and liquidity pools. Before using an LST anywhere, check which design it uses and whether the app you want to use supports it.

## Why Beginners Find LSTs Attractive

Liquid staking tokens became popular for three simple reasons:

- **No lock-up.** Your staked position stays liquid. You can exit by swapping the token on a market instead of waiting through an unstaking queue.
- **DeFi composability.** The LST can earn additional yield in lending protocols or liquidity pools, on top of the staking rewards it already carries.
- **Low minimums.** Running an Ethereum validator needs 32 ETH. Liquid staking lets you stake much smaller amounts — even fractions of a coin — through a pooled protocol.

That flexibility is real, but it is not free. Every extra layer adds risk, and beginners often underestimate how many layers they are stacking.

## The Risks Beginners Must Understand

LSTs are not a safer version of staking — they are staking plus extra risk. Here are the main ones:

### 1. Smart-Contract Risk

The protocol is code. If that code has a bug or is exploited, the staked funds can be stolen or frozen. Audits reduce this risk but never remove it. Check whether the protocol has been audited, by whom, and whether the audit is public — and remember that even audited protocols have been hacked.

### 2. Depeg Risk

An LST is supposed to trade near the price of the underlying coin, but it is not guaranteed. In stressed markets, LSTs have traded at a discount — sometimes several percent below the value of the crypto backing them. If you need to sell during such a period, you may get back less than you put in, even before any price fall in the crypto itself.

### 3. Slashing and Validator Risk

Validators can be penalised (slashed) for going offline or behaving incorrectly, which reduces the rewards — or in severe cases, the staked funds. A protocol that spreads stake across many validators limits this risk; one that concentrates it does not.

### 4. Governance and Centralisation Risk

Some protocols concentrate enormous amounts of staked crypto under one system. If a single protocol controls a large share of a network's validators, that raises questions about decentralisation — and a governance vote or a protocol decision can change fees, validator sets, or redemption rules.

### 5. The Double-Yield Trap

Using an LST as collateral to borrow, then staking or buying more, can multiply both gains and losses. A small price fall can trigger a liquidation of your collateral. Beginners should avoid stacking leverage on top of liquid staking entirely.

### 6. Fake Tokens and Phishing

Fake LSTs are a common scam. Fraudsters create tokens with the same name and logo as a real LST and promote them in Telegram or WhatsApp groups. Always verify the contract address from the protocol's official website or documentation — never from a message, a search ad, or a group post.

## Liquid Staking vs Regular Staking vs Restaking

These three ideas are often confused. Here is how they differ:

| Feature | Regular staking | Liquid staking (LST) | Restaking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What you lock | Your coins with a validator | Your coins with a protocol | Your already-staked position |
| What you get back | Rewards when you unstake | An LST you can trade or use | Extra rewards for securing more services |
| Liquidity while staked | None | Yes — the LST is tradeable | Depends on the design |
| Extra risk layers | Validator risk only | Protocol + depeg + contract risk | All of the above, plus new services' risk |
| Best for | Simple, patient holders | Users who want flexibility | Advanced users only |

For most beginners, regular staking through a reputable exchange is the simplest starting point. LSTs make sense only once you understand the extra risks and have a specific reason to need the liquidity.

## LSTs and Indian Beginners: The Practical Picture

There are a few India-specific things to keep in mind:

- **No SEBI-regulated LST product exists in India.** LSTs are accessed through global DeFi protocols, not through Indian regulated platforms. That means no Indian investor-protection framework covers them.
- **Use FIU-IND registered exchanges for the on-ramp.** If you buy the underlying crypto in India, use an exchange that is registered with FIU-IND, completes KYC, and deducts the 1% TDS where applicable. That keeps your entry and exit clean.
- **Tax applies at multiple points.** Staking rewards are generally treated as income when received, and selling or swapping an LST is a taxable transfer at a flat 30% on gains, with no loss set-off allowed. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.
- **Keep records of everything.** Note the date, amount, and value in rupees of every deposit, every reward, every swap, and every redemption. If you use an LST across several DeFi apps, the trail gets complicated fast — a simple spreadsheet started on day one saves hours at filing time.
- **Watch the gas fees.** Moving LSTs around on Ethereum can cost significant network fees, especially during busy periods. Small amounts can be eaten up by fees alone, so check the fee before every transaction.

## A 5-Minute Safety Checklist Before Using an LST

If you are considering a liquid staking token, run through this checklist first:

1. **Verify the contract address** from the protocol's official site — never from a chat group or a search result.
2. **Check the audit history.** Has the protocol been audited by a known firm, and are the reports public?
3. **Read the redemption terms.** Can you redeem directly through the protocol, or only by selling on a market? Is there a waiting period?
4. **Look at the peg history.** Has the token traded at a discount before, and by how much?
5. **Check who runs the validators.** A wide, transparent validator set is better than a small or hidden one.
6. **Start small.** Try a tiny amount first and complete a full cycle — deposit, hold, redeem — before committing more.
7. **Never share your seed phrase.** No genuine protocol, support team, or "airdrop" will ever ask for it.

## Conclusion

Liquid staking tokens solve a real problem: they let you earn staking rewards without locking your crypto away. That flexibility has made them one of DeFi's most popular building blocks. But every layer of convenience adds a layer of risk — smart-contract bugs, depeg events, validator problems, and scams all sit between you and your funds.

For Indian beginners, the sensible approach is to learn the basics first: understand regular staking, use registered exchanges for buying and selling, keep careful tax records, and only explore LSTs with small amounts once you can explain the risks in your own words. Liquid does not mean risk-free — it just means you can exit faster when things go wrong, and that only helps if you are watching.
