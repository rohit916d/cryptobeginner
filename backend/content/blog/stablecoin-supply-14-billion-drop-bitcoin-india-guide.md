The cash inside crypto is shrinking at its fastest pace in more than four years. According to Glassnode's Week Onchain report, published on 11 November 2026 and titled "Running on Empty", total stablecoin supply fell by $14.2 billion over the 30 days through 9 November — the sharpest contraction since June 2022. Bitcoin was trading near $74,000 at the time, below the $75,600 average cost at which recent buyers acquired their coins, and only about 35% of coins held by those recent buyers were in profit. This guide explains what stablecoin supply actually measures, why it shrank, what the June 2022 comparison does and does not mean, and what Indian investors should take from the numbers — without predictions and without advice.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a published onchain report; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset, and it does not predict where prices will go.

## What Happened: The Numbers at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 11–12 November 2026 by Glassnode's own report and by Decrypt, CoinDoo, ProCoinNews, Coin360, CryptoSbank and The Currency Analytics, are:

- **Stablecoin supply fell $14.2 billion in 30 days**, through 9 November 2026, according to Glassnode. That is the fastest contraction since June 2022.
- **Total stablecoin supply now sits just below $280 billion**, a level last seen in September 2025, according to Coin360's reading of the report.
- **Tether and Circle drove most of the drain.** The two issuers together redeemed about $4.4 billion in the week to 7 November, and eight of the ten largest stablecoins shrank. USDT alone fell by about $3.1 billion between 31 October and 7 November, according to DefiLlama data cited by CoinDoo.
- **A broader measure agrees.** DefiLlama's wider stablecoin series, which tracks more tokens than Glassnode's basket, shows a slightly larger decline of about $15 billion over the 30 days to 7 November, from roughly $307 billion to $292 billion.
- **Bitcoin demand fell alongside it.** CryptoQuant's apparent-demand measure dropped by 284,000 BTC over 30 days, its sharpest fall since March 2025, according to ProCoinNews. Apparent demand is an estimate of new buying interest, calculated from changes in supply on exchanges and in long-term holdings.
- **US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw about $1.6 billion of net outflows** across the 20 trading sessions through 9 November, according to Glassnode.
- **Bitcoin traded near $74,000** — around ₹69 lakh in India — below the $75,600 short-term-holder cost basis, with only about 35% of recent buyers' coins in profit.

## What Stablecoin Supply Actually Measures

To understand why a $14.2 billion number matters, it helps to understand what a stablecoin is for. A stablecoin such as USDT (Tether) or USDC (Circle) is a crypto token designed to stay close to one US dollar. Traders use them as parking spots: instead of converting crypto back into rupees or dollars and leaving the exchange, they convert into a stablecoin and keep the money inside the crypto system, ready to buy again quickly. Onchain, stablecoins are also the settlement currency for decentralised exchanges, lending protocols and payments.

That makes total stablecoin supply the closest thing crypto has to a cash balance. When supply grows, new dollars are entering the system — someone bought stablecoins with real money, and that money is now sitting inside crypto, available to buy Bitcoin or anything else. When supply shrinks, the opposite is happening: holders are redeeming stablecoins for dollars and taking the money out. The cash pool inside the system gets smaller.

Glassnode's figure comes from a fixed basket of 11 dollar tokens, each counted at $1 per unit, so it measures units leaving circulation rather than a price effect. That is an important detail: the $14.2 billion is not an estimate of how much Bitcoin was sold, and it is not a tally of losses. It is a measure of how much dollar-denominated cash left the crypto system in a month. The DefiLlama series, which counts more tokens, tells the same story with a slightly bigger number — about $15 billion — which is why the two sources corroborate each other rather than contradicting each other.

## Why the Supply Shrank

The contraction was driven by redemptions, not by a single event. When a holder redeems USDT or USDC, the issuer burns the tokens and sends dollars back. Tether and Circle together redeemed about $4.4 billion in the week to 7 November alone, and eight of the ten largest stablecoins shrank in the same window. CryptoQuant separately reported that USDT's market value fell $6.6 billion over 60 days. In plain terms, a meaningful number of holders decided to take dollars out of crypto rather than keep them parked inside it.

That happened alongside two other signs of weaker demand. First, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $1.6 billion of net outflows across 20 trading sessions through 9 November — money leaving the funds that hold Bitcoin on behalf of investors. Second, CryptoQuant's apparent-demand measure for Bitcoin fell by 284,000 BTC over 30 days, its sharpest drop since March 2025. Apparent demand is not a count of buyers; it is an estimate built from how much Bitcoin is moving onto and off exchanges and into long-term storage. A negative reading means the balance of those flows points to less new buying interest, not more.

There is also a quieter detail in Glassnode's report that rounds out the picture. Bitcoin was trading below the $75,600 average cost of coins bought in the last 155 days — the short-term-holder cost basis. Only about 35% of coins held by recent buyers were in profit, compared with almost everyone in September, according to the coverage of the report. When most recent buyers are sitting on losses, fewer of them are eager to buy more, and some are more likely to sell to cut losses. That is a description of the market's mood, not a forecast of what happens next.

## Why June 2022 Is the Comparison — and What It Does Not Mean

The phrase that travelled furthest in the coverage was "fastest since June 2022". That month matters because it was the middle of crypto's last great deleveraging: the Terra stablecoin collapsed in May 2022, and the failures of lender Celsius and hedge fund Three Arrows Capital followed in June and July. Stablecoin supply contracted sharply then because holders were fleeing the system in a panic, redeeming stablecoins and withdrawing funds wherever they could.

The comparison is useful as a scale marker — it tells you this contraction is unusually large by the standards of the last four years — but it is not a claim that the same thing is happening now. The June 2022 contraction was driven by collapsing institutions and a stablecoin losing its peg. The November 2026 contraction, as far as the reported data shows, is driven by redemptions and ETF outflows in a market where Bitcoin is down from its October highs but no major institution has failed and no large stablecoin has lost its peg. A similar-sized number can have a very different cause, and the cause matters more than the number.

It is also worth stating plainly what stablecoin supply cannot tell you. CryptoQuant itself has cautioned against treating stablecoin supply changes as an exact timing indicator for Bitcoin. Supply is a measure of cash in the system, not a signal about what any individual coin will do next. A shrinking supply means less fuel is available for a broad rally, which is why Glassnode titled its report "Running on Empty" — but markets have recovered from liquidity squeezes before, and they have also stayed weak while supply recovered. The number describes conditions; it does not decide outcomes.

## The Mixed Signals Inside the Same Report

Glassnode's report is not one-directional, and the mixed signals are part of the story. Alongside the weak demand readings, the report noted that buy orders within 5% of Bitcoin's market price have outweighed sell orders since the 5 November sell-off — buyers are still placing orders close to the current price. It also found accumulation among two groups of larger holders: wallets holding 100 to 1,000 BTC and wallets holding 1,000 to 10,000 BTC both added coins. In other words, while the overall cash pool shrank and ETF money left, some larger holders were buying the dip with liquidity already inside the system.

That combination — shrinking outside money, steady inside buying — is exactly what "running on empty" describes. The dip is being bought, but with money that was already in crypto, not with fresh dollars entering from outside. Glassnode's own framing, as reported by Coin360, is that a return to stablecoin supply growth is the development to watch: new dollars entering the system would be the first sign that the liquidity squeeze is easing. Until then, the market is working with the cash it already has.

The Fear & Greed Index, a separate sentiment measure, sat at 28 on 12 November — in "fear" territory, close to "extreme fear" — according to KuCoin's daily market report. That is consistent with the Glassnode picture: a market where recent buyers are mostly underwater, ETF flows are negative, and the cash pool is shrinking. None of this is a prediction. It is a snapshot of conditions on 11–12 November 2026, and conditions change.

## What It Means for Indian Investors

For most Indian crypto users, the honest answer is that a global stablecoin-supply figure changes very little directly — but India has its own stablecoin quirk that makes the story worth understanding.

**USDT costs more than a dollar in India.** USDT has traded at a premium of roughly 6–8% in India in recent days, meaning Indians pay around ₹100 or more for a token designed to be worth one dollar (about ₹94 at the current exchange rate). That premium exists because demand for dollar stablecoins in India is strong relative to supply, and it can widen when global dollar liquidity tightens. If you buy USDT on an Indian exchange, the premium is part of your real cost — a cost that does not exist for a buyer in the US. It is one more reason to check the actual rupee price you are paying, rather than assuming a stablecoin costs a dollar.

**Your holdings and your exchange are unaffected.** A fall in global stablecoin supply does not change how Indian exchanges operate, how your coins are stored, or whether you can withdraw. It is a measure of the global market's cash pool, not a statement about any Indian platform. The Indian rules that matter to you — registration of exchanges with FIU-IND, KYC, and the tax framework — are unchanged by this report.

**The tax rules are unchanged too.** Gains on crypto assets in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess — 31.2% in total — with no set-off of losses against other income, and sales attract 1% TDS. That applies whether you sell Bitcoin, a stablecoin or any other crypto asset. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Bitcoin's rupee price is what you actually see.** Bitcoin traded near $74,000 on 12 November, which works out to roughly ₹69 lakh per coin at the current exchange rate. The dollar figures in Glassnode's report are global numbers; on an Indian exchange, the price you see is the dollar price converted at the rupee rate, plus any premium the exchange charges. When the rupee weakens against the dollar, Bitcoin's rupee price can rise even while its dollar price falls — another reason the headline dollar number is only half the story for an Indian holder.

## What to Watch Next

Three things, all of them data rather than predictions, will show whether the liquidity squeeze is easing or deepening.

- **Stablecoin supply itself.** Glassnode's framing makes this the headline metric: a return to growth in total stablecoin supply would mean fresh dollars are entering the system again. Continued contraction would mean the drain is still going.
- **ETF flows.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now recorded outflows in most recent sessions. A return to net inflows would be a separate, independent sign of new money arriving — but remember that ETF flows and stablecoin supply measure different channels, and the two totals cannot simply be added together.
- **Short-term-holder behaviour.** With Bitcoin below the $75,600 average cost of recent buyers, the question is whether those holders keep holding or sell to cut losses. Glassnode noted buy orders outweighing sell orders near the current price, which suggests some buyers are still active — but that balance can change quickly.

None of these is a signal to act on by itself. They are the same kind of data a weather report gives you: useful for understanding conditions, useless as a promise about tomorrow. The sensible use of a report like Glassnode's is to understand the market you are in, not to time it.

## The Bottom Line

On 11 November 2026, Glassnode reported that total stablecoin supply fell by $14.2 billion over the 30 days through 9 November — the fastest contraction since June 2022 — taking the total to just below $280 billion. The drain was driven mainly by redemptions at Tether and Circle, about $4.4 billion in a single week, and it coincided with roughly $1.6 billion of net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs and a 284,000 BTC fall in CryptoQuant's apparent-demand measure. Bitcoin traded near $74,000, below the $75,600 average cost of recent buyers, with only about 35% of their coins in profit. At the same time, buy orders near the price outweighed sell orders and larger holders were adding coins — the dip is being bought with money already inside the system, which is what "running on empty" means. For Indian investors, the practical points are the USDT premium of roughly 6–8%, the unchanged 30% tax plus 1% TDS, and the fact that none of this changes how Indian exchanges work. The number to watch next is stablecoin supply itself: growth would mean fresh dollars are returning, and continued contraction would mean the squeeze is still on.
