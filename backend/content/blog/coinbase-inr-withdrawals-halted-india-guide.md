Coinbase INR withdrawals have been halted in India for nearly a week, and on 14 November 2026 the exchange confirmed the problem publicly for the first time. Indian users trying to move rupees from Coinbase to their bank accounts are finding the option unavailable, with no confirmed date for when it will return. This guide explains what Coinbase actually said, what is broken and what still works, why a banking partner outage can freeze withdrawals on an otherwise functioning exchange, and what Indian users should do while they wait.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. This guide explains a service disruption and how INR withdrawals work; it does not recommend using, leaving or trading on any platform.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as published on 14 November 2026 by Business Today, Coinpedia, CryptoTimes and TradingView, and as stated by Coinbase India itself, are:

- **Coinbase India posted a statement on X on 14 November.** It said: "INR withdrawals are temporarily unavailable due to an outage at our banking partner. The outage is affecting the bank's payouts for all of its clients, not just Coinbase. Your funds are safe. INR deposits (including UPI) and bank account linking are working as usual. We're working with the bank around the clock to restore withdrawals as soon as possible."
- **The disruption has lasted more than five days.** Business Today, Coinpedia and CryptoTimes all report that users have been unable to withdraw INR for over five days, and that several users said on social media they have been unable to get their money out for that long. Coinbase has not given a confirmed timeline for restoring the service.
- **The problem first appeared on the status page on 9 November.** CryptoTimes reports that the incident first appeared on Coinbase's status page on 9 November 2026 at 03:07 PST (4:37 PM IST) under the title "Bank Payments Unavailable — INR (India)". A second update the same day, at 19:49 PST (9:19 AM IST on 10 November), confirmed that INR withdrawals were not going through and asked customers not to start new withdrawal requests, warning that requests made during the outage could fail or remain pending. That update also said deposits, UPI and bank account linking were working normally again.
- **The bank, not the exchange, is where the failure sits.** According to Coinbase's statement, the outage is at its banking partner and is affecting that bank's payouts for all of its clients — meaning the problem is in the payment rail that carries rupees to users' bank accounts, not in Coinbase's trading engine or its crypto wallets.
- **Coinbase is not the only platform affected this week.** Business Today reports that Unocoin suspended all deposits and withdrawals on 9 November 2026 without announcing a timeline, and that ZebPay has warned users of delays in INR withdrawals due to banking issues. The two situations are different — Unocoin's suspension follows a security breach covered in this site's Unocoin posts — but for Indian users the practical effect overlaps: getting rupees out of crypto platforms has become harder this week.
- **Coinbase only recently rebuilt its INR services.** The exchange launched direct INR deposits and withdrawals through India's Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) on 1 June 2026, alongside spot and perpetual futures trading, after registering with India's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND). The current outage lands barely five months into that relaunch.

## What Is Broken — and What Still Works

The most useful way to understand the disruption is to separate the three things an exchange does with your money, because only one of them is affected:

| Service | Status on 14 November 2026 | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| INR withdrawals to your bank | Unavailable | Rupees cannot currently move from Coinbase to your bank account. Do not start a new withdrawal request. |
| INR deposits, including UPI | Working as usual | You can still add rupees to the exchange, but remember you cannot take them back out until payouts resume. |
| Bank account linking | Working as usual | Linking a bank account for future use is unaffected. |
| Crypto trading | Not part of the outage | Buying and selling crypto on the platform is unaffected by the banking outage. |
| Crypto deposits and withdrawals | Not mentioned in the outage | Coinbase's statement covers INR bank payouts only; it says nothing about crypto transfers being affected. |

That split is the key point of the whole story. Your crypto balance on the exchange, your INR balance on the exchange, and the bank transfer that moves rupees to your account are three different things. The outage has broken the third one. That is why Coinbase can truthfully say both that "your funds are safe" and that you cannot withdraw your rupees: the money is still recorded on the platform, but the pipe that carries it to your bank is down.

## Why a Banking Partner Outage Freezes Withdrawals

For a beginner, it can seem strange that a global exchange worth billions can be stopped by one bank's outage. The reason is that an exchange does not itself move rupees. When you withdraw INR, the exchange instructs its banking partner to pay your bank account through India's payment systems — IMPS for instant transfers, and in some cases NEFT or UPI rails. The exchange holds your balance as a record; the bank holds the actual rupees and executes the payment.

If that banking partner's payout system goes down, the exchange has no other way to send the money. It cannot simply switch to another bank in an afternoon, because the banking relationship involves settlement accounts, compliance checks and technical integrations that take weeks to set up. That is also why Coinbase's statement stresses that the outage "is affecting the bank's payouts for all of its clients, not just Coinbase" — the bank's other clients are in the same position, and the fix has to come from the bank's side.

This structure is not unique to Coinbase. Indian exchanges have always depended on banking partners for INR movement, and that dependence has produced disruptions before. In September 2026, FIU-IND served notices on 15 unregistered offshore platforms and ordered their apps and websites blocked, a reminder that the banking and compliance layer around crypto in India is actively enforced. The lesson for users is a practical one: the reliability of an exchange's INR services is only as strong as its banking partner's systems, and that is a question worth asking before you deposit, not after a withdrawal fails.

## Unocoin and ZebPay: The Same Week, the Same Pressure

The Coinbase outage did not happen in isolation. This week, Indian users have faced INR problems on three platforms at once:

- **Unocoin** suspended all deposits and withdrawals on 9 November 2026. Unlike Coinbase, Unocoin's suspension follows a security breach: suspicious activity on 6 November, unauthorised server access on 9 November, all services suspended, and a server rebuild estimated at four to five working days. This site covered the breach on 12 November and the BitGo wallet recovery-feature update on 13 November. Unocoin has said it is targeting Tuesday, 17 November 2026 for resuming crypto withdrawals, while INR withdrawals are operational but slower than usual because of a disruption at its own banking partner.
- **ZebPay** has warned users of delays in INR withdrawals due to banking issues, according to Business Today and TradingView. No suspension has been announced, but users are experiencing slower payouts.
- **Coinbase** has halted INR withdrawals entirely, with no timeline.

Three platforms, three different underlying causes — a security breach, a banking outage, and withdrawal delays — but one shared pattern: the INR exit ramp from crypto to a bank account is the most fragile part of the system for Indian users. Crypto can move on a blockchain in minutes. Rupees move through banks, and banks have outages, compliance holds and partner failures. Anyone keeping significant funds on an exchange should understand that distinction before they need to withdraw in a hurry.

## The Scheduled Maintenance Tonight: Do Not Confuse the Two

There is a second Coinbase event tonight that is easy to confuse with the outage, and it is important to keep them separate. According to a separate report by CoinGabbar, based on Coinbase's own status page, scheduled system maintenance is planned for Saturday, 14 November at around 1 PM ET — 11:30 PM IST — lasting up to four hours, until around 3:30 AM IST on Sunday, 15 November. The notice says some users may see degraded performance across multiple products, does not name specific products, and does not say that trading, deposits or withdrawals will stop.

That maintenance is routine, planned work announced on 2 November, and it is not the cause of the INR withdrawal halt, which began on 9 November and is attributed to the banking partner. The two events overlap in time — the maintenance window falls inside the ongoing outage — but they are different things. If you see degraded performance tonight, it may be the maintenance. If your INR withdrawal fails, that is the banking outage. Checking the status page, rather than assuming one explains the other, is the way to tell them apart.

## What Indian Users Should Do Now

If you use Coinbase India, or you are watching this story to decide which exchange to trust with your rupees, these are the practical steps that follow from what is actually known:

1. **Do not start a new INR withdrawal request.** Coinbase's own 9 November update warned that requests made during the outage could fail or remain pending. A failed request does not necessarily mean the money is lost, but a pending one can be harder to track than a request you never made. Wait for the all-clear on the status page.
2. **Keep records of what you have.** Take screenshots of your INR balance, your crypto balances, and any withdrawal request that is already pending, including its reference number and timestamp. If you later need to raise a support ticket, those records are your evidence. Support teams resolve documented cases faster than vague ones.
3. **Do not deposit more INR to "trade through" the outage.** Deposits are working, but money you deposit now cannot be withdrawn until payouts resume. If you might need those rupees in your bank account soon, keep them there.
4. **Do not panic-sell crypto because withdrawals are stuck.** Selling crypto for INR on the platform does not solve the problem — the resulting INR balance would be stuck behind the same broken withdrawal rail. The outage affects the exit to your bank, not the value of what you hold.
5. **Follow the status page, not social media rumours.** Coinbase has said it will post updates on its status page, and its India account posted the 14 November statement on X. Unverified claims about timelines, alternative withdrawal methods or "fixes" circulating on social media should be treated with suspicion — during every exchange outage, scammers impersonate support staff and offer fake workarounds.
6. **Check whether your exchange is FIU-IND registered.** Coinbase registered with FIU-IND as part of its India relaunch, which is why it can offer INR services through Indian banking partners at all. Registration is not a guarantee that nothing will go wrong — this week proves that — but it is the baseline check for whether a platform is operating inside India's rules. This site's guide on checking FIU registration explains how to verify it.
7. **Think about how much you keep on any single exchange.** This week, three platforms had INR problems at the same time for different reasons. Keeping funds you might need soon on an exchange, rather than in your bank account or in self-custody, means accepting that risk. For long-term holdings, a hardware wallet or other self-custody removes the exchange entirely from the question of whether you can access your crypto — though it does not help you convert it to rupees, which still needs a functioning INR off-ramp.

## What This Does Not Mean

Three things are worth stating plainly, because they are easy to get wrong during an outage:

- **This is not a hack.** Coinbase attributes the halt to a banking partner outage, and no report has suggested any breach, theft or compromise of Coinbase's systems. The Unocoin breach this week is a separate incident on a different exchange, and the two should not be conflated.
- **"Funds are safe" is a specific claim, not a blanket guarantee.** It means Coinbase believes customer balances are intact and the problem is the payment rail. It does not mean withdrawals will resume tomorrow, and it does not come with a timeline. The honest reading is: your balance is recorded, the transfer is stuck, and nobody outside the bank can say when it will move.
- **The tax position is unchanged.** Nothing about this outage changes Indian crypto tax rules. Gains on crypto in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS on transfers. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## The Bottom Line

On 14 November 2026, Coinbase India confirmed that INR withdrawals are temporarily unavailable because of an outage at its banking partner — an outage affecting that bank's payouts for all of its clients, not just Coinbase. The disruption has lasted more than five days, first appearing on the status page on 9 November, and Coinbase has given no timeline for restoration. The next fact that will matter is the restoration announcement — and that will be a new event, not a repeat of this one.
