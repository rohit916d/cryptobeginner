When you want to buy crypto in India, you have two main paths. The first is a regular exchange order book — anonymous, instant, priced in INR. The second is more human: **peer-to-peer (P2P) trading**, where you deal directly with another person.

Imagine buying a second-hand bicycle online. No retail store involved — you find a seller, agree on a price, pay them directly, and they hand over the bike. Crypto P2P works on the same principle: a platform provides the meeting place and the safety tools, but the trade itself happens between two people.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice, and it is not a recommendation to use P2P trading.

## P2P vs. a Regular Exchange

| | Regular exchange | P2P marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Who you trade with | The exchange's order book (anonymous) | A specific individual |
| Price | Market price, set by supply and demand | Set by each seller/buyer — varies |
| Payment | INR deposited to the exchange | Direct: UPI, bank transfer, etc. |
| Speed | Near-instant | Minutes — depends on the humans |
| Best for | Simple buying and selling | Flexible payments, negotiating, cash-like deals |

On an exchange, the platform is your counterparty. On P2P, the platform is just the referee.

## How a P2P Trade Works, Step by Step

Let us walk through buying USDT (a popular dollar-pegged token) with INR from a seller named Priya:

1. **Browse offers.** You open the P2P marketplace and filter: Buy → USDT → INR → UPI. Each listing shows the seller's price per USDT, accepted payment methods, minimum/maximum trade size, and the seller's reputation stats.
2. **Place an order.** You choose Priya's offer — good price, 1,200+ completed trades, 98% completion rate. The moment you confirm, her USDT is **locked in escrow** by the platform. She cannot move it; you do not have it yet.
3. **Pay in INR.** You send ₹10,000 to Priya via UPI, exactly as her listing instructs, then tap "Transferred, notify seller" on the platform.
4. **Seller confirms and releases.** Priya checks her own UPI app, sees the ₹10,000 arrived, and taps "Release." The escrowed USDT flows to your wallet. Trade complete.

The whole dance usually takes 5–15 minutes when both sides are responsive.

## Escrow: The Safety Net That Makes It Work

The obvious question: what stops Priya from taking your ₹10,000 and vanishing — or you from claiming you paid when you did not?

The answer is **escrow**, a neutral holding system run by the platform:

- When a trade starts, the seller's crypto locks into escrow automatically.
- Neither party can touch it mid-trade.
- It releases to the buyer only after the seller confirms receiving payment.
- If the two sides disagree, either can open a **dispute** — platform staff review the chat log and payment evidence, then decide who gets the funds.

Escrow does not make P2P risk-free, but it removes the most basic form of theft. **Golden rule: if a deal happens outside the platform's escrow, you have zero protection.** Anyone who asks you to trade "directly, no escrow needed" is someone to walk away from.

## Payment Methods in India

Indian P2P markets typically support:

- **UPI** — fastest and most common; payments settle in seconds.
- **IMPS / NEFT bank transfers** — reliable, slightly slower.
- **Digital wallets** — where supported by the platform.

Practical notes:

- Always pay from an account in **your own name** — mismatched names are a classic dispute trigger and a money-laundering red flag.
- Follow the seller's payment instructions **exactly** (correct UPI ID, correct amount, and any reference note they require). Missing reference notes cause the majority of "payment not received" disputes.
- Keep every receipt, UPI transaction ID, and screenshot until the trade fully completes.

## Safety Rules Every Beginner Must Follow

1. **Check reputation before anything else.** Completion rate, total trades, average release time, and feedback. New accounts with zero history offering suspiciously good prices are a red flag, not a bargain.
2. **Stay inside the platform chat.** Scammers try to move you to Telegram or WhatsApp "for faster communication." Off-platform, there is no chat log for dispute resolution — and no escrow either.
3. **Verify payment yourself, in your own app.** Log into your bank or UPI app and confirm the money arrived. Screenshots can be faked in seconds; your own app cannot lie to you.
4. **Never release early.** As a seller, release crypto from escrow only after funds are confirmed in your account — never on a promise, screenshot, or "payment is on the way."
5. **Beware of overpayment tricks.** A buyer "accidentally" sends extra and asks for a refund plus the crypto. The original payment then bounces or gets reversed. Cancel and re-do cleanly, or walk away.

## Common P2P Scams to Know

- **Fake payment screenshots** — doctored UPI/bank images pressuring you to release early.
- **Chargeback fraud** — buyer pays, receives crypto, then reverses the bank transfer. Favour UPI/IMPS (hard to reverse) and established platforms with dispute systems.
- **Phishing links** — "support agents" sending login links over chat. Real platform staff never ask for passwords or seed phrases.
- **Off-platform "better rates"** — anyone offering a deal too good to be true outside escrow is running the oldest trick in the book.

## One More Thing: Taxes Still Apply

A common myth is that P2P trades are "off the books." In India, crypto-to-crypto and crypto-to-INR transactions are taxable events regardless of where they happen — currently a **30% tax on gains** plus applicable surcharge and cess, with **1% TDS** on transfers above thresholds. P2P does not exempt you. Keep records of every trade: dates, amounts in INR, and transaction IDs. This is educational information, not tax advice — speak to a qualified tax professional about your situation.

## The Big Picture

P2P trading is crypto at its most human — real people, real payment apps, real negotiation, with escrow as the referee. Used carefully on reputable platforms, it is a legitimate way to move between INR and crypto. Used carelessly, it is where beginners get burned.
