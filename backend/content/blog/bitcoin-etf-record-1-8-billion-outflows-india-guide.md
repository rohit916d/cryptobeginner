# Bitcoin ETF Outflows: $1.8B Leaves in a Record Week

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.79 billion in a single week — the third-largest weekly outflow since these funds began trading in January 2024. The redemptions, reported on 23 November 2026 from SoSoValue data, marked the fourth consecutive week of outflows and pushed November's total to about $4.14 billion, already the second-biggest monthly outflow of the year. Bitcoin ETF outflows on this scale are worth understanding properly, because the headlines can make them sound simpler than they are: a big number leaving does not automatically mean the market is finished, and a record week does not automatically mean the next week will look the same. This guide explains what happened, why the figures are record-scale, what ETF flows actually tell you, and what they mean — and do not mean — for investors in India.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains reported ETF flow data; it does not recommend any token, investment or action, and it does not predict any price movement.

## What Happened: $1.79 Billion Left in One Week

In the week ending 20 November 2026, investors withdrew a net $1.79 billion from the 12 US-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs, according to SoSoValue data carried by The Block, Cointelegraph, CryptoBriefing and Bloomberg. The figures agree across outlets, and the scale is what makes this week stand out:

| Rank among weekly outflows since Jan 2024 | 3rd largest | Cointelegraph; record is $2.61B (week ended 28 Feb 2025) |

| Year-to-date net flows | Roughly $3.8 billion in net outflows | Cointelegraph |

The week was not a single bad day. The funds saw outflows on four straight trading sessions through 20 November, totalling about $1.8 billion, and the last session was the biggest of the month: $669.16 million left on Friday 20 November alone, of which $544.73 million came from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT). One day earlier, IBIT had already set a record with $541.1 million in outflows — beating its previous daily high of $528.3 million from 30 January — and then broke that record again the next day. The two-day total from IBIT alone was $1.09 billion.

Two details in the data deserve attention because they change how the story reads. First, the November total is reported slightly differently by different data providers: SoSoValue-based reporting puts it at about $4.14 billion through 20 November, while some trackers cited by CryptoBriefing estimated $3.47–3.59 billion over the same period. The difference comes from how providers count and time the flows, not from a disagreement about the direction — every source agrees the month is the second-worst of 2026, about 8% below June's record of $4.51 billion. Second, even after this run of redemptions, cumulative inflows into the US spot Bitcoin ETFs since launch remain above $52 billion, according to Hokanews. The funds have given back a meaningful share of the money that came in, but they are nowhere near empty.

## Why This Week Counts as Record-Scale

A $1.79 billion outflow is a big number on its own, but three comparisons are what make it a record story rather than a routine bad week.

**It is the third-largest weekly outflow in the ETFs' history.** The funds have only been trading since January 2024, so the history is short — but within it, only two weeks were worse: the week ended 28 February 2025, when $2.61 billion left, and the week ended 26 June 2026, which was about $2 million larger than last week. Cointelegraph's framing is precise here: last week was the third-largest weekly total since launch, and the largest since June. The Block put it slightly differently — the largest weekly withdrawal since the week ended 26 June 2026 — which is the same fact from the other side.

**It is the fourth consecutive week of outflows.** A single week of redemptions can be noise — a large holder rebalancing, a fund taking profits, a short-term reaction to a headline. Four straight weeks, totalling about $4.34 billion, is a pattern. Analysts quoted by The Block went further, describing the outflows as becoming "structural" rather than a one-off reaction — a view that the selling reflects a sustained shift in positioning rather than a single event. That is an analyst's interpretation, not a fact about the data, and it is worth labelling it as such: the flows are real, the word "structural" is someone's reading of them.

**It is happening alongside the worst November since 2018.** Bitcoin fell for a seventh consecutive week — its longest losing streak since the Terra-Luna collapse in 2022 — and was heading for its worst November since 2018, with the price down about 25% during the month. The ETF outflows and the price fall are part of the same market move, and the price context is what connects the flow data to the charts most readers are watching.

## BlackRock's IBIT Led the Selling

If one fund explains the week, it is BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust. IBIT is the largest US spot Bitcoin ETF by assets, and it accounted for about $1.37 billion of the week's $1.79 billion in outflows — roughly 76% of the total, according to Cointelegraph. The fund's daily records tell the story in miniature: $541.1 million left on Thursday 19 November, a new single-day record for the fund, and then $544.7 million left on Friday 20 November, breaking the record again within 24 hours.

That concentration matters for how to read the data. When the largest fund dominates the outflows, the weekly total is less a broad-based verdict from every ETF investor and more a reflection of what the biggest holders are doing. It also means the headline number can move sharply if a small number of large IBIT holders change their minds. Bloomberg's reporting made the same point from the other side: the exodus was removing a key source of support for Bitcoin, because these ETFs had been one of the main channels through which new money reached the market during the rally. When that channel reverses, the market loses a buyer at exactly the moment sellers are active.

It is also worth noting what IBIT's outflows do not show. They do not show that every investor is selling — the same week, some holders were buying the dip, and Bitcoin steadied above $61,000 by Monday 23 November after touching about $55,254 on Saturday, its weakest level since September 2024. Outflows are a net figure: they are what is left after inflows and outflows are added up. A large net outflow can hide substantial buying underneath it, just as a small net inflow can hide substantial selling.

## Bitcoin's Seventh Straight Weekly Fall

The price backdrop is the other half of the story, and it is the part most Indian readers will have felt directly. Bitcoin dropped as low as $55,254 on Saturday 22 November — its lowest since September 2024 — before steadying on Monday to hold just above $61,000 in Asian trading, according to Bloomberg data carried by Alpha Maven. In rupee terms, Bitcoin was trading around ₹57 lakh on 23 November, after falling below $57,000 during the weekend sell-off.

The weekly losing streak is now seven weeks, the longest since the Terra-Luna collapse in 2022. For the month, Bitcoin is down about 25% in November, which would make it the worst November since 2018. The CoinMarketCap Fear and Greed Index stood at just 8 on 23 November — deep in "extreme fear" territory — and the market recorded about $226 million in 24-hour liquidations that day.

What drove the fall is broader than crypto. The sell-off has been linked to concerns over AI-related investments, rising borrowing costs and weakness in broader financial markets — the same forces that pulled down technology stocks globally. US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost close to $1.8 billion last week in that context, and November outflows reached about $4.1 billion, the third straight week above $1 billion. The ETF flows did not cause the market fall on their own, and the market fall did not cause the ETF flows on their own: both are reactions to the same shift in risk appetite, feeding each other in the short term.

This is also the part of the story that connects to this site's recent coverage. The two-year low and the whipsaw back above $60,000 were covered in detail in the Bitcoin $55,000 guide, and the $643 million single-day ETF outflow that opened this flow story was covered on 2 November. What is new this week is not the price level — Bitcoin has been near these levels before — but the scale and persistence of the outflows: a record week, a fourth straight week, and a monthly total approaching June's record.

## What ETF Flows Actually Tell You — and What They Don't

ETF flow data is genuinely useful, but it is easy to over-read. A short explainer on the mechanics helps.

**How the flows work.** A spot Bitcoin ETF holds actual Bitcoin and issues shares that trade on a stock exchange. When investors buy shares, the fund's authorised participants create new shares and buy the underlying Bitcoin; when investors sell, shares are redeemed and Bitcoin is sold. Net outflows mean more shares were redeemed than created over the period. The flow number is therefore a real measure of money moving in or out of the funds — but it is a measure of the funds, not of the whole Bitcoin market. Most Bitcoin still trades on crypto exchanges, over the counter and on-chain, outside the ETF system entirely.

**What flows are good at.** Flows are a clean, daily, public record of institutional and adviser-driven demand. Because ETF investors are disproportionately institutions, wealth managers and retirement accounts, sustained outflows are a reasonable signal that professional money is reducing exposure. Four straight weeks of outflows, with the largest fund leading, is a clearer signal than a single day's number — it shows the selling is not one fund's one-off decision.

**What flows are bad at.** Flows do not predict prices. A week of record outflows can be followed by a strong rebound — Bitcoin did exactly that on Monday, steadying above $61,000 after the weekend low — and a week of record inflows can be followed by a fall. Flows are also a lagging indicator in one important sense: redemptions happen after investors have decided to sell, not before. Treating a flow record as a price forecast is the single most common mistake in reading this data, and it is the mistake this guide is designed to prevent.

**The "structural" debate.** Analysts quoted by The Block described the outflows as becoming "structural", meaning the selling reflects a durable change in how investors position in Bitcoin rather than a temporary reaction. That reading is plausible — four weeks and $4.34 billion is a lot of money to dismiss as noise — but it is not proven by the data alone. Flows have reversed sharply before, including earlier in 2026, and the same analysts would have to revise their view if next week's data showed inflows. Treat "structural" as a hypothesis to watch, not as a conclusion.

## What This Means for Indian Investors

For readers in India, the direct impact of US ETF flows is indirect — but real, and worth spelling out without exaggeration.

**The price channel is the main one.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs are one of the largest sources of demand for Bitcoin globally. When they redeem shares at record pace, that selling pressure reaches the global Bitcoin price, and the global price reaches Indian exchanges within minutes. The ₹57 lakh level Bitcoin traded at on 23 November is the Indian expression of the same move that produced the $1.79 billion outflow figure. Indian investors do not need to hold ETF shares to feel the effect.

**The sentiment channel matters too.** ETF flows are one of the most-watched gauges of institutional sentiment in crypto. When flows turn persistently negative, it often coincides with cautious coverage, lower trading volumes and weaker sentiment among retail investors as well. The Fear and Greed Index reading of 8 on 23 November is the retail-facing version of the same mood. Neither number is a reason to buy or sell on its own — they are context, not instructions.

**You almost certainly cannot buy these ETFs directly.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs are listed on American stock exchanges, not on Indian exchanges, so most Indian investors cannot buy them through a regular Indian brokerage or crypto app. Some investors use international brokerage accounts under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, but that route has its own limits, paperwork and tax rules, and it is not the same as buying crypto on an Indian exchange. For most Indian readers, the practical value of ETF flow data is as a gauge of institutional sentiment in the world's largest crypto market — not as a product to buy.

**The tax rules have not changed.** Any crypto gains in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus cess, with 1% TDS on transfers above a threshold and no set-off of losses against other income. That applies whether you bought Bitcoin on an Indian exchange, held it in a wallet, or accessed it through any other route. Tax note: the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**What to watch next.** The next weekly flow figures, due after the coming week's trading, will show whether the four-week outflow streak extends to five or reverses. The monthly total will also be watched: if November closes near $4.5 billion, it would overtake June as the worst month on record for these funds. Neither outcome would, on its own, tell you what Bitcoin's price will do — but a reversal would weaken the "structural" reading, and an extension would strengthen it. That is the honest way to use this data: as one input among many, updated weekly, and never as a prediction.

## The Bottom Line

In the week ending 20 November 2026, US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.79 billion — the third-largest weekly outflow since the funds launched in January 2024, the largest since June, and the fourth consecutive week of redemptions. November's total reached about $4.14 billion, the second-biggest month of the year, and BlackRock's IBIT accounted for about three-quarters of the week's selling, setting new single-day outflow records on two consecutive days. Bitcoin fell for a seventh straight week and touched about $55,254, its lowest since September 2024, before steadying above $61,000 on Monday. The flows are a real, record-scale signal that institutional money has been leaving — but they are a measure of what has already happened, not a forecast of what happens next. For Indian investors, the lesson is the same as after every market move this year: understand the data, know the tax rules, and do not let a single headline — however large the number — make a decision for you.
