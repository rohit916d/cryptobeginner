On Friday 13 November 2026, the last major US inflation report before the Federal Reserve's December meeting arrived — and it was cooler than Wall Street expected. October's Producer Price Index (PPI) rose 0.3% month over month, against a 0.4% forecast, and Bitcoin, which had fallen below $70,000 earlier in the day, rose about 1% in the hour after the release and climbed back toward $70,000. It was a small move in a week of large ones, but it mattered for a specific reason: three days earlier, the other big inflation report — the Consumer Price Index — had come in hotter than expected and pushed Bitcoin down. This guide explains what the PPI actually showed, what PPI is and why it moves crypto, how the market reacted, and what it means for Indian users.

This guide explains a published inflation report and the market's reaction to it; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset, and it does not predict where prices will go.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 13 November 2026 by the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) and covered the same day by CoinDesk, Crypto Briefing, The Coin Republic, Coindoo and BeInCrypto, are:

- **Headline PPI rose 0.3% in October, below the 0.4% forecast.** The consensus forecast compiled by Dow Jones and reported by CNBC was 0.4%. The actual print was a tenth of a point cooler, and a sharp deceleration from September's jump of about 1.3%.
- **The annual rate rose to 6.5%, also slightly below forecast.** Economists had expected 6.6%, and September's annual rate was 6.3%. At 6.5%, the yearly increase is the highest since November 2022 — so the report was cooler than expected in the monthly column, but the annual figure still climbed.
- **Core PPI was exactly in line with forecasts.** The core measure, which strips out food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month and 5.0% from a year earlier — both matching expectations, and the annual core rate unchanged from September.
- **A narrower core measure stayed warm.** Producer prices excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% for a second consecutive month, a detail that kept the report from being read as an outright soft print.
- **Bitcoin fell below $70,000 before the release, then bounced.** Earlier on Friday, Bitcoin fell to a low near $67,652 on CoinDesk's data, slipping below its 200-week moving average of around $68,000. In the hour after the 13:30 UTC release, Bitcoin rose roughly 1% — about 1.35% on The Coin Republic's reading — and traded around $69,500 to just above $70,000, depending on the snapshot.
- **Short sellers were caught out.** About $39 million in short positions was liquidated within an hour of the release, according to CoinGlass data reported by BeInCrypto, including one trader whose roughly $52 million bet on falling prices was left just 2% from forced liquidation.
- **Rate-hike bets eased slightly, but did not reverse.** After the release, the odds of a larger 50-basis-point December hike came down, while a 25-basis-point hike remains priced in, according to Crypto Briefing. CoinDesk's live coverage noted that the data would do nothing to push the Fed off its hawkish bias heading into December, and the 10-year Treasury yield stayed above 5%.

Two points deserve emphasis because they are easy to misread. First, "cooler than expected" does not mean inflation is falling. The annual rate rose to 6.5%, its highest in almost four years. What was cooler was the monthly pace — 0.3% instead of 0.4% — after September's unusually large jump. Second, the Bitcoin bounce was modest: about 1%, against a fall of more than 6% over the previous 24 hours and roughly 16% over the past month. The PPI print paused the slide; it did not reverse the week.

## What Is the PPI, and Why Does It Move Crypto?

The Producer Price Index is one of the two headline inflation reports the United States publishes each month, and for a beginner it is worth understanding what it actually measures — because the name alone does not explain why crypto traders watch it so closely.

PPI measures the prices that US businesses receive for the goods and services they sell — the wholesale level of the economy. When a factory sells steel, when a farm sells wheat, when a logistics company charges for freight, those prices feed into PPI. The Consumer Price Index, by contrast, measures the prices households pay at the shop. PPI is upstream; CPI is downstream. Because businesses usually pass their costs on to consumers with a delay, PPI often moves first, which is why traders treat it as an early signal of where consumer inflation may be heading.

The Federal Reserve watches both reports, but for a different reason than most people assume. The Fed does not set policy from a single month's number. It watches the direction of inflation against its target, the strength of the jobs market, and the state of financial conditions. A hot inflation print strengthens the case for keeping rates higher for longer, or even raising them. A cool print weakens that case. And interest rates are the bridge to Bitcoin: when rates are expected to stay high, government bonds pay more, the dollar tends to strengthen, and assets that pay no yield — Bitcoin among them — become relatively less attractive. When rate expectations ease, the reverse happens.

That chain is exactly what played out on 13 November. The monthly PPI print came in a tenth of a point below forecast, which trimmed expectations of a larger December hike. The dollar eased, Treasury yields edged lower, and Bitcoin — which had been falling all week on the opposite expectation — bounced. The move was small because the relief was small: the annual rate still rose, the narrower core measure stayed warm at 0.5%, and the market still expects a rate hike in December. But the direction of the surprise, not its size, is what moved the price in the first hour.

It is also worth knowing what PPI does not do. It does not measure crypto prices, it does not reflect Indian inflation, and it is not a forecast of anything. It is a backward-looking measurement of one month of US business prices, released with a lag. Traders react to the surprise relative to the forecast, not to the number in isolation — which is why a 0.3% print can lift markets when the forecast was 0.4%, even though 0.3% is still a meaningful monthly rise.

## The Week the PPI Landed In

The PPI print did not arrive in a calm market. It arrived at the end of a week in which almost every driver had pointed the same way — down — and understanding that week is what makes the small bounce intelligible.

Bitcoin entered Friday below $72,000, having fallen through the $72,000–$73,200 support zone near its 200-day moving average earlier in the week. The proximate trigger was corporate, not economic: Nvidia's chief executive Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November, and the AI-stock selloff that followed dragged crypto with it, pushing Bitcoin below $72,000 for the first time since 20 August. That story is covered in this site's 13 November post on the resignation.

Then came the macro layer. On 10 November, October CPI had come in hot — 0.4% against a 0.3% forecast — which pushed rate-hike expectations up and Bitcoin down toward $73,600. The dollar climbed above 103, its highest since April 2025, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose above 5%. Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $1.44 billion in outflows over five sessions through 11 November, and more than $1.3 billion in leveraged positions were liquidated in 24 hours as Bitcoin broke $70,000. The Crypto Fear and Greed Index fell to 20, labelled "extreme fear", its lowest since July.

By Friday morning, Bitcoin had fallen as low as roughly $67,652, below its 200-week moving average of around $68,000 — a level CoinDesk's live coverage flagged as a widely watched long-term line. Perpetual futures funding rates turned negative for the first time in a month, according to Glassnode data, meaning traders were paying to hold short positions. In that context, the PPI print was the first piece of news in the week that pointed even slightly in the other direction — and the market's reaction, a roughly 1% bounce and about $39 million in short liquidations within an hour, was the mechanical result: traders who had bet heavily on further falls were forced to close their positions, which itself pushed the price up a little further.

That sequence is worth understanding because it is a pattern, not a one-off. A market that has fallen hard on one expectation is primed to bounce on the first data point that challenges it — and equally primed to fall again on the next data point that confirms the original fear. The PPI bounce and the CPI fall three days earlier are two halves of the same mechanism: inflation data moves rate expectations, rate expectations move the dollar and yields, and the dollar and yields move Bitcoin. The direction of the surprise decides the direction of the move.

## What to Watch Next

Five things will show whether 13 November was the day the slide paused, or simply the day it paused briefly — and none of them is a prediction.

- **The December Fed meeting on 8–9 December.** The PPI was the last major inflation report before that meeting, and the market still expects a 25-basis-point hike, with the odds of a larger 50-basis-point move now lower than before the release. The meeting itself, and the Fed's updated projections, will matter more than any single data point.
- **Whether Bitcoin holds above $70,000.** The bounce brought Bitcoin back to around $69,500–$70,200, depending on the snapshot. CoinDesk's live coverage noted that the data would not push the Fed off its hawkish bias, and the 10-year yield stayed above 5%. A level is only a level; what matters is whether buyers defend it over the coming days.
- **ETF flows.** Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $1.44 billion in outflows over five sessions through 11 November, with BlackRock's IBIT losing more than $2 billion over nine straight sessions. Flows are a slower-moving signal than a single data release, and they will show whether institutions are returning or still stepping back.
- **The next inflation prints.** November CPI and PPI arrive in December, and the November PPI is scheduled for 15 December. The October prints pointed in opposite directions — CPI hot, PPI cool — so the November pair will be watched for which direction the trend is actually going.
- **The dollar and yields.** The Dollar Index above 103 and the 10-year yield above 5% were the two macro weights on Bitcoin all week. If either eases, the pressure on crypto eases with it; if either rises further, the pressure returns. Both are driven by the same rate expectations the PPI print nudged.

None of these is a signal to act on by itself. They are the facts to watch while the week settles, and they are the facts that will determine whether 13 November is remembered as the day a cooler PPI print steadied the market, or as a brief pause in a longer slide.

## What This Means for Indian Users

For Indian users, the PPI story lands in rupees, and the arithmetic is straightforward. Bitcoin trading around $69,500–$70,200 translates to roughly ₹65–66 lakh at recent exchange rates, and the day's low near $67,652 was roughly ₹63.5 lakh. Those are the prices Indian exchanges were quoting through the day, and the bounce after the PPI release showed up in the INR price just as it did in the dollar price — because the INR price is the dollar price converted, not a separate market.

The practical points for Indian users are the same ones that apply to every US-data-driven move. First, US inflation data moves Bitcoin because it moves US rate expectations, and US rate expectations move the dollar and global risk appetite. An Indian user does not need to follow the BLS release calendar to understand their portfolio, but knowing that a single US report can move Bitcoin by 1% in an hour — and by 6% in a day, as it did earlier in the week — explains why prices sometimes swing on news that has nothing to do with India.

Second, the tax position is unchanged. Crypto gains in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS on transfers, and a US inflation print changes none of that. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. What the PPI print does affect is the size of any gain or loss being taxed, because it moved the price — which is a reminder that tax is calculated on the rupee value of the transaction, and that keeping records of buy and sell prices matters more than following any single data release.

Third, the week's lesson about leverage applies directly to Indian users. More than $1.3 billion in leveraged positions were liquidated in 24 hours as Bitcoin broke $70,000, and a further $39 million in shorts was liquidated within an hour of the PPI release. Liquidation is what happens when a leveraged bet moves against the trader and the exchange closes it automatically. The PPI bounce liquidated the shorts; the CPI fall three days earlier liquidated the longs. Both sides of leveraged trading were caught in the same week, which is the normal outcome when volatility is high and leverage is involved. For a beginner, the takeaway is simple: leverage amplifies moves in both directions, and a 1% bounce can be as dangerous as a 1% fall if the position is large enough.

Fourth, be careful about the commentary that follows a print like this. A cooler PPI will produce headlines claiming the Fed is done hiking, just as the hot CPI produced headlines claiming a half-point hike was coming. Both were over-readings of a single month's data. The Federal Reserve decides on the basis of many reports over many months, and a tenth of a point in one monthly PPI reading is a small piece of a large picture. The right response to a data release is to understand what it measured and how the market reacted — not to treat it as a verdict on where prices are going.

Bitcoin, which had fallen below $70,000 earlier in the day to a low near $67,652, rose about 1% in the hour after the release and traded around $69,500 to just above $70,000, while about $39 million in short positions was liquidated within an hour. The odds of a larger 50-basis-point December hike came down, but a 25-basis-point hike remains priced in, the 10-year yield stayed above 5%, and the annual inflation rate still rose to its highest since November 2022. The print was cooler than expected in the monthly column, not a turn in the inflation trend — a pause in a week-long slide, not a reversal of it. For Indian users, the price in rupees moved with the dollar price to roughly ₹65–66 lakh, the 30% tax and 1% TDS are unchanged, and the week's real lesson is about leverage: both long and short bets were liquidated within days of each other, which is what high volatility does to leveraged positions. What happens next depends on the December Fed meeting, ETF flows, the November inflation prints, and the dollar — none of which can be known in advance.
