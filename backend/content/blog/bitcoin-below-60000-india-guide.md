# Bitcoin Below $60,000: What the Break Means in India

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday, 20 November 2026, for the first time since July — and it did not stop there. By the afternoon in the United States, the largest cryptocurrency had dropped below $59,000, putting it within about 2% of its June–July bear-market low of roughly $57,700, according to CoinDesk. The entire August–September rebound to $87,000 has now been erased. Ether fell about 7% to around $1,626, XRP, Solana and BNB lost 5–6% each, and about $1.5 billion of leveraged crypto bets were liquidated in 24 hours. This guide explains what happened on 20 November, why the selling spread from AI stocks to gold to crypto, what the $57,700 level is, and what — if anything — the break below $60,000 means for an Indian reader.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported market event; it does not recommend any token, investment or action, and it does not predict any price movement.

## What Happened on 20 November

The day began with Bitcoin already under pressure. In Asian trading, it slid to nearly $61,200 as the technology sell-off spread from the United States, then recovered briefly above $61,700, according to CoinDesk's markets coverage. That recovery did not hold. As the US session developed, selling picked up across crypto, stocks and gold, and Bitcoin fell below the $59,000 mark. Channel News Asia reported Bitcoin last down 3.45% at $59,381, with Ether down 5.23% at $1,648.91, and described it as the first break below $60,000 since July.

The scale of the move is best understood through what it erased. Bitcoin had climbed to about $87,000 in the August–September rebound, a rally that followed the summer slide to roughly $57,700. That whole rebound is now gone. CoinDesk put it plainly: with Bitcoin less than 2% away from a fresh 2026 low, the idea that the bear market had already bottomed at $57,700 this summer "is looking increasingly premature". That is a description of how the market is behaving, not a forecast — but it captures why Friday's session mattered more than an ordinary down day.

The damage was broad. Ether fell about 7% to $1,625.69, according to CoinDesk, while XRP, Solana and BNB lost 5–6% each. Hyperliquid's HYPE token slid 8%, and Zcash — one of the market's strongest performers in recent months — plunged 14%. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 9, in "Extreme Fear" territory, according to CoinMarketCap. This was the third straight down day for Bitcoin, following record withdrawals from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust the day before: $541.1 million in a single day, the largest since the fund launched in January 2024, as this site covered in its previous post.

## The AI Trade Unwound — and Crypto Went With It

The trigger for Friday's selling was not a crypto event. It was the rapid unwinding of Wall Street's artificial-intelligence trade, and it began with Anthropic — the company behind the Claude AI assistant.

Anthropic's shares had debuted on the Nasdaq on Thursday, 19 November, priced at $83 and opening at $111.50. By Friday, the stock had plunged more than 14% to about $84.64, erasing essentially all of the gains from its IPO price. The semiconductor ETF known as SOXX sank 8%, and the Nasdaq dropped 3.8%, with both hitting their weakest levels since April. The selling was not limited to software: Bitcoin miners that have tied their fortunes to the AI data-centre boom were hit even harder. Hut 8 tumbled nearly 15%, while Core Scientific, CleanSpark and Bitdeer lost more than 13% each, and IREN, Cipher Mining and TeraWulf fell roughly 12–13%.

Why would an AI stock's bad day move Bitcoin? Because the two trades had become linked in investors' minds and portfolios. For much of 2026, the same money that bought AI stocks also bought crypto, and the same story — that AI infrastructure spending would keep growing — supported both. When that story cracked, investors sold both at once. Channel News Asia listed the same drivers: lofty valuation concerns weighing on the AI trade, rising borrowing costs, regulatory risks following the Democrats' win in the US midterm elections, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's departure earlier in the month. This site has covered each of those threads separately — the Anthropic IPO rotation theory, the Nvidia resignation, the midterm results — and on Friday they arrived together.

The selling spread further than stocks and crypto. Gold dropped another 2% toward $3,500 an ounce, its lowest price since September 2025. That is unusual: gold is normally the asset investors buy when they sell everything else. When gold falls on the same day as stocks and crypto, it tells you something about the nature of the selling — investors were not rotating from one asset to another. They were raising cash.

## "Cash Is King": The Liquidity Story

That cash rush is the second half of Friday's story, and several market strategists quoted by CoinDesk framed it as a liquidity problem rather than a crypto problem.

Maxime Seiler, co-founder and CEO of trading firm STS Digital, called it "a one-sided risk-off, with AI and tech leading it", and said the place to hide was cash — two-year US Treasuries at around 4.7%, or Swiss francs. He added that cash "gives you the optionality to buy the correction instead of riding it", and that "it's good to have options". The point is straightforward: when everything is falling at once, holding cash is the one position that does not lose value that day, and it leaves an investor able to act later.

Scott Freeman, a market strategist at Delphi Digital, went further, arguing that the broad sell-off reflects a shortage of liquidity — the amount of readily available money in the financial system — that leaves markets exposed when investors rush to sell. "This isn't just a crypto story or an AI story. It's a liquidity story," Freeman said. "The margin of safety offered by liquidity is low, so when something breaks there's no cushion, and bitcoin, the AI names and gold all get hit on the same day." That framing also explains why the selling hit assets that have little in common: when liquidity is thin, a rush to sell in one market can spill into others because buyers are scarce everywhere at once.

The same liquidity shortage shows up in the numbers this site has tracked all month. Stablecoin supply — the dollars sitting on the sidelines in crypto, ready to buy — has contracted by $14.2 billion in 30 days, the fastest since June 2022, according to Glassnode's "Running on Empty" report covered here on 12 November. US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now seen about $3.47 billion in net outflows in November, with the 2026 total flipping to net-negative, as covered in the BlackRock IBIT post. And Bitcoin's open interest — the total value of outstanding futures bets — fell to $18 billion from $20 billion a week earlier, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk. Each of those figures is a different way of saying the same thing: there is less money standing by to buy, so selling moves prices further and faster.

## Fed Rate-Hike Fears Added to the Pressure

The third driver on Friday was the Federal Reserve. Fed Governor Christopher Waller earlier in the week floated the idea of a 50-basis-point rate hike at the December meeting — or at least a signal that rates could rise at a faster pace — to combat stubborn US inflation. That came after a hotter-than-expected October CPI print, which this site covered on 10 November, and it pushed the market's pricing of a December hike to near-certainty: CME FedWatch put the odds at 93.5% for a hike, as noted in the IBIT post.

Higher interest rates matter for crypto in two ways. First, they raise the return on holding cash — two-year Treasuries now pay around 4.7% — which makes a volatile asset like Bitcoin less attractive by comparison. Second, they raise borrowing costs across the economy, which squeezes the leveraged bets that crypto markets run on. Freeman at Delphi Digital warned that the Fed could end up raising rates just as the economy starts to slow: "Financial conditions are tightening right as our leading indicators for growth are rolling over, and the Fed still looks set to hike into it. That's the setup for a policy error." The next Federal Reserve meeting is on 8–9 December, and it is the date the market is now watching most closely.

## $1.5 Billion in Liquidations: How the Cascade Works

Friday's selling was amplified by liquidations — the automatic closing of leveraged positions — and understanding the mechanism explains why a bad day can turn into a much worse one.

A leveraged position is a bet made with borrowed money: a trader puts up a small amount of their own cash and borrows the rest, so a small price move becomes a large gain or loss. When the price moves against the bet far enough, the exchange automatically closes the position to protect itself. That forced sale pushes the price down further, which triggers the next set of automatic closures, and so on. It is a chain reaction, and it is the same mechanism this site explained in the liquidation-cascade guide of 29 October and the short-squeeze guide of 5 November — only in reverse.

On Friday, roughly $1.5 billion in leveraged crypto positions was liquidated over 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk, with most of it coming from traders who had bet on prices rising. Earlier in the day, CoinDesk's markets coverage put the figure at $718.6 million over 24 hours, with 86.2% of it from long positions, and the largest single liquidation a $9.3 million Bitcoin position on Bybit. The two figures describe different windows of the same day — the total grew as the US session's selling deepened. For comparison, the liquidation clusters this site has covered recently were about $1.3 billion on 13–14 November, about $1.9 billion on the weekend of 14–15 November, and about $2 billion at the $62,006 low on 15 November. Friday's $1.5 billion sits in the same range: large, but not the largest of the month, and driven by the same mechanism.

The key point for a beginner is that liquidations are not caused by a single person's decision. They are built into the way leveraged trading works: when prices fall fast enough, the system closes positions automatically, and those closures become selling pressure of their own. That is why a market can fall further and faster than the original news would seem to justify — and why the size of the liquidation cluster is often a better measure of a day's damage than the price move alone.

## What This Means for Indian Readers

For most Indian readers, the direct effect of Bitcoin falling below $60,000 is the same as any sharp market move: it changes the price on the screen, and it changes nothing else about how crypto works.

The price Indians actually pay is higher than the dollar figures in headlines. Gadgets 360's India price read put Bitcoin at roughly ₹57.1 lakh as the $60,000 level loomed. At the 19 November fix of The Crypto Times India Premium Index, Bitcoin cost about 12.27% more in India than the global price, as noted in the IBIT post — a premium that exists because moving money into and out of Indian exchanges is constrained by banking rules, taxes and limited liquidity. So when headlines say "$59,000", an Indian buyer is typically looking at a rupee price several lakh higher than a straight currency conversion would suggest. That gap is worth knowing about before comparing Indian prices with global ones.

The tax reality is unchanged. Gains on transferring virtual digital assets in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess — 31.2% in total — with no set-off of losses, and sales attract 1% TDS under Section 194S. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. A price fall does not change Indian tax, does not create an exemption, and does not change which platforms an Indian resident is allowed to use. It also does not change the fact that losses cannot be set off against other income under current rules — a detail that matters more, not less, in a falling market.

There is also a practical issue Indian users should check this week. INR withdrawals from several exchanges operating in India have been delayed this month: Coinbase India first reported a banking-partner outage on 14 November and said on 20 November that it was processing pending payments through an alternative banking channel, CoinDCX warned on 19 November that withdrawals were taking four to six hours or longer, and ZebPay flagged similar delays on 11 November. Gadgets 360 reported the three-exchange disruption on 20 November. Anyone moving rupees should check their exchange's current status before starting a transfer, and should not assume a withdrawal will complete at its usual speed.

## The Scam Warning

Sharp price falls are exactly when crypto scams multiply, because fear and urgency make people act quickly. Expect messages claiming that a particular coin is "the next Bitcoin", that a crash is "the last chance to buy before $100,000", or that a well-known exchange or personality is giving away crypto to "help" holders. None of that is real. Expect also messages posing as exchange support, offering to "recover" losses or "unlock" a withdrawal for a fee, and asking for a seed phrase, password or OTP. No legitimate exchange or support team will ever ask for those.

A related warning applies to the $57,700 level itself. As the price approaches a widely watched level, social media fills with confident claims about what will happen next — that the level "must hold", or that breaking it "proves" a much bigger fall. Those claims are guesses dressed up as analysis, and this guide makes no such claim: the level is a historical reference point, not a prediction. Treat any message that tells you what the price will do next — in either direction — as noise, and report fraud on India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the 1930 helpline. Never share a seed phrase or password with anyone claiming to help.

## What to Watch Next

Four things will show where this sell-off goes from here, and none of them is a price prediction.

The first is the Federal Reserve meeting on 8–9 December. The market has priced in a hike at near-certainty, with the open question being its size — 25 or 50 basis points — after Waller's comments. What the Fed actually does, and what it signals about January, will matter more for crypto than any single day's price move, because rates set the return on the cash that investors are currently fleeing to.

The second is the $57,700 level itself. It is the low from June and July, and Bitcoin was within about 2% of it on Friday. Whether the price holds above it, tests it, or breaks through it is a fact that will be visible in the coming days — and it will be reported as a fact, not forecast here. What matters for readers is simply that the level exists and is being watched.

The third is ETF flows. November's $3.47 billion in net outflows, including the record $541.1 million IBIT day, is the institutional side of the same story: money leaving the funds. If outflows slow or reverse, that will show up in the daily Farside Investors data before it shows up anywhere else, and this site will cover it when it happens.

The fourth is the AI trade itself. Anthropic's debut collapse, the 8% fall in chip stocks and the 12–15% falls in AI-linked Bitcoin miners were the trigger for Friday's selling. Whether those stocks stabilise or keep falling will tell you whether the pressure on crypto is easing or building — because the two trades, for now, are moving together.

On Friday, 20 November 2026, Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July, dropping under $59,000 and coming within about 2% of its June–July bear-market low of roughly $57,700. The trigger was the unwinding of Wall Street's AI trade — Anthropic plunging more than 14% a day after its Nasdaq debut, chip stocks down 8%, the Nasdaq down 3.8% to its weakest since April — amplified by Federal Reserve rate-hike fears and about $1.5 billion in leveraged liquidations.
