# How to Track a Crypto Portfolio in India: Apps, Sheets & Safety

You bought some Bitcoin on an exchange, maybe a little Ethereum too, and now a simple question keeps nagging you: how much is it all worth right now, and how much did you actually spend? If you cannot answer that in under a minute, you are not tracking your portfolio — you are guessing.

Tracking a crypto portfolio simply means keeping a clear, up-to-date record of what you own, what you paid for it, and what it is worth today. It sounds boring, and it is — but it is also the difference between an investor who understands their position and one who panics at every price swing. This guide explains the three practical ways Indians track their crypto, how to set each one up, and the safety rules that matter most.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## Why Tracking Matters More Than You Think

Most beginners start with a single exchange and a single coin, so tracking feels unnecessary. Then life happens: you try a second exchange because it lists a coin the first one does not, you move some crypto to a wallet, a friend sends you a small amount, and suddenly your holdings are scattered across four places. Without a single view, you cannot answer basic questions.

First, you need to know your total value. Your money is spread across apps and wallets, and each one shows only its own slice. Second, you need to know your cost — how much INR you actually put in. Without that, a green number on screen can hide the fact that you are still below what you spent. Third, you need records for tax. India taxes each crypto transfer at a flat 30%, with 1% TDS on many transactions, and your income tax return asks for transfer-wise details in Schedule VDA. A year of unrecorded trades turns filing season into a painful reconstruction exercise.

Tracking also protects your behaviour. When you can see your total position at a glance, you are less likely to panic-sell on a red day or buy impulsively on a green one. It turns crypto from a slot machine into something you can actually manage.

## Method 1: Your Exchange Dashboard (Start Here)

If you buy crypto on an Indian exchange such as CoinDCX, WazirX or ZebPay, the simplest tracker is already in front of you: the exchange's own dashboard. It shows your holdings, your average buy price, your current value and your profit or loss, all in INR. For a beginner with one exchange and a handful of coins, this is genuinely enough.

The dashboard has one big advantage: it is automatic. Every buy, sell and deposit appears without you doing anything. The disadvantage is scope. It only knows about crypto held on that exchange. The moment you move coins to a wallet or buy on a second platform, the dashboard becomes a partial picture — and partial pictures are how people lose track of money.

Use the exchange dashboard as your live snapshot, but do not treat it as your complete record. And remember that an exchange balance is not the same as money in your bank account. Until you sell and withdraw to your bank via UPI, IMPS or NEFT, the value on screen can change at any time.

## Method 2: Portfolio Tracker Apps (One View for Everything)

Portfolio tracker apps exist for one job: pull your balances from every exchange and wallet into a single screen. Popular options include CoinMarketCap Portfolio, CoinGecko Portfolio, Delta and CoinStats, among others. You add your holdings either by typing them in manually or by connecting your exchange accounts through an API key — a special code that lets the app read your balances.

Manual entry is the safer route for beginners. You type in what you bought, when, and for how much. Nothing connects to your accounts, nothing can be withdrawn, and the worst thing a bad app can do is show you wrong numbers. The trade-off is effort: you must update it yourself after every trade, and most people stop doing that within a month.

API connections are more convenient but carry real risk, so the rules matter. First, always create a read-only API key — one with withdrawal permissions switched off. A read-only key can show your balance; it cannot move your crypto. Second, create the key on the exchange's official website or app, never through a link someone sent you. Third, if an app ever asks for your seed phrase or private key to "sync" your portfolio, stop immediately. No legitimate tracker needs those. Your seed phrase is the master key to your crypto, and anyone who has it owns your funds.

Before installing any tracker app, check three things: that it comes from the app's official developer account in the Play Store or App Store, that its reviews and download count look genuine, and that it does not demand permissions it does not need, such as access to your contacts or SMS. Fake crypto apps are one of the most common scams targeting Indians, and a convincing clone of a popular tracker is a classic trap.

## Method 3: A Spreadsheet (The Boring Option That Works)

A spreadsheet is the least glamorous tracking method and, for most Indians, the most reliable. It costs nothing, needs no permissions, works offline, and — unlike an app — it cannot be shut down, hacked or sold to another company. It is also the format your chartered accountant will actually want at tax time.

Set up one row per transaction, with these columns: date, coin, action (buy, sell, swap or transfer), quantity, price per coin in INR, total INR, exchange or wallet used, and any fee or TDS deducted. That is enough to answer every question that matters: how much you invested, what you own, what you gained or lost, and what to report in your tax return.

You do not need to update it every day. A weekly habit works well: once a week, open your exchange app, note any new transactions, and add the rows. Ten minutes a week beats ten hours of panic in March. If you prefer automation, some exchanges let you download your full transaction history as a CSV file, which you can import into your sheet — but always check the imported rows against the app, because exports sometimes miss transfers or show fees differently.

A simple Google Sheet or Excel file is fine. Keep a backup copy somewhere safe, and never store your seed phrase or exchange passwords in the same file. A spreadsheet full of your holdings is sensitive information; treat it like a bank statement.

## Which Method Should You Choose?

The honest answer is that most beginners do best with a combination: the exchange dashboard for live values, plus a spreadsheet for records. Tracker apps are optional — useful when you hold crypto in many places, but never necessary.

| Method | Best for | Effort | Safety |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exchange dashboard | One exchange, few coins | None | High — built into the exchange |
| Tracker app | Many exchanges and wallets | Low after setup | Medium — needs care with API keys |
| Spreadsheet | Tax records and full control | 10 minutes a week | Highest — no connections needed |

Whichever method you pick, the real test is simple: can you answer, right now, how much INR you have put in and what your holdings are worth? If yes, your tracking works. If not, it does not — no matter how fancy the app looks.

## Five Safety Rules for Tracking Your Portfolio

Tracking involves connecting tools to your money, so a few rules are non-negotiable.

**Never share your seed phrase.** No tracker, support agent, exchange employee or "portfolio manager" ever needs it. Anyone asking for it is running a scam, no exceptions.

**Use read-only API keys only.** When connecting an app to an exchange, switch off withdrawals and trading permissions. If the app refuses to work with a read-only key, use a different app.

**Download apps only from official stores.** Search for the app yourself in the Play Store or App Store. Never install an APK file sent over WhatsApp or Telegram, and never click a download link in a message, however official it looks.

**Keep a separate email for crypto accounts.** If your main email is compromised, every exchange and tracker linked to it becomes vulnerable. A dedicated email address, protected with an authenticator app rather than SMS codes, is a cheap layer of protection.

**Review connected apps regularly.** Once every few months, check which apps and API keys still have access to your exchange accounts, and revoke anything you no longer use. Old connections are forgotten doors into your account.

## Tracking and Tax: The Part Beginners Skip

Here is the practical reason tracking matters most in India. Every crypto transfer is taxed at a flat 30%, with 1% TDS deducted on many transactions, and losses cannot be set off against gains or carried forward. Your income tax return asks for transfer-wise details in Schedule VDA: what you transferred, when, what it cost you, and what you received.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

A tracker that records every transaction as it happens makes filing straightforward. A tracker that does not exist makes filing a reconstruction project. If you use a spreadsheet, add a column for TDS deducted on each sale so you can match it against your Form 26AS or AIS later. If you use an app, check that it can export a transaction history you can hand to your accountant — if it cannot export, it is a toy, not a tool.

This is also why you should record transfers between your own wallets and exchanges. Moving crypto from an exchange to your own wallet is not a sale, but without a record it can look like one later. A single note — "transfer to own wallet, not a sale" — saves an argument at filing time.

## A Simple Weekly Routine That Actually Sticks

The best tracking system is the one you will actually maintain, so keep it small. Once a week, at a fixed time, do three things: open your exchange dashboard and note your total value; add any new transactions to your spreadsheet; and check whether any app still connected to your accounts is one you still use.

Once a month, do a slightly deeper check: reconcile your spreadsheet against your exchange transaction history, confirm your TDS entries, and back up your file. Once a quarter, review whether your tracking still matches reality — new wallet, new exchange, anything you forgot to record.

That is it. No daily price checking, no elaborate dashboards, no paid subscriptions. Ten minutes a week, half an hour a month, and you will always know where you stand — and you will have the records to prove it.

## Common Tracking Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is tracking only the value and never the cost. A portfolio worth ₹50,000 means nothing without knowing whether you put in ₹40,000 or ₹80,000. Always record what you paid.

The second mistake is trusting a single number. Exchange dashboards, tracker apps and spreadsheets can all disagree, usually because of price differences between exchanges or a missed transaction. When numbers disagree, trust the spreadsheet built from your actual transaction history.

The third mistake is counting the same crypto twice — once on the exchange and once in the tracker app, or once in INR and once in USDT. Pick one home currency, INR, and one source of truth, your spreadsheet, and let everything else be a cross-check.

The fourth mistake is forgetting small amounts. Airdrops, rewards, tiny leftover balances — often called dust — and referral bonuses are still crypto you received, and they still need records. Small amounts add up, and the tax department does not care that an amount felt too small to record.

## The Bottom Line

Tracking a crypto portfolio in India is not about fancy tools. It is about knowing, at any moment, what you own, what you paid, and what it is worth — and having the records to back that up when tax season arrives. Start with your exchange dashboard, add a simple spreadsheet, and only reach for a tracker app if your holdings are spread across enough places to justify it. Keep your seed phrase private, use read-only connections, and update your records weekly. Your future self, staring at a tax return in March, will be grateful.

Your next step is simple: open a spreadsheet today, list every crypto transaction you can remember, and mark down what you paid in INR. That one page becomes the foundation of everything else.
