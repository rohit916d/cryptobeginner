If you have ever bought crypto in India through an app, deposited rupees with UPI, and seen a balance appear on screen, you have already used CeFi. You probably just never heard the word for it. CeFi — short for centralised finance — is the name for the crypto services run by companies: the exchanges, apps and custodians that sit between you and the blockchain and handle the hard parts for you.

Understanding CeFi matters because it is where almost every Indian beginner starts, and because it is also where most beginner losses happen — not from market moves, but from trusting a central platform too much, or trusting the wrong one. This guide explains what CeFi is, how it works in India, how it differs from DeFi and traditional finance, and the risks to watch for.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## What Is CeFi?

CeFi, or centralised finance, means crypto services run by a central company. That company controls the platform, holds your crypto in its own wallets, matches buyers with sellers, and decides the rules — fees, withdrawal limits, account freezes and customer support.

Think of it like a bank for crypto. When you deposit money in a bank, the bank holds it and shows you a balance in its app. When you deposit crypto on a centralised exchange, the exchange does the same thing: your coins sit in wallets the company controls, and your app balance is the company's record of what it owes you. You do not hold the private keys — the company does.

The word "centralised" is the key. One organisation is in charge. That is the opposite of DeFi (decentralised finance), where smart contracts on a blockchain replace the company and you keep your own keys. And it is different from simply holding crypto in your own wallet, where no company is involved at all.

## What Counts as CeFi?

CeFi is a broad label. It covers every crypto service where a company is the middleman:

- **Centralised exchanges (CEXs):** platforms such as CoinDCX, WazirX and ZebPay in India, where you buy and sell crypto for INR.
- **Crypto lending and savings apps:** companies that take your crypto deposits and pay interest on them, lending the assets out in the background.
- **Custodians:** companies that store large amounts of crypto on behalf of clients, often for institutions or wealthy individuals.
- **Crypto payment cards and apps:** services that let you spend crypto through a card or app, converting it to rupees at the point of payment.
- **Broker apps:** apps that sell you crypto directly from their own stock rather than matching you with another trader.

If a company holds your coins, runs the order book, or controls the account, it is CeFi — even if the marketing calls it "decentralised".

## How CeFi Works in India: Step by Step

For an Indian beginner, the typical CeFi journey looks like this:

**1. Sign up and complete KYC.** Indian exchanges must verify your identity under anti-money-laundering rules. You submit your PAN, Aadhaar and a selfie, and link a bank account in your name. This is mandatory on FIU-IND registered platforms — a platform that lets you trade large amounts with no KYC at all is a red flag.

**2. Deposit INR.** You add rupees through UPI, IMPS or NEFT from your own bank account. Most exchanges only accept deposits from a bank account that matches your KYC name.

**3. Place a buy order.** You choose a trading pair such as BTC/INR and buy at the market price or set a limit price. The exchange matches your order with a seller, or sells from its own stock if it is a broker.

**4. The exchange holds your crypto.** After the purchase, your coins sit in the exchange's wallets. Your app shows a balance, but the actual coins are under the company's control. This is the single most important thing to understand about CeFi.

**5. Sell and withdraw INR.** When you sell, the exchange credits rupees to your account, minus trading fees and the 1% TDS that applies to crypto transfers in India. You can then withdraw the rupees to your bank account through UPI, IMPS or NEFT.

That whole flow feels familiar because it mirrors a stock-trading app or a bank app — and that familiarity is exactly why beginners start here.

## CeFi vs DeFi: The Core Differences

CeFi and DeFi both let you trade and use crypto, but they work in opposite ways:

| Feature | CeFi | DeFi |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds your crypto | The company | You, in your own wallet |
| Who matches trades | The company's order book | A smart contract or liquidity pool |
| KYC required | Yes, on Indian platforms | Usually no |
| INR deposits (UPI/bank) | Yes | No — you must bring crypto yourself |
| Customer support | Yes, a help desk | No — there is nobody to call |
| If you forget your password | Password reset works | No reset — lost keys mean lost funds |
| If the platform fails | Your funds may be frozen or lost | Your funds stay in your wallet |
| Learning curve | Low — feels like a normal app | Higher — wallets, gas fees, approvals |

Neither is "better" in every situation. CeFi trades control for convenience: it is easier, but you trust a company with your money. DeFi trades convenience for control: you keep your keys, but you carry all the responsibility yourself. Many experienced users use both — CeFi to convert INR into crypto, and DeFi or a self-custody wallet for longer-term holding.

## CeFi vs Traditional Finance

CeFi borrows the shape of traditional finance but works on crypto rails:

- **Similar:** a company holds your money, you log in with a password, support can reset your access, and regulators require KYC.
- **Different:** markets run 24/7 with no closing bell, settlement is usually faster than traditional share settlement, and the assets themselves — crypto — are far more volatile than most bank deposits or shares.
- **Different protections:** your bank deposits in India are covered by DICGC insurance up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank. Crypto held on an exchange has no equivalent government insurance. If an exchange fails, there is no deposit-insurance safety net for your coins.

That last point surprises many beginners. A crypto exchange app may look like a bank app, but it does not carry a bank's protections.

## Why Beginners in India Start With CeFi

There are good reasons CeFi is the usual first step:

- **INR on-ramps:** it is the only practical way to turn rupees into crypto, through UPI or bank transfer.
- **Familiar experience:** the app looks and behaves like a stock-trading or banking app, with prices in rupees.
- **Customer support:** if a deposit is delayed or a trade looks wrong, there is a help desk to contact.
- **No wallet setup:** you do not need to understand private keys, seed phrases or gas fees before your first purchase.
- **Regulated touchpoints:** FIU-IND registered exchanges follow Indian KYC and reporting rules, which gives beginners a clearer legal footing than an anonymous offshore platform.

These are real advantages. The mistake is treating the convenience as safety. An easy app and a safe platform are two different things.

## The Risks of CeFi Every Beginner Should Know

CeFi's centralisation is also its biggest weakness. The main risks are:

**Custodial risk.** "Not your keys, not your coins" is the phrase crypto users repeat for a reason. If the exchange holds your crypto, you depend on the exchange staying solvent, honest and operational. History has examples of large exchanges collapsing and users waiting years — or never getting funds back.

**Hacks.** Centralised exchanges hold enormous amounts of crypto in one place, which makes them attractive targets. Even large, well-known platforms have been breached. Reputable exchanges keep most funds in cold storage and publish proof-of-reserves reports, but no platform is hack-proof.

**Frozen withdrawals and account blocks.** An exchange can pause withdrawals during technical problems, regulatory action or a liquidity crisis. It can also freeze individual accounts during KYC reviews or fraud investigations. Your money is accessible only as long as the company allows it.

**Fake platforms.** Scammers clone real exchange apps and websites, or invent entirely fake ones, then advertise them through WhatsApp and Telegram groups. Victims deposit money and see a fake balance that can never be withdrawn. Always download apps from the official store listing, check the developer name, and verify the website address character by character.

**Hidden costs.** Trading fees, spreads between buy and sell prices, deposit and withdrawal fees, and currency-conversion charges all add up. A platform advertising "zero fees" may simply be charging through a wider spread. Check the full fee schedule before trading.

**Regulatory change.** Crypto rules in India are still evolving. Exchanges have faced banking restrictions, tax changes and compliance notices in the past. A platform that works smoothly today can change its policies quickly if regulations shift.

## A 5-Minute CeFi Safety Checklist

Before you trust any CeFi platform with your money, run through this check:

1. **FIU-IND registration:** confirm the exchange is registered with India's Financial Intelligence Unit and shows its legal entity name clearly. Registration is not a licence or a guarantee, but an unregistered platform operating in India is a serious warning sign.
2. **Real company, real history:** check that the company has a verifiable legal name, a registered address and a track record. Anonymous teams and brand-new platforms deserve extra caution.
3. **Two-factor authentication:** enable app-based 2FA (not just SMS) and a strong, unique password. Store backup codes offline, never in a screenshot or a chat.
4. **Small test first:** deposit a small amount, buy a small quantity, and withdraw it successfully before committing larger sums. If withdrawals are slow or blocked at small amounts, stop.
5. **Keep most funds off the exchange:** treat an exchange like a marketplace, not a savings account. Money you are not actively trading is safer in a wallet you control — provided you can keep the recovery phrase safe.
6. **Verify every link:** only use the exchange's official app and website. Never click links from random messages, and never share your password, OTP or recovery phrase with anyone — real support staff will never ask for them.

## CeFi and Tax in India

Using CeFi does not change your tax obligations — it usually makes them easier to track, because the exchange keeps records of your trades. In India, gains from crypto transfers are taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess), with no deduction except the cost of acquisition, and losses cannot be set off against other income or carried forward. A 1% TDS also applies to crypto transfers above certain thresholds, and exchanges generally deduct it automatically.

Keep your own records too: download your trade history and TDS certificates regularly, and report every transfer in Schedule VDA of your income tax return.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## Is CeFi Right for You?

For most Indian beginners, the honest answer is: CeFi is the easiest way to start, and the riskiest place to stay. Use it for what it is good at — converting INR into crypto through a regulated, supported platform — and be deliberate about what you leave there. Learn how self-custody works, even if you do not use it immediately, so that moving your crypto off an exchange is a choice you can make rather than a skill you lack.

The central question of CeFi is always the same: how much are you willing to trust one company with your money? The more you understand what that company does, what can go wrong, and what protections do and do not exist, the better your answer will be.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is CeFi the same as a normal crypto exchange?

Mostly, yes. When you buy crypto on an Indian exchange such as CoinDCX, WazirX or ZebPay, you are using CeFi — a centralised company that holds your coins, matches your orders and handles your INR deposits through UPI or bank transfer. CeFi also includes crypto lending apps and custodians, not just exchanges. The common thread is that a company sits between you and the blockchain.

### Is CeFi safe for beginners in India?

CeFi is usually the easiest starting point because it offers INR on-ramps, customer support and a familiar app experience. But it is not risk-free: the exchange holds your coins, so a hack, a shutdown or a frozen withdrawal can block your access. Reduce the risk by using FIU-IND registered exchanges, enabling two-factor authentication, keeping only small amounts on an exchange, and never sharing your password or OTP with anyone.

### What is the difference between CeFi and DeFi?

In CeFi, a company controls custody, order matching and customer support, and you must complete KYC before trading. In DeFi, smart contracts on a blockchain replace the company, you keep your own keys, and there is usually no KYC — but also no support desk if something goes wrong. CeFi trades control for convenience; DeFi trades convenience for control.

## Conclusion

CeFi is the centralised half of crypto: companies that hold your coins, run the trading, and make buying crypto with rupees feel as easy as ordering food. For Indian beginners it is the natural front door — INR deposits, UPI, KYC and customer support all in one app. But the convenience comes with a trade: you trust a company with your money, with no deposit insurance and no guarantee you can always withdraw.

Use CeFi deliberately. Verify the platform before you deposit, turn on two-factor authentication, test withdrawals with small amounts, and keep only what you are actively trading on an exchange. Learn the basics of self-custody so you always have somewhere safer to move to. And whatever you do, treat every crypto decision as education first — understand the risks before you put money in.
