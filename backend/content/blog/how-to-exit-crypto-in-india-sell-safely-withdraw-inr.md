Learning how to buy crypto is only half the journey. Every investor eventually needs to get money out — to pay a bill, rebalance a portfolio, or simply take profits. Exiting crypto in India means two steps: selling your crypto for Indian rupees, and then moving those rupees from the exchange to your bank account. Both steps are simple when done properly, but each has traps that can cost beginners real money. This guide walks through the full exit process, step by step.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. Selling decisions are personal; this guide explains the process, not whether you should sell.

## Step 1: Decide What You Are Selling

Before you touch the sell button, be clear about what you are exiting and why. Are you selling everything, or only part of your holding? Are you selling because you need the cash, or because you want to move into a different asset?

Selling everything at once is sometimes called a full exit. Selling a portion — say, one quarter of a holding — is a partial exit. Many experienced investors prefer partial exits because crypto prices can move sharply in either direction within days, and selling everything in one go means you cannot benefit if the price rises later. There is no rule that fits everyone, so think about what the money is for before you sell.

It also helps to know exactly what you hold. Check your exchange portfolio and note the coin, the quantity, and the current value in rupees. If you hold crypto in a personal wallet rather than on an exchange, you will first need to transfer it to an exchange before you can sell it for INR — that transfer itself takes time and network fees, so plan for it.

## Step 2: Sell on an Indian Exchange

For most Indians, the simplest exit route is selling on a FIU-IND registered Indian exchange such as CoinDCX, WazirX or ZebPay. These platforms offer INR trading pairs like BTC/INR and ETH/INR, so you can sell directly for rupees without converting through dollars first.

The basic process looks like this:

1. Log in to the exchange and open the trading page for your coin's INR pair.
2. Choose a sell order. A market order sells immediately at the best available price. A limit order lets you set the price you want, but it only executes if the market reaches that price.
3. Enter the quantity you want to sell and confirm the order.
4. Once the order fills, the INR appears in your exchange wallet.

A market order is faster but the final price can differ slightly from what you saw on screen, especially for large orders or less-traded coins. This difference is called slippage. A limit order gives you price control but no guarantee of execution. For small, everyday exits, a market order is usually fine. For larger amounts, a limit order — or selling in smaller batches — can reduce slippage.

Watch the fees. Exchanges charge a trading fee on every sale, usually a small percentage of the order value. There may also be a spread — a small gap between the buying and selling price — which is another hidden cost. Check the fee schedule before you sell, not after.

## Step 3: Withdraw INR to Your Bank Account

Once the sale is complete, your rupees sit in the exchange wallet. The next step is withdrawing them to your bank account. Most Indian exchanges support UPI, IMPS and NEFT withdrawals.

Before your first withdrawal, make sure your bank account is added and verified on the exchange. The account must be in your own name, and the name must match your KYC details exactly — even a small mismatch, like a missing middle name, can cause a withdrawal to fail or be held for review. Most exchanges let you add one or two bank accounts; keep the details updated if you change banks.

The withdrawal itself is straightforward: enter the amount, choose your bank account, and confirm with an OTP. Withdrawals through UPI or IMPS usually arrive within minutes to a few hours. NEFT can take a few hours to one working day. Some exchanges also have daily withdrawal limits and a small withdrawal fee, so check both before you move a large amount.

A smart habit for your first withdrawal is to send a small test amount — say ₹500 or ₹1,000 — and confirm it reaches your bank before moving the rest. This costs almost nothing and protects you from a wrong-account mistake. Once the test succeeds, withdraw the remaining balance in one or two transfers.

## The Tax Part: 30% Tax and 1% TDS

Exiting crypto in India has a tax layer that surprises many beginners, so it is worth understanding before you sell.

First, any gain from selling crypto is taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess). The only deduction allowed is the cost of acquiring the crypto — you cannot deduct exchange fees, internet costs or other expenses. Crypto losses cannot be set off against other income, and they cannot be carried forward to future years. You still need to report every sale in Schedule VDA of your income tax return, even a loss-making one.

Second, the exchange deducts 1% TDS (tax deducted at source) on the sale value when it crosses the threshold. This TDS is not an extra tax — it is advance tax that appears in your Form 26AS and AIS, and you can claim credit for it when filing your return. But it does reduce the cash you receive, so do not be surprised when the amount credited to your bank is slightly less than the sale value.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

Keep records of every exit: the date, the coin, the quantity sold, the sale value in rupees, the fees paid, and the TDS deducted. Your exchange usually provides a downloadable transaction statement and a TDS certificate — download both and store them safely. Good records make filing your return far easier and protect you if the tax department ever asks questions.

## Common Exit Mistakes to Avoid

**Selling during a panic.** Prices can fall sharply in a day, and selling in a panic often locks in a loss that might have recovered. If you are selling because of fear rather than a plan, pause and re-read your original reason for holding the asset.

**Forgetting the network when moving crypto first.** If your crypto is in a personal wallet, you must send it to the exchange before selling. Sending on the wrong network — for example, sending a token on the wrong blockchain — can mean the funds never arrive. Always match the network the exchange deposit page specifies, and send a small test amount first.

**Using P2P cash deals.** Selling crypto directly to a stranger for cash skips the exchange entirely, but it also skips every protection. Fake payment screenshots, reversed UPI payments and outright theft are common in cash deals. The exchange route costs a small fee but gives you a receipt, a bank trail and a dispute process.

**Leaving rupees idle on the exchange.** Once you have sold, withdraw the INR to your bank reasonably soon. Crypto exchanges are not bank accounts — your balance is not covered by deposit insurance, and if the platform faces trouble, withdrawals can be frozen. Your bank account is the safer home for the money.

**Ignoring withdrawal limits and timings.** Some exchanges cap daily INR withdrawals, and banks have their own processing windows. If you need a large amount by a specific date, start the exit a few days early rather than at the last minute.

## What If Something Goes Wrong?

Sometimes an exit does not go smoothly. Here is what to do in the most common situations.

If your INR withdrawal is delayed beyond a day, first check whether the exchange shows it as processing, completed or failed. A "processing" status often means a bank-side delay. If it stays stuck, raise a support ticket with the transaction reference number and a screenshot of the withdrawal page. Keep the ticket number and follow up politely but firmly.

If your sell order did not execute, check whether you placed a limit order above the current market price. A limit order simply waits until the market reaches your price — it has not failed, it is just unfilled. You can cancel it and place a market order instead, or lower the limit price.

If crypto you sent to the exchange has not arrived, check the transaction on a block explorer using the transaction ID. If the blockchain shows it as confirmed but the exchange has not credited it, contact support with the transaction ID. If the blockchain shows it as unconfirmed, you may simply need to wait for more confirmations. And if you sent on the wrong network, contact support immediately — recovery is sometimes possible but never guaranteed, and it can take weeks.

If you suspect a scam — for example, someone offering to "help you exit faster" for a fee, or a fake support agent asking for your OTP or seed phrase — stop immediately. Real exchange support will never ask for your password, OTP or seed phrase. Report the incident at cybercrime.gov.in or call the helpline 1930.

## A Simple Exit Checklist

Use this checklist every time you exit, and the process stays boring in the best possible way:

| Step | What to check |
|---|---|
| Before selling | Know what you hold and why you are selling |
| Choosing where to sell | FIU-IND registered Indian exchange with an INR pair |
| Order type | Market for speed, limit for price control |
| Fees | Trading fee, spread and withdrawal fee checked in advance |
| Bank account | Name matches KYC exactly; test withdrawal first |
| Tax | 30% on gains, 1% TDS deducted, sale recorded for Schedule VDA |
| Records | Download transaction statement and TDS certificate |
| After selling | Withdraw INR to your bank; do not leave large balances on the exchange |

## The Bottom Line

Exiting crypto safely in India comes down to three habits: sell on a registered Indian exchange, withdraw to a bank account that matches your KYC name, and keep clean records for tax filing. The process takes minutes once you have done it once, but the first exit deserves patience — a test withdrawal, a fee check and a look at the tax implications before you press sell.

The money you take out of crypto is only truly yours once it is sitting in your bank account with the tax accounted for. Plan the exit with the same care you planned the entry, and you will avoid the mistakes that catch most beginners.
