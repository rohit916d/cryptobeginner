# Crypto Adoption in India: Statistics & Key Facts 2026

Crypto adoption in India is now a global headline, not a niche story. In the Chainalysis 2025 Global Crypto Adoption Index — the most widely cited ranking of its kind — India placed first in the world for the third consecutive year, ahead of the United States. For beginners, numbers like these raise fair questions: what exactly is being measured, why does India lead, and what should you actually take away from the statistics? This guide collects the key verified figures in one place and explains them in plain language.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Statistics describe adoption and activity, not safety or profit — a widely used asset can still be risky and volatile.

## The Headline Numbers

Here are the core statistics, all from the Chainalysis 2025 Geography of Cryptocurrency Report and its Global Crypto Adoption Index, released in September 2025:

| Statistic | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| India's rank, Global Crypto Adoption Index 2025 | 1st (of 151 countries) |
| Years in a row at No. 1 | 3 (2023, 2024, 2025) |
| Sub-indices where India ranked 1st | All 4 |
| On-chain value received by India (12 months to June 2025) | ~$338 billion |
| Asia-Pacific on-chain value received (same period) | ~$2.36 trillion |
| Asia-Pacific year-over-year growth | ~69% |
| Countries ranked in the index | 151 |
| Estimated crypto owners worldwide (Crypto.com market sizing) | ~741 million |
| Top 5 countries overall | India, USA, Pakistan, Vietnam, Brazil |

Two things stand out. First, India's lead is broad, not narrow: it finished first in every sub-index, covering retail activity on centralised services, overall centralised service value, DeFi activity, and institutional-sized activity. Second, this is grassroots adoption — the index is designed to measure how much ordinary people use crypto in daily financial life, not just how much money large traders move.

## What the Adoption Index Actually Measures

It helps to understand what a ranking like this does and does not say. Chainalysis builds the index from on-chain data and web traffic across 151 countries. Each country is scored on four sub-indices, and the scores are weighted by population size and purchasing power. That weighting matters: a smaller, wealthier country cannot win just by having a few very large traders, because the method adjusts for how many people live there and what their incomes look like.

The ranking also changes if you look at it differently. Adjusted purely per person, smaller Eastern European countries such as Ukraine, Moldova and Georgia rise to the top. India leads the headline index because it combines a very large population with genuinely widespread usage across cities and smaller towns alike.

Just as important: the index measures **activity**, not approval, safety, or profitability. A country can rank first in adoption while its government taxes crypto heavily and warns citizens about the risks — which is exactly India's situation, as the next sections show.

## Why India Leads the World

Several long-term factors explain India's position better than any single headline:

**A digital-first population.** India runs one of the world's largest instant-payment systems in UPI, and hundreds of millions of people already manage money entirely on a phone. Moving from a payments app to a crypto app is a small step for users who have never visited a bank branch for routine transactions.

**Remittances and cross-border needs.** India is the world's largest recipient of remittances. Even though most families still use traditional channels, the size of these flows keeps interest in faster, cheaper digital alternatives permanently high.

**A young, mobile user base.** India's internet population skews young, and crypto apps are built for exactly that audience — small starting amounts, simple interfaces, and content in Indian languages.

**Grassroots breadth.** Chainalysis has repeatedly noted that Indian usage spans centralised exchanges, DeFi services and peer-to-peer activity at the same time. That breadth across all four sub-indices is precisely what the index rewards, and it is why India's lead has held for three years rather than appearing once.

## The Other Side: Taxes and Rules in India

Adoption statistics can make crypto sound officially welcomed in India. It is not, and beginners should hold both facts at once:

- Crypto is **not legal tender** in India, and the RBI has repeatedly cautioned the public about its risks.
- Profits from selling crypto are taxed at a flat **30%**, and a **1% TDS** applies to transfers above the specified thresholds. Losses cannot be set off against other income. Our full breakdown is in the [crypto tax guide: 30% tax and 1% TDS explained](/blog/crypto-tax-india-30-percent-1-percent-tds/).
- Exchanges that serve Indian users must register with **FIU-IND** and follow KYC and anti-money-laundering rules. Before using any platform, check it the way we explain in [how to check if an exchange is FIU-registered](/blog/how-to-check-if-exchange-is-fiu-registered-india-guide/).

> Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

In short, India pairs the world's highest grassroots adoption with one of the world's stricter tax treatments. The ranking reflects the behaviour of users, not a policy endorsement.

## What These Statistics Do Not Mean

Big adoption numbers attract hype, and hype attracts fraud. Keep three cautions in mind:

1. **Adoption is not safety.** The same popularity that tops the index also makes Indian beginners the prime target for scams — fake apps, Telegram "gurus", and guaranteed-return schemes. Read the [7 common crypto scams targeting Indians](/blog/7-common-crypto-scams-targeting-indians/) before you put money anywhere.
2. **Adoption is not profit.** An index ranking says nothing about prices or whether any coin will rise. Most of the activity measured is people buying, selling, sending and using services — much of it at a loss, like any market.
3. **Estimates vary.** Different firms count "crypto owners" differently, so treat any single figure — including the ~741 million global estimate above — as an informed approximation, not a census.

## How Beginners Should Read Crypto Statistics

Statistics pages like this one get quoted everywhere, often badly. A five-minute checklist will protect you from most misleading claims:

- **Find the source.** "Chainalysis 2025 index" is a source. "Reports say" is not.
- **Check the year and period.** Adoption data moves fast; a 2022 figure quoted in 2026 is history, not news.
- **Ask what is measured.** Total value, number of users, and per-person activity are three different stories. India's No. 1 is an activity ranking weighted by population — not a claim that India moves the most raw dollars (the United States is larger in absolute institutional volume).
- **Compare like with like.** A country's rank can rise simply because the method changed; the 2025 index, for example, added an institutional sub-index and removed the retail DeFi one.
- **Ignore price conclusions drawn from adoption data.** Nothing in an adoption index predicts what any asset will be worth next month.

It does not tell you that you should rush in.
