## Bitcoin Losses Hit FTX-Era High: What It Means in India

Bitcoin's realized losses are now the heaviest since the FTX collapse, according to blockchain analytics firm Glassnode. In its Week On-Chain report published on 24 November 2026, Glassnode said the losses Bitcoin holders locked in over the past week nearly doubled when measured against the capital that flowed into Bitcoin over the past year — reaching the highest level since FTX, one of the largest crypto exchanges in the world, collapsed in November 2022. At the same time, a record 11.6 million Bitcoin are now held at an unrealised loss, a figure that tops every previous cycle low.

The report, covered on 24–25 November by Coin Edition, crypto.news, Decrypt, FXStreet, TradingView and Crypto Briefing, is the clearest on-chain account yet of how deep the November sell-off has cut. Bitcoin was trading near $59,977–$60,242 on 24 November, more than 50% below its October 2025 peak of about $126,000, and it had touched an intraday low of $55,106–$55,254 on 21 November — its lowest price since September 2024.

This guide explains what Glassnode's numbers actually measure, what is new in this report, what the firm itself says the numbers do and do not mean, and what Indian holders should understand about them.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains an on-chain metric and what a research report said; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 24–25 November 2026 from Glassnode's own report, is:

- **Realized losses nearly doubled in a week.** Glassnode compares realized losses — the losses investors actually lock in when they sell below their purchase price — with the capital that flowed into Bitcoin over the previous year. On that measure, losses almost doubled in a single week and reached their highest level since FTX collapsed in November 2022, when Bitcoin was trading below $16,000.
- **A record 11.6 million Bitcoin are underwater.** As of 24 November, a record 11.6 million coins were held at an unrealised loss — more than at any previous cycle low. Only 8.5 million Bitcoin remained in profit, which is below the 8.6 million recorded during the FTX collapse itself.
- **The pain has spread from old hands to new buyers.** From mid-April to mid-November, investors who had held their coins for roughly five months or longer accounted for most of the realized losses. In the latest slide, newer buyers began taking heavy losses too, bringing the long-term holders' share back to roughly half of the total.
- **Glassnode calls it the first major capitulation of this bear market.** The firm's own words, quoted across the coverage: "Bitcoin losses are the heaviest since FTX," and the surge is "the first major capitulation of this bear market."
- **ETF outflows reached $4.3 billion over 20 trading days.** US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $4.3 billion in net withdrawals across 20 trading sessions, according to Glassnode. Coinbase traders were heavy net sellers over the past 30 days.
- **Bitcoin has fallen about 29% in under two months.** From roughly $86,500 in early October to around $61,000 by late November, the drop is about 29%. The total drawdown from the October 2025 peak is roughly 56% — milder than past bear markets, which saw falls of 77% to more than 85%.
- **Leverage has largely been flushed out.** Nearly 90% of the leveraged long positions that were vulnerable to liquidation between last week's price and Bitcoin's realized price had already been cleared, according to Glassnode's liquidation model. In a single 24-hour window during the slide, the market saw $2.7 billion in leveraged liquidations.
- **Options traders are the most defensive since June 2022.** The seven-day average put-to-call ratio reached 1.7, its highest since June 2022, and options open-interest positioning reached its highest level since May 2021. Bitcoin's correlation with the Nasdaq 100 climbed to its highest since September 2022.

None of this is a forecast. Glassnode's own conclusion, repeated by every outlet that covered the report, is that the data does not confirm a bottom. That caution is part of the story, not a footnote to it.

## What "Realized Losses" Actually Measure

To understand the report, it helps to start with the metric itself. "Realized losses" sounds technical, but the idea is simple. Every Bitcoin has a last price at which it moved on the blockchain — in practice, the price its current owner paid for it. When that coin is sold, the difference between the sale price and the price it last moved at is either a realized profit or a realized loss. Add up all the realized losses across the network over a period, and you get a measure of how much pain holders are actually locking in, rather than how much pain they are merely sitting on.

That distinction matters. A coin held at a loss has not been sold. Its owner may be worried, may be waiting, or may not even be watching the price. A realized loss, by contrast, is a decision: someone chose to sell below what they paid, accepting the loss rather than waiting for a recovery. When realized losses spike, it means a lot of people made that decision at the same time. Analysts call that capitulation — the point at which the discipline of holding breaks and selling at a loss feels better than waiting for more of it.

Glassnode does not report the raw dollar figure in isolation, because raw dollars grow as Bitcoin itself grows. Instead, it scales realized losses against the capital that entered Bitcoin over the preceding year. That is what lets it compare a sell-off in 2026 with one in 2022, when the market was a fraction of its current size. On that comparable measure, this week's reading is the highest since FTX collapsed.

The 11.6 million underwater figure is a different, complementary measure. It counts coins whose last on-chain price is above the current market price — coins whose owners are sitting on a paper loss whether they sell or not. The fact that it is a record, topping previous cycle lows, is why the report is being described as the first major capitulation of this bear market. The other side of the same ledger is just as stark: only 8.5 million Bitcoin remain in profit, fewer than during the FTX collapse.

## Who Is Taking the Losses — and Why That Matters

One of the most closely watched details in the report is who is selling. Glassnode splits holders into two broad groups: long-term holders, who have held their coins for roughly five months or longer, and everyone else — the more recent buyers.

From mid-April to mid-November, long-term holders accounted for most of the realized losses. That pattern is familiar from past downturns. Glassnode notes that older holders absorbed the bulk of losses around the bear-market lows of 2015, 2018 and 2022, and that the market reached its low during or close to those stretches. Long-term holders tend to sell less readily, so when they start locking in losses in large numbers, analysts read it as a sign that the selling has reached the most committed part of the market.

In the latest slide, that pattern changed. More recent buyers began taking heavy losses again, bringing the long-term holders' share back to roughly half of the total. The pain has spread: it is no longer concentrated in one group. That is consistent with a market in which the fall has gone on long enough, and deep enough, that even buyers who entered only weeks or months ago are choosing to exit at a loss.

The report is careful not to over-read this. The timing of loss spikes has varied. Glassnode pointed to early 2019, March 2020 and November 2022 as cases where spikes came close to a low — but also to earlier episodes in 2018 and 2022 when prices had further to fall. "Spikes like this often came close to past lows, but some came early," the firm said. That is the whole point of the metric: it measures the intensity of selling, not the timing of a turn.

## The $53,200 Realized Price and the $58,500 Line

Glassnode's report also puts two numbers in front of readers as markers to watch — not as predictions, but as reference points from the data itself.

The first is $53,200, Bitcoin's realized price: the average price at which all circulating coins last moved on-chain. It is, in effect, the average cost basis of the entire market. Bitcoin traded below that metric before bottoming in each of the previous three major bear markets. Glassnode said a daily close below June's $58,500 closing low could open the way toward $53,200 — and noted that applying the discounts seen at the lows of 2022 and 2018 to today's realized price would put Bitcoin at roughly $41,000 and $37,000 respectively. Those are scenario calculations from past bear markets, not forecasts of where the price will go.

The second is $58,500 itself: June's closing low. On 20 November, Bitcoin posted its lowest daily close of the sell-off, just above that June closing low, after touching $55,300 in intraday trading the next day. The price had closed below the $62,000 range on the Thursday before, breaking a level where recent buyers had accumulated. Glassnode said a recovery above $60,000, accompanied by renewed ETF inflows, would help establish whether the summer lows are holding.

There is one more signal in the report that cuts the other way. Nearly 90% of the leveraged long positions that Glassnode's model tracked between last week's price and the $53,200 realized price have already been liquidated. That reduces the scope for another cascade of forced selling in that range — the kind of chain reaction in which falling prices trigger liquidations, which push prices lower, which trigger more liquidations. The leverage that could fuel such a cascade has, to a large extent, already been removed. That does not mean prices cannot fall further; it means one particular mechanism of falling — forced selling by leveraged longs — has less fuel left in it.

## The ETF Outflow Side of the Story

The on-chain losses do not exist in isolation. They sit alongside a steady drain from the US spot Bitcoin ETFs, which have been the largest single source of institutional demand for Bitcoin since their launch.

Glassnode puts the outflow at $4.3 billion in net withdrawals over the past 20 trading days. That figure has been corroborated by daily flow data: Monday 23 November alone saw $569.7 million in net outflows, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for $509.3 million of it, and November's total is approaching $5 billion. IBIT has recorded just one day of net inflows so far this month. Ethereum ETFs lost $84.9 million on the same Monday, and Solana and XRP funds also recorded small outflows — the whole ETF complex is moving in the same direction, with Bitcoin by far the largest.

Coinbase traders, meanwhile, were heavy net sellers over the past 30 days, according to Glassnode. That matters because Coinbase is the venue where much of US institutional and large-retail flow is executed; heavy net selling there is a direct read on the behaviour of the bigger, more deliberate buyers rather than the leveraged traders on offshore venues.

The ETF outflows and the on-chain losses are two views of the same event: holders, including the institutional buyers who arrived through the ETFs, are exiting. The difference is that the ETF figure measures money leaving the funds, while the realized-loss figure measures the losses being locked in as coins change hands. Together they describe a market in which selling is broad-based — across ETFs, exchanges and on-chain holders — rather than confined to one corner of it.

## How This Differs From the Earlier Glassnode Report on This Site

This site covered a Glassnode report on realized losses on 18 November, and readers may reasonably ask what is new here. The answer is that this is a different report, published a week later, with different figures and a different conclusion.

The 17 November report, titled "Capitulation Meets Support", said realized losses had reached levels previously seen near the major market lows of 2018, 2020, 2022 and early 2023, and that buyers had absorbed roughly 66,000 Bitcoin in the $62,000 to $66,000 price band during the two heaviest days of selling. Its framing was that selling pressure was meeting support.

The 24 November report goes further. Its headline finding is that realized losses nearly doubled in a single week and are now the highest since the FTX collapse specifically — a sharper, more specific claim than "levels last seen near past lows". It adds the record 11.6 million underwater figure, which tops previous cycle lows rather than merely matching them; the 8.5 million coins still in profit, fewer than at the FTX collapse; the $53,200 realized price and the $58,500 June closing low as the markers to watch; the $4.3 billion ETF outflow figure over 20 trading days, up from about $2.2 billion in the earlier report; and the options and correlation extremes — a put-to-call ratio of 1.7, the highest since June 2022, and a Nasdaq 100 correlation at its highest since September 2022.

The 18 November post also predates the weekend low of $55,106–$55,254 and the further slide below $60,000 on 24 November. This post covers the week in which the losses doubled, not the week in which they first reached cycle-low levels. The Santiment post on this site from 11 November, about 109,000 non-empty wallets disappearing since mid-October, is a different measure again — it counts wallets, not losses — and the stablecoin-supply post from 12 November covers the contraction in stablecoin supply, not the losses holders are locking in.

## What It Means for Crypto Users in India

The Glassnode report is about the global Bitcoin market, and its numbers are in dollars. But there are five things Indian users should take from it.

**The rupee price tells the same story, with a local premium on top.** Bitcoin was trading near ₹55–56 lakh in India on 24–25 November on global spot conversions, and roughly ₹62–64 lakh on local Indian exchanges, where isolated INR liquidity pools and the stablecoin premium push prices above the global conversion. The November fall is about 20% for the month, and the drawdown from the October 2025 peak is more than half. The local premium means Indian buyers pay more than the global price to enter, and Indian sellers receive more than the global price to exit — but the direction of the move is the same in both.

**An unrealised loss is not a realized one — and in India, that distinction has a tax consequence.** The 11.6 million underwater figure counts coins that are still being held. Their owners have not locked in anything. In India, that distinction matters more than in most countries: a loss on a crypto transfer cannot be set off against other income, and gains are taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and 4% cess) under Section 115BBH, with 1% TDS under Section 194S on transactions above the specified thresholds. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Selling at a loss does not create a tax benefit in India the way it does in some other countries, so the decision to sell should not be driven by a hope of a tax saving that does not exist here.

**INR access is still the live issue at home.** The global story is about losses; the Indian story in November has also been about access to rupees. Coinbase halted INR withdrawals in India on 14 November after a banking-partner outage, CoinDCX reported INR withdrawal delays on 19–20 November, and Unocoin has had services suspended since 9 November after a security breach. Before any decision about a holding, an Indian user should know whether their exchange's INR withdrawal route is actually working — a question the Glassnode report cannot answer, but one that matters more than any on-chain metric when it comes to getting money out.

**Capitulation is a description of selling, not a signal to buy.** Glassnode's own language is the guardrail here. The firm calls this "the first major capitulation of this bear market" and says the data does not confirm a bottom. Similar readings in the past appeared near major lows — and also appeared during falls that continued. The put-to-call ratio of 1.7 and the record underwater supply are measures of how extreme the current mood is, not instructions. A market can stay extreme for longer than any single report can predict, and the report itself warns that a close below $58,500 could open the way to $53,200.

**The ETF outflows are the part of the story that reaches India indirectly.** Indian users cannot buy US spot Bitcoin ETFs directly through Indian exchanges, but the outflows matter because they remove the largest source of institutional demand that has supported Bitcoin since early 2024. When $4.3 billion leaves those funds in 20 sessions, the buyers who were absorbing selling disappear — and the price discovery that follows is what the realized-loss figures are measuring. The India-specific version of the same dynamic is the stablecoin premium: when Indian demand for dollars-through-crypto rises, the premium on USDT in India rises with it, and it has been running above 6% through much of November.

## The Bottom Line

Glassnode's 24 November Week On-Chain report says Bitcoin holders are locking in their heaviest losses since the FTX collapse in November 2022, with realized losses nearly doubling in a week against the capital that flowed into Bitcoin over the past year. A record 11.6 million Bitcoin are held at an unrealised loss, only 8.5 million remain in profit, and US spot Bitcoin ETFs have shed $4.3 billion over 20 trading days. The firm calls it the first major capitulation of this bear market — and stresses, in the same report, that the data does not confirm a bottom.

For Indian users, the practical takeaways are the ones that do not depend on any price call: understand the difference between an unrealised loss and a realized one, remember that Indian tax law does not let you set off crypto losses, check that your exchange's INR withdrawal route is working before you need it, and treat capitulation as a measure of how much selling is happening — not as a signal about what happens next. The numbers in this report describe a market under real stress. What they do not do is say when that stress ends.
