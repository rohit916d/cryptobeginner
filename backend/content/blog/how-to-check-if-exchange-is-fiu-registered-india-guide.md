If you trade crypto in India, you have probably seen exchanges describe themselves as "FIU-registered" or "FIU-compliant." That phrase matters — but only if you know how to verify it. A claim on a website costs nothing to make, and beginners are exactly the audience most likely to take it at face value. This guide shows you how to check whether a crypto exchange is genuinely FIU-registered in India, what registration actually guarantees, and what it does not.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile, and platforms can fail — never keep money on an exchange that you cannot afford to lose, and always verify a platform's claims yourself before depositing funds.

## What FIU-IND Registration Actually Means

FIU-IND is the Financial Intelligence Unit of India, the government body that collects and analyses financial intelligence related to money laundering and terrorist financing. In March 2023, the government brought Virtual Digital Asset Service Providers — the legal category that covers crypto exchanges and similar platforms — under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA).

From that point on, any platform offering crypto exchange, transfer, or safekeeping services to Indian users was required to register with FIU-IND as a reporting entity. Registration carries concrete obligations:

- **Customer verification (KYC):** the platform must verify who you are, typically with PAN, Aadhaar-linked details, and a selfie, before letting you trade.
- **Record-keeping:** it must maintain records of transactions and customer details for the period prescribed under the PMLA rules.
- **Suspicious activity reporting:** it must report suspicious transactions to FIU-IND.
- **Ongoing compliance:** it must appoint compliance staff and follow the anti-money laundering framework, not just register once and forget about it.

In short, registration is about anti-money laundering compliance. It is a legal obligation for platforms serving Indians — and a useful first filter for users choosing where to trade.

## Why This Check Matters Right Now

FIU-IND does not only register platforms; it also takes action against those that operate without complying. In September 2026, for example, FIU-IND issued non-compliance notices to fifteen offshore crypto platforms under Section 13 of the PMLA, and sought the removal of their apps and website addresses from public access in India. Earlier, in late 2023 and 2024, several well-known offshore platforms faced similar action, including show-cause notices and temporary blocks on their websites and apps in India.

The pattern is consistent: platforms that ignore the registration requirement eventually face enforcement, and when that happens, ordinary users can suddenly find deposits stuck, apps unavailable, or withdrawals delayed. Checking registration before you deposit is far easier than recovering funds after an enforcement action.

## The 5-Step Check: Verify Any Exchange in Minutes

### Step 1: Find the platform's real legal name

An exchange's brand name is rarely its registered company name. "CoinDCX," for instance, is a brand; the company behind it has a different legal name. Look for the legal entity name in the platform's terms of service, privacy policy, or the footer of its website — usually under headings like "Legal," "About Us," or "Company Details." Write this name down. Every later step depends on matching the legal name, not the logo.

### Step 2: Read the exchange's own compliance page

Most established platforms publish a compliance, legal, or regulatory page. A credible one will state plainly that the operating company is registered with FIU-IND as a reporting entity under the PMLA, and may describe its KYC and anti-money laundering policies. Treat vague wording with suspicion: phrases like "we follow Indian regulations" or "fully compliant" without naming the registration are marketing, not evidence. A platform that is genuinely registered usually has no reason to hide the details.

### Step 3: Cross-check FIU-IND's official announcements

FIU-IND publishes press releases and compliance notices through official government channels when it registers, warns about, or acts against platforms. Search FIU-IND's official announcements for the legal entity name you found in Step 1. Two things to look for:

- **Absence from enforcement lists:** if the platform appears in a recent non-compliance notice, treat that as a serious warning, even if its website still claims compliance.
- **Consistency over time:** a platform that has been operating in India for years, with full KYC and INR banking, and has never appeared in an enforcement notice, is a much stronger signal than a brand-new platform making fresh claims.

Note that FIU-IND does not maintain a simple public search box where you type a brand name and get a yes-or-no answer. That is exactly why the check is a combination of sources rather than a single lookup — and why any website that offers you a one-click "FIU verification badge" deserves scepticism.

### Step 4: Check the tax and banking signals

A platform genuinely operating within India's framework leaves visible traces in everyday use:

- **1% TDS on trades:** platforms serving Indian users are required to deduct 1% tax deducted at source (TDS) on crypto transfers above the threshold, and that TDS should appear in your transaction history and in your Form 26AS or Annual Information Statement. A platform that never deducts TDS on INR trades is either not operating as an Indian-facing registered entity or is leaving the entire tax burden on you.
- **INR deposits and withdrawals through Indian banks or UPI:** registered Indian-facing platforms support INR rails with proper banking partners. Platforms that only accept crypto deposits, or route INR through informal peer-to-peer arrangements, are harder to verify and carry extra risk.
- **Full KYC before trading:** if a platform lets you deposit and trade large amounts with only an email address, its compliance claims do not match its behaviour.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate on crypto gains and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

### Step 5: Look for the warning signs

Finally, scan for red flags that contradict a registration claim:

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| No KYC required, or KYC only after large deposits | Registered entities must verify customers; skipping KYC contradicts the claim |
| Claims "FIU-approved" or "government-guaranteed" | FIU-IND registers reporting entities; it does not approve, license, or guarantee platforms |
| No legal entity name anywhere on the site | You cannot verify a company that will not name itself |
| Appears in a recent FIU-IND non-compliance notice | Direct evidence the platform was operating outside the framework |
| App or website suddenly blocked in India | Often follows enforcement action against unregistered platforms |
| Pressure to deposit quickly, or bonuses for large first deposits | A classic scam pattern, unrelated to any real registration |

If two or more of these appear, treat the platform as unverified, whatever its homepage says.

## What Registration Does NOT Mean

This is the part beginners most often get wrong, so it deserves to be stated plainly:

- **It is not a SEBI licence.** Crypto exchanges in India are not regulated by SEBI the way stockbrokers are. FIU registration is an anti-money laundering registration, not a markets licence.
- **It is not a safety guarantee.** Registration says nothing about whether the platform is well-run, solvent, or secure. A registered platform can still be hacked, mismanaged, or shut down.
- **It is not deposit insurance.** Unlike bank deposits, which carry DICGC insurance up to a limit, crypto held on an exchange has no equivalent government-backed insurance in India.
- **It does not make crypto itself regulated.** The Finance Ministry has repeatedly cautioned that crypto assets remain unregulated as an asset class in India, with no regulatory recourse for losses from such transactions.

Registration is a necessary first filter, not a sufficient reason to trust a platform with your money. Combine it with the usual safety habits: a strong unique password, authenticator-based two-factor authentication, withdrawal address whitelisting, and keeping only trading funds on an exchange.

## Registered vs Unregistered: A Quick Comparison

| | FIU-registered platform | Unregistered platform |
|---|---|---|
| KYC | Mandatory before trading | Often skipped or minimal |
| 1% TDS | Deducted and visible in records | Usually absent |
| INR banking | Indian bank/UPI rails | Often crypto-only or informal P2P |
| Enforcement risk | Low, while compliant | High — notices, blocks, app removal |
| Your recourse if it fails | Limited, but records exist | Very limited, and records may not |
| Safety of your funds | Still your responsibility | Still your responsibility |

Notice the last row is the same in both columns. Registration improves the legal picture; it does not remove the fundamental rule of crypto custody — funds on any exchange are funds you have entrusted to someone else.

## What to Do If Your Exchange Is Not Registered

If your check comes back negative or inconclusive, do not panic, but do act:

1. **Stop depositing fresh funds** until you understand the platform's legal status.
2. **Consider withdrawing** to a platform you have verified, or to your own wallet if you understand self-custody and have backed up your recovery phrase safely offline.
3. **Keep complete records** of every deposit, trade, and withdrawal. You still need these for your income tax return, and they are your evidence if anything goes wrong.
4. **Do not rely on customer support promises.** If a platform's support team tells you registration is "in process," ask for the legal entity name and check it yourself. "In process" has been used as a stalling line by platforms that never completed registration.
5. **Report suspected fraud** through the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or the cybercrime reporting portal if you believe you have been misled.

## Your 2-Minute FIU Registration Checklist

Before you deposit on any exchange, run through this list:

- [ ] You found the platform's legal company name, not just its brand name
- [ ] Its compliance or legal page states FIU-IND reporting-entity registration plainly
- [ ] The name does not appear in recent FIU-IND non-compliance notices
- [ ] Full KYC is required before trading
- [ ] 1% TDS appears on trades, and INR deposits work through normal banking rails
- [ ] No red flags: no "government-guaranteed" claims, no pressure tactics, no hidden identity

If every box is ticked, you have done the verification a careful beginner should do. If any box fails, pause and investigate before trusting the platform with your money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I check if a crypto exchange is FIU-registered in India?

Start with the exchange's own legal or compliance page and note its registered company name, which is often different from its brand name. Then cross-check that name against FIU-IND's official press releases and compliance notices, and look for consistent signs such as full KYC, INR banking rails, and 1% TDS deduction on trades. No single badge on a website proves registration — the check is the combination of these signals.

### Does FIU registration mean my crypto is safe on an exchange?

No. FIU-IND registration means the platform is registered as a reporting entity under India's anti-money laundering law and must follow KYC and record-keeping rules. It is not a SEBI licence, not a guarantee of the platform's solvency, and not deposit insurance. Your funds on an exchange are still exposed to hacking, fraud, and business failure, so registration is only one factor in choosing a platform.

### What should I do if my exchange is not FIU-registered?

Avoid depositing fresh funds until you understand the platform's legal status, and consider moving your crypto to a platform you have verified or to self-custody if you know how to manage a wallet safely. Keep complete records of every deposit, trade, and withdrawal for your tax filing, and never trust a platform just because it claims to be registered — verify the claim yourself before trusting it with money.

## The Bottom Line

FIU-IND registration is the single most useful legal filter Indian beginners have when choosing a crypto exchange — but only when you verify it rather than accept it. Find the legal name, read the compliance page, cross-check official announcements, confirm the tax and banking signals, and watch for red flags. The whole check takes a few minutes, and it is the difference between choosing a platform deliberately and trusting a slogan.

Once you have verified a platform, the rest of the safety work is still yours: strong passwords, authenticator-based two-factor authentication, small test transfers before large ones, and keeping long-term holdings somewhere you control. Registration tells you a platform follows India's anti-money laundering law. It does not tell you it deserves your trust — that part, you earn through your own checks.
