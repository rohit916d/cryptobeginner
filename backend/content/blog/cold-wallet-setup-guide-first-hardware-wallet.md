# Cold Wallet Setup Guide: Your First Hardware Wallet

A proper cold wallet setup is the moment many beginners stop merely holding a balance on an app and start controlling their own keys. A cold wallet — usually a small hardware wallet — keeps your private keys offline, on a physical device, instead of on a phone or exchange that is always connected to the internet. This walkthrough takes you through your first setup slowly, step by step, so you understand what each screen is asking and what must never be rushed.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## Before You Unbox: Buy Safely and Prepare

The setup starts before the device arrives. Buy only from the manufacturer's official store or an authorised reseller, and avoid second-hand devices and marketplace listings from unknown sellers. A genuine device should arrive sealed, without any pre-filled recovery sheet, scratch card with words, or instruction telling you to use a phrase supplied in the box. If a recovery phrase comes printed inside the packaging, that is a serious warning sign — a real wallet generates a fresh phrase on the device during your own setup, and nobody else should ever have seen it.

In India, a hardware wallet typically costs several thousand rupees once delivery and import charges are included, so treat it like buying a safe, not a phone accessory. Keep the invoice. Before setup, prepare a quiet, private space with no onlookers, a reliable pen, and the backup cards supplied in the box.

## Step 1: Check the Device and Install the Official App

Open the box and check that the device, cable, and recovery cards are present and the packaging shows no tampering. Then type the manufacturer's official website address manually — do not click a link from an email, message, or video — and download the companion app. Fake apps and look-alike download pages are a known phishing route.

Connect the device with its supplied cable. The app will normally run a genuineness check and may offer a firmware update. Firmware is the device's internal software; updating it inside the verified official app, before you create the wallet, ensures you start with the latest security fixes. If any website asks you to enter a recovery phrase to "validate" a brand-new device, stop: a new setup never requires that.

## Step 2: Create the Wallet and Set a Strong PIN

Choose the option to set up as a new wallet. The device will ask you to create a PIN code. This PIN protects the physical device if it falls into someone else's hands, and repeated wrong guesses will typically wipe the device after a set number of attempts. Pick a PIN you can remember reliably but that is not a birth year, phone fragment, or simple sequence. Enter it only on the device, using its buttons or touchscreen as the model requires.

Understand the difference early: the PIN unlocks this one device. The recovery phrase, which comes next, can recreate the entire wallet on any compatible device. Losing the PIN is an inconvenience if you still hold the phrase. Losing the phrase is permanent. Giving the phrase to someone else gives them full control, even if they never touch your device or learn your PIN.

## Step 3: Write Down Your Recovery Phrase — Offline Only

The device will now display a recovery phrase, usually 12 or 24 ordinary English words, one by one, on its own screen. This is the most important five minutes of the entire cold wallet setup. Write each word on the supplied card, in order, checking spelling and numbering as you go. Work only from the device screen, in private, and do not read the words aloud.

Follow these rules without exception:

- **Never type the phrase** into a phone, computer, notes app, email, or website.
- **Never photograph it** or keep a screenshot, scan, or cloud copy.
- **Never enter it anywhere** except on a trusted device during a genuine recovery.
- **Never share it** — no support agent, exchange, or community group ever needs it.

Most devices then ask you to confirm a few words by position, to prove the backup was written correctly. Complete this check honestly. Afterwards, store the card somewhere dry and private, protected from fire and water as far as practical. Consider a second copy in a separate secure location, because a single card in a single place is itself a single point of failure.

## Step 4: Add an Account and Verify Your Receiving Address

With the wallet created, use the companion app to add an account for the network you intend to use, for example Bitcoin or Ethereum. The app can show balances and prepare transactions, but the address you receive into must always be verified on the device screen itself. Malware can swap a copied address for an attacker's address, so compare the app's address with the one confirmed on the hardware wallet before anyone sends funds to it.

Trust the device screen, not only the computer screen — repeat this for every new address, and be careful with network selection. The same coin can exist on several networks, and sending on the wrong network can make funds hard or impossible to recover.

## Step 5: Make a Small Test Transfer First

Do not move everything at once. Start with a small test transfer from your Indian exchange account — for example from CoinDCX, WazirX, or ZebPay — to the verified address from Step 4. On the withdrawal screen, choose the network carefully so it exactly matches the network of your receiving address, enter the amount, and double-check the first and last characters of the address before confirming.

Note the costs before confirming: the exchange may charge a withdrawal fee, a network fee applies, and 1% TDS can apply on certain transactions in India. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Keep the transaction ID and a note of the date, exchange, amount, and network in your records file. Wait for the transfer to appear in the companion app and confirm the address and amount are correct. Only after a successful test should you consider a larger transfer, repeating the same verification rather than trusting memory.

## Step 6: Practise Recovery Thinking Before You Need It

A backup you have never thought through is a hope, not a plan. At minimum, make sure you know where the phrase is stored, that the handwriting is legible, and that the words are in the correct order. Some manufacturers offer a recovery-check feature inside the official app that lets you verify your written phrase against the device without exposing it online — use only that official, device-guided method if available, and never an external website offering the same service.

Also decide now what happens if the device stops working: order a replacement from an official source, choose restore, and enter the phrase on the new device itself. Knowing this in advance prevents the panic that leads people to type their phrase into a fake support page — exactly the moment scammers wait for.

## Common Setup Mistakes Indian Beginners Make

| Mistake | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|
| Buying a used or discounted device from an unknown seller | The seller may already know the recovery phrase |
| Saving the phrase in phone photos or cloud notes | Any account breach exposes the full wallet |
| Skipping the small test transfer | A wrong network or address error affects the full amount |
| Confirming an address only on the computer screen | Clipboard or display malware can substitute an address |
| Telling family the phrase location publicly or storing it labelled "crypto password" | It invites theft and social-engineering attempts |

## Your Ongoing Cold Wallet Routine

Setup is not the end. Keep the app and firmware updated through the official app, verify every address on the device, and keep the device and its backup in separate secure places. Update your records after every transfer so statements, transaction IDs, and addresses line up at tax time. A hardware wallet protects keys from online theft — it does not protect you from approving a fraudulent transaction, so slow verification remains your real defence.

## Conclusion

A careful first setup takes under an hour: buy officially, verify the device, set a PIN, write the phrase offline, confirm your address on the device screen, and prove the route with a small test transfer. Do those six things patiently and you have built the foundation of genuine self-custody.
