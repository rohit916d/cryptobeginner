# What Is a Crypto Launchpad? New Token Sales Explained

New crypto tokens rarely appear on a big exchange out of nowhere. Before a token reaches a trading screen, it usually goes on sale somewhere first — and that somewhere is often a crypto launchpad. If you have ever seen a Telegram group promise "early access" to a brand-new token, or watched a project announce an "IDO" with a countdown timer, a launchpad is probably behind it.

A crypto launchpad is worth understanding even if you never plan to use one. It explains how new tokens reach the market, why some tokens jump and crash on listing day, and why so many "early access" offers aimed at Indian beginners turn out to be scams. This guide explains what a crypto launchpad is, how one works step by step, the main types, and the risks — in plain language.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. New-token sales are among the highest-risk corners of crypto, and most new tokens lose value.

## What Is a Crypto Launchpad?

A crypto launchpad is a platform that helps new crypto projects sell their tokens to the public for the first time. Instead of the project building its own sales website and payment system, it uses the launchpad's ready-made setup: the sale page, the token-distribution rules, the wallet connection, and the audience of people who follow the launchpad for new launches.

Think of it like a cinema's advance-booking counter. The film (the token) is ready, the counter (the launchpad) handles the tickets, and people who arrive early can book seats before the general release. The difference is that in crypto, the "ticket" is a token whose price can swing wildly the moment trading starts — and unlike a cinema ticket, there is no guarantee the show is any good.

The first sale of a token through a launchpad is usually called an IDO (Initial DEX Offering) when the token lists on a decentralised exchange, or an IEO (Initial Exchange Offering) when it lists on a centralised exchange. The older ICO (Initial Coin Offering), where a project sold tokens directly from its own website, worked similarly but with far fewer safeguards.

## Why Do Projects Use Launchpads?

Building a new crypto project is expensive, and launching a token is one of the hardest parts. A launchpad helps a project in four ways.

**Funding.** The token sale raises money the project uses to build its product. A launchpad gives the project access to a ready pool of buyers instead of starting from zero.

**Distribution.** Instead of a handful of large buyers holding all the tokens, a launchpad can spread them across hundreds or thousands of smaller buyers. A wider spread is generally seen as healthier for a token.

**Liquidity.** After the sale, the token needs somewhere to trade. Many launchpads help the project list on an exchange or add the token to a trading pool so buyers can actually buy and sell afterwards.

**Trust.** This is the most delicate point. A launchpad that checks projects before listing them — reviewing the team, the code, and the token economics — gives buyers some reason to trust a project they have never heard of. But "checked by a launchpad" is not a guarantee. Many projects that passed a launchpad's checks have still failed or turned out to be scams, so the check should be treated as a filter, not a safety certificate.

## How a Crypto Launchpad Works: Step by Step

A typical launchpad sale follows the same broad pattern, though the details differ from one platform to another.

**1. The project applies.** The project submits its details to the launchpad: what it builds, who the team is, how many tokens exist, and how the tokens are shared out. Some launchpads review the code and the token economics; others accept almost anything.

**2. The sale is announced.** The launchpad publishes a sale page with the token price, the total amount being sold, the date and time, and the rules for joining. This is the point where scammers often create fake copies of the page.

**3. Users prepare.** To join, you usually need three things: a compatible crypto wallet, the cryptocurrency used to pay (often a stablecoin or a major coin), and sometimes a holding of the launchpad's own token. Some launchpads also require a KYC identity check — a government ID and a selfie — before you can take part.

**4. The sale opens.** At the announced time, eligible users commit funds. Some sales are first-come, first-served and fill up in seconds. Others use a lottery: you enter, and a random draw decides who gets to buy. A third model guarantees an allocation based on how many of the launchpad's tokens you hold — the more you hold, the bigger your share.

**5. Tokens are distributed.** After the sale, buyers receive their tokens. Sometimes the full amount arrives at once; often the tokens unlock gradually over weeks or months through a vesting schedule, so early buyers cannot sell everything on day one.

**6. The token lists.** The token starts trading on an exchange or a decentralised trading pool. This is the moment when the price can move sharply in either direction, and it is also when many early buyers decide whether to hold or sell.

## Types of Crypto Launchpads

Not all launchpads work the same way. The main differences are who runs them and how buyers get access.

| Launchpad type | Who runs it | How access usually works |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange launchpad | A centralised exchange | Account + KYC; sometimes requires holding the exchange's token |
| DEX launchpad | A decentralised platform | Wallet connection; often requires holding or staking the platform's token |
| Multi-chain launchpad | An independent platform | Supports several blockchains; rules vary by sale |
| Community launchpad | A DAO or community project | Community voting or membership decides which projects launch |

**Exchange launchpads** are run by centralised exchanges. They feel familiar to Indian users who already trade on exchanges: you log in, complete KYC, and buy with funds already in your account. The exchange's reputation is on the line, so these platforms usually vet projects more carefully — but they also restrict access by country, and some do not accept Indian users at all.

**DEX launchpads** run on a blockchain without a central company. You connect your own wallet and interact with smart contracts directly. There is no customer support to call if something goes wrong, and a mistake such as approving a fake contract can drain a wallet. These suit experienced users, not beginners.

**Multi-chain launchpads** work across several blockchains, so a project on one chain and a buyer on another can still meet. They are flexible, but each sale can have different rules, which makes them harder to follow.

**Community launchpads** let a community vote on which projects get launched. The idea is that a crowd of users filters out weak projects. In practice, voting can be gamed by large token holders, so community approval is not proof of quality either.

## The Main Allocation Models

How much of a new token you can buy depends on the launchpad's allocation model. There are four common ones, and each favours a different kind of participant.

**First-come, first-served.** The sale opens at a fixed time and the tokens go to whoever commits funds fastest. This model rewards speed and a fast internet connection. In India, it often means sales fill up within seconds of opening, and many genuine buyers are left out entirely.

**Lottery.** Everyone who qualifies gets a ticket, and a random draw picks the winners. This is fairer for small buyers, but you can prepare perfectly and still get nothing. Some launchpads give extra tickets to users who hold more of the platform's token.

**Guaranteed allocation.** Your share is decided by how much of the launchpad's own token you hold or stake. Hold more, get a bigger allocation. This sounds fair, but it pushes users to buy and lock up the launchpad's token — which itself can fall in price — just to qualify for sales.

**Tiered access.** Users are sorted into levels based on their holdings. Higher tiers get earlier access or larger allocations. Lower tiers may only get in if tokens remain after the higher tiers have bought.

Whichever model a launchpad uses, remember that getting an allocation is not the same as making a profit. A guaranteed allocation in a weak project is worse than no allocation at all.

## What a Launchpad Sale Actually Costs You

A launchpad sale is not free money, and the costs go beyond the token price itself.

First, there is the **token price**. Launch prices are set by the project and the launchpad, and they can look cheap compared with later prices — or expensive. A low price per token means nothing on its own; what matters is how many tokens exist in total.

Second, there are **network fees**. Buying on a blockchain costs a gas fee, and fees spike when many people try to buy at the same moment. A small purchase can lose a large share of its value to fees alone.

Third, there is the **cost of qualifying**. If a launchpad requires you to hold or stake its own token, that token can fall in value while you wait for the sale. The loss on the qualifying token can easily be larger than any gain from the new token.

Fourth, there are **hidden costs after listing**. If the token's price falls after listing, or if your tokens unlock slowly through vesting while the price slides, the real return can be very different from the launch-day excitement. And in India, any gain you do make is taxed, which brings us to the tax note below.

## The Risks Beginners Must Understand

New-token sales carry risks that are bigger and less obvious than ordinary crypto buying. These are the ones that catch Indian beginners most often.

**The project may fail.** Most new crypto projects do not survive. The team may run out of money, the product may never work, or users may simply never arrive. A launchpad listing does not change those odds.

**The price can crash after listing.** Early buyers often sell as soon as trading opens, which can push the price below the launch price within minutes. Buying at launch and watching the price fall is one of the most common beginner experiences with launchpads.

**The launchpad itself may be fake.** Scammers copy real launchpad pages, run fake "pre-sale" countdowns, and promote them through Telegram and WhatsApp groups targeting Indian users. They ask you to connect your wallet or send crypto to an address — and the money never comes back. Real launchpads never ask you to send crypto to a personal wallet address, and never run sales through WhatsApp.

**Vesting can lock you in.** If your tokens unlock over months, you cannot sell during that period even if the price collapses. Always read the vesting schedule before joining a sale.

**Regulatory uncertainty.** India has no dedicated law for crypto launchpads, and rules can change. A platform that works for Indian users today may restrict access tomorrow. Using an offshore platform also raises questions about how to report the activity for tax.

**Tax note:** crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Gains from launchpad tokens are treated like other crypto gains, and you should keep a record of the purchase price, date, fees, and sale details for every transaction.

## How to Check a Launchpad Before Using It

If you are curious about launchpads, a short checklist can filter out the worst options. This is not a recommendation to use any of them — it is a way to avoid the most obvious traps.

1. **Start at the official site.** Type the launchpad's web address yourself or use a bookmark. Never click a sale link from Telegram, WhatsApp, YouTube comments, or a sponsored post.
2. **Check the project's basics.** Read the project's own website and whitepaper. Can you explain in one sentence what it does and who uses it? If not, step back.
3. **Look at the token distribution.** How much of the total supply goes to the team, early investors, and the public sale? A sale that offers only a tiny slice to the public while insiders hold most of the tokens is a warning sign.
4. **Read the vesting schedule.** When do your tokens actually unlock? A long lock-up means you cannot exit quickly.
5. **Verify the contract address.** If the token has a contract address, check it on a block explorer and compare it character by character with the address on the official sale page. Fake tokens use lookalike addresses.
6. **Check whether Indians are accepted.** Read the platform's terms or FAQ for country restrictions and KYC requirements before you deposit anything.
7. **Use a separate wallet.** If you ever join a sale, use a wallet that holds only the funds for that sale — never your main wallet with all your crypto in it.
8. **Search for scam reports.** A quick search for the launchpad or project name plus "scam" or "fake" can surface warnings from other users.

If any step feels confusing or rushed, that is a signal to stop. Legitimate sales do not need you to act in the next ten minutes.

## Launchpads and Indian Beginners: A Realistic View

For most Indian beginners, launchpads are a topic to learn about, not a place to start. The practical barriers are real: country restrictions, KYC steps, wallet setup, network fees, and the need to hold another token just to qualify. The risks are also higher than buying an established coin on a regulated Indian exchange, because the project is new, the price is untested, and the sale itself attracts scammers.

A sensible learning path is to first get comfortable with the basics — buying a small amount on an FIU-IND registered Indian exchange, moving it to a wallet you control, and keeping proper records. Once those habits are solid, you can study launchpads from the sidelines: follow a few sales, watch how prices behave after listing, and notice how often the early excitement fades. That observation costs nothing and teaches more than joining a sale unprepared.

If you do decide to explore a launchpad later, treat it as an experiment with money you can afford to lose completely, use a separate wallet, and never borrow money or use funds meant for rent, EMIs, or emergencies to buy new tokens.

## The Bottom Line

A crypto launchpad is a platform where new tokens go on sale for the first time, before they list on an exchange. It gives projects funding and an audience, and it gives buyers early access — but early access is not an advantage on its own. Most new tokens are high-risk, prices often fall after listing, and fake launchpads are a favourite tool of scammers targeting Indian users.

The safest way to think about launchpads is as a window into how the crypto market works, not as a shortcut to profits. Learn the mechanics, watch a few sales from the outside, keep your records straight for tax, and only ever risk money you can afford to lose. The crypto market will still be there tomorrow — there is no need to rush into a sale today.
