October's US inflation report came in hotter than economists expected, and crypto felt it within minutes. On Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported that the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 0.4% in October against a forecast of 0.3%, and core CPI — which strips out food and energy — rose 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast. Bitcoin, which had been trading near $74,200 just before the 8:30 AM ET release (7:00 PM IST), slipped to about $73,600 in the minutes after, according to CoinDesk. Ethereum fell further, gold dropped more than 2%, and roughly $200 million in leveraged crypto long positions were liquidated within five minutes, according to crypto.news and Coinotag. This guide explains what the hot US CPI print actually said, why it moved Bitcoin, what traders are now expecting from the Federal Reserve's 9 December meeting, and what — if anything — it means for crypto users in India.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported market development; it does not recommend any exchange, token or investment, and it does not predict any price movement.

## What Happened: The October CPI Print Came In Hot

The numbers landed at 8:30 AM ET on 10 November — 7:00 PM IST — and they were hotter than forecast on both the measures traders watch most closely.

| Measure | October reading | Forecast | September reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI (month-on-month) | +0.4% | +0.3% | +0.6% |
| Core CPI (month-on-month) | +0.3% | +0.2% | +0.3% |

Headline inflation slowed from September's 0.6% monthly rise, but it still beat the 0.3% forecast. Core inflation was the more stubborn number: 0.3% for a third consecutive month, against a forecast of 0.2%, and above every estimate in a pre-release survey that ranged from 0.19% to 0.28%, according to Coinotag. Inside the report, food prices jumped 0.5% on the month, energy rose 0.7%, gasoline gained 1.0%, and housing costs increased 0.3%, according to Bitcoin Magazine.

The market reaction was immediate. Unchained reported that Bitcoin swung more than $1,100 in the five minutes after the release, ranging from a high of $74,599 to a low of $73,400 before steadying in the high-$73,000s. Coinotag's live feed put the five-minute range at $74,770 down to $73,525, closing the window near $73,709. CoinDesk reported Bitcoin dipping about 1% to $73,600 right after the print, and CryptoPotato put the low at around $73,600 as well — the lowest price in several months. Ethereum came under heavier selling pressure, falling about 3.18% to nearly $2,064, according to Live-Rates data cited by stockbiz, and trading near $2,070 shortly after, according to Coinotag. The pain was not limited to crypto: spot gold fell more than 2% to around $4,048–$4,054 an ounce, Nasdaq 100 futures fell 1.15%, S&P 500 futures lost 0.5%, and yields on two- to five-year US Treasury bonds rose at least 10 basis points on the day, according to CoinDesk and Bitcoin Magazine.

For Indian readers, the rupee price told the same story. ET Markets' live crypto page showed Bitcoin at ₹69,35,822 on the evening of 10 November, down 1.22% on the day, with the dollar at about ₹93.44. The fall was modest in percentage terms — about 1% — but the speed of it is what made the day notable, and the speed is what this guide will unpack.

## Why a Hot Inflation Print Hits Bitcoin

Bitcoin was designed to operate independently of governments and central banks. So why does a US inflation report move its price at all? The chain of logic runs through interest rates, and it is the same chain we explained in our general guide to how US inflation reports move Bitcoin.

First, inflation data shapes what traders expect the US Federal Reserve — the Fed — to do with interest rates. When inflation runs hot, the Fed is less likely to cut rates and more likely to keep them high or raise them, because higher rates are its main tool for cooling price rises. When inflation cools, rate cuts look more likely.

Second, interest-rate expectations shape the dollar and the appetite for risk. Higher expected rates tend to strengthen the dollar and make safe assets — especially US government bonds — more attractive, because they now pay a better return. Money that might have chased riskier assets stays in safer ones instead.

Third, Bitcoin still trades like a risk asset. Despite the "digital gold" narrative, Bitcoin usually behaves more like a high-growth technology stock: it tends to do well when money is cheap and plentiful, and to struggle when money gets tighter. That is why a hot CPI print, which pushes rate expectations up, so often pushes Bitcoin down in the same hour — and why the same mechanism showed up in the Nasdaq-led sell-off we covered on 6 November, when tech stocks and crypto fell together.

There is a second channel that matters just as much on CPI days: leverage. A large share of crypto trading happens with borrowed money, and leveraged positions are fragile by design. When the price moves sharply against a leveraged trader, the exchange forcibly closes the position — a liquidation — and that forced selling pushes the price further, which liquidates the next set of positions. That cascade is exactly what happened in the minutes after the CPI release, and it deserves its own section.

## The Five-Minute Wipeout: $200 Million in Longs Liquidated

The most dramatic number from the morning was not the price of Bitcoin. It was the speed at which leveraged traders were wiped out. CryptoBriefing reported that $178 million in crypto long positions were liquidated within one hour of the CPI release. Coinotag and crypto.news put the figure at about $200 million in leveraged longs forcibly closed within five minutes of the figures landing — with Bitcoin slipping below $74,000 and Ethereum under $2,050 in the same window, both their lowest marks in 11 weeks.

A "long" is a bet that the price will rise. Traders open longs with borrowed money — leverage — so a small price move in their favour becomes a large gain. The trade-off is that a small move against them becomes a large loss, and when the loss eats through the trader's margin, the exchange closes the position automatically. On a normal day, liquidations trickle through the market. On a CPI day, the release lands at a fixed second — 8:30 AM ET sharp — and every algorithm and trader reacts at once. The first wave of selling triggers the first wave of liquidations, and the liquidations themselves become selling, which triggers more liquidations. The whole loop can run its course in minutes.

This is the same mechanism behind the liquidation cascade we explained in late October, when a market-wide chain reaction amplified a price fall. The direction was different then, and the trigger was different — but the plumbing is identical. It is also why the short squeeze we covered on 5 November ran the other way: when shorts are the crowded side, a sudden rise forces them to buy back, pushing the price up. The lesson for beginners is the same in both directions: leverage turns a 1% price move into a much larger event for the people using it, and the damage is concentrated in the minutes around scheduled news.

None of this means the market "overreacted" or "underreacted" — those are judgements nobody can make in real time. What it means is that the CPI print arrived into a market carrying heavy leveraged long positions, and the combination of a hot number and crowded positioning produced a fast, mechanical flush. By the time of writing, Bitcoin had steadied near $73,500–$73,900, according to Bitcoin Magazine and Coinotag — still below $74,000, but no longer in free fall.

## The December Fed Meeting: What Traders Are Now Pricing

The CPI print matters most for what it implies about the Fed's final meeting of 2026, on 9 December. The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75%–4.00% at its October meeting, and the question since then has been whether it will raise rates again in December or hold.

After the hot print, Reuters — cited by crypto.news — reported that markets had fully priced in a 25 basis point increase at the 9 December meeting, which would lift the federal funds target range from 3.75%–4.00% to 4.00%–4.25%. Bitcoin Magazine reported that some traders went further, boosting bets on a half-point hike at the same meeting. Bond traders moved first: yields on two- to five-year Treasuries rose at least 10 basis points on the day.

A 25 basis point move is a quarter of a percentage point — small in absolute terms, but large in what it signals. It would mean the Fed, which spent 2026 cutting rates from higher levels, has decided inflation is still too hot to leave rates where they are. It would also be the first rate hike of the current cycle, and the market is treating it as a live possibility rather than a remote one. That is a meaningful shift from just a few weeks ago, when the debate was about whether the Fed would cut again.

It is worth stating plainly what this does and does not mean. It does not mean the Fed has decided anything — the December meeting is still a month away, and two more data releases will land before it, including the Producer Price Index on Friday, 13 November and the minutes of the Fed's October meeting on 18 November. A single CPI print can move market pricing a lot, and market pricing can move back just as fast on the next release. What the print did was change the balance of expectations: the "higher for longer" case got stronger, and the "cuts resume soon" case got weaker, at least for now.

For context, the last time crypto watched a US data release this closely was the October jobs report on 6 November, which came in far stronger than expected — 165,000 jobs added against forecasts of roughly 59,000–65,000 — and also pushed rate-hike odds higher. The two reports together tell a consistent story: the US economy is running hotter than forecasters expected, and that heat is showing up in both jobs and prices.

## The Missing Number: No Year-on-Year Inflation Rate This Time

Regular readers of inflation coverage will notice something odd about this report: it carried no 12-month inflation rate — neither for headline CPI nor for core CPI. That is not an error, and it is not something the BLS is hiding. It is a quirk with a specific cause.

The year-on-year rate compares this October's prices with last October's prices. But the BLS never collected a full set of prices in October 2025, because the US government shutdown that month disrupted its survey work. With no October 2025 base to compare against, there is no year-on-year figure to publish. Bank of America estimated before the release that the annual rates were running near 3.8% for headline inflation and 2.7% for core, according to Bitcoin Magazine — but those are a bank's estimates, not the official figures, and they should be treated as such.

The BLS is scheduled to publish November CPI on 10 December, and that report should restore the year-on-year comparison. Until then, traders and journalists are working with month-on-month numbers only, which makes each monthly reading carry more weight than usual — one reason the 0.1 percentage point miss on both headline and core moved markets as much as it did.

## What This Means for Indian Crypto Users

For most Indian crypto users, the honest answer is: less than the headlines suggest, and the important parts are not about the price.

**Your tax bill has not changed.** A US inflation print does not alter Indian law. Gains on transferring virtual digital assets are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess — 31.2% in total — with no set-off of losses, and sales attract 1% TDS under Section 194S. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. If you sold during the CPI dip, the tax treatment is the same as on any other day; if you held, nothing in this report creates a tax event by itself.

**The rupee price follows the global price.** Indian exchanges price Bitcoin in rupees, but the rupee price tracks the global dollar price converted at the prevailing exchange rate. On the evening of 10 November, Bitcoin traded near ₹69.35 lakh on Indian live-price pages, with the dollar at about ₹93.44. When the global price falls 1%, the rupee price generally falls by roughly the same amount, unless the rupee itself moves sharply against the dollar in the same window.

**Volatile days can widen the gaps you pay.** Indian exchanges sometimes trade at a small premium or discount to global prices — the stablecoin premium we covered on 6 November is the clearest example — and that gap tends to widen during fast-moving macro events, when arbitrage slows down. On a CPI morning you may notice your exchange's price lagging or overshooting the global quote by a percent or two. That is normal market plumbing under stress, not a malfunction, and not an opportunity to chase without understanding the costs: fees, TDS, and transfer times all eat into any apparent gap.

**Do not trade the news event itself.** The five minutes after a CPI release are a casino. Spreads widen, prices spike in both directions, and stop-losses get hunted. Professionals with algorithms struggle here; beginners get destroyed. The $200 million in liquidations is the receipt for that lesson. If you want to learn how macro news moves crypto, the safe way is to watch and read — not to place leveraged bets into the release.

**Watch out for "CPI trade" scams.** A high-profile market event like this one attracts impersonators: fake "CPI trading signals" groups, fake analysts promising returns from the Fed meeting, and messages claiming insider knowledge of the next print. There is no legitimate way to know a CPI number before the BLS publishes it, and anyone selling that claim is running a scam. Report fraud on India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the 1930 helpline, and never share a seed phrase or password with anyone claiming to help you trade the news.

## What to Watch Next

Four dates will show whether the hot print was a one-off or the start of a new direction.

The first is Friday, 13 November, when the Producer Price Index (PPI) — the wholesale-inflation measure — is released. PPI is an early hint of where consumer prices are heading, and a hot PPI would reinforce the CPI message, while a cool one would soften it.

The second is 18 November, when the minutes of the Fed's October meeting are published. The minutes show what policymakers were actually debating, and whether any of them were already leaning toward a December hike before the CPI data arrived.

The third is the Fed's own meeting on 9 December, where the rate decision will actually be made. Between now and then, the market will re-price the odds with every new data point, and the pricing on the day of the meeting may look quite different from today's.

The fourth is 10 December, when November CPI is published — the report that should restore the missing year-on-year comparison and give the first clean read on whether October's heat carried into November.

## The Bottom Line

On 10 November 2026, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported October CPI up 0.4% on the month against a 0.3% forecast, and core CPI up 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast — the third straight month at 0.3%. Bitcoin fell about 1% within minutes to around $73,600, Ethereum dropped about 3% to near $2,064, gold lost more than 2%, and roughly $200 million in leveraged long positions were liquidated within five minutes, according to CoinDesk, Unchained, Bitcoin Magazine, CryptoBriefing, Coinotag and crypto.news. Markets moved to fully price a 25 basis point Fed rate hike at the 9 December meeting, with some traders betting on a half-point move. The report carried no year-on-year inflation rate, because October 2025 prices were never collected during that year's government shutdown.

For Indian readers, the practical takeaway is narrow but useful: nothing about Indian tax or regulation changed, the rupee price simply tracked the global move, and the real lesson is about leverage and timing rather than direction. A 1% fall on a hot data print is a normal market event. A $200 million liquidation cascade in five minutes is a reminder of what happens when crowded leveraged bets meet a scheduled surprise. The next data points — PPI on 13 November, Fed minutes on 18 November, and the December meeting itself — will say more about where rates go from here than any single CPI print can.
