On 14 November 2026, the team behind stUSDT announced that the protocol is shutting down. The yield-bearing token that once held about $2.3 billion — roughly ₹21,600 crore at about ₹94 to the dollar — stopped paying interest the same day, and holders now have a six-month window to get their money back. If you hold stUSDT or its wrapped version, wstUSDT, the deadline matters more than the reason: the self-service redemption portal closes on 14 May 2027. > **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. This guide explains a protocol shutdown and the redemption steps the team has published; it does not recommend any token, platform or action.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The announcement came on 14 November 2026, in a notice published on the project's Medium page at 06:36 UTC and shared from the project's verified account the same day. The terms, as reported by Cointelegraph on 15 November and corroborated by Blockchain Reporter, Coindaro, TokenPost, KuCoin News and TradingView, are:

- **Rebase rewards ended on 14 November 2026.** From that date, stUSDT no longer generates interest. A balance left untouched now earns nothing.
- **stUSDT and wstUSDT will be discontinued.** Both versions of the token are being wound down, not just one.
- **A redemption window opened on 14 November 2026 and runs until 14 May 2027 at 23:59 UTC.** Holders redeem through the official portal at stusdt.io.
- **After the portal closes, requests go through customer support** and are processed in monthly batches through August 2027.
- **The team says all underlying assets remain fully backed.** It gave no figure, no custodian, no audit and no reason for the wind-down.

The numbers show how far the protocol had already shrunk before the announcement. According to DefiLlama data cited in the coverage, deposits in the stUSDT contracts stood at about $47.55 million on 13 November, fell to about $9.50 million on 14 November, and were about $8.63 million by 02:01 UTC on 15 November. That remaining $8.63 million — roughly ₹81 crore — is about 0.38% of the roughly $2.3 billion peak the protocol reached in November 2023. Most of the money had already left by the time the notice went up; the announcement formalised an exit that was, in practical terms, nearly complete.

## What stUSDT Actually Was

To understand the shutdown, it helps to understand what the token was. stUSDT was a real-world-asset protocol — a project that takes something from traditional finance and represents it as a token on a blockchain. In this case, the traditional asset was short-term US government debt, known as Treasury bills.

The mechanism worked like this. A user deposited USDT — the dollar-pegged stablecoin — into the stUSDT protocol and received stUSDT in return. The protocol used the deposited USDT to hold tokenised Treasury bills, and the interest those bills earned was passed back to holders by increasing the number of stUSDT tokens in their wallets. This automatic balance growth is called rebasing: instead of paying interest as a separate payment, the token itself grew in quantity.

wstUSDT was the wrapped, non-rebasing version of the same token. It was designed for use in decentralised finance, where a token whose balance keeps changing is awkward to use in lending pools and trading pairs. wstUSDT kept a fixed balance and instead rose in value relative to stUSDT over time. Holders of wstUSDT need to unwrap it back to stUSDT before they can redeem.

The protocol ran mainly on TRON, with a version on Ethereum. Its main partner in the TRON ecosystem was JustLend, the lending protocol, which ran a market where wstUSDT could be supplied and borrowed. On 14 November, JustLend announced it would switch off new deposits and loans in that market and later change its parameters — a knock-on effect that matters to anyone who supplied wstUSDT as collateral or borrowed against it.

## The Numbers: From $2.3 Billion to $8.6 Million

The scale of the decline is the most striking part of this story, and it is worth putting in a table because it explains why the shutdown is being treated as a footnote rather than a crisis.

| Date | Deposits in stUSDT contracts | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| November 2023 | About $2.3 billion | Peak — the protocol's high point |
| 13 November 2026 | About $47.55 million | About 2% of the peak remained |
| 14 November 2026 | About $9.50 million | About $38 million left in a single day |
| 15 November 2026 | About $8.63 million | About 0.38% of the peak |

Two points follow from these figures. First, the protocol had been shrinking for years before the announcement — from $2.3 billion to under $50 million is a decline of roughly 98% over three years. The shutdown is the end of a long fade, not a sudden collapse. Second, most of the remaining money moved out on the day of the announcement itself, before or around the time the notice was published. By the morning of 15 November, only about $8.6 million was left to redeem.

That small remaining figure is also why the shutdown has drawn relatively little attention compared with the exchange incidents of the past few weeks. The Binance hack in late October involved billions of dollars; the Unocoin incident this week involved about $200,000 and a major Indian exchange's entire service suspension. stUSDT's remaining $8.6 million is far smaller, and its holders are spread across TRON and Ethereum wallets rather than concentrated on one Indian platform. But for the people who still hold it — and TRON-based USDT is widely used in India — the deadline is just as real.

## What Holders Must Do, Step by Step

The redemption process has two steps, and the distinction matters: unstaking alone does not complete it. According to the team's notice and the coverage of it, the steps are:

**1. Move your tokens to your own wallet first.** If your stUSDT or wstUSDT sits on a centralised exchange, in a DeFi protocol such as JustLend, or in any other platform, move it to a personal Web3 wallet you control. The redemption portal works with your own wallet, not with a balance held on someone else's platform.

**2. Unwrap wstUSDT if you hold the wrapped version.** wstUSDT must be converted back to stUSDT before it can be unstaked. This is a separate on-chain step, and it needs a small amount of the network's native token — TRX on TRON, ETH on Ethereum — to pay the transaction fee.

**3. Unstake on the official portal.** Go to stusdt.io — the only address the notice gives — and unstake your stUSDT. Check the address character by character before connecting your wallet; wind-downs attract fake portals.

**4. Click Claim to receive USDT.** This is the step people miss. Unstaking releases your tokens from the staking contract; claiming is what actually sends USDT back to your wallet. Both steps must be completed.

**5. Do it before 14 May 2027 at 23:59 UTC.** That is when the self-service portal closes. After that date, redemption requests must go through customer support and will be processed in monthly batches through August 2027 — slower, manual, and with no guarantee of the same speed.

The team has said the unstaking fee is 0% from the effective date of the new arrangement, and no redemption fee has been announced beyond ordinary network gas fees. Fees charged before the effective date were processed under the previous fee structure and are not refundable.

## What Is Not Known

The announcement is notable as much for what it does not say as for what it does. Four gaps matter:

**No reason was given.** The notice does not attribute the wind-down to redemptions, to the yield environment, to regulatory pressure, or to the cost of maintaining the Treasury-bill structure behind the token. The team has simply said the protocol is being discontinued.

**No reserve breakdown was published.** The team states that all underlying assets remain fully backed, but it has not published a figure for the backing, named a custodian, or provided an audit. DefiLlama's listing for the protocol shows no audit. A claim of full backing without supporting evidence is a statement, not proof — and holders should treat it that way.

**No redemption ratio was specified.** The notice does not set out a fixed rate at which stUSDT converts back to USDT, beyond the mechanics of unstaking and claiming. In practice, the value depends on what remains in the protocol's contracts.

**The JustLend timetable is open-ended.** JustLend has said it will disable new supply and borrowing in its wstUSDT market "soon" and change its parameters later, without giving dates. Anyone with wstUSDT supplied or borrowed on JustLend should check the platform's own announcements rather than assuming the market will stay open until May.

None of this means the redemption will fail — the portal is open, the team has published a process, and the remaining amount is small relative to the protocol's history. But it does mean holders should not rely on assurances that have not been backed with evidence. The practical approach is simple: redeem through the official portal, inside the window, and do not wait for more information that may never come.

## The Scam Risk in Every Wind-Down

Protocol shutdowns are a favourite hunting ground for scammers, and the stUSDT team has warned about this directly. Its notice says the team will never message holders first, never ask for a recovery phrase, and never ask anyone to send funds in order to redeem. Those three rules are worth memorising, because every fake "support" message in the coming months will break at least one of them.

The pattern is familiar from earlier incidents covered on this site. After a shutdown or a hack, fake portals appear with near-identical names, fake support accounts message holders offering to "help" with redemption, and phishing links circulate in group chats and comment sections. The ask is always the same in the end: connect your wallet to a malicious site, approve a transaction, or share a seed phrase.

Three habits protect you:

- **Type the address yourself.** Go to stusdt.io by typing it or using a bookmark you created. Never click a redemption link from a message, a social media post, or a search advertisement.
- **Never share a seed phrase or private key.** No legitimate redemption process asks for one. Anyone who asks is a scammer, whatever they claim to be.
- **Verify before you approve.** When your wallet asks you to approve a transaction, read what it actually does. A redemption should unstake and claim; it should never ask you to approve unlimited spending of other tokens.

If you are unsure whether a message or a site is genuine, stop and check the project's official channels — the same Medium page and verified account that published the original notice — rather than trusting a message that arrived in your inbox.

## What This Means for Indian Users

For Indian readers, the stUSDT shutdown lands in a week when the rupee on-ramp to crypto has already been fragile. Coinbase's INR withdrawals remain halted after an outage at its banking partner, ZebPay's INR withdrawals are delayed, and Unocoin's services have been suspended since 9 November after a security breach in which about $200,000 was stolen. Against that background, a protocol wind-down is a different kind of event — not an exchange failure, but a reminder that tokens and protocols can also end, and that the exit terms matter.

TRON-based USDT is widely used in India, partly because TRON transfers are cheap compared with Ethereum, and partly because USDT is the stablecoin most Indian traders reach for first. An Indian holder of stUSDT is most likely to have acquired it through a wallet or a DeFi interaction rather than through an Indian exchange, which means there is no Indian customer-support desk to call and no Indian regulator to complain to. The redemption portal is the only route, and the deadline is fixed.

The tax treatment is unchanged by the shutdown. Gains on virtual digital assets in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS on transfers. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Whether a redemption counts as a taxable transfer, and how any loss is treated, is a question for a qualified tax adviser — this guide does not give tax advice.

One more practical point: the amounts involved for most individual holders are likely to be small, because the protocol's remaining balance is only about $8.6 million spread across all holders. But small does not mean unimportant. A balance left unredeemed after the portal closes in May 2027 becomes a manual customer-support request processed in monthly batches — and after August 2027, even that route ends. The cost of redeeming now is a few minutes and a small network fee. The cost of forgetting is the balance itself.
