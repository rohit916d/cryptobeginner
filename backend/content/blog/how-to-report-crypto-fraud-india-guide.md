You realise the trading group has gone silent. The "profit" you saw on a dashboard was never real, the support agent has stopped replying, and the money you transferred is gone. It is a sickening moment — and the most important thing you do next is not panic, but report it properly and quickly.

This guide walks you through exactly how to report crypto fraud in India, step by step: what evidence to save, where to file, what to say on the helpline, and what to realistically expect afterwards. It is written for ordinary users, not lawyers, and it covers the routes that actually exist — not the fake "recovery agents" who will soon come calling.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. If you have lost money, treat this guide as general information about reporting channels — for legal or tax questions about your specific case, speak to a qualified professional.

## Step 0: Stop the Bleeding in the First Hour

Before you file anything, protect what is left. Scammers often strike twice: first the fraud itself, then a fake "recovery" offer aimed at victims who are desperate to get their money back.

- **Stop all transfers immediately.** Do not pay a "tax", "unlock fee", "verification charge", or "withdrawal fee" to release supposed profits. Legitimate platforms do not hold your money hostage behind surprise fees, and every extra payment is simply more money lost.
- **Do not delete anything.** Keep the chats, the scammer's profile, the website links, and the transaction confirmations — even messages that embarrass you. Evidence you delete cannot be recovered, and investigators need the full trail.
- **Secure your accounts.** If you shared a password, OTP, or seed phrase, change your exchange and email passwords right away, turn on authenticator-based two-factor authentication, and move any remaining crypto on that exchange to a fresh wallet you control. If you shared a seed phrase, assume that wallet is compromised and stop using it.
- **Warn, don't confront.** Do not threaten the scammer or try to negotiate — it rarely helps and can lead to your evidence being deleted. A short message to anyone else you know in the same group ("this looks like a scam, stop sending money") can prevent further losses.

## Step 1: Preserve Your Evidence Before You File

Every reporting channel below will ask for the same basic facts. Gathering them once, in a single folder, saves you from repeating the story five times — and a complete evidence set makes your complaint far more credible.

| Evidence to save | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Screenshots of all chats (WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram) | Shows the promises made and the pressure tactics used |
| Scammer's profile, phone number, username, and group links | Identifies the accounts investigators can request data on |
| Transaction receipts: UPI reference numbers, bank statements, exchange withdrawal records | Traces the money trail through banks and platforms |
| Wallet addresses you sent crypto to, plus transaction IDs (hashes) | Lets investigators follow funds on the blockchain |
| Website URLs, app names, and any fake dashboards or documents | Links your case to other victims of the same operation |
| Dates, amounts in INR, and a short timeline of events | Turns a confusing story into a clear, checkable complaint |

Write a one-page timeline in plain language: when you first made contact, what you were promised, each payment you made (date, amount, method), and when you realised it was a fraud. Stick to facts you can prove. Investigators do not need dramatic language — they need dates, numbers, and records.

## Step 2: Report on the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal

The main online route for cyber fraud in India is the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in, run under the Ministry of Home Affairs. It accepts complaints from anywhere in the country, and you do not need to visit a police station to start.

1. Go to cybercrime.gov.in and choose the financial-fraud reporting option.
2. Register with your mobile number and verify it with the OTP you receive.
3. Fill in the complaint form: your details, the suspect's details (whatever you have — a phone number or username is enough to start), the dates and amounts, and a description of what happened.
4. Upload your evidence: screenshots, transaction receipts, and your one-page timeline.
5. Submit and save the acknowledgement number you receive. This number is your reference for every follow-up — note it down somewhere safe, and keep a copy of the submitted complaint.

Two practical tips. First, describe the fraud in the same plain timeline you prepared, not in a long emotional paragraph — officers process many complaints, and clarity helps yours stand out. Second, if the portal asks you to choose a category and none fits perfectly, pick the closest financial-fraud option and explain the crypto angle in the description; an imperfect category with complete evidence is far better than a delayed complaint.

## Step 3: Call the Cybercrime Helpline 1930

Alongside the online complaint, call the national cybercrime helpline on **1930**. This number exists specifically for financial fraud, and it is the fastest way to trigger action while money may still be moving through banks.

When you call, have ready: your acknowledgement number (if you have already filed online), the amounts and dates, the bank account or UPI ID you paid from, and the account or wallet details you paid to. The helpline team can coordinate with banks and payment providers to try to freeze funds that have not yet been withdrawn — this is why calling within hours, not days, genuinely matters. Once money has been converted to crypto and moved across wallets, freezing becomes much harder, which is exactly why speed is the single biggest factor you control.

Note the number down even if you never need it: **1930 for financial cyber fraud**. Sharing it with family members, especially older relatives who are frequent targets, is one of the simplest protections you can offer.

## Step 4: File a Complaint at Your Local Police Station or Cyber Cell

An online complaint is a strong start, but for larger losses — or if your online complaint seems stuck — visit your local police station or your district's cyber cell in person, carrying a printed copy of your portal complaint and your evidence folder.

What to carry:

- A printed copy of your cybercrime portal complaint and acknowledgement number
- Your ID proof (Aadhaar, PAN, or passport)
- Printed bank statements or UPI transaction records showing the payments
- Screenshots of chats and the scammer's profile details
- Your one-page timeline

Explain the case using your timeline, hand over the printed evidence, and ask for a copy of whatever is registered — an FIR or a formal complaint receipt — along with its number. Keep that number with your portal acknowledgement number; you will need both for follow-ups with the bank, the exchange, and any future correspondence. If an officer is unfamiliar with crypto, stay calm and factual: describe it as an online financial fraud in which you were induced to transfer money, and let the transaction records speak for themselves.

## Step 5: Report to the Exchange, Your Bank, and the Platform

Reporting to the authorities is essential, but it is not the only report you should make. Each party in the money trail has its own complaint channel, and each can take a different kind of action.

**Your exchange.** If you bought crypto on an Indian exchange and sent it to the scammer, or if the fraud happened through an exchange account, raise a ticket with that exchange's support immediately. Share your transaction IDs and ask them to flag the destination wallet addresses. Be aware of the limit here: an exchange can freeze funds still held on its own platform, but it cannot reverse a blockchain transfer that has already been confirmed.

**Your bank.** If you paid by UPI or bank transfer, inform your bank's fraud desk as well, quoting the UPI reference numbers and dates. Banks have their own dispute and freeze procedures, and a report made within hours has a better chance of catching funds before they are withdrawn.

**The platform where you met the scammer.** Report the fake profile, group, or advertisement on WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, or wherever the contact began. Platform reports rarely recover money, but they get scam accounts removed and protect the next potential victim. If the fraud used a fake app, report it to the app store too.

Keep a simple log of every complaint: where you filed, the date, the reference or ticket number, and the name of anyone you spoke to. Follow-ups are far easier when you can quote reference numbers instead of retelling the story.

## Step 6: Know What to Realistically Expect

Honesty matters here, because false hope is what recovery scammers sell. Reporting is still worth doing — it creates an official record, it can freeze funds in some cases, it helps police link your case to larger operations, and it protects others — but you should understand the limits.

- **Blockchain transfers are effectively irreversible.** Once a crypto transaction is confirmed, no authority can press undo. This is a property of the technology, not a failure of the police.
- **Recovery is possible mainly through banks and exchanges.** If stolen money is still in a bank account or on a platform that can freeze it, quick reporting can save it. Once it has moved through multiple wallets, recovery becomes unlikely.
- **Investigations take time.** Cyber fraud cases often involve accounts, numbers, and servers spread across states or countries. A slow investigation does not mean your complaint was ignored — follow up politely using your acknowledgement numbers every few weeks.
- **No one legitimate will charge you for recovery.** Police, banks, and exchanges do not ask victims to pay a fee to release frozen funds or start an investigation. Anyone who does is running a second scam.

## Beware the Second Scam: Fake Recovery Agents

Within days or weeks of being scammed, many victims are contacted by "recovery experts", "hackers for hire", or fake law firms promising to retrieve the lost crypto for an upfront fee. Some even claim to work with the police or a major exchange. They do not. Their playbook is simple: find public complaints or leaked victim lists, promise guaranteed recovery, collect an advance fee — and disappear.

Red flags for a recovery scam: a guarantee of recovery, an upfront fee (often called a "tax" or "processing charge"), pressure to act today, contact through WhatsApp or Telegram rather than an official channel, and a request for your seed phrase or exchange login. Real investigators never ask for your seed phrase, and real recovery never requires you to pay first. If someone approaches you, save their messages and report them through the same channels above — they are committing a fresh fraud.

## Common Mistakes That Weaken a Complaint

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Waiting weeks to report, hoping the scammer returns the money | Report within hours — freezing only works while funds are still traceable |
| Deleting chats out of embarrassment | Keep everything; investigators need the full trail, not a cleaned-up version |
| Paying a "release fee" to unlock supposed profits | Stop paying — surprise fees are part of the fraud, not a way out of it |
| Sharing your seed phrase with a "support agent" | Never share it with anyone; real support never asks for it |
| Reporting only on social media, not officially | Social posts warn others, but only a portal complaint or FIR creates an official case |
| Giving up after one complaint stalls | Follow up with your acknowledgement numbers, and escalate to the cyber cell if needed |

## Tax Note: Reporting Fraud Does Not Change Your Tax Position

A brief but important qualifier. In India, crypto gains are taxed at a flat 30% and most transfers attract 1% TDS, and losses from crypto generally cannot be set off against other income or carried forward. Losing money to fraud does not automatically create a tax deduction, and filing a police complaint does not change that treatment on its own. Keep your complaint copies, transaction records, and bank statements together with your other crypto records — they are part of your financial history even if they do not change your tax bill. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing, and speak to a tax professional about your specific situation.

## FAQs

### What is the first thing I should do after realising I have been scammed?

Stop sending money immediately, even if the scammer promises that one more payment will unlock your funds — that is almost always part of the same fraud. Take screenshots of every chat, transaction receipt, wallet address, and profile involved before anything gets deleted. Then report quickly on the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in and call the cybercrime helpline 1930, because speed improves the chance that a bank or exchange can freeze funds that are still moving through the traditional banking system.

### Can the police or an exchange recover crypto that has already been sent?

Usually not, and anyone who guarantees recovery is almost certainly running a second scam. Crypto transfers on a blockchain are effectively irreversible once confirmed, so there is no undo button for the police or an exchange to press. Recovery is sometimes possible when stolen funds are still sitting in a bank account or on a regulated exchange that can freeze them, which is why reporting within hours matters far more than reporting weeks later.

### Do I need to visit a police station, or can I report crypto fraud online?

You can start online through the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal, which accepts financial-fraud complaints from anywhere in India and gives you an acknowledgement number to track. For larger losses, or if the online complaint stalls, visit your local police station or district cyber cell with a printed copy of your complaint and your evidence. Carry an ID proof, your transaction records, and screenshots — the written complaint and evidence folder make the process much smoother.

## Conclusion: Report Fast, Keep Records, Warn Others

Being scammed is not a personal failure — these operations are professional, patient, and designed to fool careful people. What you control now is the response: stop the payments, save the evidence, file on cybercrime.gov.in, call 1930, inform your bank and exchange, and keep every reference number in one place. Then warn others — a short message to friends or family about the exact trick used can prevent the same fraud from finding its next victim.
