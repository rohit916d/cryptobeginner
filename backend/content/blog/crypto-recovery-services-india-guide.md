You have just lost money to a crypto scam. Then, almost as if on cue, a message arrives: "We can recover your stolen crypto. We have recovered ₹50 lakh for clients this month. Pay a small fee and we will start today."

It feels like hope arriving at the worst moment. Unfortunately, for most Indian crypto users, that message is the second scam — the recovery scam. This guide explains what crypto recovery services actually are, what they can and cannot do, and how to tell genuine help from another trap.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto transactions are irreversible — no one can guarantee your stolen funds back, and anyone who promises to is almost certainly trying to scam you again.

## Why Recovery Is So Hard in Crypto

Before judging any recovery service, it helps to understand why crypto theft is different from a stolen bank payment.

When someone takes money from your bank account without permission, the bank can often reverse the transaction, freeze the receiving account, or raise a chargeback. Crypto has no such safety net. A crypto transfer, once confirmed on the blockchain, cannot be undone by anyone — not by the exchange, not by the wallet app, and not by the police. There is no "undo" button and no customer-care desk for a blockchain.

That is the core problem every recovery claim must answer. If someone says they can reverse your transaction, they are either misunderstanding how blockchains work or lying to you.

There are only a few realistic ways stolen crypto ever comes back:

- **An exchange freezes the funds.** If the scammer sends your coins to an exchange wallet, and you report it quickly with evidence, the exchange may freeze that account — but only while the funds are still there.
- **Law enforcement seizes assets.** Police or cybercrime investigators can seize wallets and exchange accounts, but this takes months or years and usually needs a formal complaint.
- **The scammer returns the funds voluntarily.** This is rare, and it usually only happens when the scammer is identified and pressured by authorities.

Notice what is missing from that list: a private company with a secret tool that "hacks back" your coins. That is not how crypto works.

## What Legitimate Recovery Help Actually Looks Like

Real help does exist, but it looks very different from the ads and messages that target victims. Here is what each legitimate route actually does.

### 1. Blockchain investigation firms

A small number of professional firms specialise in tracing crypto transactions across wallets and exchanges. They analyse public blockchain data to follow where your coins went — which exchange they landed on, which addresses they passed through, and whether they were mixed or converted. Their output is a report, not a refund. That report can be used by police, lawyers, and exchanges to support a freeze or seizure.

These firms typically work with law enforcement, exchanges, and lawyers — not directly with individual victims who found them through a WhatsApp message. Their fees are usually quoted in writing, in fiat currency, and they never promise a recovery.

### 2. Your exchange's support team

If you bought crypto on a FIU-IND registered Indian exchange such as CoinDCX, WazirX or ZebPay, the exchange's support team is your first free point of contact. They cannot reverse a withdrawal you already made, but they can flag the scammer's receiving address, freeze funds if they arrive on their platform, and share transaction records you will need for a police complaint.

### 3. Police and cybercrime reporting

In India, the official route is the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the helpline number 1930. You can also file an FIR at your local police station or cyber cell. Investigators can request information from exchanges and, where the law allows, freeze accounts. This is slow and not guaranteed — but it is the only route with real legal power behind it.

### 4. A lawyer

For larger losses, a lawyer experienced in cybercrime or financial fraud can send legal notices to exchanges, help draft a complaint, and advise on civil remedies. A genuine lawyer will give you a written engagement letter, a clear fee structure, and an honest assessment of your chances — including the possibility that recovery may not happen at all.

## The Recovery Scam Playbook

Recovery scammers know exactly what a victim is feeling: urgency, embarrassment, and hope. Their playbook is built around those emotions. Here are the most common versions seen in India.

**The "guaranteed recovery" firm.** A website or Telegram channel promises a 90–100% success rate and shows screenshots of "recovered" funds. The screenshots are fake, the reviews are bought, and the success rate is invented. No honest service can guarantee a recovery rate.

**The upfront-fee trap.** The firm asks for a "processing fee," "tax," "unlock fee" or "gas fee" before starting — usually in crypto, so it cannot be traced or refunded. Once you pay, they ask for another fee, then another, until you stop paying. Nothing is ever recovered.

**The fake law-enforcement account.** Someone claiming to be from the cybercrime cell, the RBI, or an international agency contacts you and says your funds have been found — but you must pay a "release fee" or "court fee" first. Real government agencies do not demand fees over WhatsApp or Telegram, and they do not ask for payment in crypto.

**The fake recovery software.** A website offers a tool that "recovers" your coins if you connect your wallet or enter your seed phrase. The moment you do, your remaining funds are drained. No legitimate service ever asks for your seed phrase or private keys.

**The second-chance scammer.** Sometimes the same person who scammed you returns under a new name, posing as a recovery agent who "found" your case. They already know the details of your loss, which makes them sound credible. They are not.

**The paid-review operation.** Recovery scam websites are often surrounded by dozens of glowing reviews, YouTube videos, and Quora answers — all posted by the same network. A large number of positive reviews is not proof of legitimacy, especially when the reviews all sound the same and none mention a single failed case.

## Recovery Scam vs Legitimate Help: How to Tell

| What to check | Legitimate help | Likely a scam |
|---|---|---|
| Guarantee | Never guarantees recovery | Promises 90–100% success |
| Payment | Written fee, usually in INR/bank transfer | Upfront fee in crypto or gift cards |
| Seed phrase | Never asks for it | Asks you to share it or connect your wallet |
| Contact method | Official website, email, office address | Only WhatsApp, Telegram or Instagram DMs |
| Track record | Verifiable cases, real company registration | Fake screenshots, bought reviews, no verifiable identity |
| First step | Asks for evidence and explains the process | Pressures you to pay immediately |
| What they deliver | A tracing report or legal advice | Vague promises and repeated fee demands |

If a service fails even one of the checks in the right-hand column, treat it as a scam and stop communicating.

## A 5-Minute Check Before You Trust Anyone

Before you pay anyone or share any information, run through this quick routine.

1. **Search the name plus "scam".** Type the company name followed by "scam" or "review" into a search engine. Look for complaints on forums and consumer sites, not just the testimonials on their own website.
2. **Check the website's age.** A site created a month ago that claims ten years of experience is lying. Free domain-check tools can show when a domain was registered.
3. **Verify the company exists.** A genuine firm has a registered company name, a physical address, and named people you can look up. A Telegram handle and a logo are not a company.
4. **Ask for a written agreement.** A real investigator or lawyer will give you a written scope of work and fee structure before taking money. A scammer will avoid putting anything in writing.
5. **Talk to someone you trust.** Victims often act alone because they feel embarrassed. Tell a family member or a friend what happened before you pay anyone — a second pair of eyes catches pressure tactics quickly.
6. **Never pay in crypto.** Any recovery service that insists on payment in crypto, gift cards, or through an untraceable route is structuring the payment so you cannot get it back. That is the point.

## What To Do If You Already Paid a "Recovery" Firm

If you have already sent money to a recovery service and now suspect it was a scam, do not feel ashamed — these operations are designed to fool people who are already stressed. Take these steps:

1. **Stop paying immediately.** No further fee will unlock your funds. Every additional payment is a fresh loss.
2. **Save everything.** Screenshots of chats, payment receipts, wallet addresses, transaction IDs, website links, and phone numbers are evidence.
3. **Report it as a new fraud.** File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930, and mention both the original scam and the recovery scam. Both are crimes.
4. **Tell your bank and exchange.** If you paid by bank transfer or UPI, inform your bank immediately. If you sent crypto from an exchange, ask the exchange to flag the receiving address.
5. **Warn others.** Reporting the scammer's accounts and website helps protect the next victim.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. If you lost crypto to fraud, keep your records anyway: a loss does not erase the need to report your transactions, and your records may be needed for a police complaint or an insurance claim.

## What To Do Instead of Hiring a Recovery Service

The unglamorous truth is that the best recovery strategy is mostly free, and it starts the moment you realise you have been scammed.

**Step 1: Preserve evidence.** Note the transaction ID, the scammer's wallet address, screenshots of chats, the website or app link, and the exact time of the transfer. Do this before anything gets deleted.

**Step 2: Report the fraud.** File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930. Then visit your local cyber cell or police station with your evidence and ask for an acknowledgement number.

**Step 3: Contact your exchange.** If you used an Indian exchange, report the fraud and ask them to flag the receiving address. If the scammer's funds ever land on that exchange, your report is what makes a freeze possible.

**Step 4: Secure what is left.** Move any remaining crypto to a fresh wallet, change your exchange passwords, turn on authenticator-app 2FA, and check whether your email or phone number was compromised.

**Step 5: Accept the realistic outcome.** Most stolen crypto is never recovered. That is painful, but accepting it protects you from the second scam — which is exactly what recovery scammers are counting on you not doing.

## The Bottom Line

Crypto recovery services sit on a spectrum. At one end are a handful of professional investigators, lawyers, and law-enforcement routes that can genuinely help — slowly, expensively, and with no guarantees. At the other end is a large industry of fake recovery firms whose only business is taking money from people who have already lost money.

The difference is easy to remember: legitimate help explains the limits, puts things in writing, and never asks for your seed phrase or an upfront crypto payment. Scams promise certainty, demand secrecy and speed, and always want money first.

If you have lost crypto, report it, preserve your evidence, and protect what remains. And if a stranger messages you tomorrow offering to get it all back — remember that the first scam hurt, but the second one is the one you can still avoid.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a recovery service really get my stolen crypto back?

In most cases, no. Once crypto leaves your wallet, the transfer is irreversible — there is no bank to reverse it. A genuine recovery only happens when an exchange freezes the funds or law enforcement seizes them, and both need evidence and time. Any service that promises a guaranteed recovery is almost certainly a scam.

### What should I do first if my crypto was stolen?

Act fast: save all transaction IDs, wallet addresses and screenshots, stop communicating with the scammer, and report the fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or the 1930 helpline. Then contact the exchange you used and ask them to flag the receiving address. These steps cost nothing and are the only ones that can genuinely help.

### How can I tell a real recovery expert from a scammer?

A legitimate investigator or lawyer will never demand an upfront fee in crypto, never guarantee results, and will explain exactly what they can and cannot do. Scammers do the opposite: they guarantee recovery, demand payment in crypto or gift cards, use fake reviews and stolen logos, and pressure you to decide quickly. When in doubt, walk away.
