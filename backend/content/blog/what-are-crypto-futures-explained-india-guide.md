# What Are Crypto Futures? Contracts and Risks Explained

If you have spent any time watching crypto prices, you have probably heard the phrase "crypto futures." It sounds advanced — and it is. A crypto futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell a cryptocurrency at a set price on a future date, without needing to own the coin right now. Traders use futures to bet on price direction, hedge, or amplify their exposure — but they come with risks that can wipe out your money far faster than simply buying and holding.

This guide explains what crypto futures actually are, how the contracts work step by step, how they differ from normal "spot" buying, and the risks every beginner in India should understand before ever touching them.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto futures involve leverage and can result in the total loss of your deposited margin. Crypto markets are volatile — never trade with money you can't afford to lose.

## Crypto Futures, Explained Simply

Imagine a wheat farmer and a flour mill. The farmer worries the price of wheat might crash before harvest; the mill worries prices might spike. So they sign a contract today: the mill agrees to buy a fixed quantity of wheat three months from now at a fixed price. Neither pays for the wheat today — they have only locked in the terms.

A **crypto futures contract** works the same way, except the "wheat" is Bitcoin, Ethereum, or another cryptocurrency, and the contract is usually settled in cash (or a stablecoin) rather than actual coin delivery.

When you trade a futures contract, you are making an agreement about **future price**:

- **Go long** = you agree to buy later. You profit if the price rises above your contract price.
- **Go short** = you agree to sell later. You profit if the price falls below your contract price.

Spot trading only lets you profit when prices rise. Futures let you profit from both directions — which is also why they attract, and ruin, so many beginners.

## Dated Futures vs Perpetual Contracts

There are two main types of crypto futures:

### 1. Dated (Quarterly) Futures

These expire on a fixed date — for example, "BTC futures expiring 31 December." When the contract expires, it is settled automatically at the market price at that time. Your profit or loss is the difference between the contract price and the settlement price, minus fees.

### 2. Perpetual Futures

These have **no expiry date**. You can hold the position open for as long as you want — as long as you maintain enough margin (more on that below). Since there is no expiry forcing the price to converge with the market price, perpetuals use a **funding rate** to keep things aligned:

- Every few hours (often every 8 hours), a small funding payment is exchanged between traders holding long positions and traders holding short positions.
- If perpetual prices run **above** the spot price, longs pay shorts — this discourages going long and pushes the price down toward spot.
- If perpetual prices run **below** spot, shorts pay longs.

The funding rate can be positive or negative, and it eats into your profits or adds to your losses the longer you hold. Many beginners lose money not from price moves at all, but from funding payments quietly draining their position over weeks.

| Feature | Dated Futures | Perpetual Contracts |
|---|---|---|
| Expiry | Yes, fixed date | No expiry |
| Funding payments | No | Yes, every few hours |
| Settlement | Automatic at expiry | When you close the position (or get liquidated) |
| Common on crypto exchanges | Yes | Yes — the most popular type |

## How a Futures Trade Actually Works, Step by Step

Let's walk through a simple example in INR terms.

Suppose Bitcoin is trading at ₹70,00,000 and you open a **long** futures position on 0.01 BTC with 10x leverage:

1. **You post margin, not the full amount.** 0.01 BTC is worth ₹70,000, but with 10x leverage you only deposit ₹7,000 as margin. The exchange effectively lends you the rest.
2. **Price moves up 5%.** Bitcoin rises to ₹73,50,000. Your 0.01 BTC position is now worth ₹73,500 — a ₹3,500 profit on a ₹7,000 deposit. That's a 50% gain from a 5% price move. This is leverage doing its magic.
3. **Price moves down 5% instead.** Bitcoin falls to ₹66,50,000. Your position loses ₹3,500 — a 50% loss from a 5% price move. Leverage multiplies losses exactly as fast as gains.
4. **Liquidation.** If the price falls roughly 10%, your ₹7,000 margin is gone — the exchange automatically closes your position to protect itself. You lose your entire deposit. This is called **liquidation**, and it happens to beginners constantly.

Key terms to know:

- **Margin** — the deposit you put up to open the position. It's your money at risk.
- **Leverage (e.g., 5x, 10x, 50x)** — how much bigger your position is than your margin. Higher leverage = faster gains AND faster liquidation.
- **Liquidation price** — the price level at which the exchange force-closes your position and your margin is lost.
- **Funding rate** — periodic payments between longs and shorts (perpetual contracts only).
- **Open interest** — the total value of all open futures positions; traders use it as a sentiment gauge, though it predicts nothing.

## Futures vs Spot Trading: What's the Real Difference?

Spot trading is the "normal" way of buying crypto: you pay money, you receive actual coins, and you own them. Futures are contracts *about* prices — you never own the coins.

| Feature | Spot Trading | Futures Trading |
|---|---|---|
| Do you own the crypto? | Yes, real coins in your account | No, only a price contract |
| Can you withdraw to your wallet? | Yes | No — nothing to withdraw |
| Profit when price falls? | No (only if you sell first and rebuy) | Yes, by shorting |
| Leverage | No (or minimal) | Yes — commonly 5x to 50x+ |
| Can you lose everything quickly? | Only if the coin goes to zero | Yes — liquidation from a small move |
| Funding payments | No | Yes (perpetual contracts) |
| Best for beginners? | Yes | No |

This is worth repeating: in spot trading, a 10% price drop leaves you with crypto worth 10% less — you still have the asset. In a 10x leveraged futures position, a 10% move against you leaves you with **nothing**.

## The Real Risks (Read This Before Anything Else)

Futures are not "spot trading with extra features." They are a fundamentally different, far riskier product.

**1. Liquidation is sudden and total.** The exchange doesn't wait for you to decide. When your margin runs out, your position is closed automatically — often during a fast price spike when you can't react. On crypto's 24/7 markets, this can happen at 3 a.m. while you sleep.

**2. Leverage fools your brain.** A winning leveraged trade feels like genius. But the maths is brutal: with 10x leverage, a 10% adverse move = 100% loss. With 50x leverage, a 2% adverse move = total loss. Crypto routinely moves 5–10% in a day.

**3. Funding rates bleed slow positions.** Holding a perpetual position for weeks can cost you a meaningful percentage of your margin in funding payments alone — even if the price ends exactly where it started.

**4. Emotional pressure is extreme.** Watching leveraged money swing by the minute leads to panic-closing winners early and holding losers too long — the classic beginner pattern that professionals profit from.

**5. "Guaranteed profit" futures scams target Indians.** Telegram and WhatsApp groups promising "5x daily profits with our futures signals" or "managed futures accounts" are almost always scams — they take your deposit, show fake profits, then vanish or demand "release fees." No legitimate trader guarantees returns.

## Crypto Futures in India: What Beginners Should Know

A few India-specific points:

- **Platform availability varies.** Some Indian exchanges offer futures/derivatives products, while many beginners encounter them on international platforms. International platforms may not be FIU-IND registered, which adds regulatory and fund-recovery risk — always check a platform's registration status before depositing money.
- **Tax still applies — painfully.** In India, profits from virtual digital assets — which can include gains from crypto derivatives — are taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess), with **no set-off of losses** against other income and no carry-forward. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Because of the no-set-off rule, a leveraged loss in futures cannot be used to reduce your tax on other gains — a detail that makes futures losses doubly expensive. How TDS applies to futures settlements can differ from spot trades, so confirm the current treatment with a tax professional.
- **INR deposits and withdrawals** on derivatives platforms can be slower and more restricted than on spot exchanges, and some platforms only settle in USDT or other stablecoins, adding conversion costs.
- **Beginner recommendation:** almost every experienced trader advises beginners to avoid futures entirely at the start. If you want exposure to crypto price movements, spot buying with money you can afford to lose is far simpler and far safer.

## If You Still Want to Learn: A Safe Path

If futures genuinely interest you, treat them like a subject to study — not a way to make money:

1. **Master spot trading first.** Understand order types, fees, and how prices move before adding leverage.
2. **Paper trade.** Many platforms offer demo/testnet futures with play money. Lose fake money for months before risking a rupee.
3. **Start absurdly small.** If you ever go live, use the smallest position size possible with the lowest leverage — treat it as tuition fees you expect to lose.
4. **Set a hard loss limit.** Decide in advance the maximum you will ever risk on futures (many educators suggest zero for the first year), and never add more margin to a losing position.
5. **Never trade on borrowed money or "tips."** Signal groups, "expert" Telegram admins, and copy-trading strangers are the fastest way to donate your savings to scammers.

## The Bottom Line

Crypto futures are contracts that let you bet on where crypto prices will go — up or down — using leverage that multiplies both gains and losses. Perpetual contracts, the most popular type, add funding payments to the mix. For beginners, the combination of liquidation risk, funding costs, emotional pressure, and India's no-loss-set-off tax treatment makes futures one of the riskiest corners of crypto.

Understanding futures is genuinely useful knowledge — it helps you read market news, understand what "liquidations" headlines mean, and recognise why experienced traders keep warning beginners away. Just keep that knowledge theoretical until you have deep experience, strict risk rules, and money you are fully prepared to lose.
