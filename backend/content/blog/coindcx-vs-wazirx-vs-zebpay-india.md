If you are buying crypto in India for the first time, one question comes before everything else: **which exchange should I sign up on?** For years, three names have dominated the Indian market — CoinDCX, WazirX and ZebPay. They all let you deposit INR, buy Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, and trade on your phone. But they differ in ways that matter for a beginner: how easy INR deposits are, what trading actually costs you, how the apps feel, and how each handled the rough patches the Indian crypto industry has been through.

This guide compares the three side by side, so you can pick the one that fits how *you* plan to use crypto.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. Also note: exchanges change their features, fees and policies frequently, so treat this as a framework for comparing exchanges rather than a fixed scorecard, and verify current details on each exchange's website before signing up.

## First, what an exchange actually does for you

A crypto exchange is simply a marketplace. You deposit INR (through UPI, bank transfer, or P2P depending on what's currently available), and the exchange matches your buy order with someone else's sell order — or sells from its own liquidity pool. Your purchased crypto sits in a wallet the exchange manages for you, and when you sell, the INR goes back to your linked bank account.

Two things every beginner should understand from day one:

1. **The exchange controls the keys.** Crypto on an exchange is in a custodial wallet — the exchange holds the private keys, not you. This is convenient, but it means your funds depend on the exchange's security and honesty. ("Not your keys, not your crypto" is the community's shorthand for this.)
2. **Fees hide in the spread.** The visible trading fee is only part of the cost. Many exchanges also build a margin into the buy/sell price — the difference between the market rate and the rate they quote you. Two exchanges advertising "0% commission" can give you meaningfully different amounts of Bitcoin for the same ₹10,000.

## CoinDCX: the investor-friendly option

CoinDCX is one of India's largest exchanges by user base and has positioned itself heavily toward beginners and long-term investors.

**INR deposits:** CoinDCX has supported UPI and bank deposits for INR, and it also built a simple "invest" mode aimed at first-time buyers who just want to buy and hold.

**Modes for different users:** CoinDCX offers a basic buying interface for beginners plus a more advanced trading platform (with order books, charts and limit orders) for experienced traders. This two-tier setup is genuinely useful: you can start simple and graduate to advanced tools without switching exchanges.

**Fees:** Like most exchanges, CoinDCX charges trading fees that generally decrease as your monthly trading volume rises. The simple-invest mode tends to include a wider spread (a built-in price margin) in exchange for convenience. If you plan to trade actively, comparing the advanced mode's fee schedule matters.

**Beginner verdict:** Arguably the friendliest starting point for someone whose plan is "buy Bitcoin or Ethereum and hold." The educational content and simple interface reduce the chances of a costly first-trade mistake.

## WazirX: the pioneer that stumbled

WazirX was arguably India's most popular exchange for several years, known for its simple app, INR trading pairs, and the WRX utility token.

**The 2024 hack changed everything.** In 2024, WazirX suffered a major security breach that resulted in the loss of a large portion of customer assets. Withdrawals were frozen, users were locked out of their funds, and the exchange entered a prolonged restructuring process. As of this writing, the situation for affected users remains unresolved in important ways.

**Why this matters for your choice:** This isn't gossip — it's the single most important lesson about Indian crypto exchanges in recent memory. WazirX was the market leader. It had millions of users. And users still lost access to their money. When you evaluate any exchange, its security track record and how it handled a crisis matter more than its marketing.

**INR deposits and trading:** WazirX historically offered UPI deposits and a large set of INR trading pairs. However, given its post-hack state, a beginner in 2026 should research its *current* operational status carefully before depositing any funds — things may not be what they were.

**Beginner verdict:** Proceed with extreme caution. Read current user reports and official updates about fund withdrawals before considering it. For a first exchange, a beginner is usually better served by an exchange without unresolved withdrawal issues.

## ZebPay: the old guard

ZebPay is one of India's oldest crypto companies, operating since well before the 2020s crypto boom. Its long history is both its strength and, at times, its complication.

**History and trust:** Being around since the early days gives ZebPay a certain institutional steadiness. It survived the 2018–2020 period when India's crypto industry faced banking restrictions and regulatory uncertainty — an era that killed or drove away several competitors.

**INR deposits:** ZebPay has supported bank-based INR deposits and, at various times, UPI. Its deposit/withdrawal options have shifted with India's banking environment, so verify what's currently available.

**Interface and fees:** ZebPay's app is functional and straightforward, and its fee structure is comparable to other Indian exchanges. It may feel slightly less "beginner-handheld" than CoinDCX's invest mode, but it's still perfectly usable for a first-time buyer.

**Beginner verdict:** A reasonable, steady choice — especially if you value an exchange with a long operating history in India. Just verify current deposit methods before committing.

## How to actually choose: five questions to ask yourself

Rather than declaring one "winner," here is a decision framework, because the right exchange depends on what you plan to do:

1. **Am I just buying and holding?** Pick the exchange with the simplest buy flow and the tightest spread on simple purchases. A beginner mode with a clear "buy" button beats a professional trading terminal you don't understand yet.
2. **Can I deposit INR easily right now?** This is the most practical filter. Check each exchange's deposits page today. UPI availability has come and gone on Indian exchanges depending on banking partnerships — never assume.
3. **What will a ₹10,000 purchase actually cost me?** Compare the *all-in* cost: trading fee plus the spread between the quoted price and the real market price. Some exchanges quote you a price that is noticeably worse than the market rate; that hidden margin is your real fee.
4. **What is the exchange's crisis record?** Look up how each exchange handled past problems — hacks, withdrawal freezes, regulatory shocks. An exchange that communicated honestly during a crisis deserves more trust than one that went silent.
5. **Do I plan to move coins off the exchange?** If yes (and you should, for anything you plan to hold long-term), check withdrawal fees and whether the exchange supports transfers to your own wallet. Some exchanges make withdrawals expensive or slow — that is a red flag.

## Safety rules that apply no matter which exchange you pick

- **Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)** with an authenticator app, not SMS, from day one.
- **Keep only trading money on the exchange.** Move long-term holdings to a wallet where you control the private keys. The WazirX episode is the proof of why.
- **Back up your account recovery details** and never share OTPs or passwords with anyone — exchange "support" will never ask for them. Impersonation scams are rampant in India.
- **Start small.** Your first deposit should be an amount you are fully comfortable losing while you learn how deposits, orders and withdrawals actually work.

## The bottom line

For most Indian beginners in 2026, the practical shortlist is **CoinDCX for the easiest start** and **ZebPay as the steady alternative** — with WazirX only worth revisiting once its post-hack situation is fully resolved and withdrawals are verifiably normal. Whichever you choose, verify current INR deposit methods, compare the true all-in cost of a purchase, turn on 2FA, and move holdings you aren't actively trading into your own wallet.

Your exchange is a doorway, not a vault. Use it to enter the market — then take custody of what matters.
