Bitcoin fell below $76,000 on 5–6 November 2026, giving back its entire post-election rally in a single session. The drop was not caused by anything inside Bitcoin. It was caused by a rout in US technology stocks: the Nasdaq fell 5.52% on Thursday 5 November, its worst day in about 18 months, and crypto fell with it. For Indian investors watching Bitcoin trade near ₹71.9 lakh, the natural question is why a US stock market sell-off should move a decentralised digital asset at all — and what the connection means for anyone holding crypto in India.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported market event; it does not predict prices and does not recommend any coin, trade or investment.

## What Happened on 5–6 November

The numbers tell the story of a single, sharp, market-wide move. Bitcoin traded above $80,800 early on Thursday 5 November, then fell as much as 5.8% during the session, touching an intraday low of about $75,369. It closed Thursday at $75,969 on Coinbase, down 5.5% from Wednesday's close of $80,428 — its lowest daily close since 15 September, and its lowest level in about seven weeks. By early Friday morning in India, Bitcoin was trading near $76,000, roughly 40% below its all-time high of about $126,080 reached in October 2025.

Ethereum fell harder, dropping about 7.8% to 8.5% to around $2,216–$2,234, its lowest level since August. Solana fell about 6.5% to 6.7% to roughly $89, and XRP fell about 5.7% to 6.4% to near $1.21. Several smaller coins fell by double-digit percentages in 24 hours, with Dogecoin and Litecoin down roughly 25% and 24% respectively, according to CoinMarketCap data carried by Gadgets 360. The total cryptocurrency market capitalisation fell about 5% to roughly $2.55 trillion, and the Fear and Greed Index registered 32, indicating fear.

The damage was not limited to coins. About $1.03 billion to $1.17 billion in leveraged crypto positions were liquidated in 24 hours, affecting roughly 211,000 traders, mostly holders of long positions. Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $1 billion in net outflows, with roughly $900 million of that coming from BlackRock's IBIT alone. Crypto-related stocks fell too: Strategy, the largest corporate Bitcoin holder, dropped about 9.3%, and Bitcoin miners fell by double digits.

## Why the Nasdaq Fell

To understand why Bitcoin fell, you first have to understand why US tech stocks fell — because the crypto drop was, in the words of several market reports, an echo of Wall Street.

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% to 24,315 on Thursday, the S&P 500 lost 4.36% to 7,353, and the Dow Jones fell 3.44%, losing 1,833 points to 51,523. The Nasdaq-100, which tracks the largest non-financial stocks, plunged 6.4%, its biggest single-day decline since March 2020. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.25%. Chip stocks led the slide: Nvidia closed 9.26% lower, AMD fell 17.26% and Micron fell 17.46%. The market capitalisation of Nasdaq-100 components alone evaporated by roughly $2.3 trillion in a single day, and the VIX volatility index surged nearly 70% to about 28.18, its highest since March.

Two worries drove the selling. The first was politics. Democrats won control of Congress in the US midterm elections on 3 November, and by Thursday the market was reading that result as a threat to the artificial-intelligence build-out: fears of tighter AI regulation and a backlash against data-centre construction in several states, which could slow the data-centre spending that has powered chip stocks all year. The second worry was valuation. After months of huge gains, investors began to doubt whether the enormous spending on AI infrastructure would pay off, and the election result gave them a reason to act on that doubt. The S&P 500's fall was its worst day since the tariff shock of April 2025, and the Nasdaq entered correction territory — more than 10% below its record set on 6 October.

The contrast with Wednesday is what makes the week so instructive. On Wednesday, stocks and crypto rallied because the election was over and oil fell sharply on bets that a weaker US president would have to settle the Iran conflict. On Thursday, the same election result was read the other way — as a threat to AI spending — and both markets fell together. The facts had not changed overnight. The market's reading of them had.

## The Transmission Chain: How a Stock Rout Reaches Bitcoin

Bitcoin has no earnings, no factory and no headquarters. So how does a fall in chip stocks reach it within hours? The answer is a chain of connections that has been built up over the past few years, and it runs through four links.

**The same holders.** The largest holders of Bitcoin today are not only individual enthusiasts. They are funds, trading desks and ETF issuers that also hold large positions in US tech stocks. When such a fund decides to cut risk, it does not sell one asset and keep the other — it sells across the whole portfolio. As one market analysis put it, when the Nasdaq loses 5%, the funds that hold Bitcoin next to chip stocks cut both at once. Bitcoin is treated, in practice, as a high-beta tech stock: an asset that moves in the same direction as tech, but by more.

**Risk budgets.** Professional investors manage risk as a total budget, not asset by asset. When volatility spikes — the VIX jumped nearly 70% on Thursday — risk models tell funds to shrink their positions, and the most volatile holdings are cut first. Crypto is among the most volatile assets in a typical portfolio, so it is among the first to be sold when the risk budget shrinks. This is why Bitcoin fell on a day when nothing about Bitcoin itself had changed.

**Leverage.** A large share of crypto trading is done with borrowed money. When prices start falling, leveraged long positions are automatically closed by exchanges — liquidated — and those forced sales push prices down further, which triggers more liquidations. On 5–6 November, roughly $1 billion in leveraged positions was wiped out in 24 hours, and about $370 million of that was liquidated within a single hour during the US session. The liquidation cascade is a mechanism, not a cause: it amplifies a fall that started elsewhere.

**ETF flows.** Since spot Bitcoin ETFs launched, a large part of Bitcoin buying and selling runs through them. When traditional investors sell ETF shares, the ETF must sell the underlying Bitcoin. The roughly $1 billion in net outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs on 5 November — mostly from BlackRock's IBIT — was a direct channel from stock-market sentiment into the Bitcoin price. A week earlier, the same ETFs had recorded a $643 million outflow in a single day after the Binance security incident, showing how quickly the flow channel can reverse.

## Why Altcoins Fell Harder Than Bitcoin

A consistent pattern in market-wide sell-offs is that altcoins fall more than Bitcoin, and 5 November followed it. Ethereum fell about 8% while Bitcoin fell about 5.5%; Solana and XRP fell about 6–7%; smaller coins fell by double digits. There are three reasons for this.

First, altcoins are smaller and less liquid. When a fund needs to raise cash quickly, it sells what it can sell — and Bitcoin, with the deepest markets, is sold first but absorbs the selling best. Smaller coins have thinner order books, so the same amount of selling moves their price further.

Second, altcoins carry more leverage relative to their size. Traders use borrowed money more aggressively on smaller coins, so the liquidation cascade hits them harder. When leveraged positions in altcoins are closed automatically, the forced selling is concentrated in exactly the coins with the least capacity to absorb it.

Third, altcoins are treated as the riskiest part of the crypto portfolio. In a risk-off move, investors sell the riskiest assets first and keep the relatively safer ones. Bitcoin, as the largest and most widely held crypto asset, is treated as the safer end of the crypto spectrum — which is why it fell less than almost everything else, even as it fell sharply in absolute terms.

## The Binance Overhang

One background factor made the market more fragile than usual. A week earlier, on 29–30 October, Binance suffered a security incident in which roughly $6.3 billion was drained from its hot wallets. Binance has since said it has replenished about 95% of the affected assets, reopened withdrawals in phases, and published a detailed incident report — but confidence was already shaken. Several reports on 5 November noted that lingering concern about the breach, and the billions of dollars in withdrawals that followed it, added to the selling pressure.

This is worth understanding because it shows how two different kinds of news can combine. The Nasdaq rout was the trigger, but the market it hit was already nervous: an exchange that had just suffered the largest crypto theft on record, ETF outflows that had already turned negative, and a price that had only just reclaimed $80,000 the day before on a short squeeze. A calm market might have absorbed the Nasdaq fall with a smaller crypto decline. A nervous, leveraged market amplified it.

## What This Means for Indian Investors

For Indian users, the first thing to note is the price in rupees. Bitcoin traded near ₹71.9 lakh in India on 6 November, down roughly 10% in 24 hours from about ₹79.8 lakh, according to CoinMarketCap data. Because crypto trades around the clock, there is no closing bell to cap a fall — Indian investors woke up to a market that had already moved while they slept. That is a structural feature of crypto: unlike Indian stocks, which stop trading at 3:30 PM, crypto prices move through the night, and global events land in Indian portfolios before Indian markets even open.

The second point is tax. Crypto gains in India are taxed at 30%, with 1% TDS on transfers. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Selling in a panic does not avoid tax: a gain realised on a sale is taxed the same way whether the sale was planned or panicked, and losses cannot be set off against other income under current rules. That is a practical reason to think before selling, not a reason to hold — the decision should be based on your own financial situation, not on a headline.

The third point is about leverage. The roughly $1 billion in liquidations on 5–6 November was concentrated in leveraged positions, and Indian users on offshore exchanges can access very high leverage. A leveraged position can be wiped out by a single sharp move even if the price later recovers — the liquidation is permanent, while the price fall may not be. If you do not fully understand how liquidation prices work, leveraged trading is not a suitable activity, and a volatile week like this one is the wrong time to learn.

The fourth point is security. Market stress is also scam season. Fake support accounts, lookalike Telegram groups and "recovery" offers appear within hours of a big fall, and they frequently target Indian users because of the size of the retail market here. No exchange will ask for your seed phrase, and no genuine support agent will contact you first about a market move. If a message claims to offer help with a loss, treat it as a scam until proven otherwise.

## What to Watch Next

Two events on Friday 6 November will shape how this story develops, and both are worth watching without trying to predict them.

The first is the US jobs report for October, due at 8:30 AM Eastern time — 7:00 PM IST. It is the first major economic release since the sell-off, and strong or weak jobs data could change expectations about US interest rates, which in turn affect both stocks and crypto. A hot report could strengthen the case for higher rates; a weak one could do the opposite. Either way, the reaction will be visible in both markets, which is itself the point of this story.

The second is a $2.65 billion Bitcoin options expiry on the Deribit exchange at 08:00 UTC on 6 November, covering about 33,135 BTC. The "max pain" price — the level at which the largest number of options contracts expire worthless — sits at about $79,000, well above the current price near $76,000. Options expiries can add volatility around the expiry time, but they do not determine the direction of the market, and they should not be read as a forecast.

Beyond those two events, the real question is whether the Nasdaq stabilises. If US tech stocks find their footing, the pressure on crypto eases, because the transmission chain runs both ways. If the sell-off continues, crypto is likely to keep moving with it. That is the practical meaning of the correlation: for now, Bitcoin's short-term direction is tied to the same risk sentiment that moves the Nasdaq.

## The Bottom Line

Bitcoin fell below $76,000 on 5–6 November 2026 because the Nasdaq had its worst day in about 18 months, and crypto is now wired into the same risk system as US tech stocks. The same funds hold both, the same risk budgets govern both, leverage amplifies moves in both, and ETF flows carry stock-market sentiment directly into the Bitcoin price. The result was a 5.5% fall in Bitcoin, larger falls in altcoins, roughly $1 billion in liquidations and roughly $1 billion in ETF outflows — all in a single session, and none of it caused by anything inside Bitcoin itself.

That does not make Bitcoin a tech stock. Over longer periods, its returns have still differed sharply from equities, and its long-term drivers — supply, adoption, regulation — are its own. But on a day when the whole market sells off, Bitcoin now sells off with it, and the days when it moved independently of Wall Street are, for now, behind us. For Indian investors, the lesson is practical: watch the Nasdaq as well as the crypto charts, understand how leverage and ETF flows amplify moves, and make decisions based on your own financial situation rather than on a single day's headlines.
