On Friday 13 November 2026, Bitcoin fell to $67,652 — below its 200-week moving average of around $68,000 — and then kept falling. By Saturday morning it had broken below $67,000, its lowest price in about three months, with more than $1.3 billion in leveraged long positions liquidated in 24 hours and the Fear and Greed Index at 13, deep in "extreme fear" territory. This guide explains what the 200-week moving average is, why this particular line carries so much weight with traders, what has happened the previous times Bitcoin has tested it, and what Indian users should — and should not — take from the move.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 13 and 14 November 2026 by CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, Blockonomi, Binance, FXStreet, The Defiant, BigGo Finance, CoinGabbar and The Economic Times, are:

- **Bitcoin fell below its 200-week moving average on Friday 13 November.** CoinDesk reported that Bitcoin fell as low as $67,652, down more than 3.5% over 24 hours and 16% over the past month, slipping below its 200-week moving average of around $68,000. Cointelegraph and Blockonomi put the line more precisely at $67,993, with Bitcoin touching $67,500 — roughly $493, or less than 1%, below it.
- **It was the first test of that line in about 11 weeks.** Bitcoin last touched its 200-week moving average on 19 August 2026, according to TradingView data cited by Cointelegraph and Blockonomi. The gap between tests is part of why the break drew so much coverage: this is not a line Bitcoin visits every week.
- **The fall continued into Saturday.** BigGo Finance reported that Bitcoin broke below $67,000 on 14 November to its lowest level in about three months, with an intraday low of $66,915 on Binance's USDT market — its lowest since 19 August. The Defiant reported $66,789 on Coinbase, a fresh four-week low. The exact low differs by exchange and by the time it was measured, which is normal for a market that trades around the clock.
- **Liquidations ran past $1 billion in a day.** CoinGlass data cited by BigGo put forced liquidations of bullish long positions at about $1.3 billion over 24 hours. FXStreet, using a different window, reported $1.64 billion in forced liquidations across 185,595 traders. The Economic Times, citing AMBCrypto, reported $856 million in liquidations with $771.7 million of that in long positions and $454.7 million of it in Bitcoin alone. The figures differ because they measure different windows and different exchanges — but every source agrees the wipeout was concentrated in long positions, not shorts.
- **Bitcoin ETFs swung to a net outflow for the year.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now lost more than $2.5 billion since 30 October, according to BigGo, and SoSoValue data cited by CoinDesk puts them at $1.56 billion in net outflows for 2026 as a whole, after being about $1.01 billion in net inflows on 29 October. The Economic Times reported $300 million in outflows on 11 November and a further $48 million on 12 November.
- **Sentiment hit extreme fear.** The Fear and Greed Index stood at 13 on 14 November, in "extreme fear" territory, according to CoinGabbar's market update. The same update put the global crypto market capitalisation at $2.4 trillion, down 3.1% in 24 hours, with Bitcoin down 3.8% to about $68,245 at 1:30 AM UTC.
- **The macro backdrop stayed hostile.** The Dollar Index held above 103, its highest since March 2025 and a 19-month high, according to CoinDesk and Binance's market update. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose to between 5.13% and 5.15% after the October producer price data, and CoinGabbar reported that the odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December had climbed to 89%.

## What Is the 200-Week Moving Average?

A moving average is exactly what it sounds like: the average price of an asset over a set number of periods, recalculated as each new period arrives and the oldest one drops out. The 200-week moving average takes the closing price of each of the last 200 weeks — nearly four years of weekly closes — and averages them into a single number. Because it covers so much history, it moves very slowly: one bad week barely shifts it, and one good week barely lifts it.

That slowness is the point. Short-term averages, like the 50-day or 200-day lines, react to weeks of trading. The 200-week average reacts to years of it. Traders therefore treat it as a long-term trend line: when the price sits well above it, the long-term trend is read as healthy; when the price falls below it, the long-term trend is read as under pressure. It is not a magic number — it is simply the average price of the last four years — but because so many traders watch the same line, the moments when Bitcoin crosses it tend to attract attention, commentary and, sometimes, further selling as stop-loss orders trigger.

The current reading illustrates how close the line is to the price. At $67,993, the 200-week average is less than 1% above Bitcoin's Friday low of $67,500. A separate live tracker put the average at $67,909 with the price at $66,757 on 14 November, about 2% below it. The precise figure depends on which data source is used and when it is sampled, but every source agrees on the same picture: Bitcoin is now trading at or just below a line that represents its average price over nearly four years.

## Why This Line Has a Reputation

The 200-week moving average has earned its reputation in Bitcoin's past cycles, and the history is worth knowing because it explains why Friday's break was treated as news rather than as a footnote.

In past bear markets, the line has acted as support — a level where selling has tended to slow and buyers have tended to step in. It has also acted as resistance on the way back up, capping recoveries until the price could reclaim it. Cointelegraph's reporting on 13 November noted that Bitcoin traded around the average from March to October 2023, and again from June to mid-August 2026 — long stretches where the price hovered near the line rather than sitting clearly above or below it. An analysis carried by AiEon added that the tension now is whether the average still acts as cycle support, as in several prior bear markets, or flips to resistance, as it did for a stretch in 2022.

That history cuts both ways, and it is important to say so plainly. In some cycles, dipping below the 200-week average marked the area where the worst of the decline was already happening, and the line later acted as a floor. In others, the break was followed by more weakness before the price found its footing. The line itself does not decide which outcome follows — it is a record of past prices, not a forecast. What it does reliably is mark the boundary between "the long-term trend is intact" and "the long-term trend is under pressure", and Friday's move crossed that boundary for the first time in about 11 weeks.

Two other indicators were being watched alongside the line on Friday, and they help explain the mood. The weekly relative strength index, a momentum gauge, had returned to 41.5 — a level it established as support in late July after several weeks of attempting a breakout, according to Cointelegraph. Keith Alan, co-founder of the analytics platform Material Indicators, was quoted as saying that a weekly RSI break below 41.5 could open the way to a retest of the $57,700 cycle low. The daily RSI, meanwhile, had fallen to 28.4, which is in oversold territory, according to Blockonomi. These are observations from named analysts about levels they are watching, not predictions from this site — and they point in opposite directions, which is itself a useful reminder that indicators disagree with each other.

## How This Fits the Week That Came Before

Friday's break did not arrive out of nowhere. It was the sharp end of a week in which several separate forces all pulled in the same direction, and each of them has already been covered in its own guide on this site.

The week began with Nvidia's chief executive resigning, which hit the AI trade and pushed Bitcoin below $72,000. Then the Dollar Index climbed to a 19-month high above 103, and Bitcoin fell below $70,000 — its lowest since August — with more than $1.3 billion in liquidations in 24 hours. On Friday, the October US producer price index came in slightly cooler than expected, and Bitcoin bounced back above $70,000 within the hour, with about $39 million in short positions liquidated. That bounce is the part that did not last: by the time New York opened, Bitcoin had lost $70,000 again, broken $68,000, and then slipped under its 200-week average.

A separate thread ran through the same days. Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, opened the subscription books for a Nasdaq listing expected around 19 November that aims to raise up to $64.4 billion, and several analysts said investors may have been selling liquid assets, including Bitcoin, to free up cash for the offering — a capital-rotation theory with a precedent in the SpaceX listing earlier this year. That theory is unproven, and the market's own coverage says so, but it is one more force pulling in the same direction as the dollar, the yields and the ETF outflows.

The scale of the month puts Friday in context. Bitcoin is down more than 20% since early October, according to The Economic Times, and down 21% in 2026 so far, according to CoinDesk's 13 November reporting — which would make 2025 and 2026 Bitcoin's first-ever back-to-back annual declines. BigGo put the fall at more than 18% in November alone, and about 46% below the all-time high near $126,000 reached in October 2025. None of those figures is a forecast. They are a record of how far the price has already moved.

## What Indian Users Should Know

For Indian users, Friday's break lands in a specific context, and four points are worth making.

**First, the price in rupees.** At roughly $67,000, Bitcoin was trading near ₹64.3 lakh on 14 November, according to Gadgets 360's India price page — down from about ₹79.8 lakh a day earlier. The Crypto Times' India premium index, which compares what Indians pay against the world price converted at the reference exchange rate, showed a premium of +8.66% on the morning of 14 November, with Bitcoin at about ₹69.6 lakh on the cheapest Indian exchange it sampled. The rupee figure moves with both the dollar price and the exchange rate, so it is a snapshot, not a fixed level — and the gap between the two rupee figures above is a reminder that prices differ across exchanges and sampling times.

**Second, the tax position is unchanged.** The 30% flat tax on crypto gains plus a 4% cess, and the 1% TDS on transfers, are Indian law. A chart line breaking does not change them, and nothing in this week's market moves changes how an Indian user's gains are taxed. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Third, understand what a weekly close means.** The 200-week average is a weekly line, and traders pay particular attention to where the price closes at the end of the week, not just where it trades during it. A brief dip below the line during thin weekend trading is treated differently from a week that closes below it. The weekly close is due on Sunday night, and the weekly RSI level of 41.5 that analysts are watching is a weekly reading for the same reason. For a beginner, the practical point is simple: a single intraday print below a line is a signal to understand, not a verdict to act on.

**Fourth, be careful with the messages this kind of week produces.** Whenever Bitcoin falls sharply, messages appear offering guaranteed recoveries, "buy the dip" schemes, or fake support teams asking for wallet details — usually through WhatsApp, Telegram or social media. No legitimate exchange asks for your seed phrase or your login, and no stranger can guarantee you a profit from a falling market. The same rule that applies to every crypto message applies here: if someone you do not know offers you a deal that sounds too good to be true, it is. Do not send money, and do not share wallet details, seed phrases or exchange logins with anyone.

## The Bottom Line

On Friday 13 November 2026, Bitcoin fell to $67,652, below its 200-week moving average near $68,000, for the first time in about 11 weeks — and by Saturday morning it had broken below $67,000 to its lowest in about three months, with more than $1.3 billion in long positions liquidated in 24 hours, US spot Bitcoin ETFs at $1.56 billion in net outflows for the year, and the Fear and Greed Index at 13, in extreme fear. The 200-week average is simply the average closing price of the last 200 weeks — nearly four years — and in past cycles it has acted as both support in bear markets and resistance on the way back up, which is why traders watch it so closely. Whether it acts as support or resistance this time is not something a chart line can answer in advance. For Indian users, the price was near ₹64.3 lakh, the tax rules are unchanged, and the practical lesson is narrow: understand what the line is, understand that a weekly close matters more than an intraday dip, and do not let a red week push you into decisions made in a hurry. The weekly close on Sunday night is the next fact that will matter — and it will be a new event, not a repeat of this one.
