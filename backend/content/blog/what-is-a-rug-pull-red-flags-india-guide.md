# What Is a Rug Pull? Red Flags Every Beginner Must Know

A rug pull is one of the fastest ways beginners lose money in crypto, and the term describes exactly how it feels. You find a new token, early buyers seem excited, the chart looks active — and then, without warning, the creators drain the funds and vanish, leaving your token almost impossible to sell. This guide explains what a rug pull is, how the exit actually works, how it differs from a Ponzi scheme, and the red flags Indian beginners can check before touching any new token.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## What Is a Rug Pull, in Simple Words?

A rug pull is a type of exit scam. A team — often anonymous — creates a new crypto token, promotes it heavily, and encourages people to buy it or add money to its trading pool. Once enough outside money has arrived, the insiders take that money out in one move and abandon the project.

The name comes from the expression "pulling the rug out from under someone." One moment you are standing on something that looks solid. The next moment, the support is gone.

Unlike an established cryptocurrency traded widely on exchanges such as CoinDCX, WazirX, or ZebPay in India, a rug-pulled token is usually tiny, new, and traded mainly on a decentralised exchange (DEX) through a liquidity pool. That pool is the key to the whole scam, so it helps to understand it in plain language.

## First, What Is a Liquidity Pool?

When a new token has no company-run exchange behind it, trading still needs a place where buyers and sellers can swap. A liquidity pool is that place: a smart contract holding two assets, for example the new token on one side and a well-known token or stablecoin on the other side. Prices adjust automatically as people trade against the pool.

If the pool holds a meaningful amount of the well-known asset, you can sell your new token back into the pool. If that well-known asset is suddenly removed, there is almost nothing left to sell into. Your token may still show a number on screen, but that number is meaningless if no liquidity remains. That removal is the "pull" in a rug pull.

## How a Rug Pull Works, Step by Step

1. **A token is created cheaply.** Creating a basic token can take minutes and very little money. A logo, a name that sounds exciting, and a social media account are often created on the same day.
2. **Hype is manufactured.** Promoters post in Telegram and WhatsApp groups, pay small influencers for excited videos, and share screenshots of early price rises. The goal is urgency: make you feel you will miss out if you wait and research.
3. **Buyers add money.** Beginners buy the token on a DEX, which pushes funds into the liquidity pool. Some early wallets shown in promotions quietly belong to the creators themselves.
4. **The exit happens.** The creators withdraw the valuable side of the liquidity pool, or sell a very large holding they kept for themselves, or use a hidden rule in the code to create new tokens and sell them.
5. **Holders are left stuck.** With liquidity gone or the price collapsed by the insider sale, ordinary holders cannot exit at a reasonable price. The website goes quiet, social accounts are deleted, and the team stops replying.

The whole cycle can take weeks of promotion and only a few minutes of exit. That speed is why prevention matters far more than hoping to react in time.

## Four Common Types of Rug Pull

### 1. Liquidity pull

The creators remove the valuable assets from the trading pool. This is the classic version and the origin of the name. After the removal, selling becomes almost impossible because there is nothing meaningful left to trade against.

### 2. Hidden mint

The token's code quietly allows the creator to create unlimited new tokens at any time. The creator mints a huge amount for free and sells it into the pool, draining its value. Buyers had no obvious way to see this power if they never checked the contract.

### 3. Sell restriction, or honeypot

Buying works normally, which makes the token look healthy, but selling is blocked or heavily penalised for everyone except insider wallets. The rising chart is a trap: money can enter, but it cannot leave. This is why a token that only ever goes up, with no visible successful sellers, deserves extra suspicion.

### 4. Team dump

There is no dramatic code trick. The team simply holds a very large share of the supply and sells it all at once once promotion has attracted enough buyers. The project may even have a real website and roadmap — the exit was planned in the allocation from the start.

## Rug Pull vs Ponzi Scheme: What Is the Difference?

These two scams are often confused, and this site has a separate guide on Ponzi schemes. Keeping them apart helps you spot each one earlier.

| Factor | Rug pull | Ponzi scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Core trick | Insiders exit and drain the token | Early users paid from new users' money |
| Needs recruitment? | No — promotion helps, but the exit is one move | Yes — collapses without constant new money |
| Typical speed of ending | Minutes to hours | Weeks to months |
| What you hold at the end | A token with no liquidity | A balance on a platform that stops paying |
| Main warning sign | Anonymous team, unlocked liquidity, hidden code powers | Promised regular returns and referral rewards |

A project can, of course, show signs of both. The labels matter less than the behaviour: if insiders can take the money and leave without consequence, the risk sits entirely with you.

## 9 Red Flags That Appear Before the Pull

No single flag proves a scam, but several together form a pattern beginners should walk away from.

- **Anonymous team with no history.** No real names, no verifiable past work, and accounts created recently.
- **Liquidity is not locked.** Honest small projects commonly lock pool funds for a set period using a third-party service. If the creators can withdraw liquidity at any moment, they hold the exit switch.
- **One wallet holds a huge share.** If a few linked wallets control a very large part of the supply, a single sale can collapse the price.
- **The contract cannot be checked.** Unverified code, or code that allows minting, blacklisting wallets, or changing fees without limits.
- **You can buy but test-selling looks difficult.** Reports from other holders about failed sales, or a contract that treats insider wallets differently.
- **Promotion is all urgency, no substance.** Countdowns, "next big coin" language, and pressure to bring friends in, instead of clear documents and slow, boring updates.
- **Copied branding and a brand-new website.** Stolen logos, stock images, and a whitepaper that is a few vague pages with no technical detail.
- **No presence on any established platform.** A token promoted only through private groups and links, never listed or reviewed anywhere independent.
- **Promoters refuse simple questions.** Ask where liquidity is locked or who holds the largest wallets. Evasion, anger, or banning you from the group is itself an answer.

## How Rug Pulls Reach Indian Beginners

Most victims do not discover these tokens by searching carefully. The token arrives through a trusted-feeling channel: a WhatsApp forward from an acquaintance, a Telegram "signals" group, a short video promising early access, or a message claiming a well-known exchange listing is about to happen. Payment is often rushed through a wallet link, and some scams add a second trap by asking for a UPI payment to "unlock" withdrawals afterwards — money that is simply stolen as well.

Treat any token that reaches you this way as unverified by default. A genuine project does not need your decision in the next ten minutes, and it does not recruit through private forwards.

## A Five-Minute Check Before You Touch a New Token

1. **Find the contract address from an official source**, never only from a chat message, and view it on a block explorer.
2. **Check holder concentration.** If the top few non-pool wallets hold a very large share, note it as a serious risk.
3. **Look for a liquidity lock** and its expiry date. No lock, or a lock ending in days, means the exit switch is live.
4. **Read independent discussions** outside the project's own group. Search the token name together with words like "scam" and "cannot sell."
5. **Test the exit before any real amount.** If you ever experiment for learning, a tiny test buy followed immediately by a test sell reveals honeypot behaviour. Never add more because the buy step worked.

If any step is impossible to complete because information is hidden, that is not a neutral result. Hidden information is the risk.

## What to Do If You Have Been Caught in One

Act quickly, but do not send more money to anyone promising recovery — recovery agents who demand an upfront fee are a common second scam. Disconnect your wallet from the token's website using a reputable revoke tool or your wallet's connected-sites settings, so remaining approvals cannot be misused. Save everything: transaction hashes, wallet addresses, chat screenshots, group names, and any payment records, including UPI references if you paid an individual. If an Indian exchange account was involved, inform its support team. Then report the fraud on cybercrime.gov.in or call the national helpline on 1930 as soon as possible.

For tax and records, keep the loss documentation with your other crypto records. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. As a general picture, gains on crypto sales in India are taxed at a flat 30%, and 1% TDS applies on sale consideration in many transactions. How a scam loss is treated can be limited under current rules, so keep complete records and take professional advice for your own filing rather than assuming a loss can simply be adjusted elsewhere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a rug pull in simple words?

A rug pull is an exit scam where the creators of a token attract buyers, then suddenly remove the funds or sell a huge hidden holding and disappear. The token is left with little or no liquidity, so ordinary holders cannot sell at any fair price. The name comes from the phrase pulling the rug out from under someone.

### How is a rug pull different from a Ponzi scheme?

A Ponzi scheme pays early participants using money from new participants and depends on constant recruitment. A rug pull does not need recruitment to keep running — it is a single exit, where insiders drain liquidity or dump tokens they control in one move. Both are scams, but a rug pull usually ends in minutes or hours, not months.

### Can you get your money back after a rug pull?

Recovery is very unlikely. Blockchain transfers are usually irreversible, creators are often anonymous, and funds move through many wallets quickly. If you used an Indian exchange or a payment app, report the fraud immediately, save all transaction records, and file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in or call the 1930 helpline.

## Conclusion

A rug pull is not bad luck or normal market volatility — it is a planned exit built into who controls the liquidity, the supply, and the code. That is good news in one way: the warning signs exist before the exit happens, in anonymous teams, unlocked liquidity, concentrated holdings, and contracts nobody is allowed to examine. Slow down, run the five-minute check, and remember that a token you cannot verify is a token you can safely skip. There will always be another project; there is rarely a way back from a drained wallet.
