## US October PCE Inflation Hits 3.3%: Crypto India Guide

US October PCE inflation came in hotter than expected on 25 November 2026, and Bitcoin slid below $58,000 in the hours after the release. The US Bureau of Economic Analysis published the October Personal Consumption Expenditures report at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time — 7:00 p.m. in India — alongside a sharply revised second estimate of third-quarter GDP growth. Core PCE, the Federal Reserve's preferred measure of underlying inflation, rose 3.3% from a year earlier, against 3.2% expected by economists, while GDP grew at a 4.3% annual rate, up from the 3.4% reported in the first estimate and above the 3.6% economists had forecast.

The two reports arrived on the same morning for a reason: with US markets closing for Thanksgiving on Thursday, a packed slate of economic data was compressed into a single Wednesday session. Traders came into the release already expecting the Fed to raise interest rates at its 8–9 December meeting, and the data removed what little doubt remained. After the numbers, traders priced a 98% chance of a quarter-point hike in December, according to coverage of the release. Bitcoin had traded near $58,400 before the data and fell to around $57,500 by the US morning, while the Nasdaq Composite tumbled about 4%, the S&P 500 fell about 3% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost about 2%.

This guide explains what PCE inflation actually is, what the October numbers said, why a one-tenth-of-a-point miss moved markets, how the data connects to the December Fed meeting, and what it means for crypto users in India — without predicting what the Fed will do or where any price will go.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 25 November 2026 by Bitcoin Magazine, CoinDoo, the Wall Street Journal, FXStreet and CoinGape, is:

- **Core PCE rose 3.3% year-on-year, above the 3.2% forecast.** The core measure, which strips out food and energy, climbed 0.3% in October from September — matching the monthly forecast — but the annual rate came in a tenth of a point above expectations and up from 3.1% in September. Three of the four inflation measures matched forecasts exactly: headline PCE rose 0.3% month-on-month and 3.8% year-on-year, both as expected.
- **Third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3%.** The Bureau of Economic Analysis raised its second estimate of Q3 growth to a 4.3% annual rate, from the 3.4% pace first reported, and above the 3.6% economists had expected. Strong growth plus sticky inflation is the combination that most supports the case for higher rates.
- **December hike bets hardened to 98%.** Before the release, futures markets already priced a December rate hike at about 93%, according to Bitwise analysis cited in the coverage. After the data, traders priced a 98% chance of a quarter-point increase at the Fed's 8–9 December meeting.
- **Bitcoin fell from around $58,800 to near $57,500.** The 30-minute candle that opened at the 8:30 a.m. ET release fell from around $58,800 to just above $58,000 on one of the session's heaviest volume bars, and the slide extended to a low of about $57,945. At the time of writing, Bitcoin trades near $57,500–$58,000, still above the weekend low near $55,100 — its lowest level in more than two years — but down about 24% in November, on course for its worst month since 2022.
- **The sell-off spread beyond crypto.** The Nasdaq sank about 4% as the technology sell-off spread from Asia, where South Korea's Kospi fell 6.16% overnight. Crypto stocks fell with the market: Strategy dropped about 8% to near $71, after closing down 8.12% at $77.55 on Tuesday; Coinbase fell about 6% to near $96; Robinhood lost nearly 6%; and Circle slid more than 7%.
- **ETF outflows continued.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $231.6 million on Tuesday, less than half of Monday's $569.7 million, in their sixth straight session of outflows. November withdrawals reached about $4.94 billion through 24 November, putting the funds on course for their worst month since launching in January 2024.
- **Americans earned more and spent less.** Personal income rose 0.7% ($201.8 billion) in October and disposable income climbed 0.8%, but inflation-adjusted consumer spending fell $5.8 billion — less than 0.1% — as real goods spending dropped about $13 billion. The saving rate rose to 4.1% from 3.6% in September, and personal saving reached $1.00 trillion.
- **Consumer confidence jumped.** The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index rose to 103.3 in November, a rare bright spot in a session otherwise dominated by the inflation and growth data.

None of this is a forecast. The numbers describe what happened in October and what traders now expect in December; they do not say what the Fed will actually decide, and the Fed's own meeting is still two weeks away.

## What Is PCE — and Why Crypto Traders Watch It

PCE stands for Personal Consumption Expenditures, and it is the US government's measure of the prices Americans actually pay for the goods and services they consume. It is published monthly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the same agency that publishes GDP, and it is built largely from business surveys — what shops, hospitals, landlords and service providers actually charged — rather than from a fixed basket of household purchases.

That construction is exactly why the Federal Reserve prefers PCE over the better-known Consumer Price Index (CPI). Three differences matter:

- **It covers more spending.** PCE includes spending on behalf of households — for example, medical care paid by an employer or the government — that CPI leaves out. It is a wider picture of what consumption actually costs.
- **It adapts as people adapt.** The CPI basket is fixed for a period, so if beef gets expensive and shoppers switch to chicken, CPI keeps pricing the beef. PCE adjusts for those substitutions, which makes it a closer read on the prices people actually end up paying.
- **It gets revised.** PCE is updated as better data arrives, so the first print is a starting point rather than a final word. CPI is revised far less. That also means today's numbers can move again in later releases — a detail worth remembering before treating any single print as settled.

Core PCE strips out food and energy, the two categories whose prices swing most violently with weather, harvests and geopolitics. A jump in petrol prices after an oil shock tells the Fed little about whether underlying inflation is entrenched, so the Fed watches the core number to judge the persistent trend. That is why a one-tenth-of-a-point miss on annual core PCE — 3.3% against 3.2% expected — carried more weight on 25 November than the headline figures, which matched forecasts exactly.

Crypto traders watch PCE for a simple reason: it is the last full read on inflation the Fed gets before its December meeting, and inflation is the variable that most directly moves interest-rate expectations. Higher expected rates make holding dollars and US government bonds more attractive, which tends to pull money away from riskier assets such as crypto, and they usually strengthen the dollar. A stronger dollar makes Bitcoin more expensive in rupee terms for Indian buyers even when the dollar price is flat. That chain — inflation print, rate expectations, dollar, risk appetite — is the transmission mechanism that connected a US data release at 8:30 a.m. in New York to a Bitcoin price move that Indian readers could watch in real time.

## Why a Hot Core Print Hardened December Hike Bets

The December rate-hike story did not start on 25 November. It has been building for weeks, and the PCE report was the latest piece of evidence in a sequence this site has been tracking.

On 10 November, October's US consumer price index rose 0.4% month-on-month against a forecast of 0.3%, and core CPI rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected. Bank of America told clients the CPI data "all but lock in" a December hike, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller had said before the release that if core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher, "a 0.5% rate hike should be considered". Core CPI then printed exactly 0.3%.

By 14 November, the CME FedWatch tool assigned an 88.5% probability to a 25-basis-point hike at the 8–9 December meeting, with 11.5% for a larger 50-basis-point move and nothing at all for rates staying unchanged or being cut. Prediction markets agreed: Kalshi priced a 25-basis-point hike at 89% and Polymarket at 88%.

The PCE release pushed those odds higher still, to 98% for a quarter-point hike. But the coverage also carried an important caveat that the headline move can obscure. CoinDoo's analysis of the release noted that three of the four inflation measures matched forecasts, that the core annual miss was driven more by last year's base — the comparison month a year ago — than by anything new in October itself, and that with hike odds already at 93% before the print, the selling looked like confirmation of a known path rather than a response to anything new in the report. In other words, the market was already positioned for a hike; the PCE data confirmed the positioning rather than creating it.

The GDP revision reinforced the same direction. Growth of 4.3% is strong by any standard, and strong growth removes the argument that the economy is too weak for higher rates. The combination — inflation a touch hotter than expected and growth a lot stronger than expected — is why the 10-year Treasury yield's fall to 4.84% offered little support to Bitcoin: investors bought bonds for protection, but the rate-hike expectation itself was the dominant force for risk assets.

One more detail from the release deserves attention because it cuts against the simple "hot inflation" story. Real consumer spending actually fell in October, by $5.8 billion, and the saving rate jumped to 4.1% from 3.6% — the widest gap between income growth and spending growth since at least April. Americans earned more and bought slightly less. That is not the profile of an economy overheating from the consumer side; it is a reminder that the inflation picture is mixed, and that a single report contains several stories at once.

## What It Means for Crypto Users in India

The PCE report is about the US economy, and its numbers are in dollars. But there are five things Indian users should take from it.

**The rupee price falls with the dollar price — and then some.** Bitcoin near $57,500–$58,000 translates to roughly ₹51 lakh on a straight dollar conversion at prevailing exchange rates, and higher on Indian exchanges, where isolated INR liquidity pools and the stablecoin premium push prices above the global conversion. The November fall is about 24% for the month, and the drawdown from the October 2025 peak of about $126,000 is more than half. The local premium means Indian buyers pay more than the global price to enter and Indian sellers receive more than the global price to exit — but the direction of the move is the same in both.

**A rate hike is a dollar story before it is a crypto story.** The mechanism that matters for India runs through the dollar. When US rate expectations rise, the dollar tends to strengthen, and a stronger dollar raises the rupee cost of buying Bitcoin even if the dollar price does not move. That is why Indian holders can see their portfolio fall in rupee terms on a day when nothing has changed in India at all. The PCE report matters in India because the dollar is the unit in which the global Bitcoin price is set.

**The Indian tax rules did not change today.** Gains on crypto transfers in India are taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and 4% cess) under Section 115BBH, with 1% TDS under Section 194S on transactions above the specified thresholds, and losses cannot be set off against other income. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. A fall in the global price does not create a tax benefit in India the way it does in some other countries, so the decision to sell should not be driven by a hope of a tax saving that does not exist here.

**INR access is still the live issue at home.** The global story on 25 November is about inflation and rates; the Indian story in November has also been about access to rupees. Coinbase halted INR withdrawals in India on 14 November after a banking-partner outage, CoinDCX reported INR withdrawal delays on 19–20 November, and Unocoin has had services suspended since 9 November after a security breach. Before any decision about a holding, an Indian user should know whether their exchange's INR withdrawal route is actually working — a question the PCE report cannot answer, but one that matters more than any US data release when it comes to getting money out.

**A 98% probability is still not a decision.** Traders price a near-certain December hike, but the Federal Open Market Committee has not announced anything, and the meeting is on 8–9 December — two weeks away. Odds can change quickly if new data arrives, and the PCE report itself will be revised as better data comes in. The Fed's own meeting minutes, the US jobs report on 4 December and the next inflation print on 10 December all land before the meeting, and each of them can move the odds. Treating a market probability as a settled outcome is the mistake the coverage of this release warns against most clearly: the selling on 25 November was confirmation of a path the market had already priced, not a verdict on what the Fed will do.

## The Bottom Line

The US Bureau of Economic Analysis reported on 25 November 2026 that October core PCE inflation — the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge — rose 3.3% from a year earlier, a tenth of a point above the 3.2% economists expected, while headline PCE matched forecasts at 3.8% and third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% from 3.4%. Traders priced a 98% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate hike at the 8–9 December meeting, and Bitcoin fell from around $58,800 to near $57,500 in the hours after the release, in a session when the Nasdaq fell about 4% and US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded their sixth straight day of outflows.

For Indian users, the practical takeaways are the ones that do not depend on any price call: understand what PCE measures and why the Fed watches the core number, remember that a market probability is not a Fed decision, know that Indian tax law does not let you set off crypto losses, check that your exchange's INR withdrawal route is working before you need it, and treat a data release as one piece of evidence in a longer sequence — not as a signal about what happens next. The numbers in this report describe an economy running hotter than expected. What they do not do is say where any price goes from here.
