Here is a habit that separates beginners who survive from beginners who donate their money to the market: before analyzing any altcoin — Ethereum, Solana, or some tiny token you just discovered — look at Bitcoin first.

It feels backwards. You want to trade Coin X, so why study Coin B? Because in crypto, Bitcoin is not just another coin. It is the weather system everything else flies through. Ignoring it is like planning a picnic without checking the sky.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## Why Bitcoin Moves Everything

Bitcoin is the largest cryptocurrency by far, usually accounting for roughly half of the total crypto market's value. That size gives it a gravitational pull on everything else:

- **Liquidity gravity.** Bitcoin has the deepest order books and the most trading volume. Big investors move in and out of crypto through Bitcoin first, so its price moves ripple outward.
- **Sentiment benchmark.** When Bitcoin crashes 10% in a day, fear spreads across the whole market. Altcoin holders panic-sell too, even if nothing changed about their specific coin.
- **Trading pairs.** A huge share of altcoin trading still happens against Bitcoin pairs. When Bitcoin's price swings, the math of those pairs shifts automatically.

The practical result: altcoins are like small boats, and Bitcoin is the tide. A skilled sailor can navigate a small boat well — but nobody out-sails a bad tide.

## What Bitcoin Dominance Tells You

Bitcoin dominance is simply Bitcoin's share of the total crypto market value, expressed as a percentage. It is one of the most useful numbers a beginner can learn to read:

- **Dominance rising** means money is flowing toward Bitcoin and away from altcoins. Altcoin trades face a headwind here — even good setups struggle.
- **Dominance falling** means altcoins are collectively outperforming Bitcoin. This is when altcoin trades have the wind at their backs.
- **Dominance flat** means a neutral environment where individual coin setups matter most.

You can find this number on any major crypto data site. Glancing at it takes five seconds and can save you from an entire category of bad trades: buying altcoins right as capital rotates into Bitcoin.

## How This Looks in Practice

Imagine this scenario. You spot an altcoin that has formed a beautiful chart pattern — bouncing perfectly off support three times. You are excited. But then you check Bitcoin: it just broke below a key support level on heavy selling volume.

What usually happens next? Bitcoin keeps falling, fear spreads, and your "perfect" altcoin pattern breaks down along with everything else. The setup was not wrong — the *timing* was. The same pattern a week later, with Bitcoin stable, might have worked.

Now the reverse scenario: Bitcoin is grinding steadily upward, dominance is flat, and your altcoin shows strength. That is a green light — not a guarantee, but the environment is cooperating instead of fighting you.

Professional traders think in layers: **market first, coin second.** Beginners do the opposite: they fall in love with a coin, then get blindsided by the market.

## When the Rule Bends

Bitcoin's leadership is not absolute. There are periods — traders call them "alt seasons" — when selected altcoins rally hard while Bitcoin moves sideways. During these windows, obsessing over Bitcoin can cause you to miss real opportunities.

But notice the asymmetry: altcoins sometimes ignore Bitcoin on the way *up*, but they almost never ignore it on the way *down*. A Bitcoin crash drags nearly everything with it. So the rule is really about defense: checking Bitcoin first is mainly how you avoid trading into a storm.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- **Analyzing the altcoin in isolation.** Spending an hour on a small coin's chart while ignoring that Bitcoin is in freefall. Always zoom out first.
- **Confusing altcoin strength with independence.** "This coin is pumping while Bitcoin is flat — it must be special!" Sometimes yes. Often it is a short-lived spike that reverses violently. Strength *against* the market deserves extra skepticism, not extra confidence.
- **Forgetting stablecoin parking.** When Bitcoin looks dangerous, you do not have to exit crypto entirely — many traders temporarily park funds in stablecoins (crypto pegged to the dollar) and wait. Doing nothing is a valid strategy.
- **Overtrading altcoins for excitement.** Altcoins move fast, which feels thrilling. That thrill is expensive. Boring Bitcoin-range days are when most beginners should be studying, not trading.

Fewer, better-timed trades (guided by Bitcoin's trend) mean lower costs and simpler taxes. Patience literally pays here.

## The 30-Second Routine

Before any altcoin trade, answer three questions:

1. **Bitcoin's trend:** up, down, or sideways on the daily chart?
2. **Bitcoin dominance:** rising, falling, or flat?
3. **Key levels:** is Bitcoin near support (safer backdrop) or resistance (caution)?

If Bitcoin is crashing through support — close the altcoin tab. The trade will still be there tomorrow, and so will your capital if you protect it today.

## Correlation Is Not a Law

"Look at Bitcoin first" works because altcoins *usually* follow Bitcoin — but "usually" is doing heavy lifting. Understanding *why* the correlation exists keeps you from treating it as a law of physics.

The correlation has three engines, and each can weaken independently:

1. **Shared liquidity.** The same traders, market makers, and funds trade both Bitcoin and altcoins. When they de-risk, they sell everything at once — correlation spikes toward 100%. But dedicated altcoin communities and separate liquidity pools are growing, slowly loosening this link.
2. **Bitcoin-denominated thinking.** Many altcoins are still quoted and traded against Bitcoin pairs, mechanically tying their fate to it. As stablecoin and fiat pairs dominate more volume, this mechanical link fades.
3. **Narrative gravity.** Crypto media frames every market move through Bitcoin. Sentiment contagion is real, but it is psychological — and psychology shifts. A major altcoin-specific event (a network upgrade, a regulatory decision) can and does decouple individual coins for days or weeks.

What does this mean for your routine? Keep checking Bitcoin first — the base rates still favour it — but treat sharp divergences as *information*, not errors. When an altcoin rallies hard while Bitcoin falls, ask what the market knows that you do not, rather than assuming the altcoin "must" fall back in line. The rule protects you from storms; curiosity about the exceptions teaches you how markets evolve.

## The Bottom Line

"Look at Bitcoin first" is one of the simplest edges in crypto trading, and it costs nothing. It will not make every trade win — nothing does — but it filters out an entire class of avoidable losses: the ones where the market was screaming "danger" and you were too focused on one coin to hear it.
