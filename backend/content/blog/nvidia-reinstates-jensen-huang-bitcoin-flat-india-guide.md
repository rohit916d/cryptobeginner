## Nvidia Brought Jensen Huang Back — and Bitcoin Barely Blinked

Nvidia's board reversed course on Monday 23 November 2026 and reinstated Jensen Huang as the company's chief executive, less than two weeks after he resigned. The market reaction was extraordinary: Nvidia shares closed up 31.48%, the Nasdaq Composite surged 6.51%, and the S&P 500 had its best day since April 2025.

Bitcoin did almost nothing. It traded near $61,000 all day, roughly flat, with the crypto Fear & Greed Index stuck at 11 — deep in Extreme Fear. After weeks in which every AI-stock sell-off dragged crypto down with it, the first big AI-stock rally of the month left Bitcoin behind.

This guide explains what happened at Nvidia, why the stock market celebrated so loudly, why Bitcoin stayed flat anyway, and what Indian crypto users should take away from a day when the two markets finally went their separate ways.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## What Happened at Nvidia

The sequence of events, as reported by CNN, Reuters, CNBC-TV18, the Indian Express and Morningstar, unfolded like this:

**12 November — Huang resigns.** Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder, resigned as president, chief executive officer and board member. His departure followed reports of extramarital affairs, including one involving a person described as a suspected Chinese spy. The reports raised questions in Washington about Nvidia's exports of advanced AI chips to China and about whether Huang posed a national-security risk, and the company's shares fell sharply — down nearly a third in the days after the resignation, according to reports at the time.

**17 November — the board says no return.** Nvidia's newly installed board chair, Harvey Jones, said on the company's earnings call that the board did not intend to bring Huang back. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, was running the company while the board said it was considering internal and external candidates, and had hired an executive-search firm to help find a permanent chief executive.

**17 November — earnings beat, shares still weak.** The same day, Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year on year, beating expectations — but the leadership uncertainty overshadowed the numbers, and the stock kept falling. Bitcoin, which had been sliding with the AI trade all month, fell below $72,000 in the same period.

**23 November — the board reverses itself.** After a series of intense weekend meetings and discussions with Huang, the board voted unanimously to reinstate him as president, chief executive officer and board director, effective immediately. The board said it was satisfied with respect to Huang's candour and his ability to lead Nvidia. Kress resumed her role as chief financial officer, and the board's independent investigation continues. Huang still has to testify on Capitol Hill in December, and congressional inquiries into chip exports to China and Southeast Asia remain open.

The reversal had been pushed from several directions. A reinstatement petition gathered more than 177,000 signatures, including those of Elon Musk and Tim Sweeney, according to Reuters. US President Donald Trump publicly backed Huang's return, posting on Truth Social on Monday that the board had made "the correct and proper decision". And the board's own chair had, only six days earlier, said the opposite — which is what makes this one of the sharpest corporate U-turns in recent memory.

## The Market Reaction: Nvidia's Best Day Since 2003

The numbers from Monday 23 November tell the story of a market that had been waiting for exactly this news:

| Market | Monday 23 November move | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Nvidia (NVDA) | +31.48% | Best day since 2003; intraday high +36.6%; about $952 billion added in market value in one session |
| Nasdaq Composite | +6.51% | Best day since April 2025 |
| S&P 500 | +4.73% | Best day since April 2025 |
| Dow Jones | +3.01% (1,448 points) | Best day since 4 November, the post-midterm rally |
| CoreWeave | +22.3% | AI cloud company heavily dependent on Nvidia |
| ASML | +9% | Dutch semiconductor-equipment maker |
| Bitcoin (BTC) | About flat, near $61,000 | +0.10% on the day; Fear & Greed Index 11, Extreme Fear |

The announcement broke shortly before 9:00 a.m. Eastern Time, and it turbocharged a day that was already set for a bounce. US stocks had been poised for a sharp recovery after a punishing week in which all three major indexes lost 8% or more, the S&P 500 recorded its seventh straight day of losses — its longest losing streak since February 2020 — and the Nasdaq plunged more than 5% on Friday alone, sitting in the red for the year before Monday's bounce.

"The shocking decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO helps to remove a key level of uncertainty from the AI trade, which has been suffering in recent weeks," Glen Smith, chief investment officer at GDS Wealth Management, wrote in a note quoted by CNN. Ryan Detrick, chief market strategist at Carson Group, put it more simply: "Love him or hate him, Jensen brings a lot of credibility and financing."

Not everyone was convinced the rally would last. Jonas Goltermann, chief markets economist at Capital Economics, told CNN the early rebound looked largely mechanical after such a big sell-off, and warned that "the main challenges for the stock market remain: late cycle red flags across the board, hawkish Fed, tighter funding environment, extremely optimistic earnings forecasts to hit." Even after Monday's surge, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq were still down 7.4% and 12.7% respectively from their peaks on 6 October, and the Dow was down 8.75% from its 5 August peak.

## Why Bitcoin Stayed Flat While Stocks Soared

This is the part that matters most for crypto readers, because it breaks a pattern this site has covered repeatedly. On 6 November, Bitcoin fell with the Nasdaq in a tech rout driven by post-midterm AI-regulation fears. On 13 November, it fell again as Nvidia's chief executive resigned. For most of the month, the AI trade and the crypto market moved as one — down together.

On 23 November, they split. Here is why the split happened, and what it does and does not mean.

**Nvidia's jump was company-specific.** The reinstatement removed uncertainty about one company — the single most important supplier of AI chips in the world. That is a powerful reason to buy Nvidia shares and the stocks of companies that depend on Nvidia, like CoreWeave. It is not, by itself, a reason to buy Bitcoin, which has no business relationship with Nvidia and no claim on its earnings.

**Bitcoin's pressures are separate.** Bitcoin entered Monday carrying its own weight: expectations that the US Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again in December, after October's hotter-than-expected inflation print and the October FOMC minutes showing almost all officials expecting more hikes; spot Bitcoin ETF outflows of about $1.79 billion in the week ending 20 November, the third-largest weekly outflow since the funds launched, with November outflows of about $4.14 billion through 20 November; and a market that had just fallen to a two-year low near $55,000 on Saturday before whipsawing back above $60,000. None of those pressures changed because Nvidia's board changed its mind.

**The correlation was never a rule.** The 90-day rolling correlation between Bitcoin and the S&P 500 stands at about 0.54, according to market data published on 24 November — a moderate relationship, not a lockstep one. On Monday, the S&P 500 gained 4.73% while Bitcoin stayed flat, which is exactly what a 0.54 correlation allows: the two markets usually move in the same direction, but often do not, and sometimes move in opposite directions. The November sell-off made the link feel absolute because both markets were falling for the same broad reason — fear about the AI boom and about tighter money. When the reason for the stock rally was specific to one company, the link broke.

**Bitcoin was already oversold and unresponsive.** Bitcoin's 14-day relative strength index sat at 26.92 on Monday, and the Fear & Greed Index at 11 — Bitcoin's most oversold technical reading since the 2022 bear market, according to the same market data. Yet the price did not respond to a strong equity rally. That is a useful reminder that an oversold reading describes how far a market has fallen, not when it will turn. Extreme fear can persist for weeks, and a market in extreme fear often needs its own catalyst — not someone else's good news.

**The next catalyst is already dated.** October US PCE inflation data is due on Tuesday 25 November at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time. A hot print would re-anchor expectations of a 25-basis-point December rate rise and remove any near-term macro tailwind for a Bitcoin recovery, while a cooler print could ease the rate-hike pressure that has weighed on crypto all month. Either way, that release — not Nvidia's boardroom — is the next event that speaks directly to Bitcoin's own pressures.

## What This Means for Indian Crypto Users

Most Indian readers will never own Nvidia shares, and the reinstatement changes nothing about Indian crypto rules. But the episode still carries four lessons that apply directly to anyone holding crypto in India.

**Watch the driver, not just the direction.** When Bitcoin and stocks move together, it is tempting to treat them as one market. Monday shows they are two markets that sometimes share a driver — fear about the AI boom and about interest rates — and sometimes do not. Before concluding that a stock-market rally will lift crypto, ask what is actually causing the rally. A company-specific reversal, like Nvidia's, is a weaker reason for Bitcoin to rise than a change in interest-rate expectations or in ETF flows, which are Bitcoin's own drivers.

**Extreme fear is a condition, not a signal.** The Fear & Greed Index at 11 and an RSI of 26.92 describe a market that has fallen hard and fast. They do not say when it turns, and Monday proved the point: Bitcoin was at its most oversold reading since 2022 and still ignored a 6.5% Nasdaq rally. Treat sentiment gauges as descriptions of mood, not as instructions.

**Your India price is not the global price.** Bitcoin traded near $61,000 globally on Monday, roughly ₹57 lakh on Indian trackers, while domestic exchange order books often quote higher because of the India premium — which stood above 12% at the 19 November fix. That premium is one reason Indian buyers can feel a sell-off differently from global holders, and one reason to compare the global price with your own exchange's quote before judging any move.

**The tax rules have not changed.** Any profit on virtual digital assets in India is taxed at a flat 30%, with 1% TDS rules applying in many cases, and losses cannot be set off against gains. A good day for Nvidia shares does not change that, and neither does a flat day for Bitcoin. Keep a record of every trade, with dates and amounts, because your own notes are the only complete record for filing.

## The Bottom Line

In eleven days, Nvidia went from losing its chief executive, to beating earnings under an interim chief executive, to bringing its co-founder back — and the market went from panic, to a shrug, to a 31% single-day surge that added about $952 billion in value. The board that said on 17 November it did not intend to bring Huang back voted unanimously on 23 November to do exactly that, after a petition signed by more than 177,000 people and public backing from the US President.

Bitcoin watched all of it happen and stayed near $61,000. That is not a failure of the crypto market or a sign that the link with stocks is gone forever. It is a reminder that correlations are averages, not promises — and that Bitcoin's own pressures, from Federal Reserve rate-hike expectations to record ETF outflows, will decide its next move more than any single company's boardroom drama. The next event that speaks directly to those pressures is the US inflation data due on 25 November, and that is the one Indian crypto users should be watching.
