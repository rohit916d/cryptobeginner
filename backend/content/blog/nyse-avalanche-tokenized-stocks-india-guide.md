The New York Stock Exchange — the world's largest stock exchange — has picked Avalanche technology to power settlement on its planned tokenized securities platform. Ava Labs, the company behind Avalanche, announced the selection on Monday, 9 November 2026, and the news was carried the same day by CoinDesk, Coin360, Bitrue, TheNewsCrypto and others. The plan is a 24/7 market for tokenized stocks: trading, clearing and settlement that never closes, with near-instant settlement and stablecoin funding. For Indian readers, the story lands close to home — tokenised US stocks are exactly the product at the centre of the Enforcement Directorate's searches at CoinDCX and Delta Exchange last week. This guide explains what the NYSE is actually building, what it is not, and what it means — and does not mean — for investors in India.

## What Happened: The Announcement

The announcement came from Ava Labs on Monday, 9 November 2026. Under the plan, Avalanche will provide the core ledger technology for the clearing firm supporting an upcoming digital alternative trading system — an ATS — affiliated with the New York Stock Exchange. An ATS is a regulated trading venue that is not a full stock exchange: it matches buyers and sellers under securities rules, but with a different licence and structure from the exchange itself.

The platform is designed to support tokenized securities — shares of listed companies represented as tokens on a blockchain — with trading, clearing and settlement running around the clock. The NYSE's existing Pillar trading engine will keep doing the job it does today: matching buy and sell orders. The blockchain layer sits behind that, handling what happens after a trade is agreed: recording it, clearing it, settling it and holding the assets in custody.

The key features reported are:

- **24/7 operation:** trading, clearing and settlement continuing outside traditional stock-market hours, subject to regulatory approval.
- **Instant settlement:** trades settling far faster than the one-to-two-day cycle of conventional stock markets.
- **Fractional shares:** investors able to own fractions of expensive shares rather than whole ones.
- **Stablecoin funding:** the ability to fund trades with stablecoins.
- **Multi-chain support:** tokenized shares issued on other blockchains connecting to the NYSE's dedicated ledger for recording and settlement.

The market reacted quickly. AVAX, Avalanche's native token, rose around 5% in the 24 hours after the announcement, according to CoinDesk, while other outlets reported gains of up to 8%. Market snapshots on 10 November put AVAX at around $8.70. But the price move is the least important part of the story, and this guide will not treat it as a signal of anything to come.

## What Is a Tokenized Stock?

A tokenized stock is a digital token on a blockchain that represents a share — or a claim on a share — of a listed company. The token's value is designed to track the underlying share. If the share goes up, the token goes up; if the share pays a dividend, the token holder is generally meant to receive the equivalent.

The idea is not new. Offshore crypto platforms have offered tokenized versions of US stocks for years, and the ED's November investigation into CoinDCX and Delta Exchange concerned precisely this kind of product: tokenised shares of companies such as Apple, Google and Tesla, allegedly offered to Indian users through INR deposits converted into USDT and routed to foreign exchanges. What is new about the NYSE announcement is who is doing it. This is the world's largest stock exchange building the regulated version of the same idea, inside a licensed venue, under a named regulator, with its own clearing infrastructure.

That difference matters more than the technology. In a tokenized-stock product, the questions that decide whether it is safe are not about the blockchain — they are about custody and law. Who actually holds the real share behind the token? Who is the custodian, and what happens if the custodian fails? Which regulator supervises the venue, and what rights does a token holder have if something goes wrong? The NYSE's answer is to put those questions inside the existing securities framework, rather than outside it.

The practical attractions are real, and they are the reason exchanges are pursuing this. Traditional stock markets close: in India, the NSE and BSE trade from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM on weekdays, and the NYSE has its own fixed hours. A tokenized market that never closes would let investors react to news at any hour. Settlement in conventional markets takes a day or more; on a blockchain ledger it can happen in minutes or seconds, which frees up the money tied up between a trade and its settlement. And fractional ownership lets a small investor own a slice of a share that costs thousands of dollars — something that is already possible through some global brokers, but that tokenization could make cheaper and more widely available.

## How the NYSE Platform Will Work

The design is deliberately conservative. The NYSE is not handing its exchange to a blockchain. The Pillar matching engine — the system that matches buy and sell orders on the NYSE today — stays in place. Avalanche's role is specific: it provides the ledger that records and settles trades for the platform's clearing firm.

There is an important detail here that early coverage sometimes blurred. The NYSE will run its own custom blockchain built using Avalanche technology, tailored to the platform's regulatory requirements, rather than settling trades on Avalanche's public network. The dedicated chain is designed for controlled participation: the exchange decides who can take part, which is essential for a regulated securities venue, where anonymous participation is not allowed. Tokenized shares issued on other blockchains would connect to that dedicated ledger for settlement, so the platform is not limited to assets created on Avalanche itself.

Ava Labs chief business officer John Nahas told CoinDesk that the selection had been over a year in the making, involving technical, business and ecosystem reviews along the way. That long evaluation period is itself part of the story: the NYSE did not pick a blockchain on the basis of a single demonstration. It tested the technology against the requirements of a regulated market — speed, reliability, cost predictability and control over participation — and chose the one that met them.

Dennis Lee, the NYSE's Head of Digital Issuance, explained the choice in similar terms, saying the ledger had to be fast and reliable, let the exchange control who participates, keep costs predictable, and come with a large developer community behind it. Those are infrastructure criteria, not investment criteria — and they are worth keeping in mind when the coverage turns to token prices.

One thing the announcement did not do is name the clearing firm that will operate on the new ledger. The clearing firm is the institution that stands between buyers and sellers, guaranteeing trades and managing settlement risk. Its identity, along with the launch date and the list of eligible securities, has not yet been disclosed. Some coverage has reported a target of the fourth quarter of 2026 for launch, but that is a reported target, not a confirmed date, and the platform remains subject to regulatory approval.

## The Regulatory Piece: Approval Still Pending

The platform cannot open until regulators allow it. The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) said in September 2026 that it had issued temporary, conditional relief for permissioned venues trading tokenized NMS stocks — the category that covers most listed US shares. SEC Commissioner Hester M. Peirce has supported that approach. But temporary, conditional relief is not a permanent licence, and the NYSE-affiliated ATS will still need the necessary permissions to operate.

That is the main reason to treat this announcement as the start of a process rather than the arrival of a product. Market analysis published on the day of the announcement made the same point: the parties have not identified the clearing firm, the chain configuration, the launch date or the eligible securities, and regulatory approval remains unresolved. An infrastructure selection is a meaningful step — it means the technology choice is made and the build is under way — but it is not a functioning marketplace, and it does not by itself establish demand for AVAX or for any other token.

## A Second Venue, a Different Chain

The NYSE announcement was not the only tokenized-securities news on 9 November. On the same day, Formation Securities notified the SEC of its plans to launch a Solana-based tokenized stock venue in early 2027, initially supporting shares from Galaxy Digital, Exodus Movement and Forward Industries, under the SEC's innovation exemption.

The two projects show how the tokenization race is now being run. It is no longer mainly crypto startups offering tokenized stocks from offshore. It is regulated venues — a NYSE affiliate on Avalanche, and a securities firm on Solana — competing to build the compliant version, each with a different chain and a different regulatory route. For beginners, the useful takeaway is that the competition is about infrastructure and licensing, not about which token's price moves first.

## Why This Matters for Indian Readers

For an Indian reader, the most important part of this story is the contrast with what happened at home last week. Between 4 and 5 November 2026, the Enforcement Directorate searched premises linked to CoinDCX and Delta Exchange, alleging that INR deposits were converted into USDT and routed through foreign exchanges to trade futures, options and tokenised US stocks — including shares of Apple, Google and Tesla — without authorisation. The agency said the volumes ran into thousands of crores per product line, and that the products appeared to violate the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, the SEBI Act and the RBI Act. The investigation is ongoing, the allegations are unproven, and both exchanges have said user funds are safe. We covered the searches in detail in a separate guide.

The NYSE platform is the regulated mirror image of the same idea. The same product — a tokenized share of a US company — offered inside a licensed venue, under a named regulator, with a named clearing process, instead of through an offshore routing arrangement that Indian authorities say was unauthorised. Understanding that contrast is more useful than any price chart: it shows where the global industry is heading, and why Indian regulators care about the route a product takes, not just the product itself.

There are three practical things Indian readers should know.

**First, you cannot simply sign up.** The NYSE platform is a US-regulated venue for subscribers. It is not open to the public yet, it is not aimed at Indian retail investors, and even when it opens, Indian residents will remain bound by Indian law. Sending money abroad to invest in foreign stocks falls under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, and any platform an Indian resident uses should be FIU-IND registered. A US development does not create a shortcut around those rules.

**Second, 24/7 trading cuts both ways.** The ability to trade at any hour sounds convenient, and for reacting to overnight news it is. But markets that never close also never give investors a pause. Prices can move sharply while you sleep, and the cooling-off period that market hours impose disappears. Around-the-clock access is a feature for professionals with risk systems; for a beginner, it is a reason to be more careful, not less.

**Third, the tax rules are unchanged.** None of this changes what an Indian investor owes. Gains on transferring virtual digital assets are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess — 31.2% in total — with no set-off of losses, and sales attract 1% TDS under Section 194S. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. A tokenized stock is not a way around Indian tax, and buying one through an unauthorised route does not make the tax disappear — it adds a legal problem on top of a tax bill.

**The scam warning.** A high-profile announcement like this one attracts impersonators. Expect fake "NYSE tokenized stock" investment schemes, fake customer-support accounts, and messages promising returns from the new platform. The NYSE platform does not take deposits from Indian retail investors and does not offer investment products to the public. If anyone contacts you about investing through it, treat it as a scam. Report fraud on India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the 1930 helpline, and never share a seed phrase or password with anyone claiming to help.

## What to Watch Next

Five things will show whether this announcement becomes a real market or stays a press release.

The first is regulatory approval. Watch whether the SEC grants the permissions the ATS needs, and whether the temporary, conditional relief for tokenized-stock venues becomes a permanent framework. Without that, there is no platform.

The second is the clearing firm. When the NYSE names the clearing firm, and when it names the eligible securities and a launch date, the project moves from an announcement to a plan with dates. Until then, every timeline is a reported target.

The third is the rival venue. Formation Securities aims to launch its Solana-based tokenized stock venue in early 2027. If it opens first, it will set the early precedent for how these venues actually operate — and what regulators ask of them.

The fourth is the response in India. The ED's CoinDCX and Delta Exchange investigation is still ongoing, and its outcome will shape how tokenised foreign stocks are treated for Indian users. Watch also for any response from SEBI or the RBI on tokenised securities, and for whether any Indian platform attempts a compliant version of the product.

The fifth is whether other exchanges follow. If the NYSE's platform works, expect other major exchanges to announce their own tokenization plans — and expect the competition to be about licensing, custody and settlement speed, not about token prices.

## The Bottom Line

On 9 November 2026, Ava Labs announced that the New York Stock Exchange had selected Avalanche technology to provide the core ledger for the clearing firm behind its planned tokenized securities platform — a 24/7 market for tokenized stocks with instant settlement, fractional shares and stablecoin funding, built on a custom Avalanche-based chain and subject to regulatory approval. The selection followed more than a year of evaluation, and it was reported the same day by CoinDesk, Coin360, Bitrue, TheNewsCrypto and others. AVAX rose around 5% in the day after the announcement, but the price move is not the story.

The story is that the world's largest stock exchange is building the regulated version of tokenized stocks, at the same time as Indian authorities are questioning the unregulated version. For Indian readers, the lesson is about the route, not the product: the same tokenized share can be a licensed market innovation or an unauthorised cross-border transaction, depending on who offers it, under which regulator, and through which legal channel. That distinction — not the technology, and not the token price — is what will decide whether tokenized stocks ever reach Indian investors in a form they can safely use.
