Most beginners start crypto trading the same way: they see a coin moving, feel excited, buy it, and then figure out what to do next while watching the price. That order — act first, plan later — is why so many beginners lose money. A **crypto trading plan** flips that order: you write down your rules before you risk a single rupee, and then you follow them like a checklist.

A trading plan is not a magic strategy and it is not a prediction tool. It is simply a personal document that answers, in advance, the hard questions: How much will I risk? When will I buy? When will I sell? What will I do when a trade goes wrong? Professional traders do not have better predictions than you — they have better rules, and they follow them.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — you can lose some or all of your money. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose, and never invest based on anything you read in a single article.

## Why You Need a Written Trading Plan

The market is designed to test your emotions. Prices can swing 10% in an hour, social media screams about the "next 100x coin," and your own brain plays tricks: greed after a win, panic after a loss, and the powerful urge to "win it back" immediately.

A plan does three things for you:

1. **It decides while you are calm.** Rules written on a quiet Sunday morning are smarter than decisions made during a red candle at midnight.
2. **It limits the damage.** Pre-set risk rules mean one bad trade cannot wipe out your account.
3. **It gives you something to improve.** Without a plan, every loss feels random. With a plan and a journal, you can see which rules work and which need adjusting.

Think of it like a recipe. A good cook *can* improvise, but a beginner who improvises usually burns dinner. The plan is your recipe until experience teaches you when to adjust it.

## Step 1: Define Your Goals and Your Role

Before anything else, answer honestly: why are you trading, and what kind of trader do you want to be?

- **What is your goal?** Learning the skill? Earning a side income? (Be honest — "getting rich fast" is a goal too, but it is a dangerous one.)
- **What is your timeframe?** Scalping (minutes), day trading (hours), swing trading (days to weeks), or position trading (months)?
- **How much time can you give?** A student or office worker in India cannot realistically day-trade the markets between meetings. Swing trading with a weekly review suits most busy beginners far better.

Write one or two sentences. For example: *"I am learning swing trading on 2–3 hours per week, aiming to build a repeatable process — not to get rich this month."*

## Step 2: Decide Your Capital and Risk Rules

This is the most important part of your entire plan. Everything else is secondary.

**Trading capital** is money that is completely separate from your savings, rent, and emergency fund. On Indian exchanges like CoinDCX, WazirX, or ZebPay, you might start with a few thousand rupees — that is fine. The amount matters far less than the rules protecting it.

Set two hard rules:

### Rule A: Risk per trade — 1–2% of capital

This means if your trading capital is ₹50,000, you risk ₹500–₹1,000 on any single trade. Risk here means the amount you lose *if your stop-loss is hit* — not the total position size. Because of this rule, you can lose ten trades in a row and still have roughly 80% of your capital left. Without this rule, two careless trades can end your journey.

### Rule B: Maximum risk per month

Decide a total monthly loss limit — say 10% of your capital. If you hit it, you stop trading for the rest of the month and review. This single rule has saved more beginners than any indicator ever will.

| Trading capital | Risk per trade (1–2%) | Monthly stop (10%) |
|---|---|---|
| ₹10,000 | ₹100–₹200 | ₹1,000 |
| ₹50,000 | ₹500–₹1,000 | ₹5,000 |
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,000–₹2,000 | ₹10,000 |

These percentages are common beginner guidelines from trading educators — not rules of nature, and not financial advice. Your personal situation matters more than any table. And remember: many beginners benefit most from paper trading (practicing with fake money) before risking real rupees at all.

## Step 3: Choose Your Markets

Beginners spread themselves too thin — watching 30 coins, chasing every trend. Your plan should narrow the field:

- **Start with 1–3 coins.** Most beginners do well starting with Bitcoin and Ethereum only, since they have the deepest liquidity and the most information available. Add more only after months of consistent process.
- **Trade only what you understand.** If you cannot explain what a coin does in two sentences, do not trade it.
- **Avoid leverage until much later.** Futures and margin trading multiply losses as fast as gains. Spot trading on an Indian exchange is enough while you learn.

Tax note: crypto profits in India are taxed at 30% plus applicable surcharge, with 1% TDS on transfers. Tax rules can change — the figures described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. Your plan should include tracking every trade (dates, amounts, fees) so taxes do not become a nightmare later.

## Step 4: Write Your Entry Rules

Entry rules answer one question: *exactly when do I buy?* The more specific, the better. "It looks like it's going up" is not an entry rule. Rules like these are:

- Buy only when the daily trend is up (price above its 50-day moving average) AND a pullback finds support at a previous level.
- Buy only after a breakout closes above resistance on above-average volume.
- Never buy a coin that is already up more than 15% in a single day (no chasing green candles).

You do not need the "best" rules — nobody knows those. You need rules that are clear enough that you can always tell whether a trade qualified. Simple, checkable rules beat clever, vague ones.

Also decide: **maximum one new trade per day** (or per week). A limit on trade frequency is one of the most effective beginner rules there is, because overtrading is how beginners turn small losses into big ones.

## Step 5: Write Your Exit Rules (Before You Enter)

Here is a strange truth: your exits matter more than your entries. Every trade needs three pre-decided numbers:

1. **Stop-loss** — the price where you admit the trade was wrong and exit with a small, planned loss. A stop-loss you move is not a stop-loss; it is decoration. Set it when you enter and honor it.
2. **Profit target** — the price where you take your planned profit. Locking in a planned gain beats watching a winner turn into a loser because "it might go higher."
3. **Position size** — how much you buy, calculated from your risk rule. If you are risking ₹1,000 and your stop-loss is 5% below your entry, your position is ₹20,000 (because 5% of ₹20,000 = ₹1,000). Risk first, position second — always.

Decide the exit rules **before** entering. "I'll see how it goes" is how small wins turn into losses and small losses turn into disasters.

## Step 6: Keep a Trading Journal

A plan without a journal is a wish. After every trade, record five things: the date, the coin, why you entered (which rule?), where your stop and target were, and the result. Then add one honest line: *did I follow my plan?*

Review the journal every month. Patterns will jump out: "My planned trades do fine, but I lose on impulsive evening trades." "I keep widening stop-losses on Fridays." Nobody else can give you this feedback — it comes only from your own data. A simple notebook, a spreadsheet, or a notes app all work; the tool does not matter, the habit does.

## A Sample One-Page Trading Plan Template

Here is what a finished beginner plan might look like. Copy the structure, fill in your own numbers:

- **Goal:** Learn swing trading, 3 hours/week. Process over profit.
- **Capital:** ₹30,000 (separate from savings). Risk per trade: 1.5% (₹450). Monthly stop: ₹3,000 — then pause and review.
- **Markets:** BTC and ETH only, spot trading on my Indian exchange. No leverage, no new altcoins for now.
- **Entries:** Buy only on daily-trend-up + pullback to support; max one new trade per day; never chase +15% single-day pumps.
- **Exits:** Stop-loss 4% below entry; profit target at next resistance; position size calculated from ₹450 risk.
- **Journal:** Log every trade the same day; review monthly on the last Sunday.
- **Rule of rules:** If a trade idea does not match this plan, skip it — no matter how tempting.

One page. Nothing fancy. That is the point.

## Common Beginner Mistakes Your Plan Should Prevent

| Mistake | What the plan does about it |
|---|---|
| Revenge trading after a loss | Monthly stop rule + cool-down rule: no new trades for 24 hours after a stop-loss |
| Buying the top on hype | "Never chase +15% single-day pumps" entry rule |
| Turning small losses into big ones | Fixed stop-loss, never moved |
| Overtrading out of boredom | Maximum one new trade per day |
| Trading coins you do not understand | Markets list: BTC and ETH only |
| Quitting after a bad month | Monthly review rule — losses become data, not drama |

## Conclusion: Your Next Three Steps

A trading plan does not need to be perfect — it needs to exist, and it needs to be followed. Here is how to start today:

1. **Draft your one-page plan** using the template above, with your own numbers and your own schedule.
2. **Practice on paper first.** Use a demo or paper-trading account for at least a few weeks and take 10–15 trades exactly by your rules. If you cannot follow the plan with fake money, you will not follow it with real money.
3. **Start small and review monthly.** Begin with an amount you could lose without changing your life. Let your journal teach you what to adjust — and adjust the plan on a quiet day, never mid-trade.

The market will still be there tomorrow. Your job is to make sure your capital — and your discipline — survive long enough to learn it. A plan is how you give yourself that chance.
