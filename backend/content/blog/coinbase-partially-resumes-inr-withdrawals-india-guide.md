After more than a week of frozen rupee withdrawals, Coinbase India has finally started moving money again — but only part of the way. On 17 November 2026, the exchange said it had begun processing the INR withdrawals that failed since 9 November, in batches, through an alternative banking route. The first batch has already been processed. The in-app withdrawal button, however, is still switched off, and there is still no date for a full return to normal. For Indian users who have been waiting nine days to get their rupees back, this guide explains what Coinbase INR withdrawals actually look like right now, what has changed, what has not, and what to do next.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a service disruption and how INR withdrawals work; it does not recommend using, leaving or trading on any platform.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 17–18 November 2026 by Inc42, Outlook Money and Lookonchain, and as stated by Coinbase India itself, is:

- **Coinbase began clearing the backlog on 17 November.** In an update posted on 17 November 2026, Coinbase said it was processing rupee withdrawals that had failed since 9 November, in batches, through an alternative banking route. The first batch has already been processed, with the affected users expected to receive the money in their bank accounts within a couple of hours of the update. The remaining withdrawals will follow in subsequent batches.
- **The in-app withdrawal option is still unavailable.** Coinbase was explicit that processing the failed withdrawals does not mean the regular facility has been restored. The INR withdrawal option in its app will remain unavailable while its banking partner continues working to resolve the payout outage.
- **There is a manual route in the meantime.** For users who want to withdraw rupees now, Coinbase has advised them to contact Coinbase Support and specify the amount they wish to withdraw. The company said it would transfer the money to their linked bank accounts within 48 to 72 hours.
- **You do not need to resubmit a failed withdrawal.** According to the reports, affected users do not need to file their requests again — Coinbase is working through the failed requests from 9 November onwards on its own.
- **The outage is at the banking partner, not at Coinbase.** The exchange's position throughout has been that an outage at one of its banking partners disrupted payouts for all of that bank's clients, not just Coinbase. Its 14 November statement said: "Your funds are safe. INR deposits (including UPI) and bank account linking are working as usual."

## How We Got Here: Nine Days of Frozen Withdrawals

To understand why a partial resumption matters, it helps to retrace the week. Coinbase first logged the problem on its status page on 9 November 2026, under the title "Bank Payments Unavailable — INR (India)". INR withdrawals stopped going through, and the exchange asked customers not to start new withdrawal requests, warning that requests made during the disruption could fail or remain pending. Deposits, UPI payments and bank account linking kept working throughout.

For five days, the exchange said little in public. Then, on 14 November, Coinbase India posted on X that INR withdrawals were temporarily unavailable because of an outage at its banking partner, repeating that funds were safe and that it was working with the bank around the clock. By then, users had been reporting on social media for more than five days that they could not get their money out, and still there was no date for when withdrawals would return.

The 17 November update is therefore the first real movement in the story — not a full fix, but the first batch of stuck money actually moving. It came a week and a day after the problem first appeared, and eight days after users first began reporting failed withdrawals.

## What "Partially Resumed" Actually Means

The phrase "partially resumes" can be misleading, so it is worth being precise about what has and has not changed:

| Service | Status as of 18 November 2026 |
|---|---|
| Failed INR withdrawals (since 9 Nov) | Being processed in batches via an alternative banking route; first batch done |
| New INR withdrawals via the app | Still unavailable — the option is switched off |
| New INR withdrawals via support | Possible — contact Coinbase Support with the amount; transfer within 48–72 hours |
| INR deposits (including UPI) | Working as usual |
| Bank account linking | Working as usual |
| Crypto trading | Not part of the outage |
| Full restoration date | Not announced |

The distinction matters. If your withdrawal failed any time since 9 November, it is now in a queue that Coinbase is clearing itself — you do not need to chase it. If you want to withdraw rupees that were never part of a failed request, you cannot simply tap a button in the app; you have to go through support and wait up to three days. And if you are hoping for the normal one-tap withdrawal experience to return, there is still no timeline for that.

The alternative banking route is also worth understanding. Coinbase has not named the banking partner involved, and it has not said which alternative channel it is using to clear the backlog. What is clear is that the original bank's payouts are still broken — which is why the app option stays off — while a different route is being used to push the stuck withdrawals through. That is a workaround, not a repair, and it is the reason the resumption is only partial.

## Why This Matters for Indian Users

This is not a small, isolated glitch. Coinbase is one of the largest crypto exchanges in the world, and it only returned to India in June 2026, after registering with the Financial Intelligence Unit and enabling INR deposits and withdrawals through the Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) following a two-year pause. For many Indian users, it is a relatively new platform — and within five months of its return, its rupee exit route has been broken for more than a week.

It is also not the only platform affected. The same week brought a cluster of banking problems across Indian crypto:

- **Unocoin** suspended all deposits and withdrawals on 9 November 2026, the same day the Coinbase outage began. Unlike Coinbase, Unocoin's suspension followed a security breach, and on 14 November the exchange admitted that about $200,000 was stolen from three of its hot wallets. It had been targeting Tuesday, 17 November 2026 for resuming crypto withdrawals, while its INR withdrawals were running slower than usual because of a disruption at its own banking partner.
- **ZebPay** told customers on 11 November that its INR withdrawals were delayed because of a technical problem at its own banking partner, and paused crypto withdrawals while it carried out infrastructure upgrades, targeting 17 November for resumption, with BNB Smart Chain and Tron withdrawals possibly taking an extra day. Unlike Coinbase and Unocoin, ZebPay's INR withdrawals kept working, just slowly. As of 18 November, no confirmed resumption announcement for ZebPay's crypto withdrawals had been found.

There is currently no confirmed evidence that the three exchanges share the same banking institution, or that a common cyberattack is responsible. But the pattern is the same one Indian crypto users keep running into: the crypto side of a platform can be working perfectly while the traditional banking rail that carries rupees in and out fails without warning. When that rail breaks, your balance on the exchange is intact — but your money is not in your bank account, and no amount of trading fixes that.

## What Indian Users Should Do Now

If you are affected, the practical steps depend on which situation you are in:

**1. If your INR withdrawal failed between 9 and 17 November, wait — but verify.** Coinbase says it is processing failed withdrawals in batches and that you do not need to resubmit. Check your bank account over the next day or two. Keep a screenshot of the failed withdrawal, the date, and the amount. If the money does not arrive and you have had no update, contact support with those details rather than starting a fresh withdrawal request, which could fail the same way.

**2. If you need to withdraw rupees now, use the support route.** Contact Coinbase Support, state the amount you want to withdraw, and expect the transfer to your linked bank account within 48 to 72 hours. Do not try the in-app withdrawal option while it is unavailable — Coinbase has warned that requests started during the outage may fail or remain pending.

**3. Do not deposit more INR to "trade through" the outage.** Deposits, including UPI, are working — but money you deposit now can be traded, not withdrawn through the app. If you might need those rupees in your bank account soon, keep them there until the withdrawal facility is fully restored.

**4. Watch the status page, not social media rumours.** The reliable sources for this story are Coinbase's own status page and its official India account. Screenshots and claims circulating on social media during an outage are often outdated or fake, and scammers use outages to impersonate support staff. Coinbase will never ask for your password, your recovery phrase, or a payment to "release" your withdrawal. If anyone does, report it on the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or call the helpline 1930.

**5. Keep records for tax purposes.** A failed or delayed withdrawal does not change your tax position by itself — tax applies to your crypto trades, not to moving rupees to your bank. But keep records of every transaction: the date, the amount, and the exchange used. If you sell crypto during this period, the usual Indian rules apply: gains are taxed at a flat rate of 30%, and 1% TDS may apply on the sale.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## The Bigger Picture: Banking Rails Are Crypto's Weakest Link in India

The Coinbase outage is a useful reminder of something this site has covered repeatedly: for Indian users, the most fragile part of crypto is rarely the blockchain. It is the bridge between crypto and the banking system. A bank's payout outage, a partner's technical failure, or a compliance freeze can leave your funds visible on screen but unreachable in your bank account for days — and there is nothing you can do on the exchange to speed it up.

That is why the partial resumption matters even though it is incomplete. It shows the exchange is finding workarounds rather than waiting for the original bank to recover, and it means the oldest stuck withdrawals are finally moving. But it is also a warning: until the in-app withdrawal option returns and a full restoration date is announced, the rupee exit route on one of the world's biggest exchanges remains a manual, multi-day process.

For now, the honest summary is this: the backlog is clearing, the app is not fixed, and nobody outside the bank can say when it will be. If you are waiting on a failed withdrawal, check your bank account before doing anything else. If you are thinking of depositing fresh rupees, wait until the normal route is back. And if you use more than one exchange, this is a good week to check that your withdrawal routes on each of them actually work — before you need them.
