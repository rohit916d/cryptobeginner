# 2026 Crypto Beginner Checklist: 15 Things Before Investing

Before you put a single rupee into crypto, run through this crypto checklist. Each of the 15 items below is something experienced educators and the collective wisdom of India's crypto community recommend doing *before* — not after — your first investment. Work through them in order, and you'll avoid the mistakes that cost beginners the most money: scams, panic selling, lost passwords, and surprise tax bills.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## Part 1: Get the Basics Right First

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Buying

Before investing in Bitcoin, Ethereum, or any token, you should be able to explain in your own words what it is and why people value it. Read our beginner explainers on Bitcoin, Ethereum, and stablecoins on this site. If a coin's purpose, technology, and risks don't make sense to you after a plain-English read, you are not ready to buy it — and that is completely fine. Investing in something you don't understand is gambling, not learning.

### 2. Learn How Exchanges, Wallets, and Private Keys Work

Crypto investing requires understanding three moving parts: exchanges (where you buy), wallets (where you hold), and private keys or seed phrases (what proves ownership). Spend an evening reading about hot vs cold wallets and what a seed phrase does. The single most common beginner disaster is losing a seed phrase and being locked out of funds forever — that is a one-minute lesson that prevents a lifetime regret.

### 3. Read Up on India's 30% Tax Rule and 1% TDS Before Your First Trade

India taxes gains from virtual digital assets at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess), and 1% TDS is deducted on qualifying transfers. You don't need to become a tax expert — but you should know these basics *before* you trade, because every buy, sell, swap, and airdrop can be a taxable event, and cleaning up a year of missing records is painful. Set up a simple habit now: a spreadsheet or notebook logging every transaction's date, amount, and INR value.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## Part 2: Protect Your Money and Your Identity

### 4. Secure Your Email With a Strong, Unique Password and 2FA

Your email is the master key to everything — exchange accounts, password resets, wallet recoveries. Use a long, unique password for it and enable two-factor authentication (2FA). An attacker who controls your email can reset nearly every other account you own, so this one step does more for your crypto safety than almost anything else.

### 5. Enable 2FA on Every Exchange Account (App-Based, Not SMS)

When you create accounts on Indian exchanges like CoinDCX, WazirX, or ZebPay, turn on app-based 2FA (Google Authenticator, Authy, or similar) from day one — not SMS-based codes, which are easier to intercept. Also back up your 2FA recovery codes somewhere safe and offline. Losing your phone without a backup can lock you out of your own exchange account for days.

### 6. Learn to Spot the Five Most Common Scams Targeting Indians

Before investing, familiarize yourself with the scam patterns that target beginners in India: fake exchange support calls, "double your crypto" Telegram schemes, phishing links promising free airdrops, Ponzi-style high-return platforms, and fake celebrity-endorsement ads. Our scam-awareness guides cover the red flags in detail. If you can't yet recognize these, a scammer can spot *you* from a mile away — learn first.

### 7. Never Share Your Seed Phrase, Private Keys, or OTPs — With Anyone

No legitimate exchange, wallet company, or support team will ever ask for your seed phrase, private key, or OTP. Real customer support will never call or DM you asking for these. Any person or website asking for them is a thief, full stop. Memorize this rule before you hold anything of value: the moment you share a seed phrase, your funds are gone.

### 8. Decide Where You'll Store Your Crypto (Not Just Where You'll Buy It)

Many beginners plan their first purchase in detail and give zero thought to storage. Before investing, decide: will you keep a small amount on the exchange for learning, and move larger amounts to a personal wallet? Order a hardware wallet in advance if you plan to hold meaningful sums, and practice the full backup-and-recovery process with a test wallet first. Storage is a decision, not an afterthought.

## Part 3: Make a Plan for the Money Itself

### 9. Only Invest Surplus Money You Can Afford to Lose

This is the most repeated rule in crypto education for a reason. Crypto can drop 30–50% in weeks, and money you need for rent, EMIs, medical emergencies, or fees should never be in it. A widely taught guideline: build your emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses) and pay down high-interest debt *before* touching crypto. If an investment would cause you real distress if it fell to zero, it is too large.

### 10. Write Down Your Plan: Why, How Much, and When You'll Exit

Before your first buy, write a simple one-page plan: why are you investing (learning? long-term exposure?), how much in total, how you'll buy (one-time or small monthly amounts?), and under what conditions you'll sell. Beginners who skip this step tend to panic-sell during crashes and FOMO-buy during rallies. A written plan — even a basic one — turns emotional reactions into decisions you made while calm.

### 11. Understand Volatility and the "Sleep Test"

Crypto swings far harder than stocks or mutual funds — double-digit moves in a single day are normal. Before investing, honestly ask yourself: if your investment dropped 50% tomorrow and stayed down for a year, would you sleep fine? If not, reduce the amount until the answer is yes. The "sleep test" is one of the simplest position-sizing tools there is, and it applies to every market, not just crypto.

### 12. Learn What a Crypto SIP (Rupee-Cost Averaging) Is

Many beginners try to "time the market" — buy low, sell high — and discover how hard that is. Rupee-cost averaging (buying a fixed INR amount regularly, regardless of price) is a calmer alternative that removes timing pressure. Read our crypto SIP guide to understand the mechanics before you start. Whether or not you use it, knowing the option exists stops you from making one giant, anxious, perfectly-timed-or-not purchase.

## Part 4: Pick Your Platform and Do Your Homework

### 13. Choose a Reputable, FIU-Registered Indian Exchange

Buy from established Indian exchanges that are registered with FIU-IND (India's Financial Intelligence Unit) and support INR deposits via UPI or bank transfer — names like CoinDCX, WazirX, and ZebPay are commonly discussed by Indian educators. Check the fee schedule (maker/taker fees, spreads, withdrawal fees), test customer support with a small question, and make a tiny trial deposit before committing real money. A quick platform test run reveals friction you'd rather find with ₹500 than ₹50,000.

### 14. Complete KYC and Test a Small Deposit and Withdrawal First

Exchanges require KYC (PAN, Aadhaar-based verification, sometimes video verification) before you can deposit INR. Complete it well before you plan to buy — verification queues can take time. Then do a full dry run: deposit a small amount, make a tiny purchase, and withdraw a small sum back. This confirms your bank account is linked correctly, UPI deposits work, and you know the withdrawal process — before any significant amount is in play.

### 15. Do Your Own Research (DYOR) on Every Coin — Then Re-Check

For every coin you consider, run a basic research routine: read the project's whitepaper summary, check who the team is, look at its real-world usage and community activity, and search for red flags (anonymous teams, guaranteed-return promises, copied code). Our DYOR guide walks through the full method. And revisit your research periodically — crypto projects change, regulations change, and an investment thesis from January may not hold in October.

## How to Use This Checklist

You don't have to complete all 15 in one day. A realistic pace: items 1–3 (knowledge) and 4–8 (security) in week one, items 9–12 (money plan) in week two, and items 13–15 (platform + research) when you're ready to make your first small purchase. Print the list or save it — tick each box as you go.

Once you've worked through the checklist, your next steps are straightforward: start with a tiny amount to learn the mechanics, keep learning with our guides on wallets, fees, and tax filing, and revisit this checklist every few months — the rules of good practice don't change, but your situation and the market will.

Crypto rewards the prepared and punishes the hasty. Doing these 15 things before investing won't guarantee returns — nothing can — but it will guarantee you're starting the way every sensible educator recommends: informed, secured, and in control of your own risk.
