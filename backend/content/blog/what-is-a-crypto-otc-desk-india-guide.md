A crypto OTC desk is one of those terms that sounds like it belongs only to institutions — and mostly, it does. But if you have ever wondered how a fund buys crores of rupees worth of Bitcoin without crashing the price, or why some large trades never appear on an exchange's order book, the answer is usually an OTC desk. This guide explains what a crypto OTC desk is, how a trade works, how it differs from buying on an exchange, and what Indian beginners should know before ever dealing with one.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This article explains how OTC desks work; it is not a recommendation to use one.

## What Is a Crypto OTC Desk?

OTC stands for "over-the-counter". In traditional finance, an OTC trade is any deal done directly between two parties rather than on a public exchange. A crypto OTC desk applies the same idea to digital assets: it is a business that matches buyers and sellers of large amounts of crypto, or trades with them directly from its own inventory, at a price agreed in private.

Think of it like this. A stock exchange is a busy public market where everyone can see the prices and the queue of buyers and sellers. An OTC desk is more like a private dealer: you tell the dealer what you want to buy or sell and how much, the dealer gives you a firm price, and the deal happens between the two of you — away from the public market.

The key word is **large**. OTC desks exist because big orders do not fit comfortably on public order books. If someone tries to sell a very large amount of Bitcoin on an exchange, the order eats through the available buy orders one by one, and each step down the book is at a lower price. The seller ends up receiving much less than the headline price. This effect is called **slippage**, and it grows with order size. An OTC desk solves it by finding a single counterparty — or several — willing to take the whole amount at one agreed price.

Most OTC desks set a minimum trade size. The exact minimum varies by desk, but it is typically high — often the equivalent of lakhs or crores of rupees per trade. That is why OTC desks are mainly used by:

- Crypto funds and investment firms
- Companies holding crypto on their balance sheet
- Miners selling the coins they earn
- Wealthy individuals, often called whales
- Exchanges and payment companies managing their own inventory

For an ordinary beginner buying a few thousand rupees of crypto, an OTC desk is simply not the right tool. A regular Indian exchange does the job better, faster and cheaper.

## How an OTC Desk Trade Works

Although every desk has its own process, a typical OTC trade follows the same five steps:

### 1. The request for quote

The buyer or seller contacts the desk — usually by email, chat or a dedicated trading platform — and states what they want to trade. For example: "I want to sell 10 BTC for INR" or "I want to buy USDT worth ₹2 crore." This is called a **request for quote (RFQ)**.

### 2. The desk gives a price

The desk checks its own inventory, its network of counterparties and the current market price, then returns a firm quote: a price that is valid for a short window, often a few seconds to a few minutes. The quote includes the desk's margin — the small difference between the market price and the price offered to the client is how the desk earns money.

### 3. The client accepts (or walks away)

If the client likes the price, they accept within the quote window. If the window expires, the price is gone and a new quote must be requested. There is no obligation to accept — a quote is an offer, not a commitment.

### 4. Settlement

Once accepted, both sides deliver what they promised. The seller sends the crypto to the desk's wallet (or to an escrow arrangement), and the buyer sends the payment — usually by bank transfer. Settlement can take minutes to a few hours depending on the payment method and the blockchain confirmations required. Some desks settle the same day; others, especially for very large amounts, settle over one or two business days.

### 5. Confirmation and records

Both sides receive a trade confirmation with the amount, price, fees and settlement details. For Indian users, this confirmation is important: it is part of the record you need for tax filing, since every crypto transfer must be reported in your income tax return.

One important detail: the price on an OTC desk is usually quoted as a single all-in figure, but it is rarely the same as the price you see on an exchange at that moment. The desk's quote reflects the size of the trade, the liquidity available, and the desk's own margin. Comparing the quote with the live exchange price is a basic sanity check any trader should do before accepting.

## OTC Desk vs Exchange: What Is the Difference?

The table below summarises the main differences for a beginner trying to understand the two routes.

| Feature | Crypto exchange | OTC desk |
|---|---|---|
| How price is set | Public order book — best buy and sell offers | Private negotiation — one firm quote |
| Who it suits | Everyone, including small buyers | Large traders, funds, businesses |
| Minimum trade size | Very low — often ₹100 or less | High — typically lakhs of rupees or more |
| Price visibility | Fully public, live | Private between the two parties |
| Slippage on big orders | High — large orders move the price | Low — price is locked before the trade |
| Speed | Instant for market orders | Minutes to hours, including settlement |
| Payment methods | UPI, bank transfer, card on some platforms | Bank transfer, sometimes stablecoins |
| Anonymity | Trades are public on the order book | Trade size and price stay private |

Neither route is "better" in general — they serve different needs. An exchange is a public marketplace built for everyone. An OTC desk is a private service built for size. A beginner buying ₹5,000 of Bitcoin on an Indian exchange gets a better deal and a simpler experience than they ever would from an OTC desk.

## Why Do Large Traders Use OTC Desks?

There are four main reasons a large trader chooses an OTC desk over an exchange:

**1. Less slippage.** This is the biggest reason. A ₹10 crore sell order on a public exchange would chew through the order book and the average price received would be far below the headline price. An OTC desk agrees one price for the whole amount in advance.

**2. Privacy.** A large order sitting on a public order book is visible to everyone, including other traders who might trade against it. An OTC trade stays private, so the market does not react to it before it happens.

**3. Flexible settlement.** OTC desks can arrange settlement in ways an exchange cannot — for example, splitting payment across several bank transfers, settling in stablecoins, or timing the trade for a specific window. This matters for businesses that need to manage cash flow.

**4. Access to liquidity.** Some assets, or very large amounts of them, simply do not have enough buyers and sellers on public exchanges. A desk with a wide network can source liquidity that is not visible on any order book.

These advantages come at a cost: the desk's margin, the minimum trade size, and the need to trust the counterparty. That last point — trust — is where most of the risk lives.

## OTC Desks in India: What Beginners Should Know

India does not have a dedicated law for crypto, and there is no specific licensing framework for OTC desks aimed at retail users. That creates a grey area beginners should understand clearly.

**There is no well-known, retail-facing OTC desk operating openly in India the way Indian exchanges do.** The OTC desks that serve Indian clients are generally international firms dealing with institutions, or desks run by large Indian exchanges as a service for their biggest clients. If you are a beginner, you will probably never be offered a genuine OTC service — and that is normal.

What you *will* encounter, especially on Telegram and WhatsApp, are groups and individuals calling themselves "OTC desks" or "OTC dealers". Many of these are unregulated intermediaries, and some are outright scams. Common patterns include:

- **"Best rate" OTC groups** that promise to buy your crypto at above-market prices and pay by UPI — then disappear after you send the coins.
- **Fake escrow services** where the "escrow" is just another account controlled by the same scammer.
- **Advance-fee traps** where you are asked to pay a "verification fee", "tax" or "release charge" before the trade settles — a legitimate desk never asks for this.
- **Stolen-funds laundering** where the person on the other side is moving money from hacks or fraud, and your wallet or bank account ends up linked to a criminal investigation.

The rule is simple: if someone offers you an OTC deal on Telegram or WhatsApp, treat it as a scam until proven otherwise. A genuine OTC desk is a registered business with a website, a compliance team, written contracts and a reputation you can verify. It does not cold-message strangers offering above-market rates.

### The P2P confusion

Some beginners confuse OTC desks with P2P (peer-to-peer) trading on exchanges. They are different. P2P trading happens inside an exchange's platform, with the exchange holding the crypto in escrow until payment is confirmed. An OTC desk trade happens outside any exchange, with no platform escrow and no automatic protection. That difference matters: on a P2P platform you have at least some recourse if the other side fails to pay; in a private OTC deal, your only protection is the contract and the counterparty's honesty.

### Tax: large trades still mean tax

Whether you trade on an exchange, through an OTC desk or privately, the tax treatment in India is the same. Gains from transferring crypto are taxed at a flat 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess, 1% TDS applies on transfers above the threshold, and losses cannot be set off against gains or carried forward. OTC trades must be reported in Schedule VDA of your income tax return, with the acquisition and transfer dates, cost and sale consideration for each transfer.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## Risks of Using an OTC Desk

Even a genuine OTC desk carries risks that do not exist on a regulated exchange:

- **Counterparty risk.** If the desk or the other party fails to deliver after you have sent your crypto or your money, recovering it can be slow and expensive — and sometimes impossible.
- **Price risk during settlement.** If settlement takes hours, the market price can move between the quote and the settlement. A good desk locks the price at acceptance, but the terms vary, so read them before agreeing.
- **Regulatory risk.** Because crypto rules in India are still evolving, a large private trade can attract attention from banks and tax authorities. Keep full records: trade confirmations, bank statements, wallet addresses and dates.
- **Scam risk.** As described above, fake OTC desks are one of the most common large-value crypto scams targeting Indians. The promise of a better rate is the bait.
- **No investor protection.** Unlike bank deposits, crypto held with an OTC desk or sent to a private counterparty has no insurance or government-backed protection. If the desk collapses or disappears, there is no DICGC-style safety net.

## 5-Minute OTC Safety Checklist

If you are ever offered an OTC deal — or are simply curious — run through this checklist before sending anything:

1. **Verify the business.** Is it a registered company with a real website, a physical address and named people? Can you find independent reviews or news coverage? If the only presence is a Telegram handle, stop.
2. **Check the minimum.** A genuine desk has a high minimum trade size. If someone offers "OTC" for ₹10,000, it is not an OTC desk — it is a stranger asking for your crypto.
3. **Never pay an advance fee.** No legitimate desk asks for a "release fee", "tax" or "verification charge" before settlement.
4. **Use a written agreement.** A real OTC trade comes with a trade confirmation or contract stating the amount, price, fees and settlement timeline. A chat message is not a contract.
5. **Start with records, not trust.** Before any large trade, confirm how settlement works, who holds what during the trade, and what happens if one side fails to deliver. If the answers are vague, walk away.
6. **Keep every record.** Save the quote, the confirmation, bank statements and wallet transaction details. You will need them for Schedule VDA at tax time.

## The Bottom Line

A crypto OTC desk is a private, negotiated way to trade large amounts of crypto without moving the public market. It exists for institutions, funds, miners and very large holders — people for whom slippage on an exchange would cost far more than the desk's margin. For most Indian beginners, it is a concept worth understanding and a service worth avoiding: the combination of high minimums, counterparty risk and the flood of fake "OTC" groups on Telegram makes it a poor fit for small investors.

If you are starting out, a FIU-IND registered Indian exchange with UPI deposits, a clear fee structure and proper records is the safer route. Learn how OTC desks work, recognise the scams that borrow their name, and keep your crypto journey on platforms where you can verify who you are dealing with.
