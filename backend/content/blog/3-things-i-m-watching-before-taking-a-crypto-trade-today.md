Most beginners treat crypto trading like a slot machine: open the app, stare at charts, and jump into whatever is moving. Then they wonder why their balance keeps shrinking even when they "called the direction right" a few times.

Here is the uncomfortable truth experienced traders learn: trading profitably is mostly about what you do *before* you click buy or sell. The trade itself takes seconds. The preparation is the actual skill. This guide walks through the three things worth checking before taking any crypto trade — a simple pre-trade routine that protects beginners from their own worst impulses.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## 1. Where Bitcoin Stands Right Now

Before looking at any individual coin, look at Bitcoin. This sounds strange if you want to trade something else, but Bitcoin still drives the entire crypto market. When Bitcoin drops sharply, almost every other coin drops with it. When Bitcoin trends steadily upward, altcoins get room to move.

So the first question is simple: **what is Bitcoin doing today?**

- Is Bitcoin trending up, down, or moving sideways? A coin that looks "ready to pump" while Bitcoin is crashing is usually a trap, not an opportunity.
- Are we near an obvious support or resistance level — a price where Bitcoin has bounced or been rejected multiple times before? Trading altcoins right as Bitcoin hits major resistance is like swimming against the current.
- Check Bitcoin dominance (BTC's share of total crypto market value). When dominance is rising, money is flowing into Bitcoin and away from altcoins — a headwind for altcoin trades.

You do not need fancy tools for this. Open any free chart, look at the daily timeframe, and ask: does the overall market look healthy or sick today? If the answer is "sick," the professional move is often to do nothing.

## 2. Is There Actually a Setup — or Am I Just Bored?

This is the question that saves beginners the most money. A real trading setup is a specific, pre-defined situation you have decided in advance is worth risking money on. Examples:

- Price bouncing off a support level that has held three times before, with volume picking up.
- A clean breakout above resistance on strong volume after days of sideways movement.
- A retest: price breaks out, pulls back to the breakout level, and holds.

Notice what is *not* on that list: "the price is going up fast and I feel excited," "someone on social media said it will pump," or "I've been watching charts for an hour and haven't traded yet."

Beginners confuse **activity** with **progress**. Professionals can go days without trading and feel fine about it. Amateurs feel that every hour without a position is wasted. The market does not owe you a trade today. Some of the best trading days end with zero trades taken.

A practical test: before entering, write down in one sentence *why* this trade makes sense and *where you are wrong* (the price level that proves your idea failed). If you cannot answer both clearly, you do not have a setup — you have an urge.

## 3. What Is My Risk Plan Before I Enter?

This is the part beginners skip entirely, and it is the part that matters most. Before entering any trade, you should already know three numbers:

1. **Entry price** — where you buy.
2. **Stop-loss price** — where you exit if wrong. This is decided *before* entry, not improvised after the price falls.
3. **Position size** — how much you are risking. The standard beginner guideline: risk 1 to 2 percent of your capital per trade.

Here is why this matters with real numbers. Suppose you have ₹50,000 and you risk 2% (₹1,000) per trade. You could lose ten trades in a row and still have ₹40,000 left — bruised, but alive and able to learn. Now suppose you risk 25% per trade with no stop-loss because you "felt confident." Two bad trades and half your account is gone. Same market, same beginner — completely different outcome, decided entirely by the risk plan.

Also decide your target in advance: where will you take profit? "I'll see how it goes" is not a plan; it is how small wins turn into losses when greed keeps you in too long.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- **Revenge trading.** You lose a trade, feel angry, and immediately jump into another to "win it back." This emotional spiral destroys more accounts than bad strategy ever will. After a loss, step away from the screen.
- **Ignoring fees and taxes.** Every trade on an Indian exchange costs fees, and 1% TDS is deducted on each crypto transaction. Frequent small trades bleed money to costs even before counting losses — and every profitable sale faces a flat 30% tax. Trading less often is not just calmer; it is cheaper.
- **Moving the stop-loss.** You set a stop, price approaches it, and you move it lower "to give it room." That is not patience — that is refusing to accept being wrong. A stop-loss you do not honor is decoration.
- **Trading every day because the market is open 24/7.** Crypto never closes, which means it can consume your sleep, work, and peace of mind. Set trading hours like a job. The market will still be there tomorrow.

*Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.*

## A Quick Pre-Trade Checklist

Before your next trade, run through this in under two minutes:

1. Bitcoin's trend today: up, down, or sideways? (If down hard — consider sitting out.)
2. My setup in one sentence: __________. My invalidation level: __________.
3. Entry: ____. Stop-loss: ____. Risk amount: ____ (max 1–2% of capital).
4. Have I already lost today? (If yes — done for the day.)

If you cannot fill in every blank, do not trade. Waiting is a position.

## The Bottom Line

The best traders are not the ones who find the most trades. They are the ones who say no the most often — no to boredom trades, no to hype trades, no to revenge trades — so that when a genuinely good setup appears, they have capital and calm left to take it. Build the habit of checking Bitcoin's health, demanding a real setup, and fixing your risk plan before entry. Do that consistently for a few months on a demo account first, and you will already be ahead of most beginners.
