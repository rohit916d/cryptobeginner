The Solana Foundation has taken a majority stake in CoinGecko, one of the most widely used crypto price-tracking platforms in the world — the website millions of people in India open every day to check the price of Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana itself. The deal was announced on Tuesday 17 November 2026, on the final day of the Solana Breakpoint conference in London, and it was confirmed the same day by the Foundation's chief product officer, Vibhu Norby, who wrote on X that the Foundation "now has a majority interest" in CoinGecko. CoinGecko published its own announcement the same day, describing its next phase of growth as backed by the Solana Foundation. This guide explains what was actually announced, what was not disclosed, why the ownership of a price tracker matters at all, and what — if anything — it changes for crypto users in India.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a business deal in the crypto industry; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 17–18 November 2026 by CoinDesk, Decrypt, Unchained, KuCoin, FinanceFeeds and CoinGecko's own announcement, is:

- **The Solana Foundation now holds a majority stake in CoinGecko.** The Foundation's chief product officer, Vibhu Norby, confirmed the majority interest on X on 17 November. CoinGecko's own announcement, published the same day, framed the arrangement as its "next phase of growth, backed by Solana Foundation". CoinGecko chief executive Bobby Ong replied to Norby's post saying he was "very excited to join the Solana family".
- **The deal was announced at Breakpoint in London.** The announcement came on the final day of the Solana Foundation's Breakpoint 2026 conference at Olympia London, the Foundation's flagship annual event. The timing was deliberate: the conference is where the Foundation makes its biggest announcements of the year, and this one was the headline of the closing day.
- **CoinGecko is twelve years old and very large.** CoinGecko was founded in 2014 in Malaysia by Bobby Ong and TM Lee. It has about 18 million registered users, and around 180,000 apps and businesses pull its market data through its programming interfaces, according to the Foundation's announcement. For scale, that makes it one of the two price trackers — alongside CoinMarketCap — that almost every crypto user in the world has used at some point.
- **The plan is to add trading to the price tracker.** The Foundation said it plans to bring Solana-powered trading to CoinGecko's website, its GeckoTerminal platform for decentralised-exchange tracking, and its mobile app. CoinGecko said it will focus on three areas: expanding its data coverage and analytics across blockchain ecosystems, including onchain assets, derivatives and real-world assets; building market insights and discovery tools; and exploring trading features that let users act on the research they are reading.
- **No price, no exact stake and no deal structure were disclosed.** Neither the Foundation nor CoinGecko has said how much was paid, what percentage of the company changed hands, how the transaction was structured, or what control rights the Foundation holds. Decrypt reported that the Foundation did not specify the size of the commitment, and a Foundation representative confirmed only that it is taking a majority stake. The terms, in other words, are private.
- **The $500 million figure is not the deal price.** Some coverage of the announcement mentions a figure of about $500 million. That number comes from a separate, earlier process: in January 2026, CoinDesk reported that CoinGecko had hired investment bank Moelis to look into a possible sale, citing people familiar with the matter who put a possible valuation near $500 million. That was a valuation estimate for a sale process that was never completed, and it should not be read as the price of the Solana deal. No outlet has reported the actual price of this transaction.
- **The Foundation is a Swiss non-profit.** The Solana Foundation is the Switzerland-based non-profit organisation that supports the development of the Solana blockchain. It is not a company in the ordinary sense, and it does not run an exchange. Its role has historically been funding development, running conferences and supporting the ecosystem — which is why buying a majority stake in a data platform is a new kind of move for it.
- **CoinGecko says nothing changes for users — for now.** CoinGecko's announcement says its commitment to reliable, transparent market data "remains unchanged", that its methodologies stay publicly accessible, and that its coverage of all chains continues as before. Its co-founders have confirmed that the platform will keep its existing operational structure and its cross-chain coverage principles. What has not been published is any governance safeguard for that neutrality — no independent oversight body, no published rules for listing or ranking decisions, and no statement about board seats.

One more piece of context from the same week: on 16 November, the day before the CoinGecko announcement, the Foundation retired the "mainnet beta" label that Solana had carried since its launch and introduced Solana 1.0. The two announcements together mark a week in which the Foundation has been asserting that Solana is no longer an experiment — and the CoinGecko deal is the commercial expression of that confidence.

The most important row in that table is the first set of "not disclosed" entries. In most acquisitions of this size, the price is announced. Here it was not, and the exact stake was not either — only that it is a majority. That is not necessarily suspicious; private transactions between a non-profit foundation and a private company are often undisclosed. But it does mean that nobody outside the two organisations knows how much control the Foundation actually bought, or what it paid for it, and any article that gives you a number for either is guessing.

## Why CoinGecko Matters to Indian Crypto Users

To understand why this deal matters, it helps to understand what CoinGecko actually is for an ordinary Indian crypto user. It is not an exchange. You cannot buy or sell crypto on it. What it does is collect prices from hundreds of exchanges around the world, combine them into a single reference price for each coin, and show you that price — along with market capitalisation, trading volume, charts, and information about the project behind the coin.

For a beginner in India, that makes it one of the first stops in almost any crypto decision. Before buying Bitcoin on an Indian exchange, a user checks CoinGecko to see the global price. Before trusting a new coin, a user checks its CoinGecko page to see whether it is listed, what its market cap is, and how much it trades. Before believing a claim on social media that a coin has doubled, a user checks the chart. The site is, in effect, the reference book of the crypto market — and its data feeds into thousands of other apps, websites and tools that Indian users rely on without ever seeing the CoinGecko name.

That is why the ownership question is not a technicality. A price tracker is only useful if its numbers are neutral — if the price it shows for Solana is calculated the same way as the price it shows for Ethereum, Avalanche or any other chain, and if the coins it lists, the order it ranks them in, and the information it highlights are decided by the data, not by who owns the company. CoinGecko has built its reputation over twelve years on exactly that neutrality: it lists thousands of coins across every major blockchain, and it publishes its listing and ranking methodologies so that anyone can check them.

Now the foundation behind one of the largest blockchains owns a majority of that reference book. That does not mean the numbers have changed — CoinGecko says they have not, and there is no evidence that they have. But it does mean that the organisation with the strongest interest in Solana's success now controls the platform that tells the world what Solana is worth, alongside everything else. The conflict is structural, not personal: even if every individual involved acts in perfect good faith, the arrangement creates an incentive that did not exist before, and incentives are exactly what careful users should watch.

There is a second reason the deal matters to Indian users specifically. Prices in India already differ from global prices. The Crypto Times India Bitcoin Premium Index showed Indians paying about 10.75% more than the world price for Bitcoin at its 18 November fix, and its Ethereum premium index stood at about 10.88% the same day. Indian users therefore already have to translate between the global price they see on CoinGecko and the price they actually pay on an Indian exchange. If the global reference price itself ever became less reliable, that translation would get harder — and beginners, who are the least equipped to spot the difference, would be the first to lose out.

## The Big Question: Can a Price Tracker Stay Neutral?

The honest answer is that it can, but only if the neutrality is protected by something stronger than a promise. CoinGecko's announcement makes the promise clearly: its commitment to reliable, transparent market data remains unchanged, its methodologies stay public, and its coverage of all chains continues. Its co-founders have repeated the same commitments in their own words. Those are real statements, made in public, by named people — and they are worth taking seriously.

What is missing is the mechanism that would make those promises verifiable. No independent governance safeguard has been published. There is no outside body that audits the data, no published set of rules for how listing or ranking decisions are made, no statement about whether the Foundation will hold board seats, and no explanation of what happens if the Foundation's interests and the data's neutrality ever point in different directions. Several outlets that covered the announcement noted exactly this gap: the formal statements are noticeably more careful than the social-media posts, and the question of governance has not been addressed publicly at all.

That gap is the story to watch, and it is worth understanding what to look for. A price tracker stays neutral through three things: published methodologies that anyone can check, separation between the people who run the data and the people who benefit from particular prices, and a track record of listing and ranking decisions that follow the data even when the data is inconvenient. CoinGecko has the first and, for twelve years, the third. The deal tests the second — and the test will be visible in the platform's behaviour over the coming months, not in its press release.

There are also practical questions that have not been answered. The trading feature CoinGecko plans to explore — Solana-powered trading on its website, GeckoTerminal and app — has no launch date, no fee schedule and no named execution partner. It is described in the announcements as something the company is "exploring", not something it is launching. For Indian users, the more immediate question is whether that feature would even be available in India, and under what rules. CoinGecko is a data platform, not a registered Indian exchange, and there is no indication in any of the announcements that the trading feature is designed for Indian users or that it has considered Indian regulations. Until a launch is announced with India-specific details, the safe assumption is that it is not available to you — and any website or message claiming otherwise should be treated with suspicion.

The comparison that helps here is with the other big platform deals of this month. On 10 November, the NYSE announced it had picked Avalanche to provide the core ledger technology for a tokenised-securities platform, and on 11 November OKX and ICE announced a tokenised-securities venue opening on 23 November. Both of those are infrastructure deals: a well-known institution choosing a blockchain for a specific, regulated purpose. The CoinGecko deal is different. It is not about infrastructure. It is about who owns the information layer — the numbers themselves — and that is a more sensitive thing to own, because the numbers are what everyone else's decisions are based on.

## What Is — and Isn't — Changing for Users

For an ordinary user opening CoinGecko today, nothing visible has changed. The website looks the same, the prices are calculated the same way, and the coins listed are the same. That is the first thing to say plainly, because a headline about a "majority stake" can sound more dramatic than the reality on the ground.

What has changed is the ownership, and what will change — if the announcements are followed through — is the product. Three things are planned. First, wider data coverage: more onchain assets, more derivatives, more real-world assets, and better research and discovery tools. That is the part of the announcement that is easiest to welcome, because more and better data helps everyone. Second, trading features that let users act on the research they are reading — the part with no launch date and no details. Third, a closer relationship between CoinGecko and the Solana ecosystem, which is the part that raises the neutrality question.

For Indian users, there is a short practical checklist that follows from all of this. It is the same checklist that applies to any price source, but it matters more now that the ownership has changed:

- **Cross-check important prices.** Before acting on any price — especially a large purchase or sale — check the same coin on at least one other source, such as another price tracker or the exchange where you actually trade. If two sources disagree by more than a small amount, find out why before you act.
- **Remember the India premium.** The global price on CoinGecko is not the price you pay in India. Indian exchanges have consistently charged a premium over the global price — about 10.75% for Bitcoin at the 18 November fix — so always check the rupee price on the exchange you use, not just the dollar price on a tracker.
- **Do not trust "CoinGecko trading" links yet.** There is no CoinGecko trading product live today, and no launch date has been announced. Any message, advertisement or website offering you CoinGecko trading, a CoinGecko token sale, or a special early-access trading account is a scam until proven otherwise. The same applies to any message claiming the deal means a particular coin will rise.
- **Watch the listings, not the press release.** The real test of neutrality will be in what CoinGecko lists, ranks and highlights over the next few months. If Solana ecosystem tokens start appearing more prominently, or if competing chains' data becomes harder to find, that is the signal that matters — and it will be visible to anyone who uses the site regularly.
- **Keep your own records.** If you trade, keep your own record of the prices you bought and sold at, from the exchange where you traded. A price tracker is a reference; your exchange statement is the record that matters for tax.

## What This Means for Crypto Users in India

For Indian readers, the deal has three layers: the price you see, the rules you live under, and the market you trade in.

The price you see is the most immediate. Bitcoin was trading near $62,800 on 19 November, or about ₹58.6 lakh, after the Fed minutes released in the early hours of the day showed almost all officials expecting more rate hikes. That is the global price — the one CoinGecko shows. The rupee price an Indian user actually pays is higher, because of the India premium described above, and because the rupee has been moving against the dollar: the rupee closed around ₹93.45 to the dollar on 18 November, its strongest level in about seven months. When the dollar strengthens against the rupee, Bitcoin becomes more expensive in rupees even if its dollar price does not move, and when it weakens, the opposite happens. The price on your screen is therefore the product of two moving numbers — the global crypto price and the exchange rate — and a price tracker only shows you the first one.

The rules are unchanged, and it is worth saying so plainly because a story this big often creates the impression that something else has changed too. There is no Indian angle to the deal itself: CoinGecko is not an Indian company, the Solana Foundation is a Swiss non-profit, and neither is regulated by Indian authorities. The 30% tax on crypto gains and the 1% TDS on covered transfers are unchanged, and losses cannot be set off against other income. The deal does not change the FIU-IND registration requirement for Indian exchanges, and it does not change the banking problems that have affected Indian users this month — Coinbase's INR withdrawals, Unocoin's suspension and ZebPay's delays are all separate stories, covered in earlier posts on this site. The market you trade in is where the deal's longer-term effect may show up. If CoinGecko adds Solana-powered trading, and if that trading proves popular, it could make Solana's ecosystem easier to reach for the millions of people who already use the site to research coins — including Indians who use it every day. That is a real competitive advantage for one blockchain over others, delivered not through technology but through distribution: the place where people look is also the place where they can buy. Whether that advantage materialises depends on whether the trading feature launches at all, and whether it is available in India — and both of those questions are unanswered today.

One more India-specific point is worth making. Indian crypto users have spent this month dealing with exchange outages, withdrawal halts and a security breach at a major Indian exchange. In that context, the lesson of the CoinGecko deal is the same as the lesson of those stories: know who holds your money, know who provides your information, and do not assume that a familiar name guarantees either. CoinGecko has earned trust over twelve years by being a neutral reference. The deal does not erase that record, but it does mean the trust now has to be re-earned under new ownership — and the users who benefit most will be the ones who keep checking.
