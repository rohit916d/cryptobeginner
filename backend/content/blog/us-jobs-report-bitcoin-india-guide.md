On Friday 6 November 2026, the US government released its October jobs report — and it was a genuine surprise. Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, nearly three times the roughly 59,000 to 65,000 that economists had expected. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, below the 4.2% forecast, and earlier months were revised higher too. Within minutes, the US dollar jumped, gold dropped, and Bitcoin whipsawed — first spiking above $76,100, then sliding to about $75,600, before settling near $75,800.

The numbers themselves are simple. The chain that connects them to Bitcoin is where most people get lost. This guide explains that chain step by step: what the jobs report actually is, why strong hiring pushes interest-rate expectations up, why that pressures Bitcoin, and why — despite a headline that beat forecasts by a huge margin — Bitcoin ended the day only slightly lower.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported economic release and how markets reacted to it; it does not predict prices and does not recommend any coin, trade or investment.

## What the October Jobs Report Actually Said

The report comes from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), the government agency that publishes the monthly Employment Situation report. It is released on the first Friday of each month at 8:30 AM Eastern time — 7:00 PM IST — and it is one of the most closely watched economic releases in the world, because it is the single most comprehensive snapshot of the American labour market.

Here are the headline figures from the 6 November release, as reported by CoinDesk, Morningstar, BeInCrypto and Bitcoin Magazine on the day:

| Figure | What was reported | What was expected |
|---|---|---|
| Jobs added in October | 165,000 | About 59,000–65,000 |
| Unemployment rate | 4.1% | 4.2% |
| September jobs (revised) | 60,000 | 29,000 as first reported |
| August jobs (revised) | 186,000 | Up 53,000 from the first estimate |
| Average hourly earnings | +0.4% on the month, +3.1% on the year | Below the reported figures |

The revisions matter as much as the headline. September's gain was revised up from 29,000 to 60,000, and August's from 133,000 to 186,000 — a combined upward revision of 84,000 jobs. That means the labour market was stronger in the summer than the first estimates suggested, and the October beat was not a one-month fluke but the continuation of a stronger trend.

The hiring was also broad. Professional and business services led with 70,000 new jobs, health care added 42,000, transportation and warehousing gained 25,000, and manufacturing rose by 19,000. A beat concentrated in one sector can be dismissed as a quirk; a beat spread across services, health care, logistics and manufacturing is harder to dismiss.

One more detail from the report is worth noting for the rate story that follows: wages. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% in the month and 3.1% over the year, both ahead of estimates. Strong wage growth matters because the Federal Reserve watches it as a signal of future inflation — if pay is rising fast, the prices of goods and services tend to follow.

## What Is a Jobs Report, and Why Do Markets Care So Much?

For a beginner, it helps to understand what this report is and is not. The BLS surveys roughly 121,000 businesses and government agencies covering about 631,000 work sites, plus about 60,000 households, to build the picture. The "nonfarm payrolls" number — the 165,000 figure — counts the net change in jobs outside farming, which is excluded because farm employment is highly seasonal.

Markets care because the jobs report is one of the few economic releases that can change what the Federal Reserve does next. The Fed has two jobs: keep prices stable and keep employment as high as possible. When hiring is very strong and wages are rising, the economy may be running hot — and a hot economy can push inflation up. The Fed's main tool for cooling things down is raising interest rates, which makes borrowing more expensive and slows spending.

That is the first link in the chain: strong jobs → more room for the Fed to raise rates → higher rate expectations.

The second link is the market's reaction before the Fed even acts. Traders do not wait for the December meeting to move money; they reprice their bets the moment the data lands. The main gauge is the CME FedWatch tool, which tracks the probabilities traders assign to different rate outcomes. Before this report, traders already put roughly a 67% to 73% chance on a quarter-point rate hike at the Fed's December meeting — the range differs slightly between sources because the odds move through the day. After the report, that probability rose to roughly 77% to 79%. The jobs data did not create the hike expectation; it strengthened one that was already there.

The third link is the dollar. When US rate-hike odds rise, the dollar usually strengthens, because higher US interest rates attract money into dollar-denominated assets. After this report, the US Dollar Index — which tracks the dollar against six major currencies — spiked to about 102.22, up from about 102.07 before the release, and settled near 102.14. Gold, which like Bitcoin pays no interest, took the sharpest hit: it dropped to about $4,032 an ounce within minutes of the release, down from roughly $4,062 just before, before recovering to about $4,050.

## The Full Chain: From 165,000 Jobs to Bitcoin's Price

Put the links together and the chain from the jobs report to Bitcoin looks like this:

**Step 1: The data beats expectations.** 165,000 jobs against forecasts of roughly 59,000 to 65,000, unemployment down to 4.1%, wages up 0.4% in a month, and 84,000 extra jobs found in the revisions to August and September.

**Step 2: Rate-hike odds rise.** A stronger labour market gives the Fed more room to raise rates without fearing a jobs collapse. Traders raise the probability of a December hike from roughly 70% toward 80%.

**Step 3: The dollar strengthens.** Higher expected US rates pull money toward the dollar. The Dollar Index jumps, and the 10-year US Treasury yield rises — it climbed 6.4 basis points to about 4.894% after the data, according to CoinDesk.

**Step 4: Assets that pay no interest come under pressure.** Bitcoin and gold earn no yield. When interest rates rise, holding them carries a higher opportunity cost — the return you give up by not holding an interest-paying asset instead. That does not make Bitcoin worthless or broken; it makes it relatively less attractive at the margin, and at the margin is where prices are set.

**Step 5: Crypto feels the same pressure, with extra volatility.** Bitcoin fell 0.3% to about $75,548 after the data, according to LSEG figures cited by Morningstar, after briefly spiking above $76,100 and dipping to about $75,600. The move was small — but it came on top of a much bigger weekly move, which brings us to the context that explains why the reaction was so muted.

## Why Bitcoin Barely Moved Despite a Huge Surprise

A reader could reasonably ask: if the report beat forecasts by nearly three times, why did Bitcoin only slip a fraction of a percent? The answer has three parts, and together they are a useful lesson in how markets actually work.

**First, the big move had already happened.** Bitcoin had already fallen to a seven-week low of about $75,131 overnight on Thursday, during a sharp sell-off in technology stocks. The Nasdaq fell 5.5% on Thursday — its worst day in about eighteen months — and crypto followed: Bitcoin ended the week down, Ethereum fell more than 7%, and total crypto liquidations topped $1 billion in 24 hours. By Friday morning, the market had already priced in a great deal of bad news. Our guide to why Bitcoin fell with the Nasdaq explains that transmission chain in detail.

**Second, a strong report was already partly expected.** BeInCrypto had flagged the jobs report earlier in the week as one of three big Bitcoin tests, alongside the midterm elections and central bank rate decisions. Traders had already pushed December hike odds to roughly 67–73% before the release. The report confirmed the direction of travel rather than reversing it, so the repricing was incremental, not dramatic.

**Third, other forces were pulling the other way at the same time.** US stock futures were pointing to a rebound in the Nasdaq on Friday morning, after Thursday's rout, and the Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 1.5% after the data. Bitcoin tends to move with stocks in the short term — it goes down when stocks go down, and often recovers when they recover. That equity rebound cushioned the crypto reaction, which is why Bitcoin traded in a tight range of roughly $75,240 to $76,280 on the day and finished near $75,800, little changed from its pre-release level.

There is a broader pattern here that CoinDesk noted in its live coverage: crypto appears to have returned to an older behaviour — going down when the stock market goes down, but not really participating when equities surge. That asymmetry is worth remembering the next time a single data release is blamed for a crypto move. Usually, the data release is the trigger, not the whole story.

## The Week That Led Here: Midterms, the Nasdaq Rout, and ETF Outflows

The jobs report did not land in a vacuum. It was the last of three big tests for Bitcoin in a single week, and the other two had already left their mark.

The first was the US midterm elections on 3 November, in which Democrats performed strongly. Our guide to the midterm results explains the outcome; the market consequence was a fear that a Democrat-controlled Congress could slow AI investment and tighten the regulatory outlook for crypto. Trump-linked cryptocurrencies fell as much as 16%, and shares of Bitcoin miners moving into AI data-centre businesses slumped.

The second was Thursday's Nasdaq sell-off, driven by doubts about AI valuations, a data-centre slowdown, and worries about tighter regulation and construction delays in Texas. That rout pulled Bitcoin down with it and produced more than $1 billion in liquidations across crypto markets in 24 hours, with about 211,000 traders liquidated.

A third pressure was building underneath both: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $430 million in net outflows on Thursday, after about $75 million in net inflows on Wednesday, according to figures cited in TradingView's market coverage. ETF flows are not the whole market, but they are a visible gauge of institutional sentiment — and three days of heavy outflows earlier in the week had already been reported in our guide to the ETF outflows.

The jobs report, then, was the week's final data point — and it pointed the same way the rest of the week had: toward higher rates, a stronger dollar, and less appetite for risk. What made it newsworthy was not that it changed the story, but that it confirmed it with hard numbers, on the record, from the government's own statistics agency.

## What Indian Users Should Take Away

For an Indian reader, the jobs report is a useful case study in how global macro data reaches your portfolio — and in what it does not do.

**Check the rupee price, not just the dollar price.** Bitcoin was quoted at roughly ₹72 lakh on Indian exchanges on Friday morning, down about 4.5% in the early snapshot, while the dollar price was around $75,900. Because a stronger dollar can push the rupee weaker, the rupee price of Bitcoin can move differently from the dollar price. If you only watch the dollar figure, you can misread what your own holdings are actually doing.

**Separate the trigger from the trend.** The jobs report was a trigger, but the week's trend was set by the Nasdaq sell-off and the midterm results. Single data releases move prices for hours; the bigger forces — rate expectations, equity markets, ETF flows — move them for weeks. When you see a headline blaming one report for a price move, ask what else happened that week.

**Know the next two dates.** The next US jobs report is due on 4 December 2026, and the Federal Reserve's rate decision follows on 8–9 December. Those are the two dates where the expectations built up by this report will be tested. The December jobs report comes just five days before the Fed meeting, so it could matter even more than this one.

**Remember the Indian tax picture.** None of this changes how crypto is taxed in India. Gains on crypto are taxed at a flat 30%, plus a 1% TDS on transfers above the threshold, and losses cannot be set off against other income. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**Do not trade the headline.** The most useful lesson from this report is how little Bitcoin actually moved after a near-three-times beat. Markets are noisy, fast, and often already positioned for the news. A single strong jobs print is not a reason to buy or sell in a hurry — it is a reason to understand the rate environment you are investing in.

## What This Report Does Not Mean

It is important to be clear about the limits of what happened on Friday, because the coverage can easily be over-read.

The report does not mean the Fed will definitely raise rates in December. A probability of roughly 77–79% is high, but it is not a certainty, and the December jobs report — due five days before the meeting — could still change the picture. It does not mean Bitcoin will keep falling, or that it will recover; the day's actual move was tiny, and the week's bigger move came from the equity sell-off, not from this data. And it does not mean gold or Bitcoin are "broken" — both fell because the dollar strengthened, which is a normal, mechanical reaction to rate expectations, not a verdict on either asset.

The honest summary is narrower: strong hiring made a December rate hike look more likely, the dollar strengthened, and Bitcoin slipped slightly on a day when it was already near a seven-week low. That is the whole story — and understanding why each step follows from the last is more valuable than any prediction about what happens next.

## The Bottom Line

The October US jobs report was a genuine surprise: 165,000 jobs against forecasts of roughly 59,000 to 65,000, unemployment down to 4.1%, wages rising faster than expected, and 84,000 extra jobs found in revisions to August and September. It strengthened the case for a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, pushed hike odds toward 80%, lifted the dollar, and pressured Bitcoin and gold — but only slightly, because the market had already absorbed a brutal week of equity selling, midterm-driven regulatory worries and ETF outflows.

For Indian crypto users, the lesson is not about one Friday's numbers. It is about the chain itself: jobs → rates → dollar → risk assets. Once you can follow that chain, the next jobs report, the next Fed meeting and the next market sell-off all become easier to read — not as isolated shocks, but as steps in a process you already understand.
