# Crypto Advance Tax in India: Quarterly Payments Explained

Most crypto beginners in India learn about the 30% tax on crypto gains only when they sit down to file their income tax return. But the tax department expects that money much earlier — in advance, in four instalments spread across the year. If you made profits selling crypto this year and wait until filing season to pay, you may face interest charges that quietly eat into your gains. This guide explains advance tax in plain language, with crypto-specific examples, so you can plan your payments instead of discovering a surprise interest bill later.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This article explains tax rules for learning purposes only; for your specific situation, consult a qualified chartered accountant or tax advisor.

## What Is Advance Tax?

Advance tax is a "pay as you earn" system. Instead of collecting all your tax in one payment after the year ends, the Income Tax Department expects you to pay it in instalments during the year itself, as the income comes in. It applies to almost every kind of income — salary, business income, interest, capital gains — and crypto gains are no exception.

The idea is simple: if you earn money during the year, the government should not have to wait until the next year to receive its share. For salaried people, most of the work is done by the employer through TDS on salary. But crypto gains are not covered by an employer, and the 1% TDS deducted by exchanges on crypto trades is only a small advance against your final bill — it is not the full tax. That gap is what advance tax covers.

Here is the key threshold to remember: **advance tax is compulsory when your total tax liability for the financial year is ₹10,000 or more, after subtracting TDS already deducted.** If your estimated liability is below ₹10,000, you can simply pay the balance when you file your return. Above that line, you are expected to pay in instalments.

## Why Crypto Gains Trigger Advance Tax So Easily

Crypto has three features that push beginners into advance-tax territory faster than they expect.

First, the flat 30% rate is high. A ₹50,000 profit from selling crypto creates a ₹15,000 tax bill on its own — already above the ₹10,000 threshold. Unlike shares, where long-term gains have a lower rate and a ₹1 lakh exemption, crypto gains under Section 115BBH are taxed at 30% from the first rupee, plus surcharge and cess where applicable.

Second, losses cannot be set off. If you made ₹1 lakh on one trade and lost ₹80,000 on another, your tax is still calculated on the ₹1 lakh gain. The loss gives you no relief. Many beginners discover this only at filing time, but the advance-tax calculation works the same way: each profitable transfer adds to your liability.

Third, gains can arrive in any quarter. Crypto trades happen all year — there is no bonus season or fixed payday. A big gain in June means an instalment was already due in June. A big gain in February means you must cover almost all of it by 15 March. Advance tax forces you to think about tax at the time you book a profit, not months later.

## The Four Quarterly Instalments

Advance tax is paid in four instalments, each with a minimum percentage of your total estimated tax for the year. The schedule for an individual taxpayer is:

| Instalment | Due date | Minimum cumulative payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | 15 June | 15% of total estimated tax |
| Second | 15 September | 45% of total estimated tax |
| Third | 15 December | 75% of total estimated tax |
| Fourth | 15 March | 100% of total estimated tax |

Notice that the percentages are cumulative. By 15 September you should have paid 45% of the year's total tax — not another 45% on top of the 15% you already paid. By 15 March you should have paid 100%.

An example helps. Suppose your only income is a salary with TDS already covering the salary tax, and you estimate crypto gains of ₹2 lakh for the year. At 30% plus 4% health and education cess, your crypto tax is roughly ₹62,400. (The exact figure can vary with surcharge, but the maths shows the pattern.) After subtracting the 1% TDS deducted by exchanges on your trades, you might still owe about ₹60,000. Your instalments would be:

- By 15 June: at least ₹9,000 (15% of ₹60,000)
- By 15 September: at least ₹27,000 in total (45%)
- By 15 December: at least ₹45,000 in total (75%)
- By 15 March: the full ₹60,000

If you only realise the gains in December, you cannot be expected to have paid in June — the interest rules allow for this, as explained below. But if the gains were already in your pocket by June and you paid nothing, interest starts accruing.

## How to Estimate Your Crypto Tax for the Year

Estimating is the hardest part, because crypto income is unpredictable. Here is a practical approach:

**Step 1: Add up gains already booked.** Go through your trades so far this year and total the profit on each sale, swap or spend. Remember, every transfer of a virtual digital asset is a taxable event — selling for INR, swapping one coin for another, or spending crypto all count. Keep a simple spreadsheet or use your exchange's tax report, and record the date, asset, quantity, sale value and cost for each transfer.

**Step 2: Estimate gains still to come.** If you plan to sell more before 31 March, make a rough estimate. If you do not plan to sell, your estimate can stop at the gains already booked. It is better to estimate conservatively and pay a little extra than to underestimate and pay interest later.

**Step 3: Apply the 30% rate.** Multiply your total estimated gains by 30%, then add 4% health and education cess on the tax amount. Surcharge applies only at higher income levels, so many beginners can ignore it — but check with a tax advisor if your total income is high.

**Step 4: Subtract TDS already deducted.** Exchanges deduct 1% TDS on crypto transfers above the threshold. That amount counts towards your tax bill. Check Form 26AS or the Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the income tax portal to see how much TDS has actually been deposited in your name.

**Step 5: Compare with ₹10,000.** If the remaining liability is ₹10,000 or more, advance tax applies. If it is less, you can pay the balance at filing time without interest for missing advance tax — though interest under other sections can still apply in some cases.

**Step 6: Revisit every quarter.** Your estimate is not fixed. Before each due date, update your gains and adjust the next instalment. If you paid too much in an early quarter, you can pay less later. If gains surged, top up the next instalment.

## What Happens If You Miss an Instalment?

Two sections of the Income Tax Act charge interest on missed advance tax:

- **Section 234B:** If you paid less than 90% of your total tax liability by 31 March, interest of 1% per month (or part of a month) is charged on the shortfall, from 1 April until the date you actually pay. This applies even if you file your return on time — the interest is for paying late, not for filing late.
- **Section 234C:** Interest of 1% per month is charged for shortfalls in individual instalments — for example, if you paid less than 15% by 15 June, or less than 45% by 15 September. The interest is calculated on the shortfall for a set number of months (three months for the first three instalments, one month for the last).

A worked example shows why this matters. Suppose your total crypto tax for the year was ₹60,000 and you paid nothing in advance. Under Section 234C, the interest could be roughly ₹1,800 for the first instalment shortfall (1% × ₹9,000 × 3 months, with the actual calculation being slightly different), plus similar amounts for the later instalments, and then Section 234B interest from 1 April until you pay. The total interest can easily reach ₹3,000–₹4,000 on a ₹60,000 bill — around 5–7% extra, simply for paying late. On larger gains the interest is proportionally larger.

There is one important relief: **interest under Section 234C does not apply to income that arises suddenly and could not have been estimated.** The law specifically lists capital gains as an example — if a gain arose late in the year and you paid the tax in the next instalment, no 234C interest is charged for the earlier instalments. This helps crypto traders, because a big gain in February cannot reasonably have been predicted in June. But the relief only covers the instalments that fell due before the gain arose — you still have to pay the tax in the next instalment, and Section 234B still applies if less than 90% is paid by 31 March.

## How to Pay Advance Tax Online

Paying advance tax takes about ten minutes. The steps are:

1. Go to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and open the **e-Pay Tax** service. You do not need to log in for a basic payment, though logging in lets you save the challan to your account.
2. Enter your PAN and mobile number, and verify with the OTP.
3. Select **Challan No./ITNS 280** and choose **(0021) Income Tax (Other than Companies)** as the tax type.
4. Under "Type of Payment", select **(100) Advance Tax**.
5. Choose the assessment year — for income earned in the financial year 2026–27, the assessment year is 2027–28. (For the current year, check the portal: the assessment year is always the year after the financial year in which the income was earned.)
6. Enter the tax amount, plus any interest if you are paying late. You can pay through net banking, UPI, a debit card or over the counter at an authorised bank.
7. Save the challan receipt (Challan 280) immediately. The challan number (BSR code + challan serial number) is what links the payment to your PAN.

After paying, verify the credit appears in your **Form 26AS** and **AIS** within a few days. If it does not appear, the payment may have been made against the wrong assessment year or the wrong payment type — check the challan details and correct it well before filing.

## Advance Tax, TDS and Your Return: How They Fit Together

It helps to see the whole picture:

- **1% TDS on trades:** Deducted by the exchange or buyer on each transfer above the threshold. It is an advance payment, not the final tax. It appears in your Form 26AS.
- **Advance tax:** The quarterly instalments you pay yourself to cover the gap between the TDS and your full 30% liability.
- **Self-assessment tax:** Any balance still unpaid when you file your return. Paying this at filing time does not remove the interest that accrued for missing the earlier instalments.

When you file your return, crypto gains go in **Schedule VDA**, and your advance tax and TDS payments are claimed as taxes already paid. The return calculates the final liability and tells you whether you owe more or are due a refund. Overpaying advance tax is not wasted money — the excess is refunded with interest — so when in doubt, paying a little extra early is safer than paying too little.

## Common Mistakes Indian Crypto Beginners Make

- **Treating TDS as the full tax.** The 1% TDS is only a small fraction of the 30% liability. The rest is yours to pay.
- **Forgetting that swaps are taxable.** Swapping one crypto for another is a transfer under the law, so it creates a gain even though no INR ever touched your bank account. Each swap adds to the year's liability.
- **Using the exchange's TDS figure as the tax figure.** The TDS is 1% of the transaction value, not 1% of your profit. Your advance tax must be calculated on profit, not on turnover.
- **Waiting for the exchange to send a reminder.** Exchanges do not remind you about advance tax. The responsibility is entirely yours.
- **Paying under the wrong assessment year.** This is the most common online-payment error. Double-check the year before submitting — a payment under the wrong year does not count until it is corrected.
- **Ignoring small gains.** A few ₹10,000 profits across the year can add up past the ₹10,000 threshold. Track every transfer from the start.
- **Not keeping records.** Without a transaction log, estimating gains each quarter is guesswork. Keep records from day one — it also makes Schedule VDA filing far easier.

## A Simple Quarterly Routine for Crypto Traders

You do not need to become a tax expert. A short routine before each due date is enough:

1. **15 June:** Total your gains from April to June. Estimate the year's total. Pay 15% of the estimated liability.
2. **15 September:** Update the gains total. Pay enough to reach 45% of the revised estimate.
3. **15 December:** Update again. Pay enough to reach 75%.
4. **15 March:** Final update. Pay the remaining balance so the full year's liability is covered.
5. **After 31 March:** Check Form 26AS and AIS. Make sure every advance-tax payment and every TDS deduction is visible before you file.

Set a calendar reminder for the 10th of June, September, December and March — five days before each deadline — so you have time to gather figures and pay without rushing.

## The Bottom Line

Advance tax is not an extra tax. It is the same 30% crypto tax, paid earlier in the year instead of all at once at filing time. The system exists so that the tax department is not kept waiting, and the interest charges exist to make waiting expensive. For crypto beginners, the practical lesson is simple: every time you book a profit, set aside roughly 30% of it for tax, and pay it in the next quarterly instalment. That habit protects you from interest, keeps your records clean, and makes filing your return a calm exercise instead of a scramble.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to pay advance tax on crypto gains in India?

Yes, if your total estimated tax liability for the financial year is ₹10,000 or more after accounting for TDS. Because crypto gains are taxed at a flat 30% with no set-off of losses, even a few profitable trades can push you over the ₹10,000 threshold. Advance tax is paid in four quarterly instalments during the year, not at filing time.

### What happens if I don't pay advance tax on my crypto gains?

Interest is charged under Sections 234B and 234C of the Income Tax Act — 1% per month on the shortfall — and it keeps adding up until you pay. Skipping advance tax does not reduce your final tax bill; it only adds interest on top of it. Paying on time in quarterly instalments is the cheapest way to stay compliant.

### How do I pay advance tax on crypto income online?

Use the Income Tax e-filing portal's e-Pay Tax service and pay through Challan 280, selecting code 0021 (Income Tax — Other than Companies) and the option for advance tax. You can pay through net banking, UPI or a debit card. Save the challan receipt and check that the payment appears in your Form 26AS and AIS before you file your return.
