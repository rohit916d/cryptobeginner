On 28 October 2026, India's crypto industry took a step it had been signalling for months: the Bharat Web3 Association (BWA), the industry body representing crypto and Web3 companies in India, announced an independent Self-Regulatory Body (SRB) for the country's virtual digital asset (VDA) sector. The announcement, made in New Delhi, was reported the same day by Moneycontrol, The Crypto Times and NewsBytes, among others.

If you are a beginner, the headline raises obvious questions: is this now the official regulator for crypto in India? Does it make crypto safer, legal or tax-free? The short answer is no — it is an industry-led standards body, not a government regulator, and it changes no law or tax rule. But it could still matter, if members actually follow its standards.

This guide explains the BWA-SRB in plain language: what it is, who runs it, what it will work on, why it was created now, and what it means for an ordinary Indian user.

## First, the Basics: What Is a VDA, and What Is an SRB?

Indian law uses the term "virtual digital asset" (VDA) for cryptocurrencies, non-fungible tokens (NFTs) and similar digital tokens. When news reports talk about the "VDA sector", they mean the exchanges, wallet providers, custodians and other firms that let Indians buy, sell, hold or transfer these assets.

A self-regulatory body (SRB) is different from a government regulator. A government regulator — such as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) for banks, or the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) for stock markets — gets its powers from a law passed by Parliament. It can grant licences, impose fines and, in serious cases, shut a firm down.

An SRB, by contrast, is created by an industry itself. Its members agree, voluntarily, to follow a shared set of standards. The SRB can review members, publish guidelines and recommend corrective action, but it cannot pass laws, levy fines with legal force or replace the statutory authorities. That distinction is the single most important thing to understand about the BWA-SRB: it is a standards and oversight effort from inside the industry, not a new government watchdog.

Self-regulation is not new in Indian finance: the RBI published a Framework for Recognising Self-Regulatory Organisations for the FinTech sector on 30 May 2024, and the BWA says its framework draws on that RBI framework and global practices.

## What Was Announced on 28 October 2026?

The Bharat Web3 Association Self-Regulatory Body (BWA-SRB) will set voluntary guidelines for BWA members and periodically review their governance and compliance, recommending corrective measures where needed.

The body is chaired by Rohit Kumar Singh, a retired IAS officer and former secretary to the Government of India, with Praggya Das, a former adviser-in-charge of the RBI's Monetary Policy Department, as a member. BWA chairperson Dilip Chenoy said the launch reflected the association's commitment to risk mitigation and compliance-focused regulation.

The BWA's own announcement framed the purpose in trust terms: as India's VDA industry grows, consistent guidelines, strong compliance practices and effective safeguards are essential to building trust and enabling responsible growth. Trust has been the industry's weak point in India, from exchange security incidents to confusing listings to users with no clear route to complain.

## What Will the BWA-SRB Actually Work On?

The SRB's stated focus areas, as reported on 28 October, are broad. In plain language, they are:

- **Market integrity.** Rules against practices that mislead users or distort trading, such as wash trading or misleading promotion of tokens.
- **Cybersecurity.** Standards for protecting user accounts, wallets and data against hacks and breaches.
- **Consumer protection.** Clearer disclosures, fairer treatment of users, and protection of digital assets and customer data.
- **Transparent token listings.** Clearer criteria for which tokens an exchange lists, so users can see why a token is available — and what risks it carries.
- **Grievance redressal.** A clearer route for users to raise complaints and get them resolved, rather than being passed between support desks.
- **Responsible marketing.** Standards against hype-driven advertising that promises returns or hides risks.
- **Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing (AML/CFT) practices.** Checks meant to stop illicit funds moving through crypto platforms, in line with the obligations exchanges already carry under Indian law.

The SRB will also review members' governance and compliance practices periodically. In practice, that means the body is supposed to check, on an ongoing basis, whether members are living up to the guidelines — not just signing up to them on launch day.

## Why Now? The Gap the SRB Is Trying to Fill

India still has no dedicated, comprehensive law for crypto assets. Instead, it currently governs crypto through a patchwork:

- Crypto is not banned, but it is not legal tender either — you cannot be forced to accept it as payment, and the RBI has repeatedly warned about its risks.
- Gains are taxed at a flat 30%, with 1% tax deducted at source (TDS) on many transactions. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.
- Exchanges operating in India must register with Financial Intelligence Unit–India (FIU-IND) as reporting entities and follow anti-money-laundering rules, including the strengthened KYC guidelines issued in January 2026, which require a live selfie with liveness detection, a second identity document, and penny-drop bank verification.
- A separate central bank digital currency, the e-Rupee, is being piloted by the RBI — but that is RBI-issued digital cash, not the same thing as Bitcoin or other VDAs.

Earlier in 2026, the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance, in its 36th report on the proposed Securities Markets Code, 2025, recommended that the government comprehensively examine a proper statutory framework for VDAs — and, pending such a framework, consider an interim mechanism involving recognised self-regulatory organisations under a designated regulator. The BWA-SRB arrives directly in that context: the industry is, in effect, building the interim self-regulatory layer the committee described, while a full law remains pending.

There is also a user-demographics reason the timing matters. According to remarks reported alongside the launch, around 75% of VDA investors in India are aged between 18 and 35, and adoption is growing in Tier 2, Tier 3 and Tier 4 cities — places where users may have less access to traditional investment advice and more exposure to social-media-driven crypto promotion. The SRB's marketing and grievance standards are aimed partly at that audience.

## What the SRB Does Not Do

Because headlines can overstate a launch like this, it is worth being precise about what has not changed:

**It does not make crypto legal tender.** Only RBI-issued currency is legal tender in India. The SRB cannot change that.

**It does not change taxes.** The 30% tax on gains and 1% TDS remain in force. Only Parliament, through the Finance Act, can change tax law.

**It does not licence exchanges.** FIU-IND registration remains the key regulatory check for exchanges operating in India, and it is a separate process from BWA membership. An exchange being a BWA member, or following SRB guidelines, is not the same as being FIU-registered — and FIU registration itself does not guarantee that your funds are safe.

**It does not guarantee your funds.** The SRB has no deposit-insurance-style protection for users. If an exchange fails or is hacked, SRB membership alone does not return your money. Your own security habits still matter more than any industry badge.

**It does not bind non-members.** The guidelines are voluntary and apply to BWA members. A firm that is not a member is not covered by them at all. When you see an exchange claim to follow SRB standards, it is worth checking whether it is actually a member and what, specifically, it claims to follow.

## What Should Indian Beginners Watch For Next?

The launch is the easy part. What will decide whether the BWA-SRB matters is what happens next:

**Published guidelines, not just promises.** Are the SRB's voluntary guidelines published in full, in language ordinary users can read? Vague commitments are easy; detailed standards, with clear do's and don'ts for listings, marketing and complaint handling, are the real test.

**Real reviews with real consequences.** Does the SRB publish the results of its periodic reviews? Does it name members that fall short, or recommend corrective action publicly? A self-regulatory body that never criticises a member will struggle to be believed.

**A grievance route users can actually use.** If you have a complaint against a member exchange, is there a clear, documented path through the SRB — with timelines and outcomes — or does it lead back to the same support desk you started with?

**How the government responds.** The Parliamentary committee's idea was an interim SRO mechanism under a designated regulator. If the government formally recognises the SRB, or folds it into a future VDA framework, its weight grows considerably. If it is ignored, the SRB remains a purely voluntary industry club. Either outcome will become clear over time.

**Whether marketing actually changes.** One of the most visible tests is the advertising you see. If member firms stop running hype-driven promotions — guaranteed returns, countdown timers, influencer posts that hide risks — that is evidence the standards bite. If the same ads keep running, they do not.

## How to Protect Yourself Regardless of the SRB

Whatever the SRB becomes, the basics of staying safe do not change:

1. **Use FIU-IND-registered platforms.** Verify registration yourself rather than trusting a badge on a website. Our guide to checking FIU registration walks through the steps.
2. **Treat every "approved" or "regulated" claim as a claim to verify.** Ask: approved by whom, under what law, and where is the proof? "Self-regulatory body member" and "government regulated" are very different statements.
3. **Keep records in INR.** For tax purposes, what matters is your INR buy and sell values and the TDS deducted, not the dollar price on a chart. Keep clean records from day one.
4. **Be sceptical of SRB-themed scams.** Whenever a new crypto body launches, scammers follow: fake "SRB verification" messages, fake refund schemes for exchange losses, and fake support accounts claiming to "protect" your funds under the new rules. No genuine body will ask you to move coins to a "safe wallet" or share your seed phrase.
5. **Start small and learn first.** If you are new, the safest way to understand crypto is with small amounts you can afford to lose, while you learn how exchanges, wallets and taxes actually work. No industry body changes the fact that crypto prices can fall sharply and stay down for a long time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is India's new crypto self-regulatory body?**
It is the Bharat Web3 Association Self-Regulatory Body (BWA-SRB), an industry-led body launched on 28 October 2026 to set voluntary standards for India's virtual digital asset sector. It covers areas such as market integrity, cybersecurity, consumer protection, transparent token listings, grievance redressal and anti-money-laundering practices. It is not a government regulator and does not replace the RBI, SEBI or FIU-IND.

**Does the BWA self-regulatory body change crypto tax or legality in India?**
No. Crypto remains taxed in India at a flat 30% on gains with 1% TDS on many transactions, and it is still not legal tender. The SRB sets voluntary industry guidelines for its members; it cannot change tax law, grant licences or make crypto legal tender. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**What should Indian crypto beginners do differently after the SRB launch?**
Nothing urgent changes for ordinary users. You can still check whether an exchange is FIU-IND registered, keep your own security habits strong, and treat any new "SRB-approved" marketing claim with scepticism until you verify it. The practical value of the SRB will depend on whether member firms actually follow its guidelines and publish how they handle complaints — so watch for that evidence over the coming months rather than assuming protection on day one.

## The Bottom Line

India's new crypto self-regulatory body is best understood as the industry trying to govern itself while the law catches up. The BWA-SRB, launched on 28 October 2026 under a former government secretary and a former RBI monetary-policy adviser, sets voluntary standards on market integrity, cybersecurity, consumer protection, token listings, grievance redressal and anti-money-laundering practices. That is a meaningful ambition — and, if enforced transparently, it could raise standards across Indian crypto platforms.

But it is not a regulator, not a licence, not a tax change and not a safety guarantee. For beginners, the right response is to keep using the checks that already protect you — FIU registration, strong security, clean INR records, scepticism toward hype — and judge the SRB by what it publishes and enforces, not by launch-day headlines.
