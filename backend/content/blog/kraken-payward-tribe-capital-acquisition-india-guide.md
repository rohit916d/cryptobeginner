Kraken's parent company Payward has agreed to buy Tribe Capital, a venture capital and asset management firm with $2.7 billion in assets under management — roughly ₹2,518 crore. The deal, announced on 18 November 2026, brings a firm whose portfolio includes OpenAI, SpaceX and the Indian logistics company Shiprocket under the same roof as one of the world's oldest crypto exchanges. It is also an unusual deal in one specific way: Tribe Capital was co-founded and is still chaired by Arjun Sethi, who is also Kraken's co-CEO — so Payward is, in effect, buying the asset management business of its own chief executive. This guide explains what was actually announced, what was not disclosed, why the deal matters, and what — if anything — it changes for crypto users in India.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a business deal in the crypto industry; it does not recommend buying, selling or holding any asset.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The story, as reported on 18–19 November 2026 by The Economic Times, Gadgets 360, Unchained, CoinCentral, Coinotag, CryptoNews.net and Weex, is:

- **Payward signed a definitive agreement on 18 November 2026 to acquire Tribe Capital Management.** Payward confirmed the deal in a statement on Wednesday and on its official X account on 19 November, writing that it had "signed an agreement to acquire Tribe Capital, a data-driven VC and asset manager" and that the deal "adds private-market sourcing, underwriting and fund management to the Payward platform. Subject to closing conditions."
- **Tribe Capital manages about $2.7 billion in assets under management.** The firm invests in early- and late-stage private companies through pooled funds and co-investment vehicles, using data science to underwrite and deploy capital. The Economic Times put the figure at $2.7 billion, roughly ₹2,518 crore.
- **The price was not disclosed.** Neither company said what Payward is paying. The transaction is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary closing conditions, including required consents.
- **Tribe will sit inside Payward's Asset Management division.** Payward says its business is organised into pillars — trading, banking, services, artificial intelligence and now asset management — and Tribe becomes the asset management piece. The plan is to build new private-market investment offerings on Tribe's platform and connect them with Payward's custody, trading, collateral and tokenisation services.
- **No new product has been announced.** Payward has not named any product, launch date, fee or eligible region for the planned private-market offerings. Nothing changes for Kraken accounts yet, and no availability in India has been confirmed.
- **The advisers are named.** Moelis & Company advised Payward on the transaction, with Jones Day and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz providing legal counsel; Ropes & Gray represented Tribe Capital.

## Who Is Tribe Capital?

Tribe Capital was founded in 2018 by Arjun Sethi, Jonathan Hsu and Ted Maidenberg. It is a venture capital firm and asset manager based in Menlo Park, California, that describes itself as data-driven: it uses quantitative methods and data science to decide which private companies to back, and it invests across early- and late-stage rounds through commingled funds and co-investment vehicles.

Its portfolio is the part of the story that travelled furthest in the Indian press, because the names in it are familiar. Tribe Capital's investments include OpenAI and SpaceX — two of the most valuable private companies in the world — and, as Gadgets 360 highlighted in its headline, the Indian firm Shiprocket, the logistics platform used by thousands of Indian online sellers. That combination — artificial intelligence, space, and an Indian e-commerce infrastructure company — is a fair summary of what Tribe does: it backs private technology companies that are hard for ordinary investors to reach, because their shares do not trade on a public stock exchange.

There is one more detail in the reporting that matters for understanding the deal. According to CryptoNews.net, Tribe's portfolio report dated 30 June lists Payward — the company that does business as Kraken — among the companies held directly or indirectly by Tribe's investment vehicles. In other words, Tribe already had a financial interest in Payward before Payward agreed to buy Tribe. That is not unusual in venture capital, where funds often hold stakes in companies they later transact with, but it is another thread connecting the two sides of this deal.

## Who Is Payward — and What Is Kraken?

Payward is the parent company of Kraken, the crypto exchange founded in 2011 and one of the oldest continuously operating exchanges in the industry. Kraken lets users buy, sell and trade cryptocurrencies, and over the years Payward has expanded beyond simple trading into custody, banking services and artificial intelligence products. The Tribe acquisition is the next step in that expansion: from an exchange that trades crypto, Payward wants to become a broader financial platform that also manages investments in private companies.

That ambition is the context for the deal. Crypto exchanges have spent the last few years trying to grow beyond trading fees, because trading volumes rise and fall with the market. Asset management — charging fees to manage other people's money — is a steadier business, and private markets are where much of the growth in finance has been over the past decade. Companies such as OpenAI and SpaceX stay private far longer than companies used to, which means the biggest gains in their value happen before any public listing, in rounds that ordinary investors usually cannot join. Payward's bet is that it can sell access to that world to its own customers, using Tribe's team to pick the investments and its own custody and trading infrastructure to deliver them.

## The Related-Party Question

The part of this deal that deserves the most careful reading is the leadership overlap. Arjun Sethi co-founded Tribe Capital in 2018 and still chairs it. He has also been co-CEO of Kraken since October 2024. So Payward — the parent of the exchange Sethi runs — is buying the venture firm Sethi founded and chairs.

The Economic Times put it plainly: "In effect, Payward is buying the asset-management business of its own chief executive." That does not mean anything improper has happened. No regulator, auditor or party to the deal has alleged misconduct, and related-party transactions are a normal, disclosed feature of corporate life — a company buying a business from its own executive is legal when it is properly approved and the terms are fair. But the structure raises a straightforward governance question: when the buyer and the seller share a chief executive, who negotiates the price, and who checks that it is fair?

What has been published so far does not answer that question. As CryptoNews.net noted, Payward's acquisition announcement did not describe an independent approval or recusal process — no mention of a special committee, an independent valuation, or a statement that Sethi stepped aside from the decision. The price was also not disclosed, so outside observers cannot compare what Payward is paying with what Tribe is worth. Coinotag made the same point: the undisclosed price and the handling of Sethi's chairmanship are details "investors cannot yet assess."

For a beginner, the lesson is simpler than the corporate language. When you read about a deal, ask three questions: who is on each side, what was paid, and who checked the terms. Here, the first question has an interesting answer — the same person sits on both sides — and the second and third have not been answered publicly yet. That does not make the deal bad. It makes it unfinished as a story, and worth watching until the closing documents, the price or the governance details become public.

## What Payward Plans to Build

Payward's stated plan is to place Tribe inside its Asset Management division and develop new private-market investment offerings on Tribe's investment platform. In plain language, that means products that let Kraken's customers invest in private companies — the kind of companies Tribe already backs — rather than only buying and selling crypto.

The company has said it wants to connect those offerings with the services it already runs: custody (holding assets safely), trading, collateral (using assets as security for other transactions) and tokenisation (representing real-world assets as digital tokens on a blockchain). Tokenisation is the bridge between the two businesses. If a stake in a private company can be represented as a token, it can in principle be traded, custodied and used as collateral in the same systems that already handle crypto. That is the long-term vision Payward is buying into.

But the honest version of the plan is also the short one: nothing has been announced. No product name, no launch date, no fee, no minimum investment and no list of countries where the products will be available. As one tracker of the deal put it, "no new Kraken products have been announced" and "nothing changes for Kraken accounts yet." The deal is a platform purchase first and a product launch second — and the second part may take months or longer after the deal closes in early 2027.

## What the Deal Means for India

For Indian crypto users, the short answer is: not much changes right now, and the changes that do come will arrive slowly.

**Nothing is available in India yet.** No private-market product has been announced, and no India availability has been confirmed. Indian users should not assume they will be able to buy into Tribe's funds or into tokenised shares of OpenAI or SpaceX through Kraken. Private-market investments are also heavily regulated in India, where rules on who can invest in unlisted securities, how foreign investments are treated and what an Indian resident can hold abroad are set by the Reserve Bank of India and the Securities and Exchange Board of India — not by a crypto exchange's press release.

**The India connection is real but indirect.** Tribe's portfolio includes Shiprocket, the Indian logistics firm, and its co-founder Arjun Sethi is Indian-American. Gadgets 360's coverage led with exactly that angle, and it is the reason this deal travelled in the Indian press when most crypto business deals do not. But an Indian company in a portfolio is not the same as an Indian product. Shiprocket's presence in Tribe's portfolio tells you about Tribe's taste in investments, not about what Indian users can buy.

**The tax rules do not change.** Whatever Payward builds, Indian users' crypto tax treatment stays the same: gains on crypto are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS deducted on covered transfers, and losses cannot be set off against other income. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. If a future Payward product ever reaches India, its tax treatment would depend on what the product actually is — a fund unit, a tokenised share, a foreign security — and that would need its own explanation at the time.

**Check the platform before you use it.** Any exchange or platform serving Indian users should be registered with India's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND), the body that oversees virtual-asset service providers in the country. That is a basic check that applies to every platform, whether it is a household name or a new entrant, and it is worth doing before depositing money anywhere. The same applies to any private-market product: check who regulates it, what it actually holds, and what it costs, before assuming it is available to you.

## Why This Deal Fits a Bigger Pattern

The Kraken–Tribe deal is not happening in isolation. It is the second major deal in three days in which a crypto organisation has bought its way into a neighbouring business. On 17 November, the Solana Foundation took a majority stake in CoinGecko, the price-tracking website millions of Indians check every day — a deal this site covered in a separate guide. In that case a blockchain foundation bought the numbers people read; in this case an exchange's parent bought the team that picks private investments. Both deals point in the same direction: crypto companies are trying to become broader financial platforms, and they are buying the missing pieces rather than building them.

The timing is also worth noting. The deal was announced on 18 November, the day before Anthropic — the artificial intelligence company — priced its initial public offering at $83 a share and began trading on Nasdaq on 19 November in one of the largest listings of the year. Tribe backs OpenAI, Anthropic's great rival, and the week has been dominated by the question of who gets access to the world's most valuable AI companies and on what terms. Payward's answer is to buy the team that already has that access. Whether ordinary investors ever get it too is the question the next year will answer.

There is a parallel in the traditional finance world. For years, large asset managers have bought venture firms to offer their clients private-market exposure, because the biggest companies now stay private longer and the public markets capture less of their growth. Crypto exchanges are following the same playbook, with one difference: they hope tokenisation will eventually let them offer private-market stakes in smaller pieces, to more people, with more liquidity. That is a real possibility and a real promise — and it is also, for now, only a promise. The deal buys the team. The products, the regulation and the access still have to be built.

## What to Watch Next

Four things will tell you whether this deal matters in practice:

1. **The closing.** The deal is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary closing conditions and required consents. A delay or a failed closing would be the first sign of trouble.
2. **The price.** If the price is disclosed in regulatory filings or later reporting, it will show what Payward thought Tribe was worth — and whether the related-party question was handled with an independent valuation.
3. **The governance details.** Any disclosure of an independent committee, a recusal by Sethi, or an outside fairness opinion would answer the question the announcement left open.
4. **The first product.** The first private-market offering, with a named product, a fee, a minimum investment and a list of eligible countries, will show whether the plan is real. Until then, the deal is a platform purchase, not a product.

For Indian users, the most important watch item is the last one — and specifically whether any product is ever offered in India, under which regulator, and on what terms. Until that happens, the deal is a story about the crypto industry's direction, not a change in what you can buy.
