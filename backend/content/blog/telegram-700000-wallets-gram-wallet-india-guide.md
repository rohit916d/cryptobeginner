Telegram added more than 700,000 self-custodial crypto wallets in just 48 hours, founder Pavel Durov said on 9 November 2026 — and the wallets switched on by themselves. The Telegram wallets story is not about people downloading a new app or signing up for a new service. It is about a wallet that activates the moment someone receives Gram tokens, Telegram's native cryptocurrency, without the recipient having to set anything up. This guide explains what actually happened, what Gram Wallet is, why the headline number needs careful reading, and what Indian users should know before touching it.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. This guide explains a reported wallet milestone; it does not recommend Gram, Telegram's wallet, or any token, and it does not predict any price movement.

## What Happened: 714,844 Wallets in Two Days

On 9 November 2026, Pavel Durov posted on X that more than 700,000 self-custodial wallets had been created on Telegram in the last 48 hours alone. The dashboard screenshot he shared showed 714,844 registrations across the two-day period, with 329,952 of them created on 9 November alone. The post was picked up the same day by crypto news outlets including CoinEdition, crypto.news, CryptoBriefing, U.Today and CryptoTimes, all reporting the same figures from the same source.

The number is striking because of how fast it arrived. Telegram is one of the world's largest messaging apps, with hundreds of millions of users, so a wallet built into the app can reach an enormous audience overnight. But the speed also has a mechanical explanation, and that explanation matters more than the number itself: these wallets were not created because 700,000 people decided to open a crypto wallet. Most of them were created because someone sent them Gram tokens.

Durov credited the surge to a feature called automatic activation. When a Telegram user receives Gram tokens for the first time, a wallet is created for them automatically. There is no download, no sign-up form, no seed phrase to write down at that moment. The wallet simply exists, attached to the Telegram account, ready to hold the tokens that were sent. That is why the count can jump by hundreds of thousands in two days: every first-time receipt of Gram creates a wallet, whether or not the recipient asked for one.

## What Gram Wallet Is

Gram Wallet is Telegram's native self-custodial wallet, also referred to as Money in Telegram's own rollout. It is built directly into the Telegram app and runs on The Open Network, or TON, the blockchain whose native token is Gram. Gram was previously known as Toncoin, and it traded at around $1.05 to $1.07 on 9 November 2026, more than 87% below its 2024 high of about $8.25.

The wallet lets users store and send Gram tokens inside the app, and Telegram has also tied it to purchases of digital gifts and collectible usernames. It is separate from the older @wallet mini-app and bot that Telegram users may already know. The native wallet is the newer, deeper integration: it is part of the app itself rather than a bot you open inside it.

The rollout has moved in stages. Telegram first outlined plans for a native non-custodial Gram wallet in July 2026, with limited access starting in August, followed by an expansion of the Money wallet in October. The 9 November figures are the latest step in that staged introduction, and the staged nature matters: availability, features and supported regions can differ from one user to the next while the rollout is still under way.

## Why 700,000 Wallets Does Not Mean 700,000 Active Users

The headline number deserves the same careful reading that any big crypto number deserves. A wallet creation is not the same as an active user, and several of the outlets reporting the story made exactly that point.

First, the count includes wallets with no on-chain activity. According to Gram's own documentation, a wallet is created automatically when a user receives a first token transfer, so a wallet can exist simply because someone sent its owner something. The wallet dashboard counted 203,663 Telegram wallets with a balance, but that figure covers the whole tracked wallet population, not just the latest 48 hours — and a balance is still not the same as regular use.

Second, there has been no independent verification of on-chain activity or retention for these wallets. The figures come from Telegram's own dashboard, shared by Durov. Independent reporting has also noted that the wider TON ecosystem shows limited decentralised-finance liquidity and relatively low active-wallet participation, with TON's total value locked at roughly $42 million. A wallet count can surge while actual usage stays small, and that gap is the honest way to read the story.

Third, the dashboard figures themselves vary slightly depending on when they are read. One check of the same dashboard put the 48-hour count at 714,871 rather than 714,844, and the single-day figure has been reported as both 329,952 and 333,188. The difference is small and comes from the rolling 48-hour window moving as time passes, but it is a reminder that these are live dashboard readings, not audited totals.

None of this makes the milestone meaningless. Seven hundred thousand wallets in two days is a real demonstration of how fast distribution can be when a wallet is built into a messaging app used by hundreds of millions of people. It does mean, though, that the right question is not "how many wallets were created" but "how many of those wallets will ever be used" — and that question has no answer yet.

## Why It Matters for India

Telegram is one of India's most-used messaging apps, and that is what makes this story relevant to Indian readers even though the announcement came from Telegram's founder and the figures are global. When a wallet arrives inside an app that millions of Indians already open every day, the distance between "I have never used crypto" and "I hold crypto" shrinks to a single incoming transfer. That is genuinely new: earlier generations of crypto wallets required a download, an account, and a deliberate decision. Gram Wallet can arrive without any of those.

That ease cuts both ways. A wallet that appears automatically is also a wallet whose owner may not know how self-custody works. In a self-custodial wallet, you control the keys — and that means nobody can recover your funds if you lose access, and nobody can reverse a transaction you were tricked into making. The convenience of automatic activation does not remove that responsibility; it hides it until the moment it matters.

The Indian tax angle is straightforward but easy to overlook. Receiving Gram tokens is not itself the taxable event that matters most — the tax applies when you transfer a virtual digital asset. Gains on transferring crypto are taxed at 30% plus a 4% cess, which works out to 31.2%, with no set-off of losses against other income, and sales attract 1% TDS. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. If you receive Gram tokens on Telegram and later sell or swap them, keep a record of the dates and the rupee values, because that record is what a tax filing needs.

There is also a practical point about where the wallet sits. Telegram's wallet is not an Indian exchange, and it is not registered with India's Financial Intelligence Unit the way Indian exchanges are. That does not make it illegal to use, but it does mean the protections Indian users get on a registered domestic exchange — rupee deposits through UPI, grievance channels under Indian law, and the exchange's own compliance checks — do not apply in the same way. Treat it as a self-custody tool, not as a regulated Indian platform.

## The Safety Checklist for Indian Users

A wallet that activates automatically is a gift to scammers as well as to users, because the same mechanism that creates wallets can be used to send tokens with a message attached. Telegram is already one of the main places where fake crypto apps, impersonation accounts and "double your money" schemes reach Indian users. If Gram Wallet grows, expect the scams around it to grow too. This checklist covers the basics.

**1. Never share your seed phrase or recovery words.** No genuine wallet, support agent or Telegram official will ever ask for them. Anyone who has your seed phrase controls your wallet completely. Write the words down on paper, store them offline, and never type them into a website, a bot, or a message.

**2. Do not trust wallet-verification messages.** Messages claiming your Telegram wallet needs verification, that your funds are frozen, or that you must "claim" tokens by clicking a link are phishing. Telegram will not ask you to verify a wallet through a link in a chat. If you receive such a message, do not click, and report it inside Telegram.

**3. Check that you are using the real wallet, not a fake one.** Fake Telegram wallet bots and lookalike apps already circulate. Open the wallet only through the official Telegram app, and be suspicious of any bot or channel that asks you to connect a wallet or approve a transaction. A genuine wallet feature lives inside the app; a bot asking for permissions is a different thing.

**4. Treat unexpected Gram tokens with suspicion.** If you receive tokens you did not expect, along with a message urging you to act quickly, pause. The tokens may be real, but the message around them may be a trap — a common trick is to send a small amount of tokens and then direct the recipient to a fake site to "claim" more. Verify through official Telegram channels before acting.

**5. Separate small experiments from real money.** If you want to understand how the wallet works, try it with a trivial amount first and learn the steps — sending, receiving, and backing up — before putting any meaningful sum in it. Self-custody rewards patience and punishes haste.

**6. Report fraud through official channels.** If you lose money to a Telegram wallet scam, report it on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in or call the 1930 helpline. Keep screenshots, transaction details and the usernames involved. Reporting does not guarantee recovery, but it is the route Indian authorities use to track these schemes.

## What to Watch Next

Three things will show whether this milestone becomes a real shift or a one-week headline.

The first is active usage. The dashboard counts creations, not transactions. If Telegram or independent trackers start reporting how many of these wallets actually send, receive or hold tokens over the following weeks, that will be the meaningful number. Watch for it, and be sceptical of any new headline that repeats the creation count as though it were an adoption count.

The second is the rollout in India specifically. Because the wallet is being introduced in stages, Indian users should watch whether the feature appears in their own app, what it allows them to do, and what terms Telegram attaches to it. A feature that exists for users in one country may not exist, or may work differently, for users in another.

The third is the scam wave that follows any wallet milestone. Every time a well-known platform adds a crypto feature, impersonators move in within days. The practical test for Indian users is not whether Gram Wallet succeeds, but whether they can tell the real wallet from the fake one — and the answer to that is boring and reliable: use only the official app, never share a seed phrase, and never click a wallet link in a message.

## The Bottom Line

Telegram added 714,844 self-custodial wallets in 48 hours, according to founder Pavel Durov, with 329,952 of them created on 9 November 2026 alone. The wallets were created by automatic activation: receiving Gram tokens for the first time creates a wallet without the recipient having to set one up. The figures come from Telegram's own dashboard, they count creations rather than active users, and they have not been independently verified — but they do show how quickly a wallet built into a messaging app can spread.

For Indian users, the story is a preview of how crypto reaches people who never went looking for it. A wallet can now arrive inside an app you already use, holding tokens someone sent you, before you have learned what self-custody means. The right response is to learn it first: understand that you control the keys and the risk, keep your seed phrase offline and private, treat unexpected tokens and verification messages as suspicious, and remember that Indian tax rules apply when you sell or swap what you hold. The wallets may be automatic. The responsibility is not.
