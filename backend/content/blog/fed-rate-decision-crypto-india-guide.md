Today, 28 October 2026, the US Federal Reserve announces its interest rate decision, and the crypto market is holding its breath. Bitcoin is trading near $80,000 — around ₹77 lakh on Indian listings in the morning trade reported by Indian market pages — after slipping below that level on 27 October and then recovering. Ethereum is near $2,346. The mood, measured by one popular sentiment gauge, is described as neutral rather than panicked or euphoric. And yet, across crypto news sites, exchange blogs and market desks, one event dominates the conversation: the FOMC decision.

If you are new to crypto in India, this can feel strange. Why would a meeting in Washington move the price of Bitcoin on an Indian exchange? Why does everyone care so much about a decision that most market watchers expect to be "no change"? And what, if anything, should you actually do about it?

This guide explains the Fed rate decision in plain language: what the FOMC is, what is expected today, why the words that follow the decision often matter more than the decision itself, and how the whole chain reaches an Indian beginner. It makes no price prediction and offers no investment advice. It is here so that the next time a headline says "crypto falls after Fed" or "crypto rallies after Fed", you understand what really happened.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose.

## What Is the Fed, and What Is the FOMC?

The Federal Reserve, often just called "the Fed", is the central bank of the United States — roughly the American equivalent of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Just as the RBI sets interest rates that influence loans, deposits and economic activity in India, the Fed sets the benchmark interest rate for the United States.

Inside the Fed, the body that actually votes on that rate is the Federal Open Market Committee, or FOMC. It meets eight times a year on a published schedule. At each meeting it decides the target range for the federal funds rate — the rate at which banks lend to each other overnight. That single rate ripples outward: it influences what banks charge for mortgages and business loans, what savers earn, what bonds yield, and how attractive different investments look compared with each other.

Today's meeting is the October 2026 meeting. The decision is due at 2:00 p.m. Eastern Time in the US, which is 11:30 p.m. Indian time on 28 October, followed by a press conference with the Fed Chair at 2:30 p.m. Eastern Time, or midnight in India. That late-night timing is one reason Indian beginners often wake up to a market that has already moved: the announcement lands while most of India is asleep, and the reaction plays out overnight.

## What Is Expected Today — and Why "No Change" Still Moves Markets

The current federal funds target range is 3.75% to 4.00%. Ahead of today's meeting, market pricing reported by Reuters and crypto market coverage put the probability of the Fed holding rates steady at roughly 88% to 91%. In other words, the widely expected outcome is no change at all.

So why would a widely expected "no change" move prices? Because markets do not only react to the rate itself. They react to three things at once:

1. **The decision.** Did the Fed hold, cut, or hike?
2. **The statement.** The short written statement released with the decision, where even a single changed word can shift expectations.
3. **The guidance.** What the Fed Chair says at the press conference about inflation, growth, and what might happen at the next meeting, in December.

As one market strategist quoted by Reuters put it ahead of this meeting, the Fed does not need to hike today to move markets — it simply needs to change the perceived probability of a hike in December. That is the key insight for beginners: crypto prices often move not on what the Fed does today, but on what traders now think it might do next.

The backdrop explains the tension: US consumer confidence improved in October, with the Conference Board index reported at 91.7, up from 84.2 in September, while September's US consumer price inflation was reported at 3.7% year on year, up from 3.4% in August. Stronger confidence and stickier inflation pull in opposite directions, which is what makes this meeting hard to call even with a "hold" widely expected.

## The Chain: From a US Interest Rate to a Bitcoin Price in INR

Here is the chain, step by step, in the simplest form that still captures what is really happening.

**Step 1: Interest rates set the price of safety.** When US interest rates are high, holding US government bonds — considered among the safest investments in the world — pays a relatively attractive return. The yield on the US 10-year Treasury bond was reported at about 5.0% ahead of today's meeting. When safe returns are that high, some investors feel less need to chase riskier assets to earn a return.

**Step 2: The dollar moves.** Higher-for-longer US rates tend to support the US dollar, because global money is attracted to higher dollar returns. Ahead of today's decision, the dollar index — which measures the dollar against six major currencies — was reported at 102.40, its highest since mid-October. A stronger dollar often coincides with weaker prices for assets priced in dollars, including Bitcoin, because it takes fewer dollars to represent the same value and because dollar strength tightens financial conditions globally.

**Step 3: Risk appetite shifts.** Crypto is treated by most large investors as a risk asset, similar in behaviour to technology stocks: it tends to do better when money is cheap and investors feel adventurous, and worse when money is expensive and investors turn cautious. When the Fed signals that rates will stay high, or might even rise, risk appetite usually cools. When it signals that cuts are coming, risk appetite usually warms. This is a tendency, not a law — and it can reverse quickly on other news — but it explains why Fed days are crypto days.

**Step 4: Leverage amplifies everything.** A large share of crypto trading uses leverage — borrowed money that multiplies both gains and losses. When prices move sharply after a Fed announcement, leveraged positions are closed out automatically, and those forced sales push prices further in the same direction. That is one reason a modest initial move after a Fed statement can turn into a much larger swing within an hour. Indian beginners should understand this mechanism even if they never use leverage themselves, because it explains the speed of Fed-day moves.

**Step 5: The move reaches India.** Indian exchanges quote crypto in INR, but their prices broadly track global dollar prices. If Bitcoin rises in dollars after a Fed announcement, Indian listings usually rise too; if it falls globally, Indian listings usually fall. The INR-to-dollar exchange rate adds a small layer of difference — a weaker rupee can cushion a dollar fall in INR terms, and a stronger rupee can do the opposite — but the direction of the global move usually dominates. That is why an Indian beginner watching a Fed night can see their portfolio value change overnight without any India-specific news at all.

## Why Crypto Reacts More Violently Than Stocks

If you have ever wondered why Bitcoin can move several percent on a Fed day while a broad stock index moves less than one percent, there are four structural reasons.

First, crypto never closes. Stock exchanges shut at night and on weekends, so news can be absorbed in stages. Crypto trades 24 hours a day, seven days a week, so the entire reaction happens immediately, in one continuous move.

Second, crypto is smaller and more concentrated than the global stock or bond market. The total crypto market was reported at roughly $2.66 trillion on 28 October. That sounds enormous, but it is far smaller than global equity markets, so the same amount of buying or selling moves prices more.

Third, leverage is more widespread and more extreme in crypto than in traditional markets. As explained above, forced liquidations turn small moves into large ones.

Finally, crypto has no earnings or dividends to anchor its price on a given day, so an asset whose value depends heavily on future expectations swings more than one with steady cash flows today.

## What Else Is in the Mix This Week

A Fed decision never happens in isolation, and this week is busier than most. Market coverage ahead of today's meeting also flagged US GDP and PCE inflation data due on 29 October, and the expiry of substantial Bitcoin options contracts on 30 October. Options expiry can add its own volatility, because large positions rolling off can shift supply and demand around specific price levels.

There is also the fund-flow backdrop: Indian market coverage on 28 October noted ETF outflows and macroeconomic uncertainty weakening sentiment as Bitcoin slipped near $79,000 ahead of the decision, while one data report described roughly $1 billion of Bitcoin leaving exchanges in a single outflow event. A single day's flow proves nothing on its own and should not be treated as a signal to buy or sell. On a Fed week, several triggers land within 48 hours, so attributing any single price move to any single headline is usually guesswork.

## The India Angle: Tax, Exchanges and Timing

For Indian readers, three practical points matter more than the Washington timetable.

First, timing. The decision lands at 11:30 p.m. IST and the press conference at midnight. If you check prices first thing in the morning, you are seeing the overnight reaction, not a live event. Avoid making a rushed decision on a half-read headline before you have seen the full statement and the press conference summary.

Second, tax does not change on Fed day. India taxes crypto gains at a flat 30%, with 1% TDS on many transactions, under rules that are separate from anything the Fed does. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing. If you do trade around a volatile event, keep clean records of every transaction in INR, because your tax calculation will depend on them later, not on the dollar price you saw on a chart.

Third, use only verified, FIU-registered Indian platforms and beware of Fed-day scams. Volatile news days are prime time for impersonators: fake "Fed announcement" trading groups, fake exchange support accounts promising to "protect" your funds, and social-media posts claiming the Fed Chair said something he did not. The Fed does not message individual investors, does not offer crypto schemes, and does not ask you to move coins anywhere. If a message claims otherwise, it is a scam.

## What Beginners Should — and Should Not — Do on Fed Days

This section is education, not advice. Nobody can tell you what the right move is for your money, and anyone who claims certainty about a Fed-day outcome is selling something.

What helps, in general, is slowing down. Read the actual statement and a reputable summary of the press conference before reacting. Distinguish the decision ("held at 3.75% to 4.00%") from the guidance ("December is still on the table" or "inflation remains a concern") — the second part is usually what moved the market. Check more than one source, and check the time stamp: a morning headline may describe last night's move, not today's outlook.

What tends to hurt beginners is the opposite: trading on the first red or green candle, using leverage without understanding liquidation, borrowing money to "catch" a Fed move, or copying a social-media influencer's Fed-day trade. Leverage deserves special emphasis. On a normal day it is risky; on an announcement day, when prices can gap several percent in minutes, it can wipe out a position before you have time to react. If you do not fully understand how liquidation works, Fed day is not the day to learn by doing.

A useful habit is to note, after each meeting, what was expected, what was decided, what the Chair emphasised, and how prices behaved over the following day — not just the first hour. Over several meetings you start to see the pattern: expectations are often already "priced in", guidance surprises more than decisions, and the first move is not always the lasting move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the FOMC and why does it matter for crypto?**
The FOMC is the committee inside the US Federal Reserve that sets the country's benchmark interest rate. That rate influences borrowing costs, the strength of the US dollar, and how much risk investors around the world are willing to take. Because crypto trades globally and around the clock, changes in that risk appetite often show up in Bitcoin and other crypto prices within minutes of an FOMC announcement.

**Does a Fed rate decision change crypto prices in India directly?**
Not directly, but the effect reaches India quickly. Indian crypto prices are quoted in INR on Indian exchanges, yet they broadly follow global prices, which are driven in US dollars. When a Fed decision moves global Bitcoin prices or the dollar, Indian listings usually move in the same direction, with the INR exchange rate adding a small extra difference. Indian tax rules do not change because of a Fed meeting.

**Should beginners trade around a Fed announcement?**
Announcement days are among the most volatile days in crypto, and that volatility cuts both ways. Many beginners lose money by reacting to the first headline, using leverage, or chasing a sudden move. A calmer approach is to learn how the mechanism works first, avoid leveraged positions you do not fully understand, never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and make any decision only after the full statement and press conference are out — not on a single flashing price.

## The Bottom Line

Today's Fed decision matters for crypto not because one committee in Washington controls Bitcoin, but because interest rates set the background music for every risk asset in the world — and crypto, trading around the clock with heavy leverage, dances to that music faster and harder than most. The decision itself is widely expected to be a hold at 3.75% to 4.00%. The real news, as usual, will be in the guidance: what the Fed signals about inflation, growth and December.

For Indian beginners, the most useful takeaway is not a prediction but a framework. Rates up or expected to stay high tends to cool risk appetite; rates expected to fall tends to warm it; guidance surprises more than decisions; leverage turns small moves into large ones; and Indian prices follow global prices, with the rupee adding a small twist. Keep that framework, keep your records clean, keep scams at arm's length, and let the Fed's words — the full statement and press conference, not a single headline — do the talking before you do anything at all.
