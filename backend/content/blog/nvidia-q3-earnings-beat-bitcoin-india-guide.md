Nvidia earnings landed after the US market closed on Tuesday 17 November 2026, and they were the number the whole risk market had been waiting for. The chipmaker reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% on a year earlier and well ahead of the roughly $109 billion analysts had expected, with adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share against forecasts of $2.47. It was the first earnings report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive last week, and the first real test of whether the AI trade — the trade that has dragged Bitcoin down with it for a month — still has its engine intact. The answer, on the numbers at least, was yes: guidance for the fourth quarter came in at $130 billion, above consensus, and Nvidia shares rose about 2% in after-hours trading.

This guide explains a reported earnings result and its market context; it does not recommend any stock, coin or trade, and it makes no prediction about future prices.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The results, as reported on 17 November 2026 by CoinDesk, TechDefused, WorldMoneyRoadmap and Midgard Finance, are:

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| What was reported | Nvidia fiscal Q3 2027 results, for the quarter ended 25 October 2026 |
| When | After the US market close on Tuesday 17 November 2026 (published about 21:25 UTC) |
| Revenue | $115.8 billion, up 103% year on year and 20% on the previous quarter |
| Against expectations | Ahead of the roughly $108.8–109.1 billion consensus, and $7.8 billion above Nvidia's own August guidance of $108.0 billion |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $2.68, against forecasts of $2.47 |
| GAAP earnings per share | $2.70, up from $2.46 in Q2 and $1.30 a year earlier |
| Net profit | $65.3 billion, more than double a year earlier |
| Data-centre revenue | $107.6 billion, up 110% year on year, against estimates of $101.9 billion |
| Gross margin | 74.8%, down 20 basis points from 75.0% in Q2, up 140 basis points year on year |
| Q4 guidance | Revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, against a consensus of about $125.3 billion |
| Shareholder returns | $28.1 billion in buybacks and dividends in the quarter; $150 billion added to the buyback authorisation |
| Share reaction | Closed at $155.46, down 0.44% in the regular session; about $158.88 in after-hours trading, up about 2.2% |
| Leadership | First results since Jensen Huang resigned; interim CEO Colette Kress led the earnings call |

The data-centre figure is the one that matters most for the AI story. At $107.6 billion it is not just a beat — it is 93% of the company's total revenue, and it was driven by Vera Rubin NVL72, Nvidia's newest AI computing system. The fourth-quarter outlook of $130 billion is for a 14-week quarter, which includes an extra week, and it assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. Gross margin is guided lower, to 71.0%–72.0%, on rising memory costs. And the $150 billion added to the buyback authorisation is a statement of confidence in its own right: the company returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter just ended and still has about $227.3 billion of authorisation left.

## Why Bitcoin Traders Were Watching Nvidia

To understand why a chipmaker's earnings report belongs on a crypto education site, start with the last month. Bitcoin is down roughly 30% since early October, and Nvidia shares are about 40% below their October peak of $243.37. The two falls have not been a coincidence. They have been funded by the same money.

The mechanism is straightforward. The same funds that hold Nvidia and other AI stocks also hold Bitcoin and crypto-linked equities. When those funds get nervous — about AI valuations, about interest rates, about a chief executive resigning — they sell both, and they prefer to hold cash. Nansen research analyst Nicolai Søndergaard put it plainly: AI and crypto draw on the same capital, so Nvidia weakness sends money to cash, not into Bitcoin. CoinDesk's market coverage has made the same point for weeks: funds that hold both tend to sell both when markets get shaky.

The last few days made the link concrete. When Jensen Huang resigned, Bitcoin fell from above $72,000 to about $71,250 within an hour, and crypto markets saw more than $1.6 billion in liquidations, about 82% of them long positions. On Monday 16 November, the Nasdaq fell 2.5% and the S&P 500 lost nearly 2% as Nvidia slid ahead of its results. On Tuesday, Bitcoin slipped below $64,000 as tech stocks fell again, touching about $63,269 at its low before steadying near $64,000–64,500. The options market had priced the moment too: traders expected an 8.5% swing in Nvidia shares around the report, the largest earnings move in two years, and the biggest open Bitcoin options position on Deribit is now a $60,000-strike put, with about $900 million in notional open interest.

There is a second thread running alongside the earnings. The Federal Reserve is now expected to raise rates in December, with markets putting the odds at around 88–92% for a 25-basis-point move, and the 10-year Treasury yield is back above 5%. Market maker Wintermute's latest note captures the mood: "In a rates-driven market, Bitcoin is the non-yielding funding leg, the ETF bid has disappeared, and the technicals have broken." US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in a net $7.9 million on Monday, ending seven straight sessions of outflows worth about $1.91 billion — a small positive, but the first in more than a week. That is the backdrop Nvidia reported into: a market where the AI trade, the rate trade and the crypto trade have become one trade.

## How This Differs From the Resignation Sell-Off

This post covers a different event from the one covered in our earlier guide to Huang's resignation, and the difference matters. The resignation post, published on 13 November, covered a leadership shock: a chief executive leaving without warning, a 14% intraday fall in Nvidia shares, and Bitcoin dropping below $72,000 in the hour that followed. The story was uncertainty — nobody knew who would run the company, what the board's investigation would find, or whether AI demand would hold up without its most famous advocate.

This post covers the answer to the demand question, and the answer is strong. Revenue of $115.8 billion against a $108 billion guide, data-centre revenue up 110%, and a fourth-quarter outlook of $130 billion all say that customers are still buying Nvidia's chips at a record pace, regardless of who sits in the chief executive's chair. The Anthropic IPO post, published on 14 November, covered a different mechanism again: the theory that capital was rotating out of liquid crypto markets into the AI company's $64.4 billion listing. That theory is still unproven, and this earnings beat does not settle it — but it does remove the fear that the AI trade itself was collapsing.

There is one more distinction worth making. The resignation was a one-day shock. The earnings are a quarterly fact. A chief executive can resign in an afternoon; $107.6 billion of data-centre revenue takes a quarter of orders, shipments and installations to build. That is why the market treated the two events differently: the resignation sent Nvidia down 14% in a session, while the earnings beat sent it up about 2% after hours. The first was a vote on leadership. The second is a vote on demand.

## Did Bitcoin React?

Honestly, and at the time of writing — in the first hour after the results — the honest answer is: not much, and not yet. Bitcoin was trading near $64,000–64,500 when the results came out, roughly where it had spent most of Tuesday, and there was no immediate crypto-market surge to match Nvidia's after-hours rise. That is worth saying plainly, because the temptation after a big beat is to claim a rally that has not happened.

What did happen, earlier in the week, is more interesting. On Monday, Bitcoin held near $64,800–65,000 while the Nasdaq fell 2.5% — the first time in weeks it did not fall harder than tech. One crypto newsletter called it the first sign the tether might be loosening, and framed Tuesday night as the real test: if Nvidia disappointed and Bitcoin held again, the link really was weakening. The beat makes that test harder to read, not easier. A strong result should, in theory, lift all risk assets. If Bitcoin does not follow Nvidia higher in the Asian session, the loosening-tether reading gains weight. If it does follow, the old link is intact. Either way, the answer comes in the hours after this guide is published, not before it.

Two numbers will tell the story. The first is whether Bitcoin can reclaim its 200-week moving average, now near $68,000 — the level analysts are watching as the line between a fragile bounce and a real recovery. The second is ETF flows: Monday's $7.9 million net inflow ended a seven-session outflow streak, but one day of inflows does not rebuild a bid. Watch whether the inflows continue after the earnings, or whether the $1.91 billion that left over the previous seven sessions was the start of a longer exit.

## The Cautions: A Beat Is Not a Bitcoin Signal

A strong earnings report removes one source of fear. It does not, by itself, create demand for Bitcoin. Three cautions are worth keeping in mind.

First, history warns against drawing a straight line from Nvidia's results to Bitcoin's price. Analysts who tracked Nvidia's August earnings noted that Bitcoin's rally that week was already under way before the chipmaker reported, and credited Treasury bond buybacks — the US Treasury had nearly doubled its monthly purchases of long-dated bonds — rather than the results. The beat did not create crypto demand; it confirmed a risk-on mood those dollars were already in. The same caution applies now: Bitcoin's next move will be driven by rates, ETF flows and its own positioning, not by a chipmaker's income statement.

Second, the bearish case has not gone away. Wintermute still warns that Bitcoin could face pressure toward the $50,000s, with Saturday's $62,000 low as the first level and the $60,000 put cluster as the next magnet. The firm expects the path of least resistance to remain lower while flows are negative and the 200-week average is lost. That is one market maker's view, not a fact — but it is a reminder that a single earnings beat does not erase a month of outflows, a broken technical level and a market pricing a December rate hike.

Third, the rates backdrop is unchanged. The 10-year Treasury yield is above 5%, December hike odds sit near 90%, and the minutes from the Federal Reserve's October meeting land on Wednesday — the same day Anthropic is expected to price its IPO, ahead of its Nasdaq debut on Thursday. Either of those events can move Bitcoin on its own. Nvidia's beat is one data point in a week full of them, and it would be a mistake to treat it as the only one that matters.

## The Leadership Question Is Not Over

The earnings beat answers the demand question. It does not answer the leadership question, and that question is still open.

On Tuesday, Jensen Huang said in his first public statement since resigning that he would be "honoured" to return as chief executive if the board decided it was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America. He called Nvidia his life's work, apologised for personal failings, and accepted responsibility for the problems he created for the board. On Monday, CNBC reported that the board is not considering bringing him back, and the board's independent investigation is still under way. Nvidia has also formed a board committee to oversee national security, export controls and global trade compliance — a sign of how central the company's geopolitical exposure has become.

None of this changes the earnings numbers. But it matters for what comes next, because the market's next test is not a quarterly report — it is the permanent appointment of a chief executive, and the outcome of an investigation whose findings have not yet been published. Interim CEO Colette Kress led the earnings call, and she now runs a company that has just posted the strongest quarter in its history while its board searches for a permanent leader. That tension — record results, unresolved leadership — is the story to watch into December.

## What It Means for Indian Investors

For Indian crypto users, the practical picture is unchanged by the earnings, and it is worth stating plainly. Bitcoin was trading at roughly ₹59.5–60.8 lakh on Indian exchanges on 17 November, and the India premium — the gap between what Indians pay and the world price converted at the rupee rate — was +10.56% at the 17 November daily fix, with a live reading of about +11.10% in the early hours of 18 November. That premium is a structural feature of the Indian market, driven by banking friction and limited supply, and an American chipmaker's earnings do not move it.

The tax position is also unchanged. Gains on crypto transfers are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS on sales, and those rules apply whether the market is rising or falling. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

There is one India-specific warning worth repeating. Indian investors who want exposure to Nvidia itself, rather than to Bitcoin, face a different set of rules: buying US shares means sending money abroad under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, with its own limits and paperwork, and it is taxed as a foreign equity investment, not as crypto. Do not confuse the two. A good quarter for a chipmaker is not a reason to buy its shares, and it is certainly not a reason to buy Bitcoin. The two are linked by the capital that funds them, not by any shared value — and the link has, for a month now, mostly meant that they fall together.

Indian exchange users should also remember the week's other lesson. Coinbase India has halted INR withdrawals, Unocoin has suspended all services since 9 November, and ZebPay has warned of delays — three exchanges, one week, one shared weak point in banking access. An earnings beat in California does not fix a banking outage in India. If you hold crypto on an Indian exchange, the more important question this week is whether you can withdraw it, not whether Nvidia beat its revenue guide.

## What to Watch Next

Four things will determine how this story develops, and all four are worth watching over the coming days.

The first is the Federal Reserve's October meeting minutes, due on Wednesday. The minutes will show how the committee talked about the December decision, and whether the case for a hike was as settled then as the market now assumes. A hawkish read would reinforce the rate pressure on Bitcoin; a dovish surprise would do the opposite. Either way, it is the next scheduled catalyst.

The second is Anthropic's IPO. The AI company is expected to price its shares on Wednesday ahead of a Nasdaq debut on Thursday, with a listing valued at up to $64.4 billion. The capital-rotation theory — that subscription books for the listing pulled money out of liquid crypto markets — has been blamed for part of the weekend sell-off. The pricing and the first day of trading will show whether that demand was real, and whether the money comes back to crypto once the books close.

The third is Bitcoin's own technical picture. The 200-week moving average near $68,000 is the level analysts are watching, and the $62,000 weekend low is the level Wintermute calls the first test. A reclaim of $68,000 would change the technical story; a break below $62,000 would confirm it. Neither has happened yet, and both are more important for Bitcoin's direction than any single earnings report.

The fourth is the Nvidia leadership question. The board's search for a permanent chief executive, the outcome of its independent investigation, and whether Huang's offer to return becomes a real discussion will shape the AI trade for months. A clean appointment would remove the last overhang from the stock. A prolonged search, or a contested return, would keep the uncertainty alive — and while it lasts, Bitcoin will keep sharing the AI trade's fortunes, for better or worse.

## The Bottom Line

On Tuesday 17 November 2026, Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% on a year earlier and well ahead of the roughly $109 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share against forecasts of $2.47 and fourth-quarter guidance of $130 billion against a consensus of about $125.3 billion. Data-centre revenue rose 110% to $107.6 billion. It was the company's first earnings report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, and the numbers say that AI demand is still growing at a record pace. Shares rose about 2% in after-hours trading.

For Bitcoin, the meaning is narrower than the headlines suggest. The beat removes the fear that the AI trade was collapsing, and that fear has been one of the forces dragging crypto down for a month. But Bitcoin was trading near $64,000 when the results came out, below its 200-week average near $68,000, with ETF flows only just ending a seven-session outflow streak and a December rate hike priced at around 90%. A chipmaker's earnings do not, by themselves, create demand for Bitcoin — and the analysts who warned of pressure toward the $50,000s have not changed their minds. The real test is not the beat. It is whether Bitcoin follows Nvidia higher in the sessions that follow, or whether the two finally start to move apart.
