OKXICE tokenized stocks go live on 23 November 2026. OKX and Intercontinental Exchange — the company behind the New York Stock Exchange — announced on 11 November that their joint venture's Tokenized Securities Venue will open for trading at 9 a.m. ET on Monday, 23 November, offering more than 70 tokenized US stocks and exchange-traded funds around the clock. The launch was reported the same day by CoinDesk and Unchained, and covered by Silicon Report, CoinStats, Spendnode and others. It is the second venue to open under the US Securities and Exchange Commission's five-year "Innovation Exemption" for tokenized securities — and it arrives with an unusual twist: 67 of the biggest companies in America, including Apple, Meta, Nvidia and Microsoft, have formally objected to their shares being tokenized on it. This guide explains what OKXICE is, how the trading works, why so many companies said no, and what it means — and does not mean — for Indian investors.

## What Happened: The Facts at a Glance

The facts, as reported on 11–12 November 2026 by CoinDesk, Unchained, Silicon Report, CoinStats, Spendnode and The Crypto Guide, are:

- **OKXICE is a 50-50 joint venture** between crypto exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange. The venture is co-chaired by former New York Governor Andrew Cuomo and ICE Senior Vice President of Futures Markets Trabue Bland, according to Silicon Report.
- **Its Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV) opens on 23 November 2026 at 9 a.m. ET**, for eligible US-based customers of OKX Inc., trading inside the OKX app.
- **More than 70 assets are expected at launch**, though not all of them will be available on day one, a spokesperson told CoinDesk. Named issuers include Better.com, BitGo, Circle, Coinbase, Robinhood, Securitize and Strategy — which will also list its preferred stock, including the STRC series — plus 59 exchange-traded funds from Direxion.
- **Trading runs continuously**, rather than during New York Stock Exchange hours. US equities normally settle on a T+1 cycle inside a session that runs from 09:30 to 16:00 New York time on weekdays; OKXICE runs around the clock.
- **Trading happens through automated market maker pools on Uniswap v4**, deployed on X Layer, OKX's own Ethereum layer-2 network. Securitize supplies the tokenization infrastructure and creates the tokens.
- **The venue is not a registered exchange.** OKXICE's own disclosure states that OKXICE LLC "is not a registered exchange or broker-dealer and is not approved by the SEC." It operates under the SEC's Innovation Exemption, a temporary five-year order issued on 17 September 2026 that runs until 17 September 2031.
- **Access requires identity checks.** Users must be eligible US-based OKX Inc. customers who activate the required CeDeFi Wallet, pass know-your-customer checks and complete onboarding with OKXICE.
- **Token holders keep shareholder rights.** According to CoinDesk, holders of the tokenized shares retain rights to dividends, voting and corporate actions on the underlying stock.
- **67 companies objected.** A public notice from the venture dated 5 November 2026 lists 67 public companies that submitted formal written objections, up from 50 on 30 October. The objectors include Apple, Meta, Walmart, Nvidia, JPMorgan Chase, Microsoft, Alphabet and Coca-Cola, according to The Crypto Guide and Silicon Report.
- **It is the second venue under the exemption.** CoinDesk reports OKXICE is the second venue to launch under the SEC's five-year experiment for blockchain-based securities trading, following a Canton-based tokenized securities venue earlier in November.

## What a Tokenized Stock Actually Is

A tokenized stock is a blockchain token that represents a share in a listed company. The token is meant to track the price of the underlying share, and the holder is meant to receive the economic benefits of owning it — dividends when the company pays them, a vote when shareholders vote, and the proceeds of corporate actions such as splits. What changes is the plumbing: instead of the share sitting in a brokerage account and settling through the traditional clearing system over one business day, the token sits in a crypto wallet and trades on blockchain rails at any hour.

OKXICE's version of that plumbing is specific. There is no central order book — the kind of buy-and-sell queue a stock exchange runs. Instead, trading goes through automated market maker pools on Uniswap v4, software that prices trades from the ratio of assets in a pool rather than from matching buyers with sellers. Those pools run on X Layer, OKX's Ethereum layer-2 network, and Securitize creates the tokens that represent the shares. The venture's November 5 notice states that it does not extend credit, hold private keys or conduct primary offerings — it is a secondary trading venue, not a place where companies raise new money.

The 24/7 element is the part most often highlighted in the launch coverage, and it is real: the venue trades continuously, including nights and weekends, when the New York Stock Exchange is closed. That is a genuine difference from holding the same share through a broker, where you can only trade during market hours and settlement takes a business day. Whether continuous trading is an advantage for an ordinary investor is a separate question — prices can move at 3 a.m. just as easily as at 3 p.m. — but the capability itself is new for US stocks.

There is also a limit that matters. OKXICE says more than 70 assets are confirmed, but its own announcement and its spokesperson both make clear that not all of them will trade on opening day. The confirmed roster is larger than the opening-day trading list, and some assets will become available after launch. The 59 Direxion ETFs make up most of the asset count; the individual company names are a smaller list, and they are dominated by crypto-connected businesses — Circle, Coinbase, Robinhood, Strategy, BitGo, Securitize and Better.com — rather than the broad market.

## Why 67 Companies Said No

The most striking number in the OKXICE story is not 70 assets or 23 November. It is 67: the number of public companies that formally objected to having their shares tokenized on this venue.

The mechanism is part of the SEC's Innovation Exemption. Under the exemption order, a tokenized securities venue must give 30 days' notice to the companies whose shares it wants to tokenize. A company that does not want its shares tokenized on that venue can object in writing within those 30 days — and once it does, a third-party token of its shares cannot trade on that venue. The objection does not change anything about the company's regular shares. It only keeps a token version off this one venue.

The November 5 notice listed 67 objectors, up from 50 on October 30. The names are the core of the US market: Apple, Meta, Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Walmart, JPMorgan Chase and Coca-Cola, among others. CoinDesk's count differs slightly — it reports objections from 56 of the 62 companies originally proposed — but every source agrees on the substance: most of the big names the venture wanted did not want to be there.

The notice does not give the companies' reasons, and it would be wrong to invent them. What can be said is what the objection achieves: those companies' shares will not be among the tokens trading on OKXICE. The practical result is a launch roster that leans heavily on crypto-native companies and ETFs, because those are the issuers that did not object. As Spendnode put it, the opening follows resistance from companies whose shares the venture initially wanted to include, and the resulting roster gives the launch a narrower identity — a stock-trading experiment anchored in businesses already connected to digital assets, with ETFs supplying much of the asset count.

Nasdaq has also warned that trading the same shares across disconnected platforms could fragment liquidity — the same stock trading in two places, with different prices and different pools of buyers, rather than one deep market. That warning was reported in the crypto press on 12 November, and it is the structural concern hanging over every tokenized-stock venue: a token is only as useful as the market around it, and a market split across venues can be thinner and more volatile than a single one.

## The SEC Innovation Exemption, Explained Simply

OKXICE exists because of a regulatory experiment, and understanding the experiment is essential to understanding the venue.

On 17 September 2026, the SEC issued a five-year Innovation Exemption order that lets venues trade tokenized stocks on blockchain rails without registering as national securities exchanges. The order runs until 17 September 2031. It is, as CoinStats put it, a path rather than a status: it provides a route for permissioned on-chain trading of digital securities, but it does not make the venue a licensed exchange. OKXICE's own fine print is explicit on this point — the company is not registered as an exchange or a broker-dealer, and it has not been approved by the SEC.

That distinction matters because it sets the level of protection a user gets. A registered US exchange operates under a full set of SEC rules on market surveillance, investor protection, capital and custody. A venue operating under a temporary exemption operates under the conditions of that exemption instead. The exemption is time-limited, it can be withdrawn, and the venue's own disclosure tells users plainly where they stand. None of that makes the venue illegitimate — the SEC created the exemption deliberately, as an experiment — but it does mean the venue is not the same thing as the New York Stock Exchange, even though the NYSE's parent company is a 50% owner.

OKXICE is the second venue to open under the exemption, after a Canton-based tokenized securities venue earlier in November. That sequence is worth noting: the exemption is producing venues, but each one is a separate experiment with its own roster, its own technology and its own list of objecting companies. There is no single tokenized stock market yet — there are individual venues, each negotiating its own permissions.

## What the Launch Is — and What It Is Not

Because tokenized-stock headlines are easy to overread, it is worth being precise.

**It is a trading venue, not a stock exchange.** OKXICE trades tokenized representations of shares through automated market maker pools. It is not registered as an exchange or a broker-dealer, and it says so itself.

**It is US-only at launch.** Only eligible US-based OKX Inc. customers who complete identity verification and onboarding can trade. There is no international access, no India access, and no announced date for either.

**It is 70-plus assets, not the market.** The roster is dominated by crypto-connected companies and 59 Direxion ETFs. The biggest US companies — Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, Meta, Walmart, JPMorgan — objected and will not be there.

**It is a launch, not a verdict.** The venue's real test comes on 23 November, when trading begins without the country's most heavily traded public companies. Silicon Report's summary is blunt on this point: the primary test is whether there is demand for the selection on offer, traded around the clock for eligible US customers. If volume is thin, the venue will be a niche product. If it grows, other venues and other issuers will follow.

**It is not an endorsement of tokenized stocks by the companies on the roster.** Being listed means the company did not object, not that it partnered with the venue. Strategy, Coinbase and Circle are on the list because they did not file objections; that is a different thing from an active endorsement.

## What It Means for Indian Investors

For most Indian crypto users, the direct answer is that OKXICE changes nothing you can use — but the story connects to India in a way that is worth understanding, because the same product was at the centre of the biggest Indian crypto enforcement story of the month.

**You cannot use OKXICE from India.** Access is limited to eligible US-based customers. There is no route for an Indian resident to open an account, and the venue has announced no international expansion. If you see anyone offering Indian access to OKXICE, treat it with the same caution you would apply to any offer that routes around a stated eligibility rule.

**Tokenised foreign stocks are exactly what the ED searched CoinDCX and Delta Exchange over.** On 4–5 November 2026, the Enforcement Directorate searched three premises in Bengaluru linked to CoinDCX operator Neblio Technologies and Delta Exchange India operator Excelium Technologies, under Section 37 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999. The agency alleges the platforms converted Indian users' rupee deposits into USDT and routed trades to foreign exchanges — Binance, KuCoin, Bybit and Gate.io — and that orders for tokenised stocks of foreign companies such as Apple, Google and Tesla were fulfilled as derivatives positions on the decentralised exchange Hyperliquid. The ED shared its 6 November statement on X on 12 November, which is why the story is back in the news today. Both exchanges deny wrongdoing: CoinDCX called the visit a "routine enquiry", and Delta Exchange said its operations do not involve cross-border digital asset transfers. The investigation is ongoing, no findings have been made, and the allegations are unproven. The point for Indian users is not the outcome of that case — it is that tokenised foreign stocks sold to Indian residents through offshore routes are precisely the product Indian regulators are scrutinising. A regulated US venue launching the same product for Americans does not make the offshore route legal in India.

**The legal routes to foreign shares have not changed.** Indian residents who want exposure to US stocks use the established channels under FEMA and the Reserve Bank of India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), through registered brokers and within the annual remittance limit. Those routes involve real shares held with a regulated broker, not blockchain tokens on an unregistered venue. Tokenised stocks do not create a shortcut around those rules, and using an offshore crypto route to get the same exposure is the behaviour the ED is investigating.

**The tax rules are unchanged.** Gains on crypto assets in India are taxed at a flat 30% plus a 4% cess — 31.2% in total — with no set-off of losses against other income, and sales attract 1% TDS. That applies to crypto assets, and it is separate from how foreign shares held through legal channels are taxed. A tokenized stock is a crypto asset in form and a share in substance, and that mismatch is exactly the kind of thing that creates tax and regulatory confusion. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

**The NYSE connection is the other India-relevant thread.** OKXICE is a joint venture with ICE, the NYSE's parent. Just two days earlier, the NYSE picked Avalanche to provide the core ledger technology for its own tokenised securities platform — a separate project from OKXICE, covered in our earlier guide. Two different tokenized-stock projects connected to the same exchange group, announced within days of each other, show how seriously the traditional exchange world is taking tokenization. But both are US projects, under US rules, for US customers. The Indian equivalent does not exist, and there is no sign one is being built.

**Bitcoin's rupee price is what you actually see.** Bitcoin traded near $73,600 on 12 November, roughly ₹69 lakh per coin at the current exchange rate. The dollar figures in the OKXICE coverage are global numbers; on an Indian exchange, the price you see is the dollar price converted at the rupee rate, plus any premium the exchange charges. Tokenized stocks do not change that arithmetic, and they do not change the fact that the rupee price of any dollar asset moves with the exchange rate as well as with the asset itself.

## What to Watch Next

Three things, all of them data rather than predictions, will show whether OKXICE becomes a real market or a footnote.

- **23 November itself.** The launch is the test. Which of the 70-plus assets actually trade on day one, how much volume the automated market maker pools attract, and whether prices on the venue track the underlying shares closely or drift away from them. A venue that opens with thin liquidity and wide spreads will struggle to attract the next wave of issuers.
- **Whether any objecting company changes its mind.** The 67 objections are not permanent. A company can decide later that it wants its shares tokenized on the venue, and the roster would change materially if even a few large names joined. Equally, more companies could object to future venues. The objection list is a live negotiation, not a final verdict.
- **The other venues.** OKXICE is the second venue under the SEC's Innovation Exemption, after the Canton-based venue earlier in November, and the NYSE's Avalanche project is a third, separate effort. If several venues launch and survive, tokenized stocks become a market structure rather than an experiment. If they stall, the exemption will look like a door nobody walked through.

None of these is a signal to act on by itself. They are the same kind of data a weather report gives you: useful for understanding conditions, useless as a promise about tomorrow. The sensible use of a story like this one is to understand the market you are in, not to time it.

## The Bottom Line

On 11 November 2026, OKX and Intercontinental Exchange announced that their joint venture OKXICE will open its Tokenized Securities Venue on 23 November 2026 at 9 a.m. ET, offering more than 70 tokenized US stocks and ETFs — including Coinbase, Circle, Robinhood and Strategy, plus 59 Direxion ETFs — to eligible US customers trading around the clock through Uniswap v4 pools on X Layer. Sixty-seven companies, including Apple, Meta, Nvidia and Microsoft, formally objected to their shares being tokenized on the venue, so the biggest US stocks will not be there. The venue operates under the SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption and is not a registered exchange or broker-dealer. For Indian investors, the practical points are that the venue is not available in India, that tokenised foreign stocks sold to Indians through offshore routes are the subject of the Enforcement Directorate's ongoing FEMA investigation into CoinDCX and Delta Exchange, and that the tax rules — 30% plus a 4% cess, with 1% TDS — are unchanged. The launch matters because it is the first live test of whether Americans want to trade stocks on crypto rails, around the clock, on a venue the biggest companies chose not to join.
