# Crypto Portfolio Rebalancing Explained for Indians

Imagine you start the year with a simple plan: 60% of your crypto money in Bitcoin, 30% in Ethereum, and 10% in a few other coins. Six months later, you check your portfolio and discover the plan has quietly fallen apart. Bitcoin has grown so much that it now makes up 75% of everything you hold, while the other coins have shrunk to a small slice. You did nothing wrong — prices simply moved at different speeds. **Crypto portfolio rebalancing** is the routine that puts your mix back to the plan you started with, by trimming the parts that grew too big and topping up the parts that shrank.

Think of it like a diet plan for your money. You decide the portions in advance, and every so often you check whether the portions have drifted. If they have, you gently adjust them back. You are not trying to predict which coin will rise next — you are simply keeping your risk at the level you chose when you were thinking clearly.

> **Educational content only.** This is not financial advice. Crypto markets are volatile — never invest money you can't afford to lose. Rebalancing does not guarantee profits or protect against losses.

## What Is Crypto Portfolio Rebalancing?

Rebalancing means selling a little of what has grown past its target share and buying a little of what has fallen below its target share, until your portfolio matches your original plan again. Your target allocation is simply the percentage mix you decided in advance — for example, 60% Bitcoin, 30% Ethereum, and 10% other coins.

Here is a small example. Suppose you hold ₹1,00,000 worth of crypto split 60/30/10: ₹60,000 in Bitcoin, ₹30,000 in Ethereum, and ₹10,000 in other coins. Over a few months, Bitcoin rises sharply while the others stay flat. Your portfolio is now worth ₹1,40,000, but the mix is roughly 71% Bitcoin, 21% Ethereum, and 7% other coins. To rebalance, you would sell about ₹15,000 of Bitcoin and use that money to buy roughly ₹11,000 of Ethereum and ₹4,000 of the other coins. You end up with the same total value — about ₹1,40,000 — but the mix is back to 60/30/10.

Notice what rebalancing does not do: it does not tell you which coin to buy, it does not predict prices, and it does not promise better returns. What it does is keep your risk steady. Without it, a single coin can slowly take over your portfolio, and a sharp fall in that one coin then hurts far more than you planned for.

## Why Your Allocation Drifts (Even When You Do Nothing)

Crypto prices move fast and unevenly, so drift happens quickly. Three forces cause it:

- **Different coins move at different speeds.** In the same month, one coin can rise 40% while another falls 10%. Your mix changes with every price move, even if you never touch anything.
- **New money changes the mix.** If you keep buying only your favourite coin, its share keeps growing while the rest shrink.
- **Rewards and airdrops add new assets.** Staking rewards, airdrops, or cashback in a new coin quietly add holdings you never planned for.

The table below shows how fast drift can happen with a ₹1,00,000 portfolio:

| Asset | Start value | Start share | After 6 months | New share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bitcoin | ₹60,000 | 60% | ₹1,05,000 | 75% |
| Ethereum | ₹30,000 | 30% | ₹27,000 | 19% |
| Other coins | ₹10,000 | 10% | ₹8,000 | 6% |
| **Total** | **₹1,00,000** | **100%** | **₹1,40,000** | **100%** |

Your money grew, which is good news. But your risk also changed: three-quarters of your portfolio now depends on a single coin. Rebalancing is the habit that notices this and corrects it before a single bad week does the damage.

## Two Ways to Decide When to Rebalance

There are two simple methods beginners use. Neither is "correct" — the best one is the one you will actually follow.

### 1. Calendar rebalancing

You rebalance on a fixed schedule, such as once a month or once a quarter. This is the simplest method: put a reminder on your phone, check your mix on that date, and adjust only if it has drifted. Monthly checks are usually too frequent for a small portfolio, because every rebalance costs fees. Many beginners find a quarterly check is a good balance between staying on plan and keeping costs low.

### 2. Threshold rebalancing

You rebalance only when an asset drifts past a limit you set in advance, such as 5 or 10 percentage points from its target. For example, if Bitcoin's target is 60% and it reaches 70%, that 10-point drift triggers a rebalance. If it only reaches 63%, you do nothing. This method avoids unnecessary trades when markets are calm, but it needs you to actually check your portfolio from time to time.

| Method | How it works | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calendar | Rebalance on a fixed date | Beginners who like routine | Trading when nothing has changed |
| Threshold | Rebalance when drift passes a limit | Investors who want fewer trades | Forgetting to check for months |

Some investors combine both: they check once a quarter, but only act if the drift is larger than 5 percentage points. That combination is a sensible starting point for most Indian beginners, because it keeps both the number of trades and the amount of drift under control.

## A Simple Rebalancing Walkthrough

Here is a step-by-step example using the ₹1,40,000 portfolio from the table above, with a 60/30/10 target:

1. **Write down your target.** Bitcoin 60%, Ethereum 30%, other coins 10%. Write it in a notebook or a spreadsheet so you are not guessing later.
2. **Check your current mix.** Bitcoin is 75% (₹1,05,000), Ethereum is 19% (₹27,000), other coins are 6% (₹8,000).
3. **Work out the difference.** Bitcoin is ₹21,000 above its ₹84,000 target. Ethereum is ₹15,000 below its ₹42,000 target. Other coins are ₹6,000 below their ₹14,000 target.
4. **Sell the excess.** Sell about ₹21,000 of Bitcoin on your exchange.
5. **Buy the shortfall.** Use the proceeds to buy about ₹15,000 of Ethereum and ₹6,000 of the other coins.
6. **Record the trade.** Note the date, amounts, and prices in your records. Good records make tax filing far easier later.

After these steps, your portfolio is worth roughly the same ₹1,40,000, but the mix is back to 60/30/10. The whole exercise takes about ten minutes once you know your target.

## Rebalancing Without Selling Anything

Selling is not the only way to rebalance. If you regularly add new money — for example, a monthly deposit through UPI on an Indian exchange — you can use that fresh money to fix the drift instead. If Bitcoin is overweight and Ethereum is underweight, send your next deposit to Ethereum only. Repeat this until the mix is back in line.

This approach has three advantages for Indian investors. First, it avoids extra trading fees. Second, it avoids creating a taxable sale, which matters because every sale in India can trigger 30% tax on gains and 1% TDS. Third, it is psychologically easier: many beginners find it hard to sell a coin that has just risen, even when the plan says to. Buying the lagging part with new money feels simpler, and it achieves the same result over time.

The trade-off is speed. With small monthly deposits, it can take several months to fix a large drift. If one coin has grown to dominate your portfolio, selling a part of it may still be the faster way to bring risk back under control. Some investors use a mix: fresh deposits for small drifts, and selling for large ones.

## What Rebalancing Costs in India

Rebalancing is not free, and the costs are worth knowing before you start:

- **Trading fees.** Every buy and sell on an exchange carries a fee, usually a small percentage of the trade. Two trades (one sell, one buy) mean two fees.
- **Spreads.** The gap between the buying price and the selling price on a trading pair is a hidden cost, and it is wider for less popular coins.
- **1% TDS.** Under Indian tax rules, 1% TDS is usually deducted when you sell crypto on an exchange. The deducted amount is not lost — it can be claimed as a credit when you file your return — but it does reduce the cash you receive.
- **30% tax on gains.** If the part you sell has gained value, the gain is taxed at a flat 30%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, and the loss (if any) cannot be set off against other gains or carried forward. This is often the biggest cost of rebalancing by selling.

Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

Because of these costs, rebalancing too often can eat into your portfolio. A quarterly check, or a 5-to-10-point threshold, keeps the number of trades — and the tax events — low. Keep every trade record: date, coin, amount, price in INR, and the TDS deducted. These records belong in your Schedule VDA filing, and they make life much easier at tax time.

## 5 Mistakes Beginners Make When Rebalancing

1. **Rebalancing too often.** Checking daily and trading weekly turns a discipline into a churn machine. Fees and tax events pile up, and the benefit of each tiny adjustment is small.
2. **Chasing the winner.** Some beginners "rebalance" by selling the lagging coin and buying more of the winner. That is the opposite of rebalancing — it increases concentration in one coin instead of reducing it.
3. **Ignoring tax and TDS.** A rebalance that looks sensible on paper can be expensive once 30% tax and 1% TDS are counted. Always check the tax cost before selling.
4. **Rebalancing into coins you have not researched.** Topping up a coin just because it is underweight is risky if you no longer understand or trust that coin. If your view of a coin has genuinely changed, update the target allocation first — do not blindly buy to hit an old number.
5. **Having no written target.** Without a written plan, every rebalance becomes a guess driven by the mood of the market. Write the target down, date it, and only change it for a good reason, not because of one week's price move.

## A 5-Minute Rebalancing Checklist

Use this routine every quarter, or whenever your threshold is crossed:

1. Open your exchange app or portfolio tracker and note the current value of each holding.
2. Work out each holding's percentage of the total.
3. Compare against your written target allocation.
4. If the largest drift is under 5 percentage points, do nothing and check again next quarter.
5. If the drift is larger, decide: fix it with your next deposit, or sell the excess and buy the shortfall.
6. Before selling, check the tax and TDS cost and keep the trade records.
7. After rebalancing, update your tracker and note the date for next time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I rebalance my crypto portfolio?

There is no single right answer. Many beginners use a simple calendar rule, such as once a quarter, or a threshold rule, such as rebalancing only when an asset drifts more than 5 to 10 percentage points from its target. Rebalancing too often can rack up fees and tax events, while waiting too long lets one asset dominate your portfolio. Pick a simple rule, write it down, and stick to it.

### Does rebalancing crypto trigger tax in India?

Yes, selling crypto to rebalance is a transfer under Indian tax rules, so gains are taxed at a flat 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess, and 1% TDS usually applies on the sale. Losses cannot be set off against gains or carried forward. That is why many Indian investors rebalance with fresh deposits instead of selling, so they top up the underweight part without creating a taxable sale. Tax note: crypto tax rules can change — the 30% rate and 1% TDS described here were current as of September 2026. Verify the latest rules before filing.

### Can I rebalance without selling any crypto?

Yes. If you add new money, you can direct it only to the parts of your portfolio that are below their target until the mix is back in line. For example, if Bitcoin has grown past its target share and Ethereum is below its target, your next deposit goes to Ethereum only. This keeps your allocation balanced without selling, paying extra trading fees, or triggering a tax event.

## Conclusion

Crypto portfolio rebalancing is not a way to predict the market — it is a way to keep the risk you signed up for. Decide your target mix when you are calm, check it once a quarter, and adjust only when the drift is big enough to matter. For Indian investors, the cheapest way to rebalance is usually with fresh deposits, because selling adds fees, 1% TDS, and a 30% tax bill on gains. Whatever method you choose, write it down and keep your records tidy.

Your next step is simple: open your portfolio tracker today, work out your current percentages, and write down the target mix you want. That one small act turns rebalancing from a vague idea into a routine you can actually follow.
